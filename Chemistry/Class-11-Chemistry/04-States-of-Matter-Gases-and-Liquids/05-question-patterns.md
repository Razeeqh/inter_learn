# States of Matter: Gases and Liquids — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — NAME THE INTERMOLECULAR FORCE                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What type of intermolecular force exists in / between ...?"
            or a list of pairs to classify.
  METHOD    1. Are BOTH species non-polar?  ->  DISPERSION only.
            2. Are BOTH polar?              ->  dispersion + DIPOLE-DIPOLE.
            3. One polar + one non-polar?   ->  dispersion +
                                                DIPOLE-INDUCED DIPOLE.
            4. Is H bonded to F, O or N ?   ->  add HYDROGEN BONDING.
            5. Always give an EXAMPLE - it carries marks.
  TRAP      DISPERSION FORCES ARE PRESENT IN EVERY CASE. Never write
            "only dipole-dipole". Also: HCl has NO hydrogen bonding -
            Cl is not on the "FON" list.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — COMPARE BOILING POINTS / VOLATILITY               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does A boil higher than B?" or "Arrange in order of
            increasing boiling point."
  METHOD    1. Check for HYDROGEN BONDING first. Whoever has it wins.
                 (H2O > H2S ,  HF > HCl ,  NH3 > PH3 , alcohol > alkane)
            2. If neither has H-bonding, check POLARITY.
                 polar beats non-polar of the same size.
            3. If both non-polar, the BIGGER / heavier one wins
                 (more electrons -> stronger dispersion).
                 F2 < Cl2 < Br2 < I2
            4. Same molar mass? The LONGER, less compact shape wins.
                 n-pentane (36 degC) > neopentane (9 degC)
  TRAP      Do NOT say "H2S is heavier so it boils higher". Hydrogen
            bonding beats molar mass every time.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — STATE A GAS LAW + DRAW ITS GRAPH                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Boyle's / Charles's / Gay-Lussac's / Avogadro's law"
            or "Represent it graphically".
  METHOD    1. Write the sentence in words. Include the words
               "at constant ___" and "fixed mass".
            2. Write the proportionality, then the constant form,
               then the two-state form.
            3. Draw the graph and LABEL BOTH AXES.
                 Boyle       P vs V   = hyperbola  (ISOTHERM)
                             P vs 1/V = straight line through origin
                 Charles     V vs T(K) = straight line through origin
                             (ISOBAR)
                 Gay-Lussac  P vs T(K) = straight line through origin
                             (ISOCHORE)
                 Avogadro    V vs n = straight line through origin
            4. Say which quantity is being held CONSTANT on the graph.
  TRAP      P vs V is a CURVE. P vs 1/V is a STRAIGHT LINE. Swapping
            these loses the whole graph mark. Also, T on a graph must
            be in KELVIN if the line is to pass through the origin.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — BOYLE'S LAW NUMERICAL                             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two pressures and two volumes, and the words "at the same
            temperature" or no mention of temperature at all.
  METHOD    1. Write   P1 V1 = P2 V2
            2. Put the numbers in and solve for the missing one.
            3. Units of P1 and P2 must MATCH each other (both atm, or
               both mm Hg). You do NOT have to convert to SI.
            4. Sense check: pressure up -> volume down.
  TRAP      Do not convert pressures needlessly - as long as both are in
            the same unit, the ratio is all that matters.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — CHARLES'S LAW NUMERICAL                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two temperatures and two volumes, "at constant pressure".
  METHOD    1. CONVERT BOTH TEMPERATURES TO KELVIN (t + 273).
            2. Write   V1 / T1  =  V2 / T2
            3. Solve.  V2 = V1 T2 / T1   or   T2 = T1 V2 / V1
            4. If a temperature is asked for in degC, subtract 273
               at the very end.
            5. Sense check: heated -> expands.
  TRAP      Using Celsius. This single mistake costs more marks in this
            chapter than everything else combined.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — GAY-LUSSAC NUMERICAL                              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   The words "rigid vessel", "sealed cylinder", "closed steel
            container", or "at constant volume".
  METHOD    1. Convert both temperatures to KELVIN.
            2. Write   P1 / T1  =  P2 / T2
            3. Solve for the missing quantity.
  TRAP      Spot the hidden phrase. "Steel cylinder" and "sealed vessel"
            both mean CONSTANT VOLUME even if the question never says so.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — AVOGADRO'S LAW / MOLAR VOLUME                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "at STP", "22.4 L", "how many molecules", "equal volumes of
            two gases".
  METHOD    1. n  =  V(at STP) / 22.4
            2. N  =  n x 6.022 x 10^23
            3. m  =  n x M
            4. Equal volumes at the same T and P  ->  EQUAL NUMBER OF
               MOLECULES, whatever the gas.
  TRAP      22.4 L applies ONLY at STP. At 25 degC and 1 atm the molar
            volume is 24.4 L; at 0 degC and 1 BAR it is 22.7 L.
            Read the conditions before you use 22.4.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — IDEAL GAS EQUATION NUMERICAL                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   ONE state of a gas, with three of P, V, n(or mass), T given
            and the fourth asked for.
  METHOD    1. Convert T to KELVIN.
            2. If a MASS is given, find n = m / M first.
            3. Choose R to match the units:
                  atm + litres  ->  R = 0.0821
                  bar + litres  ->  R = 0.083
                  Pa + m^3 / joules -> R = 8.314
            4. P V = n R T. Rearrange, substitute, compute.
            5. Write the UNIT of the final answer.
  TRAP      Mixing units of pressure with the wrong R. If the question
            gives mm Hg, convert to atm by dividing by 760, or use
            R = 62.4 L mm Hg K^-1 mol^-1.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — COMBINED GAS LAW NUMERICAL                        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A gas goes from "condition 1" to "condition 2" and BOTH the
            pressure and temperature change. Very often "... what volume
            at STP?"
  METHOD    1. Make two columns: P1 V1 T1  |  P2 V2 T2. Fill them in.
               STP means P = 1 atm (760 mm Hg) and T = 273 K.
            2. Convert both temperatures to KELVIN.
            3. Write  P1 V1 / T1  =  P2 V2 / T2
            4. Rearrange for the unknown, e.g.
                       V2  =  P1 V1 T2 / ( T1 P2 )
            5. Compute numerator and denominator separately.
            6. Sense check each effect (P up -> shrink, T up -> expand).
  TRAP      Only use this when the AMOUNT of gas is unchanged. If gas is
            added or removed you must use P V = n R T twice instead.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — DENSITY OR MOLAR MASS OF A GAS               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   The word "density" (g/L), "vapour density", or "find the
            molar mass of the gas".
  METHOD    1. At STP, use the one-liners:
                  d  =  M / 22.4        M  =  d x 22.4
            2. Otherwise:
                  d  =  P M / R T       M  =  d R T / P
            3. If mass and volume are given instead of density:
                  n = P V / R T   then   M = m / n
            4. Molar mass = 2 x vapour density.
  TRAP      Density of a gas is in g per LITRE, not g per mL. And keep
            'd' with 'M' - a density of 1.25 g/L never gives M = 1.25.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — PROPORTIONALITY / "WHAT HAPPENS IF" MCQ         objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "If the pressure is doubled and the temperature halved, the
            volume becomes ..."
  METHOD    Use  V2 = V1 x (P1/P2) x (T2/T1)  and multiply the factors.
            Quick reference from P V = n R T:
                 T, n fixed  ->  P ~ 1/V
                 P, n fixed  ->  V ~ T
                 V, n fixed  ->  P ~ T
                 P, T fixed  ->  V ~ n
                 P, T fixed  ->  d ~ M
                 T fixed     ->  d ~ P
                 P fixed     ->  d ~ 1/T
  TRAP      Temperatures in these questions are ALWAYS absolute. "Halve
            the temperature" means halve the KELVIN value.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — DALTON'S LAW / PARTIAL PRESSURE                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A MIXTURE of two or more gases in one container, with a
            total pressure given or asked for.
  METHOD    1. Convert every mass to MOLES (n = m / M).
            2. n(total) = sum of the moles.
            3. Total pressure, if needed:  P = n(total) R T / V
            4. Mole fraction  x(i) = n(i) / n(total)
            5. Partial pressure  p(i) = x(i) x P(total)
            6. CHECK: the partial pressures must add up to the total.
  TRAP      Mole fraction, not MASS fraction. Equal masses of H2 and O2
            do NOT give equal partial pressures - H2 gives 16/17 of the
            total, because it is 16 times lighter.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — GAS COLLECTED OVER WATER                         4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "collected over water", "aqueous tension", "moist gas".
  METHOD    1. P(dry gas) = P(measured total) - aqueous tension.
            2. Now treat that dry pressure as P1 and use the combined
               gas law to bring the gas to STP or to whatever conditions
               are asked for.
  TRAP      Subtract the aqueous tension BEFORE you do anything else.
            Forgetting it is a guaranteed 2-mark loss.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — STATE THE KINETIC MOLECULAR THEORY               4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the postulates of the kinetic molecular theory" or
            "How does the kinetic theory explain Boyle's law?"
  METHOD    1. Write the postulates as a NUMBERED list (aim for 6-9).
               Do not write them as a paragraph - numbering earns marks.
            2. The two that matter most:
                  - the volume of the molecules is NEGLIGIBLE
                  - there are NO attractive forces
               (these are exactly the two that fail for real gases)
            3. For "explain the gas laws":
                  Boyle    smaller V -> more wall hits per second -> P up
                  Charles  higher T -> faster -> must expand to keep P
                  Gay-L.   higher T at fixed V -> harder hits -> P up
                  Avogadro P depends on the NUMBER of molecules and T
                  Dalton   no attraction -> each gas acts independently
                  Graham   same KE -> heavier molecule is slower
  TRAP      "Elastic collisions" means NO LOSS of kinetic energy, not
            "no collisions". And momentum/energy is conserved, not lost.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — MOLECULAR SPEED NUMERICAL                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the rms / average / most probable speed of ..."
  METHOD    1. T to KELVIN.
            2. M to KILOGRAMS per mole  (O2 -> 0.032 , N2 -> 0.028 ,
               H2 -> 0.002 , CO2 -> 0.044) because R = 8.314 is SI.
            3. u(rms) = sqrt( 3 R T / M )
               u(av)  = sqrt( 8 R T / pi M )
               u(mp)  = sqrt( 2 R T / M )
            4. Compute the numerator, divide, then take the square root.
            5. The answer is in m/s and is normally a few hundred.
  TRAP      Using M = 32 instead of 0.032. If your answer comes out near
            15 m/s instead of 480 m/s, this is what happened.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — CONVERT BETWEEN THE THREE SPEEDS               objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "If u(rms) is 500 m/s, what is u(av)?" or "Arrange the three
            speeds in increasing order."
  METHOD    u(mp) : u(av) : u(rms)  =  1 : 1.128 : 1.224
                  u(av)  = 0.9213 x u(rms)
                  u(mp)  = 0.8165 x u(rms)
                  u(av)  = 1.128  x u(mp)
            ORDER (never changes):   u(mp)  <  u(av)  <  u(rms)
  TRAP      Do not reverse the ratio. The MOST PROBABLE speed is the
            SMALLEST of the three, even though it sounds like it should
            be the "main" one.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — COMPARE TWO GASES / TWO TEMPERATURES           objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "At what temperature will the rms speed of O2 equal that of
            H2 at 300 K?" or "Which gas moves faster?"
  METHOD    1. Use     u1 / u2  =  sqrt( T1 M2 / T2 M1 )
            2. For equal speeds, set them equal and cross-multiply:
                    T1 / M1  =  T2 / M2
            3. Remember  u ~ sqrt(T)  and  u ~ 1 / sqrt(M).
  TRAP      "4 times the temperature" gives only "2 times the speed",
            because speed depends on the SQUARE ROOT of T.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — MAXWELL DISTRIBUTION CURVE                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the Maxwell distribution of molecular speeds and show
            the effect of temperature / molar mass."
  METHOD    1. Draw axes: fraction of molecules (y) vs speed (x).
            2. Draw a curve that starts at the origin, peaks, and has a
               LONG TAIL to the right (NOT symmetrical).
            3. Mark u(mp) at the peak, then u(av), then u(rms) to its
               right.
            4. Second curve for higher T: peak moves RIGHT, becomes
               LOWER and BROADER. Label both temperatures.
            5. State: the AREA under both curves is the SAME.
            6. Molar mass version: lighter gas -> peak further RIGHT,
               curve broader and flatter.
  TRAP      Drawing a symmetrical bell curve. It must be skewed with a
            tail on the high-speed side. Also, the peak must get SHORTER
            as it moves right, not taller.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — KINETIC ENERGY AND TEMPERATURE               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the kinetic energy of n moles of a gas at ..." or
            "Compare the average KE of H2 and O2 at the same temperature."
  METHOD    1. Per mole:      KE = (3/2) R T
               For n moles:   KE = (3/2) n R T
               Per molecule:  KE = (3/2) k T ,  k = 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K
            2. Answer in joules; convert to kJ if large.
            3. COMPARISON QUESTIONS: at the same temperature the average
               KE is the SAME for every gas. No calculation needed.
  TRAP      Average KE depends ONLY on temperature. Never on molar mass,
            pressure or volume. Students routinely say "O2 has more KE
            because it is heavier" - that is wrong.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — GRAHAM'S LAW NUMERICAL                       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "diffuses", "effuses", "rate of diffusion", "how long will
            it take to escape".
  METHOD    1. Write  r1 / r2  =  sqrt( M2 / M1 )
            2. If VOLUMES and TIMES are given, first make rates:
                    rate = volume / time
               and remember  r1 / r2  =  t2 / t1  (the times FLIP).
            3. For an unknown molar mass, square both sides and solve.
            4. Vapour density may be given: M = 2 x VD.
  TRAP      The molar masses are UNDER the square root and CROSSED OVER
            (M2 goes with r1). Writing sqrt(M1/M2) inverts the answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — DEVIATION FROM IDEAL BEHAVIOUR / Z            4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the deviation of real gases", "compressibility
            factor", "Z vs P plot".
  METHOD    1. Define  Z = P V / n R T  (dimensionless).
            2. State the three cases: Z = 1 ideal, Z < 1 attraction,
               Z > 1 molecular volume/repulsion.
            3. Draw the Z vs P plot: all curves start at Z = 1 when
               P -> 0; H2 and He rise straight away; N2, CH4 and CO2
               dip first, then rise. Label the curves.
            4. Give the two reasons: molecules DO occupy volume and
               DO attract one another.
            5. End with: real gas behaves ideally at LOW PRESSURE and
               HIGH TEMPERATURE.
  TRAP      Z has NO units. And Z < 1 means MORE compressible, not less.
            Say "attraction dominates" for Z < 1 and you cannot go wrong.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — CALCULATE Z FROM DATA                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A molar volume plus P and T, and "find the compressibility
            factor", or Z given and the molar volume asked for.
  METHOD    1. Z = P V(m) / R T          with V(m) = V / n
            2. Or, quicker at STP:   Z = V(real) / 22.4
            3. Rearranged: V(m) = Z R T / P
            4. Interpret the answer in one line (attraction or size).
  TRAP      V in the formula must be the MOLAR volume. If the question
            gives the volume of 2 moles, divide by 2 first.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — VAN DER WAALS EQUATION: WRITE AND EXPLAIN    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the van der Waals equation", "What is the significance
            of a and b?", "Give the units of a and b."
  METHOD    1. ( P + a n^2 / V^2 ) ( V - n b )  =  n R T
            2. b - the volume correction; free volume = V - nb;
                   the excluded / co-volume; units L mol^-1.
            3. a - the pressure correction; molecules pull each other
                   back from the wall so the measured P is too low;
                   units atm L^2 mol^-2.
            4. Large a -> strong attraction -> easily liquefied ->
               high critical temperature.
            5. Ideal gas: a = 0 and b = 0.
  TRAP      The a-term is ADDED to P and the b-term is SUBTRACTED from V.
            Getting the signs the wrong way round loses the whole mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — VAN DER WAALS NUMERICAL                          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   a and b are given along with n, V and T, and "calculate the
            pressure".
  METHOD    1. Rearrange:     P  =  nRT/(V - nb)  -  a n^2 / V^2
            2. Compute nb, then (V - nb).
            3. Compute nRT, then divide by (V - nb).   [TERM 1]
            4. Compute a n^2, then divide by V^2.       [TERM 2]
            5. P = TERM 1 - TERM 2.
            6. Compare with the ideal value nRT/V and comment.
  TRAP      n^2, not n, in the attraction term. And V^2, not V.
            Also keep a in atm L^2 mol^-2 so that it matches R = 0.0821.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — CRITICAL CONSTANTS                            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define critical temperature / pressure / volume", "Why can
            a gas not be liquefied above Tc?", or a and b given and Tc
            asked for.
  METHOD    1. Definitions in one line each.
            2. Tc = 8a / (27 R b)    Pc = a / (27 b^2)    Vc = 3b
            3. Pc Vc / R Tc = 3/8 = 0.375 for every gas.
            4. Inverse: a = 3 Pc Vc^2 ,  b = Vc / 3.
            5. "Why not above Tc": the thermal energy exceeds the maximum
               possible intermolecular attraction, so however close you
               push the molecules they fly apart again.
  TRAP      Do NOT confuse Tc = 8a/27Rb with the BOYLE temperature
            TB = a/Rb. They look similar and are different questions.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — ANDREWS ISOTHERMS OF CO2                         8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the liquefaction of gases using Andrews' isotherms",
            "critical point", "the meniscus disappears".
  METHOD    1. Axes: P (y) against V (x). Draw four or five curves for
               different fixed temperatures.
            2. Low-temperature curves: gas region on the right, then a
               FLAT horizontal portion (liquid + vapour together), then
               a nearly VERTICAL rise (liquid only).
            3. As T rises the flat portion gets SHORTER.
            4. At 31.1 degC it shrinks to a single POINT C = the
               CRITICAL POINT (point of inflexion). Mark Pc and Vc.
            5. Above 31.1 degC the curves are smooth hyperbolas with NO
               flat portion - no liquefaction is possible.
            6. Label the three regions: LIQUID, LIQUID+VAPOUR, GAS.
            7. State the CO2 values: Tc = 31.1 degC, Pc = 73 atm,
               Vc = 95.6 cm^3/mol.
  TRAP      Along the flat portion the PRESSURE STAYS CONSTANT while the
            volume falls. That constant pressure is the vapour pressure
            of liquid CO2 at that temperature - say so and gain a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — VAPOUR PRESSURE AND BOILING POINT             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define vapour pressure", "What is the effect of pressure on
            the boiling point?", pressure cooker / hill station questions.
  METHOD    1. Vapour pressure = pressure of vapour in EQUILIBRIUM with
               its own liquid at a given temperature.
            2. Factors: increases with TEMPERATURE; higher for liquids
               with WEAK intermolecular forces; NO effect of surface
               area or amount of liquid.
            3. Boiling point = the temperature at which vapour pressure
               EQUALS the external pressure.
            4. External pressure UP -> boiling point UP (pressure cooker,
               water boils near 120 degC).
               External pressure DOWN -> boiling point DOWN (a hill
               station, water boils near 82 degC).
            5. Evaporation: any temperature, surface only.
               Boiling: one temperature, throughout the liquid.
  TRAP      On a mountain water boils at a LOWER temperature - so food
            is UNDERCOOKED. Students often say "it boils faster so it
            cooks faster". Wrong: it boils sooner but is NOT as hot.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — SURFACE TENSION                               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define surface tension", "Give its units", "Why are drops
            spherical?", "Effect of temperature on surface tension."
  METHOD    1. Definition: force per unit LENGTH acting at right angles
               to an imaginary line on the surface (= work needed to
               increase the surface area by one unit).
            2. Units: N m^-1 = J m^-2 (SI) ; dyne cm^-1 (CGS).
               Dimensions M T^-2.
            3. Cause: a surface molecule has no molecules above it, so
               it feels a NET INWARD pull; the liquid minimises its area.
            4. Spherical drops: for a given VOLUME a SPHERE has the
               SMALLEST surface area, hence the lowest surface energy.
            5. Temperature UP -> surface tension DOWN (zero at Tc).
            6. Examples: insects on water, capillary rise, mercury beads,
               detergents lowering surface tension.
  TRAP      Surface tension is force per unit LENGTH, not per unit area.
            Its unit is N/m, not N/m^2 (that is pressure).
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — VISCOSITY                                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define viscosity / the coefficient of viscosity", "Give its
            units", "Effect of temperature on viscosity."
  METHOD    1. Viscosity = the resistance of a liquid to flow, due to
               internal friction between the layers.
            2. F = eta x A x (du/dz)
            3. Coefficient of viscosity = force per unit area needed to
               maintain a unit velocity difference between two parallel
               layers a unit distance apart.
            4. Units: N s m^-2 = Pa s = kg m^-1 s^-1 (SI) ;
                      POISE = g cm^-1 s^-1 (CGS) ; 1 poise = 0.1 Pa s.
            5. Temperature UP -> viscosity DOWN.
            6. Increases with hydrogen bonding (glycerol) and with
               longer molecular chains (heavy oils).
  TRAP      Viscosity goes DOWN on heating; vapour pressure goes UP.
            Learn "heat it: pressure up, the other two down."
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — THREE STATES COMPARISON TABLE                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare the properties of solids, liquids and gases" or
            "How do intermolecular forces determine the state of matter?"
  METHOD    1. Open with the BALANCE:
                  forces >> thermal energy  ->  SOLID
                  forces ~= thermal energy  ->  LIQUID
                  forces << thermal energy  ->  GAS
            2. Draw a three-column table covering: shape, volume,
               intermolecular forces, distance, type of motion, density,
               compressibility, diffusion.
            3. One line on what happens on heating and on cooling.
  TRAP      Write it as a TABLE, not a paragraph. Examiners award marks
            per row; a paragraph makes them hunt for the points.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — DERIVE THE IDEAL GAS EQUATION                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Derive PV = nRT from the gas laws" or "What is the equation
            of state?"
  METHOD    1. Boyle    V ~ 1/P      (T, n constant)
            2. Charles  V ~ T        (P, n constant)
            3. Avogadro V ~ n        (P, T constant)
            4. Combine: V ~ nT/P  ->  V = nRT/P  ->  P V = n R T
            5. Give R its values and show one calculation of R from
               STP data (1 atm, 22.4 L, 273.15 K -> 0.0821).
            6. Mention that it is called the EQUATION OF STATE.
  TRAP      You must show all THREE laws being combined. Jumping
            straight to PV = nRT gets only 1 mark of the 4.
```

```
  PATTERN 32 — KINETIC GAS EQUATION AND KE-TEMPERATURE LINK     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the kinetic gas equation" or "Show that the average
            kinetic energy of a gas is proportional to its absolute
            temperature."
  METHOD    1. State  P V  =  (1/3) m N c^2
               m = mass of one molecule, N = number of molecules,
               c^2 = mean square speed.
            2. For 1 mole, m NA = M , so  P V = (1/3) M c^2 .
            3. Total KE = (1/2) m N c^2 = (3/2) P V .
            4. For 1 mole P V = R T, so  KE = (3/2) R T .
            5. Per molecule: KE = (3/2) k T with k = R/NA.
            6. CONCLUSION: average KE depends only on T, and is the same
               for all gases at the same temperature.
            7. Rearranging step 2 also gives u(rms) = sqrt(3RT/M).
  TRAP      c^2 is the MEAN SQUARE speed, and c = u(rms) - not the
            average speed. Do not write (1/3) m N c.
```
