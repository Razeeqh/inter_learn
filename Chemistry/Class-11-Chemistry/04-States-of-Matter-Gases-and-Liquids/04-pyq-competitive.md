# PATTERN 1 — Intermolecular forces

### Q1
The only intermolecular force present between two argon atoms is

(a) dipole-dipole &nbsp; (b) hydrogen bonding &nbsp; (c) London dispersion &nbsp; (d) ion-dipole

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Argon is a non-polar monatomic gas -> it has NO permanent dipole and
  no H attached to F/O/N. Only instantaneous-dipole (London) forces act.
```

---

### Q2
Which of the following has the highest boiling point?

(a) F2 &nbsp; (b) Cl2 &nbsp; (c) Br2 &nbsp; (d) I2

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
  All are non-polar -> only dispersion forces.
  Dispersion force INCREASES with size / number of electrons.
  F2 < Cl2 < Br2 < I2      ->  I2 has the highest boiling point.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** For a family of NON-POLAR molecules, "biggest = highest boiling point".
> No thinking required.

---

### Q3
Hydrogen bonding is NOT present in

(a) HF &nbsp; (b) H2O &nbsp; (c) NH3 &nbsp; (d) HCl

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
  Hydrogen bonding needs H bonded to F, O or N ("FON").
  Chlorine is too big and not electronegative enough. HCl has only
  dipole-dipole + dispersion forces.
```

---

### Q4
Which pair shows dipole-induced dipole interaction?

(a) H2O and H2O &nbsp; (b) HCl and Ar &nbsp; (c) He and Ne &nbsp; (d) HCl and HCl

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Dipole-induced dipole needs ONE POLAR + ONE NON-POLAR species.
  HCl is polar, Ar is non-polar.
```

---

### Q5
The correct increasing order of the strength of the interactions is

(a) dipole-dipole < dispersion < H-bond
(b) dispersion < dipole-dipole < H-bond
(c) H-bond < dipole-dipole < dispersion
(d) dispersion < H-bond < dipole-dipole

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  dispersion  <  dipole-induced dipole  <  dipole-dipole  <  hydrogen bond
```

# PATTERN 2 — Boyle / Charles / Gay-Lussac one-liners

### Q6
A gas occupies 1 L at 1 atm. At 4 atm and the same temperature its volume is

(a) 4 L &nbsp; (b) 2 L &nbsp; (c) 0.5 L &nbsp; (d) 0.25 L

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   P1 V1 = P2 V2   ->   V2 = (1 x 1) / 4  =  0.25 L
```

---

### Q7
At constant pressure, at what temperature will the volume of a gas at 27 degC
become double?

(a) 54 degC &nbsp; (b) 327 degC &nbsp; (c) 600 degC &nbsp; (d) 273 degC

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   T1 = 27 + 273 = 300 K.
   Volume doubles -> absolute temperature doubles ->  T2 = 600 K
   t2  =  600 - 273  =  327 degC
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "Double the volume" NEVER means "double the Celsius temperature".
> Convert to kelvin, double, convert back. This trap is set every single year.

---

### Q8
A sealed rigid cylinder of gas at 1 atm and 27 degC is heated to 327 degC.
The new pressure is

(a) 1 atm &nbsp; (b) 2 atm &nbsp; (c) 4 atm &nbsp; (d) 12 atm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Rigid vessel -> volume constant -> Gay-Lussac
   T1 = 300 K , T2 = 600 K
   P2 = P1 x T2/T1  =  1 x 600/300  =  2 atm
```

---

### Q9
Which of the following graphs is a straight line passing through the origin?

(a) P vs V &nbsp; (b) P vs 1/V &nbsp; (c) PV vs V &nbsp; (d) V vs 1/T

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   P = k (1/V)  ->  of the form y = mx  ->  straight line through origin.
   P vs V is a hyperbola; PV vs V is a horizontal line; V vs 1/T is a
   hyperbola-type curve.
```

---

### Q10
The temperature at which the volume of a gas theoretically becomes zero is

(a) 0 degC &nbsp; (b) -273.15 degC &nbsp; (c) 273 K &nbsp; (d) -100 degC

**ANSWER: (b)** — absolute zero, the zero of the kelvin scale.

# PATTERN 3 — The ideal gas equation

### Q11
The pressure exerted by 4.4 g of CO2 in a 2 L vessel at 27 degC is

(a) 0.62 atm &nbsp; (b) 1.23 atm &nbsp; (c) 2.46 atm &nbsp; (d) 12.3 atm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   n  =  4.4 / 44   =   0.1 mol

          n R T        0.1  x  0.0821  x  300        2.463
   P  =  --------  =  -------------------------  =  --------  = 1.23 atm
            V                    2                     2
```

---

### Q12
The volume occupied by 2 g of hydrogen at STP is

(a) 11.2 L &nbsp; (b) 22.4 L &nbsp; (c) 44.8 L &nbsp; (d) 5.6 L

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   n = 2/2 = 1 mol   ->   V = 1 x 22.4 = 22.4 L
```

---

### Q13
The number of molecules in 1 L of a gas at STP is

(a) 6.022 x 10^23 &nbsp; (b) 2.688 x 10^22 &nbsp; (c) 2.688 x 10^23 &nbsp; (d) 22.4 x 10^23

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   n = 1 / 22.4 = 0.04464 mol
   N = 0.04464 x 6.022 x 10^23  =  2.688 x 10^22
```

---

### Q14
If the pressure and the absolute temperature of a fixed mass of gas are both
doubled, its volume

(a) doubles &nbsp; (b) halves &nbsp; (c) stays the same &nbsp; (d) becomes 4 times

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
    P1 V1 / T1  =  P2 V2 / T2

    V2 = V1 x (P1/P2) x (T2/T1)  =  V1 x (1/2) x (2)  =  V1
    The two effects cancel exactly.
```

---

### Q15
The value of R in SI units is

(a) 0.0821 &nbsp; (b) 8.314 &nbsp; (c) 1.987 &nbsp; (d) 62.4

**ANSWER: (b)** — `R = 8.314 J K^-1 mol^-1` (SI). The others are L atm, cal and
L mm Hg units respectively.

# PATTERN 4 — Density and molar mass of a gas

### Q16
The density of oxygen at STP is

(a) 1.43 g/L &nbsp; (b) 0.71 g/L &nbsp; (c) 2.86 g/L &nbsp; (d) 32 g/L

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
              M            32
     d  =  --------  =  --------  =  1.428  ~  1.43 g/L
             22.4         22.4
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Density at STP = molar mass / 22.4. One division, done.

---

### Q17
A gas is 1.5 times as dense as oxygen at the same temperature and pressure.
Its molar mass is

(a) 16 &nbsp; (b) 24 &nbsp; (c) 48 &nbsp; (d) 64

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   At the same T and P,  d ~ M      ->     M = 1.5 x 32  =  48 g/mol
```

---

### Q18
The molar mass of a gas whose density is 1.964 g/L at STP is

(a) 22 &nbsp; (b) 28 &nbsp; (c) 44 &nbsp; (d) 64

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   M  =  d x 22.4  =  1.964 x 22.4  =  43.99  ~  44   (CO2)
```

---

### Q19
At constant pressure, the density of a gas is

(a) directly proportional to T &nbsp; (b) inversely proportional to T
(c) independent of T &nbsp; (d) proportional to T^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
          P M
    d  = -------      at constant P and fixed M   ->   d ~ 1/T
          R T

    Hot air is less dense - which is why hot-air balloons rise.
```

# PATTERN 5 — Dalton's law of partial pressures

### Q20
A vessel contains 1.6 g of O2 and 0.2 g of H2 at a total pressure of 3 atm.
The partial pressure of H2 is

(a) 1 atm &nbsp; (b) 1.5 atm &nbsp; (c) 2 atm &nbsp; (d) 2.5 atm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   n(O2) = 1.6/32 = 0.05      n(H2) = 0.2/2 = 0.10
   n(total) = 0.15

   x(H2) = 0.10/0.15 = 0.667

   p(H2) = 0.667 x 3  =  2 atm
```

---

### Q21
A gas is collected over water at 25 degC. The total pressure is 760 mm Hg and
the aqueous tension is 24 mm Hg. The pressure of the dry gas is

(a) 784 mm &nbsp; (b) 760 mm &nbsp; (c) 736 mm &nbsp; (d) 24 mm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   P(dry)  =  760  -  24   =   736 mm Hg
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "Collected over water" -> **SUBTRACT** the aqueous tension. Always.

---

### Q22
Equal masses of H2 and O2 are placed in a container. The fraction of the total
pressure contributed by H2 is

(a) 1/2 &nbsp; (b) 8/9 &nbsp; (c) 16/17 &nbsp; (d) 1/16

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Take mass m of each.
       n(H2) = m/2        n(O2) = m/32
       ratio = (m/2) : (m/32)  =  16 : 1

       x(H2) = 16 / 17

   So H2 contributes 16/17 of the total pressure.
```

---

### Q23
The partial pressure of a gas in a mixture is equal to

(a) total pressure x mole fraction &nbsp; (b) total pressure / mole fraction
(c) total pressure x mass fraction &nbsp; (d) total pressure x volume

**ANSWER: (a)** — `p(i) = x(i) x P(total)`

# PATTERN 6 — Kinetic theory and kinetic energy

### Q24
Which of the following is NOT a postulate of the kinetic molecular theory?

(a) collisions are perfectly elastic
(b) molecules attract one another strongly
(c) the volume of the molecules is negligible
(d) average KE is proportional to absolute temperature

**ANSWER: (b)** — the theory assumes there are **no** attractive forces.
That assumption is exactly why real gases deviate.

---

### Q25
At the same temperature, the average kinetic energy of H2 and O2 molecules

(a) is greater for H2 &nbsp; (b) is greater for O2 &nbsp; (c) is equal
(d) depends on the pressure

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   KE = (3/2) k T per molecule.
   It depends ONLY on temperature, not on the identity of the gas.
   (H2 is FASTER, but its average kinetic ENERGY is the same.)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "Same temperature" -> **same average kinetic energy**, always.
> Speeds differ; energies do not.

---

### Q26
The average kinetic energy of one molecule of any gas at 27 degC is
(k = 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K)

(a) 6.21 x 10^-21 J &nbsp; (b) 3.74 x 10^3 J &nbsp; (c) 4.14 x 10^-21 J &nbsp; (d) 2.07 x 10^-23 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   KE = (3/2) k T = 1.5 x 1.38 x 10^-23 x 300
      = 2.07 x 10^-23 x 300
      = 6.21 x 10^-21  J
```

---

### Q27
If the absolute temperature of a gas is doubled, the average kinetic energy
of its molecules becomes

(a) half &nbsp; (b) double &nbsp; (c) four times &nbsp; (d) sqrt(2) times

**ANSWER: (b)** — `KE ~ T`, so doubling T doubles KE.
(The rms *speed* becomes only `sqrt(2)` times larger — do not confuse the two.)

# PATTERN 7 — Molecular speeds

### Q28
The root mean square speed of hydrogen molecules at 27 degC is about
(R = 8.314 J/K/mol)

(a) 193 m/s &nbsp; (b) 484 m/s &nbsp; (c) 1934 m/s &nbsp; (d) 19340 m/s

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   M(H2) = 2 g/mol = 0.002 kg/mol , T = 300 K

                  +----------------------------
                 /   3 x 8.314 x 300              +-----------------
   u(rms)  =    /  --------------------      =   /  3 741 300
              \/         0.002                 \/

           =  1934  m/s
```

---

### Q29
The ratio u(mp) : u(av) : u(rms) is

(a) 1 : 1.128 : 1.224 &nbsp; (b) 1 : 1.224 : 1.128 &nbsp; (c) 1.128 : 1 : 1.224 &nbsp; (d) 1 : 2 : 3

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   sqrt(2) : sqrt(8/pi) : sqrt(3)  =  1.414 : 1.596 : 1.732
   Divide by 1.414                 =  1 : 1.128 : 1.224

   ALWAYS:   u(mp)  <  u(av)  <  u(rms)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "mp is the smallest, rms is the biggest, av is in between."
> That one sentence answers most speed-comparison MCQs without any arithmetic.

At what temperature will the rms speed of oxygen equal the rms speed of

(a) 300 K &nbsp; (b) 1200 K &nbsp; (c) 4800 K &nbsp; (d) 18.75 K

**ANSWER: (c)**

     3 R T(O2)        3 R T(H2)

### Q31
If the temperature of a gas is increased four times, the rms speed becomes

(a) 2 times &nbsp; (b) 4 times &nbsp; (c) 16 times &nbsp; (d) unchanged

**ANSWER: (a)** — `u(rms) ~ sqrt(T)`, and `sqrt(4) = 2`.

---

### Q32
In the Maxwell distribution curve, on raising the temperature the peak

(a) shifts right and becomes taller &nbsp; (b) shifts right and becomes shorter and broader
(c) shifts left and becomes taller &nbsp; (d) does not move

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Higher T -> molecules faster (peak moves RIGHT) and the spread of
   speeds is bigger (peak LOWER and BROADER). The AREA stays the same,
   because the number of molecules has not changed.
```

# PATTERN 8 — Graham's law

### Q33
The rate of diffusion of hydrogen compared with oxygen is

(a) 1/4 &nbsp; (b) 4 &nbsp; (c) 16 &nbsp; (d) 1/16

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
    r(H2)        +--------          +-----
   -------  =   /   32      =      /  16    =   4
    r(O2)     \/    2            \/
```

---

### Q34
A gas diffuses twice as fast as oxygen. Its molar mass is

(a) 4 &nbsp; (b) 8 &nbsp; (c) 16 &nbsp; (d) 64

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
    2  =  sqrt( 32 / M )     ->    4  =  32 / M    ->    M  =  8 g/mol
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "n times faster" -> divide the known molar mass by `n^2`.
> "n times slower" -> multiply by `n^2`.

---

### Q35
Equal volumes of helium and methane effuse through a pinhole. If helium takes
10 seconds, methane takes about

(a) 5 s &nbsp; (b) 10 s &nbsp; (c) 20 s &nbsp; (d) 40 s

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   M(He) = 4 ,  M(CH4) = 16

    r(He)          +---------         +-----
   --------  =    /   16     =       /  4     =   2
    r(CH4)      \/     4           \/

   Rate ratio 2 means the TIME ratio is reversed:

    t(CH4)  =  2  x  t(He)  =  2 x 10  =  20 s
```

### Q36
In the classic experiment, NH3 and HCl gases are released from opposite ends
of a long tube. The white ring of NH4Cl forms

(a) exactly in the middle &nbsp; (b) nearer the NH3 end
(c) nearer the HCl end &nbsp; (d) at the NH3 end

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   M(NH3) = 17 ,  M(HCl) = 36.5

    r(NH3)         +-----------
   --------  =    /   36.5      =   sqrt(2.147)  =  1.465
    r(HCl)      \/     17

   NH3 is LIGHTER, so it travels FASTER and covers MORE distance.
   The two gases meet nearer the HCl end.
```

# PATTERN 9 — Real gases and the compressibility factor

### Q37
For a real gas, Z > 1 indicates that

(a) attractive forces dominate &nbsp; (b) the gas is more compressible than ideal
(c) molecular volume / repulsion dominates &nbsp; (d) the gas is ideal

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Z > 1  ->  V(real) > V(ideal)  ->  the gas is HARDER to compress
              -> the finite size of the molecules is dominating.
   Z < 1  ->  attraction dominates.
```

---

### Q38
One mole of a gas at STP has a molar volume of 11.2 L. Its compressibility
factor is

(a) 2 &nbsp; (b) 1 &nbsp; (c) 0.5 &nbsp; (d) 0.25

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
            V(real)         11.2
    Z  =  -----------  =  --------  =  0.5
            V(ideal)        22.4
```

---

### Q39
Which of the following gases shows the LEAST deviation from ideal behaviour?

(a) CO2 &nbsp; (b) NH3 &nbsp; (c) SO2 &nbsp; (d) He

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   Least deviation = smallest size + weakest attraction + smallest 'a'.
   He has a = 0.034 (the smallest of any gas) and the lowest critical
   temperature (5.3 K). It is the most nearly ideal real gas.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "Easily liquefied" = "deviates most". H2/He deviate least;
> NH3/SO2/CO2 deviate most.

### Q40
A real gas most closely approaches ideal behaviour at

(a) high P, low T &nbsp; (b) low P, high T &nbsp; (c) high P, high T &nbsp; (d) low P, low T

**ANSWER: (b)** — **LOW pressure and HIGH temperature.** Learn this as one phrase.

# PATTERN 10 — The van der Waals equation

### Q41
The units of the van der Waals constant 'a' are

(a) L mol^-1 &nbsp; (b) atm L^2 mol^-2 &nbsp; (c) atm L mol^-1 &nbsp; (d) dimensionless

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   a n^2 / V^2 must have the units of PRESSURE.
   So  a  =  P x V^2 / n^2   ->   atm L^2 mol^-2

   And b must have the units of volume per mole  ->  L mol^-1
```

---

### Q42
Which gas has the largest van der Waals constant 'a'?

(a) H2 &nbsp; (b) He &nbsp; (c) NH3 &nbsp; (d) N2

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   'a' measures the intermolecular ATTRACTION.
   NH3 has hydrogen bonding -> the strongest attraction of the four,
   the largest a (4.17), and the highest critical temperature.
```

---

### Q43
At very HIGH pressure, the van der Waals equation for one mole reduces to

(a) PV = RT &nbsp; (b) P(V - b) = RT &nbsp; (c) (P + a/V^2)V = RT &nbsp; (d) PV = RT - a/V

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   At high pressure V is very SMALL, so P is huge and the term a/V^2
   is negligible next to P.

        ( P + a/V^2 ) ( V - b ) = R T      ->      P ( V - b ) = R T

   Expanding:  P V = R T + P b   ->   Z = 1 + P b / R T   ->   Z > 1
```

---

### Q44
At very LOW pressure, the van der Waals equation for one mole becomes

(a) P(V - b) = RT &nbsp; (b) (P + a/V^2) V = RT &nbsp; (c) PV = RT &nbsp; (d) PV = nRT + a

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   At low pressure V is very LARGE, so b is negligible next to V:

        ( P + a/V^2 ) V  =  R T
        P V  =  R T  -  a / V     ->     Z = 1 - a/(V R T)   ->   Z < 1
```

---

### Q45
For an ideal gas the van der Waals constants are

(a) a = 0, b = 0 &nbsp; (b) a = 1, b = 1 &nbsp; (c) a large, b small &nbsp; (d) a = 0, b large

**ANSWER: (a)** — with `a = 0` and `b = 0` the equation collapses to `PV = nRT`.

If b = 0.04 L/mol for a gas, its critical volume is

(a) 0.04 L &nbsp; (b) 0.12 L &nbsp; (c) 0.013 L &nbsp; (d) 0.4 L

**ANSWER: (b)** — `V(c) = 3b = 3 x 0.04 = 0.12 L per mole`

The critical compressibility factor `P(c)V(c) / RT(c)` for a van der Waals gas is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 0.375 &nbsp; (c) 0.5 &nbsp; (d) 8/3

**ANSWER: (b)** — it equals `3/8 = 0.375` for **every** gas.

### Q49
Which of these gases is the MOST easily liquefied?

(a) H2 (Tc = 33 K) &nbsp; (b) N2 (Tc = 126 K) &nbsp; (c) O2 (Tc = 154 K) &nbsp; (d) NH3 (Tc = 405 K)

**ANSWER: (d)** — the higher the critical temperature, the stronger the
intermolecular forces and the easier the liquefaction.

### Q50
A gaseous substance above its critical temperature

(a) can be liquefied by pressure alone &nbsp; (b) cannot be liquefied by pressure alone
(c) is called a vapour &nbsp; (d) has zero pressure

**ANSWER: (b)** — above `T(c)` it is called a **gas**, and no pressure will

# PATTERN 12 — The liquid state

### Q51
The SI unit of surface tension is

(a) dyne cm^-1 &nbsp; (b) N m^-1 &nbsp; (c) N m^-2 &nbsp; (d) Pa s

**ANSWER: (b)** — `N m^-1` (equivalently `J m^-2`). `dyne cm^-1` is the CGS unit;
`N m^-2` is pressure; `Pa s` is viscosity.

---

### Q52
On raising the temperature of a liquid,

(a) surface tension and viscosity both increase
(b) surface tension and viscosity both decrease
(c) surface tension increases, viscosity decreases
(d) vapour pressure decreases

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   ON HEATING:   vapour pressure   UP
                 surface tension   DOWN
                 viscosity         DOWN
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "Heat it: pressure up, the other two down." One phrase, three marks.

---

### Q53
Liquid drops are spherical because

(a) gravity acts on them &nbsp; (b) of viscosity
(c) surface tension minimises the surface area &nbsp; (d) of high vapour pressure

**ANSWER: (c)** — a sphere has the **least surface area** for a given volume,
so it has the lowest surface energy.

---

### Q54
The CGS unit of the coefficient of viscosity is

(a) poise &nbsp; (b) newton &nbsp; (c) dyne cm^-1 &nbsp; (d) pascal

**ANSWER: (a)** — `1 poise = g cm^-1 s^-1 = 0.1 Pa s`

---

### Q55
Which liquid has the highest viscosity?

(a) water &nbsp; (b) ether &nbsp; (c) glycerol &nbsp; (d) acetone

**ANSWER: (c)** — glycerol has **three -OH groups**, so it forms an extensive
hydrogen-bonded network and flows very slowly.

---

### Q56
The vapour pressure of a liquid

(a) increases with surface area &nbsp; (b) increases with the amount of liquid
(c) increases with temperature &nbsp; (d) decreases with temperature

**ANSWER: (c)** — vapour pressure depends **only on the liquid and the
temperature**, never on surface area or on how much liquid is present.

---

### Q57
Water boils at a lower temperature on a hill because

(a) the atmospheric pressure is lower there &nbsp; (b) the air is colder
(c) water is purer there &nbsp; (d) the vapour pressure of water is lower there

**ANSWER: (a)** — boiling occurs when vapour pressure = external pressure.
Lower external pressure is reached at a lower temperature.

  |  Both P and T change                 |  P1V1/T1 = P2V2/T2                     |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Mass in grams given                 |  n = m / M first, then PV = nRT        |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "at STP" + a volume                 |  n = V / 22.4                          |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "at STP" + a density                |  M = d x 22.4                          |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Density asked at given P and T      |  d = P M / R T                         |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  P in atm and V in litres            |  use R = 0.0821                        |
  |  Anything in SI / joules / m/s       |  use R = 8.314 (and M in kg/mol!)      |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "collected over water"              |  SUBTRACT the aqueous tension          |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  A mixture of gases                  |  p(i) = x(i) x P(total)                |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "same temperature", KE compared     |  the answer is EQUAL. No calculation.  |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Compare speeds of two gases         |  u ~ sqrt(T / M). Lighter = faster.    |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  u(mp), u(av), u(rms) compared       |  1 : 1.128 : 1.224 , mp smallest       |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "diffuses / effuses n times faster" |  divide the molar mass by n^2          |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Times given instead of rates        |  r1/r2 = t2/t1  (they FLIP)            |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Z < 1                               |  ATTRACTION dominates                  |
  |  Z > 1                               |  molecular SIZE / repulsion dominates  |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "behaves ideally"                   |  LOW pressure, HIGH temperature        |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "deviates most / least"             |  most = easily liquefied (NH3, SO2,    |
  |                                      |  CO2). least = H2, He, Ne              |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Units of a or b                     |  a: atm L^2 mol^-2   b: L mol^-1       |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  van der Waals at HIGH pressure      |  P(V - b) = RT ,  Z = 1 + Pb/RT        |
  |  van der Waals at LOW pressure       |  PV = RT - a/V ,  Z = 1 - a/(VRT)      |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Critical constants asked            |  Tc = 8a/27Rb , Pc = a/27b^2 , Vc = 3b |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  "most easily liquefied"             |  the one with the HIGHEST Tc (or 'a')  |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Heating a liquid                    |  vapour pressure UP, surface tension   |
  |                                      |  DOWN, viscosity DOWN                  |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
  |  Surface tension unit                |  N m^-1 (SI) / dyne cm^-1 (CGS)        |
  |  Viscosity unit                      |  Pa s (SI) / poise (CGS)               |
  +--------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
