## Topic: Basic terms

### Q A1
Define environmental pollution and a pollutant.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ENVIRONMENTAL POLLUTION - the undesirable change in the physical,
  chemical or biological characteristics of air, water or soil which
  harms human life and other living organisms.

  POLLUTANT - a substance present in the environment in a concentration
  HIGHER than its natural level, which produces a harmful effect.
      Example: excess SO2 in air.
```

---

### Q A2
What is a sink? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A SINK is the medium (or material) that RETAINS, absorbs or interacts
  with a pollutant for a long period, removing it from circulation.

  Examples:  the ocean is a sink for CO2
             marble of buildings is a sink for the H2SO4 of acid rain
             soil and plants are sinks for many gaseous pollutants
```

---

### Q A3
What is a receptor? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A RECEPTOR is the medium or organism that is AFFECTED by a pollutant.

  Example:  man is the receptor of air pollutants such as SO2 and CO.
            A lake is the receptor of the effluent discharged into it.
```

---

### Q A4
Define speciation. Why does it matter?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SPECIATION is the occurrence of an element in DIFFERENT CHEMICAL FORMS
  in the environment.

  It matters because the TOXICITY depends on the form, not just on the
  element:
       Chromium(III)  ->  relatively harmless, even essential in traces
       Chromium(VI)   ->  highly toxic and carcinogenic

  Similarly mercury as the metal is far less dangerous than mercury as
  methyl mercury.
```

---

### Q A5
Distinguish between a pollutant and a contaminant.

**ANSWER:**
```
  POLLUTANT   - a substance that occurs naturally, but is now present
                in a HIGHER than normal concentration and causes harm.
                Example: CO2 in the atmosphere.

  CONTAMINANT - a substance that does NOT occur in nature at that place
                at all, and has been introduced by human activity.
                Example: DDT in a river.

  A contaminant becomes a pollutant once it starts causing harm.
```

---

### Q A6
What are biodegradable and non-biodegradable pollutants? Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BIODEGRADABLE POLLUTANTS - decomposed rapidly by micro-organisms into
  simple harmless substances.
       Example: domestic sewage (also cow dung, paper, vegetable waste)

  NON-BIODEGRADABLE POLLUTANTS - NOT decomposed by micro-organisms; they
  persist for very long periods and accumulate in the food chain, so
  they are more harmful.
       Example: DDT (also plastics, mercury, lead, glass)
```

### Q A7
Name the segments of the environment.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. ATMOSPHERE   -  the layer of air surrounding the Earth
  2. HYDROSPHERE  -  all the water: oceans, rivers, lakes, groundwater
  3. LITHOSPHERE  -  the solid crust: soil, rocks, minerals
  4. BIOSPHERE    -  all living organisms and the region where the other
                     three segments interact to support life
```

Name the layers of the atmosphere in order from the Earth's surface.

**ANSWER:**
```

  2. STRATOSPHERE  (10 - 50 km)    temperature INCREASES  (ozone layer)
```

## Topic: Air pollution

### Q A9
Why is carbon monoxide a poisonous gas?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CO binds to the haemoglobin of the blood about 300 TIMES more strongly
  than oxygen does, forming a stable complex called CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN.

        Hb  +  CO   ---->   carboxyhaemoglobin

  Haemoglobin bound in this way cannot carry oxygen to the body cells.
  The oxygen-carrying capacity of blood falls, causing headache,
  dizziness, unconsciousness and finally death by suffocation.
```

### Q A10
What is the effect of sulphur dioxide on plants?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Even a low concentration of SO2 causes:

  1. CHLOROSIS - loss of chlorophyll, so the leaves turn yellow.
  2. Stiffness of the flower buds, which then fall off the plant.
  3. Reduced growth and lower crop yield.

  It also forms H2SO4 in the atmosphere, giving acid rain which damages
  the leaves further.
```

### Q A11
What is acid rain? What is its pH?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ACID RAIN is rain water having a pH LESS THAN 5.6, caused by the
  presence of sulphuric acid and nitric acid formed from the SO2 and
  NO2 released into the air by burning fossil fuels.

  Normal rain has a pH of 5.6 because of dissolved CO2:
        CO2 + H2O  -->  H2CO3
```

### Q A12
Which oxides cause acid rain? Write the equations for the formation of
sulphuric acid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The oxides of SULPHUR (SO2, SO3) and the oxides of NITROGEN (NO, NO2).

        2 SO2  +  O2   -->   2 SO3
        SO3    +  H2O  -->   H2SO4

  (and for nitric acid:  4 NO2 + O2 + 2 H2O  -->  4 HNO3 )
```

### Q A13
What is the greenhouse effect?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The Earth's surface absorbs sunlight and re-radiates the energy as
  long-wavelength INFRARED radiation. Gases such as CO2, CH4, water
  vapour, O3 and CFCs allow the incoming short-wavelength sunlight to
  pass through, but ABSORB the outgoing infrared radiation and send it
  back towards the Earth.

  This trapping of heat, which warms the lower atmosphere just as the
  glass of a greenhouse does, is called the GREENHOUSE EFFECT.
```

### Q A15
What is global warming? Mention two of its consequences.

**ANSWER:**
```
  GLOBAL WARMING is the continuous rise in the average temperature of
  the Earth caused by the increasing concentration of greenhouse gases
  in the atmosphere.

  Consequences:
    1. Melting of the polar ice caps and glaciers, raising the sea level

    2. Change in rainfall patterns causing droughts and crop failure.
```

### Q A16
What is smog? Name its two types.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SMOG is a thick, low-lying haze formed by the combination of SMOKE
  and FOG (the word is smoke + fog). It reduces visibility and is very
  harmful to the respiratory system.

  Two types:
    1. CLASSICAL SMOG      - London smog, REDUCING in nature
    2. PHOTOCHEMICAL SMOG  - Los Angeles smog, OXIDISING in nature
```

---

### Q A17
Why is classical smog called reducing smog and photochemical smog called
oxidising smog?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CLASSICAL SMOG contains SO2, smoke and fog. SO2 is a REDUCING agent,
  so the smog as a whole is reducing in nature.

  PHOTOCHEMICAL SMOG contains O3, NO2, PAN and aldehydes, all of which
  are OXIDISING agents, so this smog is oxidising in nature.
```

### Q A18
What is PAN? What are its harmful effects?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PAN is PEROXYACETYL NITRATE,  CH3 - CO - OO - NO2 , a secondary
  pollutant formed in photochemical smog.

  Harmful effects:
    1. It is a powerful EYE IRRITANT - it makes the eyes water and burn.
    2. It is highly TOXIC TO PLANTS - it attacks the young leaves and
       causes bronzing and glazing of the leaf surface.
```

### Q A19
Which is the secondary pollutant formed in photochemical smog?
Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Secondary pollutants are those FORMED IN THE AIR by reactions between
  primary pollutants, not emitted directly.

  In photochemical smog they are:
       OZONE  O3
       PAN    (peroxyacetyl nitrate)

```

## Topic: Stratospheric pollution

### Q A20
What is the ozone hole? Where does it occur?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The OZONE HOLE is a region of the stratosphere where the concentration
  of ozone has been SEVERELY DEPLETED. It is not a physical hole.

  It occurs over ANTARCTICA (the South Pole), and appears mainly during
  the Antarctic SPRING (September - October), because the polar
  stratospheric clouds formed in the very cold winter release a burst of
  chlorine radicals when the sunlight returns.
```

---

### Q A21
Which compounds are mainly responsible for the depletion of the ozone
layer? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CHLOROFLUOROCARBONS (CFCs), also called FREONS.

  Examples:  CFCl3 (freon-11) ,  CF2Cl2 (freon-12)

  They are unreactive in the troposphere, so they drift up into the
  stratosphere where UV light breaks them and releases chlorine free
  radicals which destroy ozone.
```

---

### Q A22
Write the reactions showing how a chlorine free radical destroys ozone.

**ANSWER:**
```
        CF2Cl2   --(UV)-->   Cl*   +   *CF2Cl

        Cl*   +   O3   -->   ClO*  +  O2

        ClO*  +   O    -->   Cl*   +  O2

  The chlorine radical is REGENERATED in the last step, so it goes on to
  destroy more ozone. One chlorine radical can destroy about 100 000
  ozone molecules.
```

### Q A23
Mention any two harmful effects of ozone layer depletion.

**ANSWER:**
```
  More ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation reaches the Earth. Therefore:

  1. In humans - skin cancer, ageing of the skin, sunburn, CATARACT of
     the eye, and weakening of the immune system.
  2. In plants - reduced photosynthesis and lower crop yields; the
     phytoplankton in the sea are killed, damaging the whole marine
     food chain.
```

## Topic: Water and soil

### Q A24
What is BOD? What does a high value indicate?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BOD (BIOCHEMICAL OXYGEN DEMAND) is the amount of OXYGEN REQUIRED BY
  BACTERIA to decompose the organic matter present in a given volume of
  water (usually measured over 5 days at 20 C).

  A HIGH BOD means there is a lot of organic waste in the water, so the
  bacteria are consuming a lot of dissolved oxygen. The water is
  therefore heavily POLLUTED and fish will suffocate.

       Clean water    ->  BOD less than 5 ppm
       Polluted water ->  BOD 17 ppm or more
```

---

### Q A25
What is DOD?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DOD (DISSOLVED OXYGEN DEMAND) is the amount of dissolved oxygen
  actually CONSUMED from the water by the decomposition of the organic
  waste present in it.

  A high DOD means the water is losing its dissolved oxygen rapidly,
  which endangers aquatic life.
```

---

### Q A26
What is eutrophication?

**ANSWER:**
```
  EUTROPHICATION is the process in which a water body becomes
  OVER-ENRICHED WITH NUTRIENTS (mainly nitrates and phosphates from
  fertilisers and detergents).

  This causes an excessive growth of algae (an ALGAL BLOOM). When the
  algae die, bacteria decompose them and consume the dissolved oxygen.
  The oxygen level falls and the fish and other aquatic life die.
```

### Q A27
What is the maximum permitted concentration of fluoride and of lead in
drinking water, and what happens if it is exceeded?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FLUORIDE   -  1 ppm.
       About 1 ppm is actually useful - it converts tooth enamel into
       the harder fluorapatite and prevents tooth decay.
       Above 2 ppm  ->  brown MOTTLING of the teeth.
       Above 10 ppm ->  damage to bones and kidneys (skeletal fluorosis).

  LEAD       -  50 ppb  (parts per BILLION, i.e. 0.05 mg per litre).
       Excess lead damages the kidney, liver, brain and central nervous
       system, and reduces intelligence in children.
```

### Q A28
What causes blue baby syndrome?

**ANSWER:**
```
  NITRATE (NO3-) in drinking water above 50 ppm, coming from fertiliser
  run-off.

  In an infant the nitrate is reduced to nitrite, which converts
  haemoglobin into methaemoglobin. This cannot carry oxygen, so the baby
  turns blue. The disease is called METHAEMOGLOBINAEMIA or BLUE BABY
  SYNDROME.
```

### Q B1
What is acid rain? How is it formed? Write its harmful effects.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  Rain water having a pH LESS THAN 5.6 is called acid rain. Normal rain
  has pH 5.6 because of dissolved CO2 (CO2 + H2O --> H2CO3). The extra
  acidity is due to H2SO4 and HNO3 formed from SO2 and NO2 in the air.

  FORMATION
      2 SO2  +  O2   -->  2 SO3
      SO3    +  H2O  -->  H2SO4

      N2  +  O2  --(high temperature)-->  2 NO
      2 NO  +  O2  -->  2 NO2
      4 NO2  +  O2  +  2 H2O  -->  4 HNO3

  These acids dissolve in the rain water and fall to the ground.

  HARMFUL EFFECTS
  1. BUILDINGS - marble and limestone are corroded ("stone leprosy"):
        CaCO3 + H2SO4  -->  CaSO4 + H2O + CO2
        The Taj Mahal is being damaged in this way.
        Iron and steel structures corrode.
  2. SOIL - nutrients (Ca, Mg, K) are leached away and toxic aluminium
        ions are released; the soil becomes infertile.
  3. PLANTS - the waxy leaf coating is damaged, leaves yellow and die,
        growth is stunted, forests are destroyed.
  4. AQUATIC LIFE - lake water becomes acidic; below pH 5 fish eggs do
        not hatch and below pH 4 most fish die, giving "dead lakes".
```

### Q B2
Explain the greenhouse effect and global warming.

**ANSWER:**
```
  About 75 % of the sunlight reaching the Earth is absorbed by its
  surface, which warms up and re-emits the energy as LONG-WAVELENGTH
  INFRARED radiation.

  Certain gases - CO2, CH4, water vapour, O3, N2O and CFCs - are
  TRANSPARENT to the incoming short-wavelength sunlight but OPAQUE to
  the outgoing infrared. They absorb it and radiate it back towards the
  Earth. The heat is therefore trapped in the lower atmosphere.

  This warming is called the GREENHOUSE EFFECT and the gases responsible
  are GREENHOUSE GASES, because they behave like the glass of a
  greenhouse.

  Order of contribution:  CO2 (50-60 %) > CH4 (20 %) > CFCs (14 %)
                          > N2O (6 %) > O3 and water vapour

  The natural greenhouse effect is essential - without it the Earth
  would be at about -18 C instead of +15 C. But the burning of fossil
  fuels and deforestation have raised the CO2 level sharply, so more
  heat is trapped than before. The resulting continuous rise in the
  average temperature of the Earth is called GLOBAL WARMING.

  CONSEQUENCES
  1. Melting of polar ice caps and glaciers -> rise in sea level ->
     flooding of low-lying coastal cities and islands.
  2. Changed rainfall patterns - floods in some places, droughts and
     desertification in others.
  3. Failure of crops and shortage of food.
  4. Spread of tropical diseases such as malaria to new regions.
```

### Q B4
How is photochemical smog formed? Write the equations. How can it be
controlled?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FORMATION
  In a warm, dry and sunny climate, the oxides of nitrogen and the
  unburnt hydrocarbons released from automobile exhaust react in the
  presence of sunlight.

  Step 1 - nitrogen burns in the hot engine:
        N2   +  O2   --(high temperature)-->  2 NO
        2 NO +  O2                        -->  2 NO2

  Step 2 - sunlight splits NO2 (the photochemical step):
        NO2  --(sunlight, hv)-->   NO  +  O

  Step 3 - the free oxygen atom makes ozone:
        O    +  O2   -->   O3

  Step 4 - ozone oxidises NO and the cycle continues:
        NO   +  O3   -->   NO2  +  O2

  Step 5 - ozone and oxygen atoms attack the unburnt hydrocarbons:
        hydrocarbons + O3 / O  -->  formaldehyde HCHO
                                    acrolein CH2=CH-CHO
                                    PAN (peroxyacetyl nitrate)

  The mixture of O3, NO2, PAN and aldehydes is the brown, OXIDISING
  photochemical smog.

  CONTROL
  1. Fit CATALYTIC CONVERTERS to vehicles - they convert NO and CO in
     the exhaust into harmless N2 and CO2 before release.
  2. Use unleaded petrol (lead poisons the catalyst), CNG and public
     transport.
  3. Plant trees such as Pinus, Juniperus, Quercus, Pyrus and Vitis,
     which metabolise oxides of nitrogen.
  4. Control the PRIMARY pollutants NO2 and hydrocarbons - if they are
     absent, the secondary pollutants O3 and PAN can never form.
```

### Q B5
Explain how chlorofluorocarbons deplete the ozone layer.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CFCs (freons) such as CF2Cl2 and CFCl3 are used as refrigerants and
  aerosol propellants. They are chemically UNREACTIVE, so nothing
  destroys them in the troposphere and they slowly diffuse upward into
  the stratosphere.

  There the intense UV radiation breaks the C-Cl bond:

        CF2Cl2   --(UV)-->   Cl*   +   *CF2Cl

  The chlorine free radical attacks ozone:

        Cl*   +   O3   -->   ClO*   +   O2

  The chlorine monoxide radical then reacts with a free oxygen atom and
  REGENERATES the chlorine radical:

        ClO*  +   O    -->   Cl*    +   O2

  Because Cl* is produced again, the last two steps repeat over and over
  as a CHAIN REACTION. Chlorine therefore acts as a CATALYST and is not
  consumed. A single chlorine radical can destroy about 100 000 ozone
  molecules.

  The rate of destruction of ozone becomes greater than the rate of its
  natural formation, so the ozone layer becomes thin and more UV
  radiation reaches the Earth.
```

### Q B6
What is the ozone hole? Explain how it is formed over Antarctica.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The OZONE HOLE is a region of the stratosphere over ANTARCTICA where
  the concentration of ozone has been severely depleted. It appears
  mainly in the Antarctic SPRING (September - October).

  FORMATION
  1. In the Antarctic WINTER a "polar vortex" isolates a mass of
     extremely cold air (below -80 C) over the South Pole for months.

  2. At that temperature, ice clouds called POLAR STRATOSPHERIC CLOUDS
     (PSCs) form in the stratosphere.

  3. Normally the chlorine is locked away in unreactive reservoir
     compounds, chlorine nitrate (ClONO2) and hydrogen chloride (HCl).
     On the surfaces of the polar stratospheric clouds these react:

          ClONO2  +  H2O  -->  HOCl  +  HNO3
          ClONO2  +  HCl  -->  Cl2   +  HNO3

     The HNO3 remains trapped in the ice, so it cannot lock the chlorine
     up again.

  4. When the SPRING sunlight returns, it splits the stored compounds:

          HOCl  --(sunlight)-->  *OH  +  Cl*
          Cl2   --(sunlight)-->  2 Cl*

  5. A sudden burst of chlorine radicals is released and destroys ozone
     very rapidly by the chain reaction

          Cl*  +  O3  -->  ClO*  +  O2
          ClO* +  O   -->  Cl*   +  O2

     producing the ozone hole.
```

### Q B7
Mention the harmful effects of the depletion of the ozone layer.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Depletion allows far more ultraviolet (UV-B) radiation to reach the
  Earth's surface.

  ON HUMAN BEINGS
  1. SKIN CANCER, because UV-B damages the DNA of skin cells.
  2. Ageing of the skin, wrinkles and severe sunburn.
  3. CATARACT of the eye, which can lead to blindness.
  4. Weakening of the immune system, so more infections.

  ON PLANTS AND CROPS
  5. Damage to the leaves and reduced photosynthesis, giving lower

  6. Death of the PHYTOPLANKTON in the oceans, which form the base of

  ON MATERIALS
  7. Paints and dyes fade faster; plastics, rubber and fabrics degrade
     and crack.

  ON CLIMATE
  8. Increased surface temperature and increased evaporation, adding to
     global warming.
```

### Q B8
What are particulate pollutants? Explain the different types with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PARTICULATE POLLUTANTS are the tiny SOLID or LIQUID particles that
  remain suspended in the air.

  TYPES
  1. DUST   - solid particles larger than 1 micrometre produced by
              crushing, grinding or blowing of solid material.
              Examples: sand, fly ash, cement dust, pollen, sawdust.

  2. MIST   - liquid droplets formed by the condensation of vapour in
              air or by spraying a liquid.
              Examples: sprayed herbicide mist, sulphuric acid mist.

  3. FUMES  - solid particles formed by the condensation of the vapours
              of metals or of organic solvents.
              Examples: metal oxide fumes above a furnace.

  4. SMOKE  - solid or liquid particles produced by the combustion of
              organic matter.
              Examples: cigarette smoke, oil smoke, coal and wood smoke.

  5. SMOG   - smoke mixed with fog; a thick low haze.

  They are also classified as:
       VIABLE     - living particulates: bacteria, fungi, algae, moulds
       NON-VIABLE - non-living: dust, mist, fumes, smoke, fly ash

  EFFECTS: particles smaller than 1 micrometre reach the alveoli of the
  lungs and remain there, causing irritation, cough, asthma, bronchitis
  and even lung cancer. They also reduce visibility.
```

### Q B9
Write the sources and the harmful effects of the oxides of sulphur and
the oxides of nitrogen.

**ANSWER:**
```
  OXIDES OF SULPHUR  (SO2, SO3)

  Sources: burning of coal and fuel oil in thermal power stations and
           factories; smelting of sulphide ores; petroleum refining.

  Effects: In man - irritation of the eyes, throat and lungs,
           breathlessness, cough, bronchitis; even 10 ppm causes severe
           irritation.
           In plants - CHLOROSIS (yellowing due to loss of chlorophyll)
           and stiffness of the flower buds, which then fall off.
           In the air - oxidised to SO3 which forms H2SO4, the main
           cause of ACID RAIN.

  OXIDES OF NITROGEN  (NO, NO2)

  Sources: motor vehicle engines, where the high temperature makes
           N2 and O2 combine; thermal power stations; lightning.

  Effects: In man - irritation of the lungs, cough, bronchitis; NO2 is
           more harmful than NO.
           In plants - damage to leaves, retarded photosynthesis and
           falling of leaves.
           In the air - forms HNO3 causing acid rain, and starts
           PHOTOCHEMICAL SMOG.
           On materials - NO2 fades and weakens cotton and rayon fabrics
           and corrodes metals.
```

### Q B10
What are the causes of water pollution? Explain briefly.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. PATHOGENS - disease-causing bacteria and viruses entering water
     with human and animal excreta in domestic sewage.
     Examples: Escherichia coli, Streptococcus faecalis.
     They cause cholera, typhoid, dysentery, jaundice and polio.

  2. ORGANIC WASTES - leaves, grass, sewage, animal and food-processing
     waste. Bacteria decompose them and use up the dissolved oxygen, so
     the BOD rises and the fish suffocate.

  3. INORGANIC WASTES - heavy metals (Pb, Hg, Cd, As), acids, alkalis,
     salts, nitrates, phosphates and fluorides from industry and mining.

  4. AGRICULTURAL CHEMICALS - fertilisers and pesticides washed off the
     fields by rain. Nitrates and phosphates cause EUTROPHICATION;
     pesticides such as DDT accumulate in the food chain.

  5. INDUSTRIAL EFFLUENTS - waste from tanneries, textile mills, paper
     mills and chemical plants; also hot water from power stations,
     which causes THERMAL pollution and lowers the dissolved oxygen.

  6. OIL SPILLS - the oil film cuts off oxygen and sunlight from the
     water and kills marine life.
```

### Q B11
Explain eutrophication.

**ANSWER:**
```
  EUTROPHICATION is the over-enrichment of a water body with nutrients,
  leading to the death of its aquatic life.

  THE STEPS
  1. Fertilisers containing NITRATES and PHOSPHATES are washed from
     farm fields into a lake by rain. Detergents containing phosphates
     add more.

  2. These are plant nutrients, so the water becomes over-rich in them.

  3. Algae and other water plants multiply explosively, forming an
     ALGAL BLOOM that covers the surface and turns the lake green.

  4. The thick mat of algae BLOCKS THE SUNLIGHT from the plants below
     the surface, and those plants die.

  5. The algae themselves die after a short life. Bacteria attack all
     this dead organic matter and decompose it.

  6. The decomposing bacteria CONSUME the dissolved oxygen, so the BOD
     rises sharply and the dissolved oxygen falls almost to zero.

  7. Fish and other aquatic animals DIE of suffocation. Anaerobic
     bacteria take over, producing foul-smelling H2S, CH4 and NH3, and
     the lake becomes a lifeless swamp.
```

### Q B12
What is BOD? Distinguish it from dissolved oxygen. Why is a high BOD
harmful?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BOD (BIOCHEMICAL OXYGEN DEMAND) is the amount of oxygen REQUIRED BY
  BACTERIA to break down the organic matter present in a given volume of
  water, measured over 5 days at 20 C.

  DO (DISSOLVED OXYGEN) is the amount of oxygen actually DISSOLVED in
  the water and available to fish.

  +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
  |  DO                  |  BOD                                        |
  +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
  |  oxygen PRESENT      |  oxygen DEMANDED                            |
  |  clean water 6-8 ppm |  clean water less than 5 ppm                |
  |  low DO = polluted   |  HIGH BOD = polluted                        |
  +----------------------+--------------------------------------------+

  WHY A HIGH BOD IS HARMFUL
  A high BOD means a large amount of organic waste is present. The
  bacteria decomposing it consume most of the dissolved oxygen, so the
  DO falls below the 6 ppm needed by fish. The fish and other aquatic
  organisms suffocate and die, anaerobic decay begins and the water
  becomes foul-smelling and unusable.

  Polluted water has a BOD of 17 ppm or more.
```

### Q B13
Write a note on soil pollution caused by pesticides.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Pesticides are the chemicals sprayed on crops to kill unwanted
  organisms. They are the chief cause of soil pollution. They fall into
  three classes.

  1. INSECTICIDES (kill insects)
     Organochlorine type: DDT, BHC (gammexane), aldrin, dieldrin, endrin.
     These are NON-BIODEGRADABLE, so they remain in the soil for years.
     They are fat-soluble and become more concentrated at each level of
     the food chain (biomagnification), finally reaching man. They
     damage the nervous system and the liver and are carcinogenic.
     Insects also develop RESISTANCE, so stronger doses are needed.
     Organophosphate type: malathion, parathion. These are biodegradable
     but far more acutely toxic and have poisoned many farm workers.

  2. HERBICIDES (kill weeds)
     Sodium chlorate NaClO3, sodium arsenite Na3AsO3, 2,4-D, 2,4,5-T.
     The arsenic-based ones are highly toxic to mammals and to man.
     They kill the useful soil micro-organisms and reduce fertility.
     Some are teratogenic (cause birth defects).

  3. FUNGICIDES (kill fungi)
     Organomercury compounds and Bordeaux mixture (CuSO4 + lime).
     Organomercury compounds break down in the soil to release MERCURY,
     which is highly toxic to the brain and kidneys.

  Along with pesticides, INDUSTRIAL WASTE - fly ash, slag, plastics,
  heavy metals, acids and alkalis - accumulates in the soil, changes its
  pH, kills the soil micro-organisms and makes the land barren.
```

### Q B14
What is green chemistry? Explain its applications.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  Green chemistry is the use of the existing knowledge and principles of
  chemistry to design chemical processes and products in such a way that
  harmful substances are NOT PRODUCED at all, so that damage to the
  environment is minimised. It uses non-toxic reagents, safer solvents
  (preferably water) and aims at the maximum atom economy.

  APPLICATIONS

  1. DRY CLEANING OF CLOTHES
     Tetrachloroethene, Cl2C=CCl2, was used as the dry-cleaning solvent.
     It contaminates groundwater and is CARCINOGENIC.
     It has been replaced by LIQUEFIED CARBON DIOXIDE together with a
     suitable detergent. Liquid CO2 is non-toxic, leaves no harmful
     residue and does not pollute groundwater.
     Hydrogen peroxide is now also used for bleaching clothes, giving
     better results with less water.

  2. BLEACHING OF PAPER
     Chlorine gas was formerly used to bleach wood pulp, producing toxic
     chlorinated organic compounds in the effluent.
     It has been replaced by HYDROGEN PEROXIDE (H2O2) with a suitable
     catalyst. H2O2 decomposes into water and oxygen only, so no toxic
     by-product is formed and the bleaching is more efficient.

  3. SYNTHESIS OF CHEMICALS IN AQUEOUS MEDIUM
     Ethanal (acetaldehyde) is now manufactured in one step by the
     oxidation of ethene in an AQUEOUS medium:

        CH2=CH2  +  O2  --(Pd(II)/Cu(II) catalyst in water)-->  CH3CHO

     with about 90 % yield. Water replaces the toxic and flammable
     organic solvent, and water is cheap, non-toxic and non-polluting.
```

### Q B15
Explain the strategies used to control environmental pollution.

**ANSWER:**
```
  WASTE MANAGEMENT

  1. COLLECTION - segregate waste at the source into biodegradable
     (kitchen and garden waste) and non-biodegradable (plastic, glass,
     metal) and collect them separately. Do not throw waste into drains.

  2. DISPOSAL
     * LANDFILL - waste dumped in low-lying land and covered with soil.
       Cheap, but the leachate can pollute groundwater.
     * COMPOSTING - biodegradable waste is allowed to rot into MANURE,
       which is a useful product.
     * INCINERATION - burning at high temperature greatly reduces the
       volume, but the gases must be scrubbed before release.
     * SEWAGE TREATMENT before releasing water into rivers.

  3. RECYCLING - paper, glass, plastic, aluminium and iron scrap are
     returned to industry as raw material. This saves natural resources
     and energy and removes the waste at the same time.

  OTHER MEASURES
  * AIR   - electrostatic precipitators, scrubbers and bag filters on
            chimneys; catalytic converters on vehicles; low-sulphur
            fuel and unleaded petrol; solar and wind energy;
            afforestation.
  * WATER - treat industrial effluent before discharge; sewage treatment
            plants; ban phosphate detergents; controlled use of
            fertilisers.
  * SOIL  - use biofertilisers and biopesticides; crop rotation and
            organic manure; proper disposal of industrial solid waste.

  The guiding rule is the three R's:  REDUCE , REUSE , RECYCLE.
```

### Q B16
Explain the harmful effects of carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CARBON MONOXIDE (CO)

  Source: INCOMPLETE combustion of fuel; petrol engines are the biggest
  source; also cigarette smoke and coal burnt with a poor air supply.

  Why it is poisonous: CO binds to haemoglobin about 300 times more
  strongly than oxygen, forming CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN.

        Hb  +  CO   -->   carboxyhaemoglobin

  That haemoglobin can no longer carry oxygen. Only 3-4 % conversion
  already reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood. The effects
  are headache, nervousness, drowsiness, dizziness, blue skin,
  unconsciousness, coma and finally death by suffocation.

  CARBON DIOXIDE (CO2)

  Source: COMPLETE combustion of all fuels, respiration, and the loss of
  the natural sink through deforestation.

  Effect: CO2 is not directly poisonous, but it is the MAIN GREENHOUSE
  GAS, contributing 50-60 % of the greenhouse effect. It absorbs the
  infrared radiation given out by the Earth and traps the heat, causing
  GLOBAL WARMING. This melts the polar ice caps, raises the sea level,
  floods coastal regions, changes rainfall patterns and reduces crop
  yields.
```

### Q C1
Write an essay on tropospheric pollution.

**MODEL ANSWER (with the mark split):**
```
  INTRODUCTION  (1 mark)
  Tropospheric pollution is the pollution of the lowest layer of the
  atmosphere (0-10 km), the air we actually breathe. Its pollutants are
  of two kinds: GASEOUS and PARTICULATE.

  A. GASEOUS POLLUTANTS  (4 marks - one for roughly each pair)

  1. OXIDES OF SULPHUR (SO2, SO3)
     Source: burning of coal and fuel oil, smelting of sulphide ores.
     Effects: irritation of eyes, throat and lungs; bronchitis; in
     plants chlorosis and falling of flower buds; forms H2SO4 giving
     acid rain.

  2. OXIDES OF NITROGEN (NO, NO2)
     Source: motor vehicle engines (N2 + O2 combine at high temperature),
     power stations, lightning.
     Effects: lung irritation; leaf damage; forms HNO3 giving acid rain;
     starts photochemical smog; fades fabrics.

  3. CARBON MONOXIDE (CO)
     Source: incomplete combustion, mainly petrol engines.
     Effects: binds haemoglobin 300 times more strongly than oxygen
     forming CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN; the blood cannot carry oxygen; causes
     headache, coma and death.

  4. CARBON DIOXIDE (CO2)
     Source: complete combustion, respiration, deforestation.
     Effects: the chief greenhouse gas, causing global warming, melting
     of ice caps and rise in sea level.

  5. HYDROCARBONS
     Source: unburnt petrol and diesel, petroleum leaks.
     Effects: carcinogenic; cause ageing and shedding of leaves and
     flowers; feed photochemical smog.

  6. OZONE (at ground level)
     A secondary pollutant made from NO2 and sunlight. Irritates eyes
     and nose, causes headache and chest pain, damages crops, cracks
     rubber.

  B. PARTICULATE POLLUTANTS  (2 marks)
     DUST   - solid particles from crushing and grinding (fly ash, sand)
     MIST   - liquid droplets from condensation or spraying
     FUMES  - solids from condensation of metal or solvent vapours
     SMOKE  - particles from combustion of organic matter
     SMOG   - smoke + fog
     VIABLE particulates are living (bacteria, fungi, algae, moulds);
     NON-VIABLE ones are non-living (dust, mist, fumes, smoke).
     Particles below 1 micrometre reach the alveoli and stay there,
     causing asthma, bronchitis and lung cancer, and reducing visibility.

  C. EFFECTS THAT FOLLOW  (1 mark)
     Acid rain (pH < 5.6), the greenhouse effect and global warming, and
     the two kinds of smog all arise from these tropospheric pollutants.
```

### Q C2
Write an essay on smog: its types, the chemistry of photochemical smog,
its harmful effects and its control.

**MODEL ANSWER (with the mark split):**
```
  DEFINITION  (1 mark)
  Smog is a thick, low-lying haze formed by the combination of SMOKE and
  FOG. It reduces visibility and harms the respiratory system. There are
  two kinds - classical and photochemical.

  COMPARISON TABLE  (3 marks)
  +-------------------+---------------------+-----------------------+
  |  POINT            |  CLASSICAL          |  PHOTOCHEMICAL        |
  +-------------------+---------------------+-----------------------+
  |  Other name       |  London smog        |  Los Angeles smog     |
  |  Nature           |  REDUCING           |  OXIDISING            |
  |  Climate          |  cool and humid     |  warm, dry, sunny     |
  |  Sunlight         |  not needed         |  essential            |
  |  Pollutants       |  SO2 + smoke + fog  |  NO2 + hydrocarbons   |
  |  Source           |  burning of coal    |  automobile exhaust   |
  |  Products         |  H2SO4 aerosol      |  O3, PAN, aldehydes   |
  |  Look             |  grey-black         |  brown haze           |
  +-------------------+---------------------+-----------------------+

  CHEMISTRY OF PHOTOCHEMICAL SMOG  (2 marks)
        N2 + O2 --(hot engine)--> 2 NO ;  2 NO + O2 --> 2 NO2
        NO2  --(sunlight)-->  NO  +  O
        O    +  O2   -->  O3
        NO   +  O3   -->  NO2  +  O2
        hydrocarbons + O3 / O --> HCHO, acrolein and PAN
        PAN = peroxyacetyl nitrate, CH3-CO-OO-NO2

  HARMFUL EFFECTS  (1 mark)
        1. Severe irritation of the eyes (PAN and formaldehyde).
        2. Irritation of nose and throat, dry cough, difficulty in
           breathing, headache and chest pain.
        3. Damage to plants - PAN causes bronzing and glazing of leaves.
        4. Ozone cracks rubber and corrodes metals, stone and fabrics.
        5. Reduced visibility and a brown colour in the air.

  CONTROL  (1 mark)
        1. Catalytic converters on vehicles convert NO and CO to N2
           and CO2 before release.
        2. Unleaded petrol, CNG and public transport.
        3. Plant Pinus, Juniperus, Quercus, Pyrus and Vitis, which
           metabolise the oxides of nitrogen.
        4. Control the primary pollutants NO2 and hydrocarbons, so that
           the secondary pollutants O3 and PAN cannot form.
```

### Q C3
Write an essay on stratospheric pollution — the ozone layer, its
depletion and the effects.

**MODEL ANSWER (with the mark split):**
```
  THE OZONE LAYER  (2 marks)
  The stratosphere (10-50 km) contains a layer of ozone at about
  20-30 km which absorbs the harmful UV-B and UV-C radiation of the Sun
  and shields life on Earth.

  Ozone is formed and destroyed naturally at the same rate, so its
  amount stays constant (a dynamic equilibrium):

      FORMATION      O2  --(UV)-->  O + O  ;  O + O2 --> O3
      DESTRUCTION    O3  --(UV)-->  O2 + O ;  O3 + O --> 2 O2

  THE ROLE OF CFCs  (2 marks)
  Chlorofluorocarbons (freons) such as CFCl3 and CF2Cl2 are used as
  refrigerants and aerosol propellants. They are unreactive, so they
  survive in the troposphere and drift up into the stratosphere.

  DEPLETION MECHANISM  (2 marks)
      CF2Cl2  --(UV)-->  Cl*  +  *CF2Cl
      Cl*   +  O3  -->  ClO*  +  O2
      ClO*  +  O   -->  Cl*   +  O2      <-- Cl* regenerated

  The chain repeats, so chlorine acts as a catalyst and ONE chlorine
  radical destroys about 100 000 ozone molecules. Destruction now
  outpaces formation and the layer thins.

  THE OZONE HOLE  (1 mark)
  Over ANTARCTICA the winter polar vortex allows POLAR STRATOSPHERIC
  CLOUDS to form. On their surfaces:
      ClONO2 + H2O --> HOCl + HNO3   ;   ClONO2 + HCl --> Cl2 + HNO3
  The HNO3 is locked in the ice. When the SPRING sunlight returns,
      HOCl --(hv)--> *OH + Cl*   ;   Cl2 --(hv)--> 2 Cl*
  releasing a burst of chlorine radicals which rapidly destroy the ozone
  and create the ozone hole.

  EFFECTS OF THE EXTRA UV  (1 mark)
      Humans   - skin cancer, ageing of skin, cataract, weak immunity
      Plants   - reduced photosynthesis, lower crop yields, death of
                 marine phytoplankton
      Materials- fading of paints and dyes, degradation of plastics,
                 rubber and fabrics
      Cure     - the MONTREAL PROTOCOL (1987) banning CFCs; replacement
                 by HFCs which contain no chlorine.
```

### Q C4
Write an essay on water pollution — its causes, effects and the
international standards for drinking water.

**MODEL ANSWER (with the mark split):**
```
  CAUSES  (3 marks)
  1. PATHOGENS from human and animal excreta in sewage - E. coli,
     Streptococcus faecalis - causing cholera, typhoid, dysentery,
     jaundice and polio.
  2. ORGANIC WASTES - leaves, sewage, animal and food waste; bacteria
     decomposing them use up the dissolved oxygen.
  3. INORGANIC WASTES - heavy metals, acids, alkalis, salts, nitrates,
     phosphates, fluorides.
  4. AGRICULTURAL CHEMICALS - fertilisers and pesticides washed off the
     fields.
  5. INDUSTRIAL EFFLUENTS and hot water (thermal pollution).
  6. OIL SPILLS.

  BOD AND DO  (1 mark)
  Dissolved oxygen of clean water is 6-8 ppm; below 6 ppm fish growth
  is hindered. BOD is the oxygen required by bacteria to decompose the
  organic matter: clean water below 5 ppm, polluted water 17 ppm or
  more. A high BOD means the oxygen is being stripped from the water and
  the fish suffocate.

  EUTROPHICATION  (2 marks)
  Nitrates and phosphates from fertilisers and detergents over-enrich
  the lake -> explosive growth of algae (algal bloom) -> sunlight is
  blocked and the plants below die -> the algae die too and bacteria
  decompose them -> the decomposition consumes the dissolved oxygen ->
  fish and other aquatic life die -> anaerobic decay produces H2S, CH4
  and NH3 and the lake becomes a stinking swamp.

  SPECIFIC POISONS  (1 mark)
  Pesticides such as DDT are non-biodegradable and biomagnify up the
  food chain. Mercury is converted to methyl mercury and causes MINAMATA
  DISEASE. Lead damages the kidney, liver and brain. Cadmium causes
  itai-itai disease.

  INTERNATIONAL STANDARDS FOR DRINKING WATER  (1 mark)
  +--------------+-------------+-----------------------------------+
  |  Fluoride    |  1 ppm      |  >2 ppm mottling of teeth;        |
  |              |             |  >10 ppm harm to bones and kidney |
  |  Lead        |  50 ppb     |  kidney, liver and brain damage   |
  |  Nitrate     |  50 ppm     |  blue baby syndrome               |
  |  Sulphate    |  500 ppm    |  laxative effect                  |
  +--------------+-------------+-----------------------------------+
```
