# PATTERN 1 — Definitions of the basic terms

### Q1
The medium in which a pollutant is retained or absorbed for a long time is
called a

(a) receptor &nbsp; (b) sink &nbsp; (c) contaminant &nbsp; (d) speciation

**ANSWER: (b) sink**
```
  SINK      -  RETAINS the pollutant  (ocean is a sink for CO2)
  RECEPTOR  -  is AFFECTED by the pollutant (man, plants)
```

---

### Q2
A substance which does not occur in nature but is introduced by human
activity is called a

(a) pollutant &nbsp; (b) receptor &nbsp; (c) contaminant &nbsp; (d) sink

**ANSWER: (c) contaminant**
```
  CONTAMINANT = should not be there AT ALL.
  POLLUTANT   = belongs there, but there is now TOO MUCH.
```

---

### Q3
Which of the following is a non-biodegradable pollutant?

(a) sewage &nbsp; (b) cow dung &nbsp; (c) DDT &nbsp; (d) vegetable peel

**ANSWER: (c) DDT**
```
  Non-biodegradable: plastics, DDT, BHC, Hg, Pb, glass, radioactive waste.
  Biodegradable: sewage, cow dung, paper, food and garden waste.
```

---

### Q4
The occurrence of an element in different chemical forms in the environment
is called

(a) biomagnification &nbsp; (b) speciation &nbsp; (c) eutrophication &nbsp; (d) leaching

**ANSWER: (b) speciation**

> **SHORTCUT:** if the option mentions **Cr(III) vs Cr(VI)** anywhere in the
> question, the answer is speciation.

# PATTERN 2 — Layers of the atmosphere

### Q5
The layer of the atmosphere in which the temperature INCREASES with
altitude and which contains the ozone layer is

(a) troposphere &nbsp; (b) stratosphere &nbsp; (c) mesosphere &nbsp; (d) thermosphere

**ANSWER: (b) stratosphere**
```
  Temperature pattern going UP:   DOWN , UP , DOWN , UP
                                  Tropo Strato Meso Thermo
```

---

### Q6
The coldest layer of the atmosphere is the

(a) troposphere &nbsp; (b) stratosphere &nbsp; (c) mesosphere &nbsp; (d) thermosphere

**ANSWER: (c) mesosphere** — about −100 C at the mesopause.

---

### Q7
The correct order of the atmospheric layers, starting from the Earth's
surface, is

(a) troposphere, mesosphere, stratosphere, thermosphere
(b) troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere
(c) stratosphere, troposphere, thermosphere, mesosphere
(d) thermosphere, mesosphere, stratosphere, troposphere

**ANSWER: (b)** — *"The Sun Makes Toast."*

# PATTERN 3 — Match the pollutant to its effect

### Q8
Carbon monoxide is poisonous because it

(a) reacts with oxygen in the lungs
(b) forms carboxyhaemoglobin with haemoglobin
(c) dissolves in blood plasma
(d) reacts with carbon dioxide

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Hb + CO --> CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN, bound about 300 times more strongly
  than oxygen. The blood can no longer carry O2.
```

---

### Q9
Chlorosis in plants is caused by

(a) CO &nbsp; (b) SO2 &nbsp; (c) CO2 &nbsp; (d) O2

**ANSWER: (b) SO2** — loss of chlorophyll, leaves turn yellow.

---

### Q10
Which gas is produced by the INCOMPLETE combustion of fuel?

(a) CO2 &nbsp; (b) SO2 &nbsp; (c) CO &nbsp; (d) NO2

**ANSWER: (c) CO**

> **SHORTCUT:** *inComplete -> CO. Complete -> CO2.* Examiners flip this every
> single year.

---

### Q11
The oxides of nitrogen in the atmosphere are mainly produced by

(a) burning of coal
(b) high temperature combustion in automobile engines
(c) respiration of animals
(d) volcanic eruption

**ANSWER: (b)** — `N2 + O2 --(high T)--> 2 NO` inside the hot engine.

---

### Q12
Which of the following is a SECONDARY pollutant?

(a) SO2 &nbsp; (b) NO &nbsp; (c) PAN &nbsp; (d) CO

**ANSWER: (c) PAN**
```
  PRIMARY   - emitted as such: SO2, NO, NO2, CO, hydrocarbons
  SECONDARY - made in the air:  O3, PAN, formaldehyde, acrolein
```

# PATTERN 4 — Particulates

### Q13
Solid particles formed by the condensation of the vapours of metals are
called

(a) dust &nbsp; (b) mist &nbsp; (c) fumes &nbsp; (d) smoke

**ANSWER: (c) fumes**
```
  DUST  - crushing/grinding of SOLIDS
  MIST  - condensation/spraying of LIQUIDS
  FUMES - condensation of VAPOURS of metals or solvents
  SMOKE - COMBUSTION of organic matter
```

---

### Q14
Which of the following is a VIABLE particulate?

(a) fly ash &nbsp; (b) bacteria &nbsp; (c) cement dust &nbsp; (d) sulphuric acid mist

**ANSWER: (b) bacteria** — viable = **living**.

---

### Q15
Particulates of which size are the most harmful, because they reach the
alveoli of the lungs and stay there?

(a) greater than 10 micrometre &nbsp; (b) 5 to 10 micrometre
(c) 1 to 5 micrometre &nbsp; (d) less than 1 micrometre

**ANSWER: (d) less than 1 micrometre**

# PATTERN 5 — Acid rain

### Q16
The pH of normal rain water is about

(a) 7.0 &nbsp; (b) 6.5 &nbsp; (c) 5.6 &nbsp; (d) 4.0

**ANSWER: (c) 5.6** — due to dissolved CO2 forming H2CO3.

---

### Q17
Acid rain is caused mainly by

(a) CO and CO2 &nbsp; (b) SO2 and NO2 &nbsp; (c) CH4 and O3 &nbsp; (d) CFCs

**ANSWER: (b) SO2 and NO2** — they give H2SO4 and HNO3.

---

### Q18
The damage to the Taj Mahal by acid rain involves the reaction

(a) `CaCO3 + H2SO4 --> CaSO4 + H2O + CO2`
(b) `CaO + H2SO4 --> CaSO4 + H2O`
(c) `CaCl2 + H2SO4 --> CaSO4 + 2 HCl`
(d) `Ca(OH)2 + CO2 --> CaCO3 + H2O`

**ANSWER: (a)** — marble is CaCO3; the effect is called **stone leprosy**.

---

### Q19
Fish begin to die when the pH of a lake falls below about

(a) 6.5 &nbsp; (b) 5.6 &nbsp; (c) 4.0 &nbsp; (d) 2.0

**ANSWER: (c) 4.0** — below pH 5 the eggs fail to hatch; below pH 4 most fish die.

---

# PATTERN 6 — Greenhouse effect

### Q20
The greenhouse gas contributing the MOST to global warming overall is

(a) CH4 &nbsp; (b) CO2 &nbsp; (c) CFC &nbsp; (d) N2O

**ANSWER: (b) CO2** — about 50–60 % of the effect.

> **SHORTCUT — read the wording.** "Contributes most **overall**" -> **CO2**
> (there is simply a lot of it). "Most effective **per molecule**" -> **CFCs**.
> Two different answers to two nearly identical questions.

---

### Q21
The correct order of the contribution of greenhouse gases to global
warming is

(a) `CH4 > CO2 > CFC > N2O` &nbsp; (b) `CO2 > CH4 > CFC > N2O`
(c) `CFC > CO2 > CH4 > N2O` &nbsp; (d) `N2O > CFC > CH4 > CO2`

**ANSWER: (b)** — CO2 (~55 %) > CH4 (~20 %) > CFC (~14 %) > N2O (~6 %).

---

### Q22
Greenhouse gases warm the Earth because they

(a) reflect sunlight back into space
(b) absorb the incoming ultraviolet radiation
(c) are transparent to sunlight but absorb the outgoing infrared radiation
(d) increase the pressure of the atmosphere

**ANSWER: (c)** — that one-way behaviour is the whole mechanism.

---

### Q23
Which of the following is NOT a greenhouse gas?

(a) CO2 &nbsp; (b) CH4 &nbsp; (c) N2 &nbsp; (d) N2O

**ANSWER: (c) N2**

> **SHORTCUT:** the diatomic gases **N2 and O2 are never greenhouse gases** —
> a symmetrical diatomic molecule cannot absorb infrared. Any option offering
> N2 or O2 as a greenhouse gas is a distractor.

---

# PATTERN 7 — Smog (the highest-yield pattern)

### Q24
Classical smog is also called

(a) oxidising smog &nbsp; (b) Los Angeles smog
(c) reducing smog &nbsp; (d) photochemical smog

**ANSWER: (c) reducing smog** — it contains SO2, a reducing agent.

---

### Q25
Photochemical smog does NOT contain

(a) O3 &nbsp; (b) PAN &nbsp; (c) SO2 &nbsp; (d) formaldehyde

**ANSWER: (c) SO2** — SO2 belongs to CLASSICAL smog.

---

### Q26
Photochemical smog occurs in a climate that is

(a) cool and humid &nbsp; (b) warm, dry and sunny
(c) cold and dry &nbsp; (d) warm and humid

**ANSWER: (b)** — *Los Angeles is hot and sunny.*

---

### Q27
PAN stands for

(a) peroxyacyl nitrite &nbsp; (b) peroxyacetyl nitrate
(c) phenyl ammonium nitrate &nbsp; (d) polyacrylonitrile

**ANSWER: (b) peroxyacetyl nitrate** — `CH3-CO-OO-NO2`.

---

### Q28
The chief harmful effect of PAN is

(a) it causes acid rain &nbsp; (b) it depletes the ozone layer
(c) it is a powerful eye irritant and is toxic to plants
(d) it forms carboxyhaemoglobin

**ANSWER: (c)** — eye irritation plus bronzing and glazing of leaves.

---

### Q29
In photochemical smog, ozone is formed by

(a) `O2 + O2 --> 2 O3` &nbsp; (b) `NO2 --(hv)--> NO + O ; O + O2 --> O3`
(c) `NO + O2 --> O3 + N` &nbsp; (d) `CO2 + O2 --> O3 + CO`

**ANSWER: (b)** — the photolysis of NO2 is the starting point.

---

### Q30
Which of the following plants is used to control photochemical smog
because it metabolises the oxides of nitrogen?

(a) Pinus &nbsp; (b) Mangifera &nbsp; (c) Azadirachta &nbsp; (d) Ficus

**ANSWER: (a) Pinus**
```
  The five to remember:  Pinus, Juniperus, Quercus, Pyrus, Vitis
                         (pine, juniper, oak, pear, grape vine)
```

---

# PATTERN 8 — Ozone layer and its depletion

### Q31
Ozone in the stratosphere is formed by the reaction

(a) `O2 + O2 --> 2 O3` &nbsp; (b) `O2 --(UV)--> O + O ; O + O2 --> O3`
(c) `O3 --> O2 + O` &nbsp; (d) `2 O3 --> 3 O2`

**ANSWER: (b)**

---

### Q32
The compounds mainly responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer are

(a) SO2 and NO2 &nbsp; (b) CO and CO2 &nbsp; (c) CFCs &nbsp; (d) hydrocarbons

**ANSWER: (c) CFCs (freons)**

---

### Q33
In the ozone depletion chain, the species that regenerates the chlorine
free radical is

(a) `ClO*` &nbsp; (b) `Cl2` &nbsp; (c) `HCl` &nbsp; (d) `ClONO2`

**ANSWER: (a) ClO\***
```
  Cl*  +  O3  -->  ClO*  +  O2
  ClO* +  O   -->  Cl*   +  O2      <-- this step gives Cl* back
```

---

### Q34
Approximately how many ozone molecules can ONE chlorine free radical
destroy?

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 100 &nbsp; (c) 1000 &nbsp; (d) 100 000

**ANSWER: (d) about 100 000** — because chlorine acts as a **catalyst**.

---

### Q35
The ozone hole is observed over

(a) the Arctic in winter &nbsp; (b) Antarctica in spring
(c) the equator in summer &nbsp; (d) Europe in autumn

**ANSWER: (b) Antarctica in spring**

> **SHORTCUT:** Antarctica + spring. The cold winter *prepares* the chemicals in
> the polar stratospheric clouds; the returning **spring sunlight** triggers the
> destruction.

---

### Q36
The chlorine reservoir compounds that react on the surface of polar
stratospheric clouds are

(a) `Cl2` and `HOCl` &nbsp; (b) `ClONO2` and `HCl`
(c) `CFCl3` and `CF2Cl2` &nbsp; (d) `ClO*` and `Cl*`

**ANSWER: (b) `ClONO2` and `HCl`**
```
  ClONO2 + H2O --> HOCl + HNO3
  ClONO2 + HCl --> Cl2  + HNO3
```

---

### Q37
The international agreement to phase out the production of CFCs is the

(a) Kyoto Protocol &nbsp; (b) Montreal Protocol
(c) Paris Agreement &nbsp; (d) Basel Convention

**ANSWER: (b) Montreal Protocol (1987)**

> **SHORTCUT:** **M**ontreal -> ozone. **K**yoto / **P**aris -> greenhouse gases
> and climate. Do not mix them up.

---

### Q38
Increased UV radiation due to ozone depletion causes

(a) skin cancer and cataract &nbsp; (b) acid rain
(c) blue baby syndrome &nbsp; (d) Minamata disease

**ANSWER: (a) skin cancer and cataract**

# PATTERN 9 — Water pollution numbers

### Q39
The dissolved oxygen of clean water is about

(a) 1–2 ppm &nbsp; (b) 6–8 ppm &nbsp; (c) 17 ppm &nbsp; (d) 50 ppm

**ANSWER: (b) 6–8 ppm**

---

### Q40
The BOD of clean water is

(a) less than 5 ppm &nbsp; (b) 17 ppm &nbsp; (c) 50 ppm &nbsp; (d) 500 ppm

**ANSWER: (a) less than 5 ppm** — polluted water is **17 ppm or more**.

---

### Q41
A HIGH value of BOD indicates that the water

(a) is rich in dissolved oxygen &nbsp; (b) is highly polluted with organic matter
(c) is safe to drink &nbsp; (d) contains dissolved minerals

**ANSWER: (b)**

> **SHORTCUT:** BOD is a **demand**, not a supply. **High demand = dirty water.**
> This is the single most common trap in the chapter.

---

### Q42
The maximum permitted concentration of LEAD in drinking water is

(a) 1 ppm &nbsp; (b) 50 ppm &nbsp; (c) 50 ppb &nbsp; (d) 500 ppm

**ANSWER: (c) 50 ppb**

> **SHORTCUT:** lead is the only one of the four measured in **ppb**. If lead is
> in the question and an option says ppb, that option is almost certainly right.

---

### Q43
Match the standards for drinking water:

```
  Fluoride  ...  Lead  ...  Nitrate  ...  Sulphate
```

(a) 1 ppm, 50 ppb, 50 ppm, 500 ppm &nbsp; (b) 50 ppm, 1 ppm, 500 ppm, 50 ppb
(c) 1 ppb, 50 ppm, 50 ppb, 500 ppm &nbsp; (d) 500 ppm, 50 ppm, 1 ppm, 50 ppb

**ANSWER: (a)** — *"One, fifty tiny, fifty, five hundred."*

---

### Q44
Blue baby syndrome (methaemoglobinaemia) is caused by an excess of

(a) fluoride &nbsp; (b) nitrate &nbsp; (c) sulphate &nbsp; (d) lead

**ANSWER: (b) nitrate**, above 50 ppm.

---

### Q45
Brown mottling of teeth is caused by fluoride in drinking water above

(a) 1 ppm &nbsp; (b) 2 ppm &nbsp; (c) 10 ppm &nbsp; (d) 50 ppm

**ANSWER: (b) 2 ppm**
```
  ~1 ppm   -> GOOD, prevents tooth decay (forms fluorapatite)
  > 2 ppm  -> brown mottling of teeth
  > 10 ppm -> harmful to bones and kidney
```

---

### Q46
Minamata disease is caused by

(a) lead &nbsp; (b) cadmium &nbsp; (c) mercury &nbsp; (d) arsenic

**ANSWER: (c) mercury** — as **methyl mercury** accumulated in fish.

> **SHORTCUT:** **M**inamata -> **M**ercury. **Itai-itai** -> **Cadmium**
> (soft bones). Easy pair to remember together.

---

### Q47
Eutrophication of a lake results in

(a) an increase in dissolved oxygen &nbsp; (b) a decrease in dissolved oxygen and death of fish
(c) purification of the water &nbsp; (d) an increase in fish population

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  nutrients -> algal bloom -> algae die -> bacteria decompose them
  -> oxygen consumed -> fish suffocate
```

---

### Q48
Eutrophication is caused mainly by an excess of

(a) chloride and sulphate &nbsp; (b) nitrate and phosphate
(c) lead and mercury &nbsp; (d) carbonate and bicarbonate

**ANSWER: (b) nitrate and phosphate** — from fertilisers and detergents.

---

# PATTERN 10 — Soil pollution and pesticides

### Q49
2,4-D is a

(a) insecticide &nbsp; (b) herbicide &nbsp; (c) fungicide &nbsp; (d) fertiliser

**ANSWER: (b) herbicide** — 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid, a weed killer.

---

### Q50
Which of the following is a fungicide?

(a) DDT &nbsp; (b) aldrin &nbsp; (c) organomercury compound &nbsp; (d) sodium chlorate

**ANSWER: (c) organomercury compound**
```
  INSECTICIDE  ->  DDT, BHC, aldrin, dieldrin, malathion
  HERBICIDE    ->  2,4-D, 2,4,5-T, NaClO3, Na3AsO3
  FUNGICIDE    ->  organomercury, Bordeaux mixture
```

---

### Q51
DDT is a serious pollutant chiefly because it is

(a) volatile &nbsp; (b) non-biodegradable and accumulates in the food chain
(c) explosive &nbsp; (d) water soluble

**ANSWER: (b)** — this is **biomagnification**.

---

### Q52
Which class of insecticides is biodegradable but far more acutely toxic?

(a) organochlorines &nbsp; (b) organophosphates
(c) carbamates &nbsp; (d) pyrethroids

**ANSWER: (b) organophosphates** — malathion, parathion; they attack the
nervous system.

# PATTERN 11 — Green chemistry

### Q53
Green chemistry means

(a) the chemistry of green plants
(b) designing chemical processes so that harmful substances are not produced
(c) treating pollution after it has been produced
(d) the study of chlorophyll

**ANSWER: (b)**

---

### Q54
In green dry cleaning, tetrachloroethene has been replaced by

(a) benzene &nbsp; (b) liquefied CO2 with a detergent
(c) chloroform &nbsp; (d) liquid ammonia

**ANSWER: (b) liquefied CO2**

---

### Q55
Paper is now bleached green-chemically using

(a) chlorine &nbsp; (b) bleaching powder
(c) hydrogen peroxide &nbsp; (d) sodium hypochlorite

**ANSWER: (c) hydrogen peroxide** — it gives only water and oxygen.

---

### Q56
Ethanal is manufactured greenly by the oxidation of ethene in an aqueous
medium using the catalyst

(a) `V2O5` &nbsp; (b) `Pd(II) / Cu(II)` &nbsp; (c) `Ni` &nbsp; (d) `Fe / Mo`

**ANSWER: (b) Pd(II)/Cu(II)**
```
  CH2=CH2  +  O2  --(Pd(II)/Cu(II) in water)-->  CH3CHO    (~90 % yield)
```

---

### Q57
The principle of "atom economy" in green chemistry means

(a) using the fewest atoms possible
(b) maximising the number of reactant atoms that end up in the product
(c) reducing the atomic mass of the reagents
(d) using only light atoms

**ANSWER: (b)**

# PATTERN 12 — Assertion / odd-one-out style

### Q58
Which of the following statements is INCORRECT?

(a) The ozone layer is present in the stratosphere
(b) CO is produced by incomplete combustion
(c) BOD of clean water is greater than 17 ppm
(d) Acid rain has a pH below 5.6

**ANSWER: (c)** — BOD of **clean** water is **less than 5 ppm**; 17 ppm or more
means polluted.

---

### Q59
Which pair is correctly matched?

(a) Classical smog — oxidising &nbsp; (b) Photochemical smog — reducing
(c) PAN — eye irritant &nbsp; (d) CFC — acid rain

**ANSWER: (c) PAN — eye irritant**

---

### Q60
The odd one out among the following greenhouse gases is

(a) CO2 &nbsp; (b) CH4 &nbsp; (c) O2 &nbsp; (d) N2O

**ANSWER: (c) O2** — a symmetrical diatomic molecule; it does not absorb infrared.
