# Hydrogen and its Compounds — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

This chapter has only ONE kind of numerical (volume strength of H2O2). Every
other pattern is a *fact + reason*, a *reaction + condition*, or a *structure
+ values*. Once you can name the pattern, you already know the first line of
the answer.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — POSITION OF HYDROGEN IN THE PERIODIC TABLE          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Justify the position of hydrogen", "Hydrogen resembles both
            alkali metals and halogens - explain", "Why is the position
            of hydrogen anomalous?"
  METHOD    Answer in THREE parts. Never skip one.
            PART A - resemblance to ALKALI METALS (give 4 to 6):
                 1s1 like ns1 ; valency 1 ; forms H+ ; oxidation
                 state +1 ; reducing agent ; forms HCl / H2O / H2S
                 like NaCl / Na2O / Na2S ; liberated at the CATHODE.
            PART B - resemblance to HALOGENS (give 4 to 6):
                 one electron short of He ; diatomic H2 like X2 ;
                 forms H- ; -1 state in NaH like NaCl ; ionisation
                 enthalpy 1312 kJ/mol is HIGH like a halogen ;
                 non-metal ; covalent compounds.
            PART C - the CONCLUSION sentence:
                 "Since hydrogen resembles both but belongs fully to
                  neither, its position is ANOMALOUS. It is placed at
                  the top of Group 1 only by convention."
  TRAP      Giving only ONE list caps you at 2 out of 4. The examiner
            is paying for BOTH lists plus the conclusion.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — THE THREE ISOTOPES                             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the isotopes of hydrogen", "Compare protium,
            deuterium and tritium", "Which isotope is radioactive?"
  METHOD    Draw a TABLE with these rows every time:
                 name / symbol / protons / neutrons / mass number /
                 abundance / radioactive? / use
                 1H1 protium   0 n  A=1  99.985 %      no
                 1H2 deuterium 1 n  A=2  0.0156 %      no
                 1H3 tritium   2 n  A=3  1 in 10^18    YES, beta,
                                                        t½ 12.33 y
            Add: chemically identical (all have 1 electron), differ
            only physically because they differ in MASS.
  TRAP      Neutrons are 0, 1, 2 - NOT 1, 2, 3. The mass number is
            1, 2, 3. Do not swap them.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — HEAVY WATER                                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is heavy water?", "Give two uses of D2O", "How is
            heavy water obtained?"
  METHOD    1. D2O = deuterium oxide.
            2. Obtained by the PROLONGED ELECTROLYSIS of ordinary
               water containing alkali (H2O decomposes faster, so
               D2O concentrates).
            3. Give 2 physical differences: b.p. 374.4 K vs 373.0 K ;
               density 1.106 vs 0.997 ; max density at 284.2 K.
            4. USES: MODERATOR in nuclear reactors (slows neutrons
               without absorbing them) ; source of deuterium ; TRACER.
            5. If asked, add an exchange equation:
                    CaC2 + 2 D2O -> C2D2 + Ca(OD)2
  TRAP      D2O is HARMFUL to living things. Do not write that it is
            "purer water" or "good to drink".
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — LABORATORY PREPARATION OF DIHYDROGEN           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is dihydrogen prepared in the laboratory?",
            "Why is granulated zinc used?", "Why not nitric acid?"
  METHOD    1. Zn + dil H2SO4 -> ZnSO4 + H2   (draw the flask if asked)
            2. Zn + 2 NaOH -> Na2ZnO2 + H2    (amphoteric metals)
            3. Collected by DOWNWARD DISPLACEMENT OF WATER.
            4. WHY zinc: reacts at a convenient speed, cheap;
               GRANULES to control the rate.
            5. WHY dilute: conc. H2SO4 and HNO3 of any strength are
               OXIDISING and give SO2 / oxides of nitrogen instead.
            6. Very pure H2: CaH2 + 2 H2O -> Ca(OH)2 + 2 H2.
  TRAP      "Why not nitric acid" is worth a mark on its own. The
            answer is ONE word - OXIDISING - followed by the product
            you would get instead.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WATER GAS AND THE SHIFT REACTION               2 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is water gas?", "What is the water gas shift
            reaction?", "What is coal gasification?", "Describe the
            commercial preparation of dihydrogen."
  METHOD    Three equations, always with the conditions on the arrow:
            1. C + H2O --(1270 K)--> CO + H2          WATER GAS
               (same with coal = COAL GASIFICATION)
            2. CO + H2O --(673 K, Fe2O3/Cr2O3)--> CO2 + H2   SHIFT
            3. remove CO2 by SCRUBBING with water under pressure.
            For the 8-mark version add: CH4 + H2O --(1270 K, Ni)-->
            CO + 3 H2 ; electrolysis of water ; the chlor-alkali
            by-product ; and DRAW THE FLOW CHART.
  TRAP      The temperatures are DIFFERENT: 1270 K for water gas,
            673 K for the shift. Swapping them loses a mark.
            Water gas is CO + H2. PRODUCER GAS is CO + N2 - not the
            same thing.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — WHY DIHYDROGEN IS UNREACTIVE                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is dihydrogen relatively inert at room temperature?",
            "Why does every reaction of H2 need a catalyst?",
            "Why is atomic hydrogen more reactive than H2?"
  METHOD    1. Quote the number: H2 -> 2 H , dH = +435.88 kJ/mol.
            2. Say it is the HIGHEST for any single bond between two
               atoms.
            3. Conclude: at ordinary temperature there is not enough
               energy to break it, so H2 is unreactive; its reactions
               need HEAT, a CATALYST or an ELECTRIC ARC.
            4. For the atomic-hydrogen version, add that the bond has
               already been broken, so the expensive step is paid for.
  TRAP      "It is stable" is not an answer, it is a restatement.
            Write the NUMBER 435.88 kJ/mol - that is what is being
            paid for.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — REACTIONS OF DIHYDROGEN                             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the chemical properties of dihydrogen",
            "How does H2 react with metals / non-metals / metal oxides?"
  METHOD    Answer under FIVE headings, one equation each:
            (a) with METALS   2 Na + H2 --(673 K)--> 2 NaH
                              (H2 is the OXIDANT here, H goes to -1)
            (b) with HALOGENS H2 + Cl2 --(sunlight)--> 2 HCl
                              order F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2
            (c) with OXYGEN   2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O, dH = -285.9 kJ/mol
            (d) with NITROGEN N2 + 3 H2 <--(Fe, 200 atm)--> 2 NH3
            (e) with METAL OXIDES  CuO + H2 -> Cu + H2O
                              (H2 is the REDUCTANT here)
            Add the sentence about 435.88 kJ/mol at the start.
  TRAP      Al2O3, MgO, CaO, Na2O and K2O are NOT reduced by H2.
            Writing "H2 reduces all metal oxides" loses the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — ORTHO AND PARA HYDROGEN                        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define ortho and para hydrogen", "How does temperature
            affect the ortho:para ratio?", "How do they differ?"
  METHOD    1. Define by NUCLEAR (proton) spin:
                 ORTHO = spins PARALLEL, higher energy
                 PARA  = spins ANTI-PARALLEL, lower energy
            2. Give the ratio: 75 % ortho : 25 % para at room
               temperature (3 : 1).
            3. Temperature: cooling INCREASES para ; at 0 K it is
               100 % PARA ; ortho can NEVER exceed 75 %.
            4. Say chemical properties are IDENTICAL ; only physical
               ones differ (m.p., b.p., thermal conductivity - para
               conducts heat about 50% better).
            5. If asked: pure para is made by cooling H2 to about
               20 K over ACTIVATED CHARCOAL. Pure ortho is impossible.
  TRAP      It is the NUCLEAR spin, not the ELECTRON spin. Say
            "electron spin" and you lose the whole mark for the
            definition.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — USES OF DIHYDROGEN                             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the uses of dihydrogen", "What is the oxy-hydrogen
            flame?", "How is vanaspati made?"
  METHOD    List with an equation or a number attached to each:
              1. AMMONIA (biggest use) N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3 -> fertilisers
              2. HYDROGENATION of oils -> vanaspati (Ni, 473-523 K)
              3. METHANOL  CO + 2H2 --(ZnO-Cr2O3)--> CH3OH
              4. HCl       H2 + Cl2 --(sunlight)--> 2HCl
              5. OXY-HYDROGEN FLAME about 2800 K - welding, cutting
              6. ATOMIC HYDROGEN TORCH about 4000 K - tungsten
              7. ROCKET FUEL (liquid H2 + liquid O2)
              8. FUEL CELLS
  TRAP      2800 K is the oxy-hydrogen flame and 4000 K is the atomic
            hydrogen torch. Do not swap them.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — CLASSIFICATION OF HYDRIDES                    4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Classify hydrides with examples", "What are saline /
            interstitial hydrides?", "Explain the types of hydrides."
  METHOD    THREE headings, and for each give FOUR things:
            WHO forms it (region of the table) / BONDING / TWO
            PROPERTIES / TWO EXAMPLES.
              IONIC (SALINE)      s-block, not Be/Mg ; M+ H- ;
                                  solids, conduct when molten, H2 at
                                  the ANODE ; NaH, CaH2
              COVALENT (MOLECULAR) p-block + Be, Mg ; covalent ;
                                  gases/volatile liquids, low m.p.,
                                  non-conductors ; CH4, NH3
              METALLIC (INTERSTITIAL) d and f block ; H in the holes ;
                                  NON-STOICHIOMETRIC, conduct, used
                                  for H2 storage ; TiH1.73, LaH2.87
            Finish with the HYDRIDE GAP (groups 7, 8, 9) and the
            Be/Mg exception.
  TRAP      Do not put Be and Mg with the ionic hydrides. BeH2 and
            MgH2 are COVALENT because Be2+ and Mg2+ are small with
            high polarising power.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — THE HYDRIDE GAP                                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the hydride gap?"
  METHOD    1. The elements of GROUPS 7, 8 and 9 of the d-block -
               Mn, Tc, Re ; Fe, Ru, Os ; Co, Rh, Ir -
               do NOT form hydrides.
            2. This blank region in the middle of the periodic table
               is called the HYDRIDE GAP.
  TRAP      It is groups 7, 8, 9 - NOT groups 3, 4, 5 and NOT the
            p-block. Learn the three element families as a check.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — ELECTRON DEFICIENT / PRECISE / RICH           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Classify covalent hydrides", "What is an electron
            deficient hydride?", "Why is B2H6 a Lewis acid?"
  METHOD    Three rows, group number first:
              Group 13        ELECTRON DEFICIENT  B2H6, AlH3
                              -> fewer electrons than needed, form
                                 3-centre-2-electron BRIDGES,
                                 act as LEWIS ACIDS
              Group 14        ELECTRON PRECISE    CH4, SiH4
                              -> exactly enough, tetrahedral, neutral
              Groups 15,16,17 ELECTRON RICH       NH3, H2O, HF
                              -> extra electrons appear as LONE PAIRS,
                                 act as LEWIS BASES
  TRAP      "Electron rich" means it has LONE PAIRS, not that it has
            more hydrogens. NH3 has 3 H and H2O has 2 H, but H2O is
            richer (2 lone pairs versus 1).
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — STRUCTURE OF A WATER MOLECULE                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the structure of water in the gas phase", "Why is
            the bond angle 104.5 and not 109.5?"
  METHOD    1. Oxygen is sp3 hybridised, 2 bond pairs + 2 LONE PAIRS.
            2. Electron geometry TETRAHEDRAL, molecular shape BENT.
            3. H-O-H = 104.5 deg ; O-H = 95.7 pm ; dipole 1.84 D.
            4. Reason for 104.5: lp-lp repulsion > lp-bp > bp-bp, so
               the lone pairs squeeze the O-H bonds together.
            5. Add that water is POLAR because the bent shape stops
               the two O-H dipoles cancelling.
  TRAP      104.5 deg is the GAS PHASE value. In ICE the angle opens
            to about 109.5 deg. Read which one the question wants.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — STRUCTURE OF ICE AND WHY IT FLOATS            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the structure of ice", "Why does ice float on
            water?", "Why is ice less dense than water?"
  METHOD    Four numbered steps, and DRAW the hexagon:
            1. Each O is surrounded TETRAHEDRALLY by FOUR others -
               two covalent O-H (about 101 pm), two hydrogen bonds
               (about 175 pm). Each H2O makes FOUR hydrogen bonds.
            2. This gives an OPEN HEXAGONAL CAGE with large HOLES.
            3. Same mass, greater volume -> lower density:
                    ice 0.917 , water 0.9998 g/cm3.
            4. On melting about 15% of H-bonds break, the cage
               collapses, molecules fill the holes -> liquid denser.
            5. FINISH with the significance: floating ice INSULATES
               the lake, water below stays at 277 K, aquatic life
               survives winter.
  TRAP      The last step is worth a mark on its own in a 4-mark
            question. Never stop at "so it floats".
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — ANOMALOUS PROPERTIES OF WATER                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the anomalous behaviour of water", "Why does
            water have a high boiling point / specific heat?",
            "Why is water called the universal solvent?"
  METHOD    1. State the ONE cause first: HYDROGEN BONDING, four
               bonds per molecule.
            2. Then list the consequences with numbers:
                 b.p. 373 K vs H2S 213 K
                 m.p. 273 K vs H2S 187 K
                 specific heat 4.18 J/g/K
                 enthalpy of vaporisation 40.7 kJ/mol
                 surface tension 72.75 dyne/cm
                 maximum density at 277 K
                 ice less dense than water
            3. Universal solvent: dielectric constant 78.4 plus
               polarity -> weakens ionic attraction ~78-fold ->
               then HYDRATES each ion.
  TRAP      Give NUMBERS and a COMPARISON (H2O versus H2S). "It has
            a high boiling point" with no comparison is half an
            answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — CHEMICAL PROPERTIES OF WATER                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the chemical properties of water", "Show that
            water is amphoteric", "What are hydrolysis reactions?"
  METHOD    FOUR headings, one or two equations each:
            (a) AMPHOTERIC  H2O + NH3 <--> NH4+ + OH-   (acid)
                            H2O + H2S <--> H3O+ + HS-   (base)
            (b) REDOX       2 Na + 2 H2O -> 2 NaOH + H2 (oxidant)
                            2 F2 + 2 H2O -> 4 HF + O2   (reductant)
            (c) HYDROLYSIS  SO3 + H2O -> H2SO4     (oxide -> acid)
                            CaO + H2O -> Ca(OH)2   (oxide -> base)
                            CaC2 + 2 H2O -> C2H2 + Ca(OH)2
            (d) HYDRATES    coordinated / interstitial /
                            hydrogen-bonded ; CuSO4.5H2O =
                            [Cu(H2O)4]SO4.H2O
  TRAP      For "amphoteric" you must show BOTH behaviours with a
            partner in each case. One equation is half a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — HARD WATER: CAUSE AND TYPE                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is hard water?", "What causes temporary /
            permanent hardness?", "Why does soap not lather?"
  METHOD    1. Hard water does not lather with soap; it forms a
               white curdy SCUM.
            2. Cause: dissolved Ca2+ and Mg2+ salts.
            3. TEMPORARY = BICARBONATES Ca(HCO3)2, Mg(HCO3)2 -
               removed by boiling.
               PERMANENT = CHLORIDES and SULPHATES CaCl2, MgCl2,
               CaSO4, MgSO4 - not removed by boiling.
            4. The soap equation:
               2 C17H35COONa + CaCl2 -> (C17H35COO)2Ca + 2 NaCl
  TRAP      The single most-swapped pair in the chapter. Say it out
            loud: "TEMPORARY is BICARBONATE."
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — DISADVANTAGES OF HARD WATER                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the disadvantages of hard water", "What is boiler
            scale?"
  METHOD    Three headings:
            DOMESTIC  - wastes soap, scum on clothes and skin,
                        pulses do not cook, bad taste
            BOILERS   - SCALE or FUR of CaCO3/CaSO4/Mg(OH)2, bad
                        conductor so WASTE OF FUEL, blocks pipes,
                        can crack the plate and cause a BOILER
                        EXPLOSION
            INDUSTRY  - ruins dyeing, tanning, paper, sugar, textiles
  TRAP      The boiler explosion point is the one the examiner is
            looking for. Always include it.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — REMOVING TEMPORARY HARDNESS                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is temporary hardness removed?", "What is Clark's
            method?"
  METHOD    1. BOILING:
                 Ca(HCO3)2 --(boil)--> CaCO3(down) + H2O + CO2
                 Mg(HCO3)2 --(boil)--> Mg(OH)2(down) + 2 CO2
            2. CLARK'S METHOD - a CALCULATED quantity of slaked lime:
                 Ca(HCO3)2 + Ca(OH)2 -> 2 CaCO3(down) + 2 H2O
                 Mg(HCO3)2 + 2 Ca(OH)2 -> 2 CaCO3 + Mg(OH)2 + 2 H2O
            3. Say WHY calculated: excess Ca(OH)2 would itself make
               the water hard again.
  TRAP      Magnesium bicarbonate gives the HYDROXIDE Mg(OH)2, not
            the carbonate, because Mg(OH)2 is even less soluble.
            And "calculated quantity" is worth a mark on its own.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — REMOVING PERMANENT HARDNESS                   4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is permanent hardness removed?", "Describe any three
            methods of softening water."
  METHOD    Four methods, each with its equation:
            (a) WASHING SODA  CaCl2 + Na2CO3 -> CaCO3(down) + 2 NaCl
            (b) CALGON Na2[Na4(PO3)6]
                  2 CaSO4 + Na2[Na4(PO3)6] -> Na2[Ca2(PO3)6]
                                               + 2 Na2SO4
                  ADVANTAGE: NO PRECIPITATE - soluble complex
            (c) PERMUTIT Na2Z (Na2Al2Si2O8.xH2O)
                  Na2Z + Ca2+ -> CaZ + 2 Na+
                  REGENERATE with BRINE: CaZ + 2NaCl -> Na2Z + CaCl2
            (d) SYNTHETIC RESINS
                  2 RSO3H + Ca2+ -> (RSO3)2Ca + 2 H+
                  RNH3OH + Cl-   -> RNH3Cl + OH-
                  then H+ + OH- -> H2O -> DE-IONISED water
  TRAP      Never leave out REGENERATION for the permutit method -
            it is always worth a mark. And say WHY calgon is better
            (no precipitate).
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — PREPARATION OF HYDROGEN PEROXIDE              2 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is H2O2 prepared?", "Explain the industrial
            preparation of H2O2", "Why is hydrated BaO2 used?"
  METHOD    Three routes:
            (a) BaO2.8H2O + H2SO4 -> BaSO4(down) + H2O2 + 8 H2O
                ice-cold DILUTE acid, added SLOWLY
                WHY H2SO4: BaSO4 is insoluble and separates itself
                WHY HYDRATED BaO2: dry BaO2 gets coated with BaSO4
                and the reaction stops
            (b) ELECTROLYSIS of 50% H2SO4, Pt, high current density:
                2 HSO4- -> H2S2O8 + 2 e-        (Marshall's acid)
                H2S2O8 + 2 H2O -> 2 H2SO4 + H2O2
                (via H2SO5, Caro's acid)
            (c) 2-ETHYLANTHRAQUINOL AUTOXIDATION (industrial):
                quinol + O2 -> quinone + H2O2
                quinone + H2 --(Pd)--> quinol
                net: H2 + O2 -> H2O2 ; the organic is RECYCLED
            Finish with: concentrated by DISTILLATION UNDER REDUCED
            PRESSURE, because H2O2 decomposes on heating.
  TRAP      Marshall's = H2S2O8 (two sulphurs). Caro's = H2SO5 (one
            sulphur). Swapping them is the classic error.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — STRUCTURE OF HYDROGEN PEROXIDE                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the structure of H2O2", "Give the dihedral angle",
            "Why do the gas and solid values differ?"
  METHOD    1. Say the two words: NON-PLANAR and OPEN BOOK. The two
               O-H bonds lie in different planes, with the O-O bond
               as the SPINE.
            2. DRAW it.
            3. Give the table of SIX values:
                                    GAS        SOLID (110 K)
                 O-O length       147.5 pm     145.8 pm
                 O-H length        95.0 pm      98.8 pm
                 O-O-H angle       94.8 deg    101.9 deg
                 DIHEDRAL angle   111.5 deg     90.2 deg
            4. WHY they differ in the solid: HYDROGEN BONDING between
               molecules twists the O-H groups round.
  TRAP      The DIHEDRAL angle is the one being asked for 90% of the
            time. 111.5 GAS , 90.2 SOLID. Do not confuse it with the
            O-O-H bond angle (94.8 / 101.9).
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — STORAGE OF HYDROGEN PEROXIDE                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is H2O2 stored in dark bottles?", "Why is a
            stabiliser added?", "How is H2O2 stored?"
  METHOD    1. State the decomposition: 2 H2O2 -> 2 H2O + O2.
            2. Give the catalysts of that decomposition: LIGHT, heat,
               ALKALI, rough surfaces, dust, metal ions, catalase.
            3. Match each precaution to its reason:
                 DARK bottle       - light catalyses it
                 wax-lined glass   - ordinary glass is alkaline and
                                     rough
                 cool, dust-free   - heat and dust catalyse it
                 STABILISER        - urea, phosphoric acid, glycerol,
                                     acetanilide; keeps it faintly
                                     acidic and traps metal ions
                 loosely stoppered - the O2 must escape
  TRAP      Name at least one actual STABILISER. "A stabiliser is
            added" without a name usually loses half the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — H2O2 AS AN OXIDISING AGENT                         4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "H2O2 as an oxidising agent in acidic and basic media",
            "Give two reactions where H2O2 oxidises."
  METHOD    Write the HALF REACTION first, then two examples each:
            ACIDIC:  H2O2 + 2 H+ + 2 e- -> 2 H2O
                 2 Fe2+ + 2 H+ + H2O2 -> 2 Fe3+ + 2 H2O
                 2 I- + 2 H+ + H2O2 -> I2 + 2 H2O
                 PbS + 4 H2O2 -> PbSO4 + 4 H2O  (black -> white)
            BASIC:   H2O2 + 2 e- -> 2 OH-
                 2 Fe2+ + H2O2 -> 2 Fe3+ + 2 OH-
                 Mn2+ + H2O2 -> Mn4+ + 2 OH-
            Add the reason: oxygen in H2O2 is at -1 and is REDUCED
            to -2.
  TRAP      In an OXIDISING reaction NO oxygen gas is produced. If
            you have written O2 among the products, you have written
            a reducing reaction by mistake.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — H2O2 AS A REDUCING AGENT                           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "H2O2 as a reducing agent in acidic and basic media",
            "Why does H2O2 decolourise acidified KMnO4?"
  METHOD    Half reaction first, then two examples each:
            ACIDIC:  H2O2 -> O2 + 2 H+ + 2 e-
                 2 MnO4- + 6 H+ + 5 H2O2 -> 2 Mn2+ + 8 H2O + 5 O2
                 Cl2 + H2O2 -> 2 HCl + O2
                 Ag2O + H2O2 -> 2 Ag + H2O + O2
            BASIC:   H2O2 + 2 OH- -> O2 + 2 H2O + 2 e-
                 I2 + H2O2 + 2 OH- -> 2 I- + 2 H2O + O2
                 2 MnO4- + 3 H2O2 -> 2 MnO2 + 3 O2 + 2 H2O + 2 OH-
            Add the reason: oxygen in H2O2 is at -1 and is OXIDISED
            to 0.
  TRAP      Every single reducing equation MUST show O2 among the
            products. If it does not, it is wrong. Use this as your
            own check before you move on.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — VOLUME STRENGTH NUMERICAL                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the strength / molarity / normality of x
            volume H2O2", "What is meant by 20 volume H2O2?"
  METHOD    1. Define it: 1 mL of the solution gives x mL of O2 at
               STP, from 2 H2O2 -> 2 H2O + O2.
            2. Apply the right formula:
                  strength (g/L) = V x 17/5.6  = V x 3.035
                  molarity       = V / 11.2
                  normality      = V / 5.6
                  normality      = 2 x molarity
            3. CROSS-CHECK: divide your g/L by 34; you must get the
               molarity. If not, you used the wrong constant.
            4. State the units.
  TRAP      /11.2 gives MOLARITY and /5.6 gives NORMALITY - not the
            other way round. Anchor yourself on
            "11.2 volume = 1 M = 2 N = 34 g/L".
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — PERCENTAGE TO VOLUME STRENGTH                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A bottle is labelled 30% H2O2. Find its volume strength."
  METHOD    1. % (w/V) means grams per 100 mL. Multiply by 10 to get
               g/L.       30 % -> 300 g/L
            2. Then divide by 3.035 (or multiply by 5.6/17):
                    V = 300 x 5.6 / 17 = 98.8 , about 100 volume
            3. Quote the standard pairs as a check:
                    3 % = 10 V  ,  6 % = 20 V  ,  30 % = 100 V
  TRAP      Do not forget the x10 step. Working straight from "30"
            instead of "300" gives an answer ten times too small.
```

  PATTERN 28 — KMnO4 TITRATION OF H2O2                            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "x mL of H2O2 needed y mL of z N KMnO4. Find the volume
            strength / strength."
  METHOD    1. Write the equation to justify the titration:
                 2 MnO4- + 6 H+ + 5 H2O2 -> 2 Mn2+ + 8 H2O + 5 O2
            2. Equate equivalents:  N1 V1 = N2 V2
                                   (H2O2)   (KMnO4)
            3. Get N(H2O2).
            4. Convert: volume strength = N x 5.6
                        strength g/L    = N x 17
            5. Cross-check with molarity = N/2.
  TRAP      N1V1 = N2V2 needs NORMALITIES on both sides. If the
            KMnO4 is given as a MOLARITY, multiply it by 5 first
            (MnO4- accepts 5 electrons in acid medium).

  PATTERN 29 — USES OF HYDROGEN PEROXIDE                          2 marks

  TRIGGER   "Write any two / four uses of H2O2."
  METHOD    Pick from these, and attach the reason or equation:

              2. BLEACHING delicate things - hair, silk, wool, ivory,

  PATTERN 30 — HYDROGEN AS A FUEL / HYDROGEN ECONOMY         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the hydrogen economy?", "Why is hydrogen called
            a clean fuel?", "What are the problems in using H2 as a
            fuel?"
  METHOD    1. Definition: storing and transporting energy as LIQUID
               or GASEOUS DIHYDROGEN rather than as electricity.
            2. The killer sentence: hydrogen is an energy CARRIER,
               not an energy SOURCE.
            3. Advantages with numbers:
                 150 kJ/g versus 45 kJ/g for petrol (3 times)
                 only product is WATER - no CO2, CO, SO2, NOx, soot
                 fuel cells are 60-70% efficient versus 25-30%
            4. Problems: storage / transport (low density),
               explosive from 4% to 75% in air, cost of production,
               metal embrittlement.
            5. Solutions: metal hydride storage (LaNi5, FeTi,
               Mg2Ni) ; HCNG (5% H2 in CNG).
  TRAP      150 kJ is PER GRAM. 285.9 kJ is PER MOLE. Quoting 285.9
            as the "energy per gram" is a common and costly slip.
