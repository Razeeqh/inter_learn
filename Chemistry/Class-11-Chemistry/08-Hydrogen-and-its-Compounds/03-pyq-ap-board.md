## Topic: Position and isotopes

### Q A1
Why is the position of hydrogen in the periodic table said to be anomalous?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrogen resembles the ALKALI METALS in some ways (1s1 configuration,
  valency 1, forms H+, is a reducing agent) and resembles the HALOGENS
  in other ways (diatomic H2, forms H-, high ionisation enthalpy,
  is a non-metal).

  Since it fits BOTH groups partly and NEITHER group fully, its
  position cannot be fixed with certainty. Hence it is ANOMALOUS.
```

---

### Q A2
Give any two points in which hydrogen resembles the alkali metals.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Both have ONE electron in the outermost shell
     (H is 1s1 ; alkali metals are ns1).
  2. Both lose that electron to form UNIPOSITIVE ions:
         H -> H+ + e-      and     Na -> Na+ + e-
     (any of: valency 1, oxidation state +1, both are reducing
      agents, both form halides and oxides of similar type,
      both are liberated at the cathode)
```

---

### Q A3
Give any two points in which hydrogen resembles the halogens.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Like the halogens, hydrogen is ONE electron short of the nearest
     noble gas configuration (He, 1s2), and it exists as a DIATOMIC
     molecule H2, just as halogens exist as X2.
  2. Both form UNINEGATIVE ions and show the -1 oxidation state
     with metals:
         NaH  (Na+ H-)      and     NaCl  (Na+ Cl-)
  (also: high ionisation enthalpy, non-metallic, covalent compounds
   with non-metals)
```

### Q A6
What is heavy water? Write any two of its uses.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HEAVY WATER is DEUTERIUM OXIDE, D2O - water in which both hydrogen
```

## Topic: Preparation of dihydrogen

### Q A8
How is dihydrogen prepared in the laboratory? Write the equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Granulated ZINC is treated with DILUTE SULPHURIC ACID:

       Zn  +  dil H2SO4   ->   ZnSO4  +  H2

  The gas is collected by the DOWNWARD DISPLACEMENT OF WATER,
  because dihydrogen is almost insoluble in water and is lighter
  than air.
```

---

### Q A9
Why is nitric acid not used for the laboratory preparation of dihydrogen?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Nitric acid, at any concentration, is a strong OXIDISING agent.
  It oxidises the dihydrogen as soon as it is formed, so OXIDES OF
  NITROGEN (NO, NO2, N2O) are obtained instead of H2.
  For the same reason CONCENTRATED sulphuric acid is not used -
  it gives SO2.
```

---

### Q A10
What is water gas? How is it prepared?

**ANSWER:**
```
  WATER GAS (also called SYNTHESIS GAS or SYNGAS) is the mixture of
  CARBON MONOXIDE and DIHYDROGEN.

  It is prepared by passing STEAM over RED-HOT COKE at 1270 K:

       C(s)  +  H2O(g)  --(1270 K)-->   CO(g)  +  H2(g)

  When coal is used instead of coke the process is called
  COAL GASIFICATION.
```

---

### Q A11
What is the water gas shift reaction?

**ANSWER:**
```
  It is the reaction used to convert the CARBON MONOXIDE of water gas
  into carbon dioxide, so that MORE dihydrogen is produced:

    CO(g) + H2O(g) --(673 K, Fe2O3 + Cr2O3 catalyst)--> CO2(g) + H2(g)

  The CO2 is then removed by SCRUBBING with water under pressure,
  leaving pure dihydrogen.
```

---

### Q A12
How is dihydrogen obtained as a by-product in the chlor-alkali industry?

**ANSWER:**
```
  When concentrated aqueous sodium chloride (BRINE) is electrolysed
  to make NaOH and chlorine, dihydrogen is released at the cathode:

    2 NaCl(aq) + 2 H2O --(electrolysis)--> 2 NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g)

       cathode :  2 H2O + 2 e-  ->  H2  +  2 OH-
       anode   :  2 Cl-         ->  Cl2 +  2 e-
```

## Topic: Properties of dihydrogen

### Q A13
Why is dihydrogen relatively unreactive at room temperature?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because the H-H bond is extremely strong:

       H2(g)  ->  2 H(g)     delta H  =  + 435.88 kJ/mol

  This is the HIGHEST bond dissociation enthalpy for any single bond
  between two atoms. At ordinary temperature there is not enough
  energy to break it, so dihydrogen does not react.
  Its reactions need HEAT, a CATALYST, or an ELECTRIC ARC.
```

---

### Q A14
What is occlusion? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  OCCLUSION is the ABSORPTION of a gas by a metal.

  EXAMPLE: palladium occludes dihydrogen - it can absorb about
  900 TIMES its own volume of H2. Platinum and nickel also
  occlude hydrogen.
  This is the basis of storing hydrogen in metal hydrides.
```

---

### Q A15
Define ortho and para hydrogen.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In a dihydrogen molecule the two protons (nuclei) spin.

  ORTHO hydrogen : the two NUCLEAR spins are PARALLEL
                   (in the same direction)  ->  higher energy

  PARA  hydrogen : the two NUCLEAR spins are ANTI-PARALLEL
                   (in opposite directions) ->  lower energy, stable

  Ordinary hydrogen at room temperature is 75% ORTHO and 25% PARA.
```

---

### Q A16
What happens to the ortho : para ratio when hydrogen is cooled to 0 K?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Para hydrogen has the LOWER energy, so as the temperature is
  lowered the proportion of PARA increases.

  At ABSOLUTE ZERO (0 K), hydrogen is 100 % PARA and 0 % ORTHO.

  (At room temperature it is 75% ortho and 25% para, and the ortho
   form can never exceed 75% however high the temperature.)
```

---

### Q A17
What is the oxy-hydrogen flame? Give its temperature and one use.

**ANSWER:**
```
  When dihydrogen is burnt in a stream of pure OXYGEN, the flame
  produced is called the OXY-HYDROGEN FLAME:

       2 H2  +  O2   ->   2 H2O      delta H = -285.9 kJ/mol

  TEMPERATURE:  about 2800 K
  USE:  for CUTTING and WELDING metals.

  (The ATOMIC HYDROGEN TORCH, in which H atoms recombine on the
   metal surface, is even hotter - about 4000 K - and is used for
   welding high melting point metals such as tungsten.)
```

## Topic: Hydrides

### Q A18
What are ionic (saline) hydrides? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  IONIC or SALINE HYDRIDES are the hydrides formed by the highly
  electropositive s-block metals, in which hydrogen is present as
  the HYDRIDE ION, H-, in the -1 oxidation state.

  They are white crystalline solids, non-volatile, and conduct
  electricity only when molten.

  EXAMPLES:  NaH  ,  CaH2   (also LiH, KH, BaH2)
```

---

### Q A19
What is an interstitial (metallic) hydride? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  METALLIC or INTERSTITIAL HYDRIDES are formed by d-block and f-block
  metals. The hydrogen atoms simply occupy the HOLES (interstices)
  between the metal atoms in the crystal lattice.

  They are NON-STOICHIOMETRIC - the formula is not a whole-number
  ratio - and they conduct electricity.

  EXAMPLES:  TiH1.73  ,  LaH2.87   (also ZrH1.30, VH0.56, PdH0.6)
```

---

### Q A20
What is the hydride gap?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The elements of GROUPS 7, 8 and 9 of the d-block -
  that is Mn, Tc, Re ; Fe, Ru, Os ; Co, Rh, Ir -
  DO NOT form hydrides at all.

  This blank region in the middle of the periodic table is called
  the HYDRIDE GAP.
```

---

### Q A21
Why are BeH2 and MgH2 not ionic hydrides?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Be2+ and Mg2+ are very SMALL ions with a HIGH CHARGE DENSITY, so
  they have a very high POLARISING POWER. They distort the electron
  cloud of the H- ion so strongly that the bond becomes COVALENT.

  Hence BeH2 and MgH2 are COVALENT (polymeric) hydrides, not ionic,
  even though beryllium and magnesium are s-block metals.
```

---

### Q A22
During the electrolysis of molten sodium hydride, at which electrode is
hydrogen liberated? Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrogen is liberated at the ANODE.

  REASON: in NaH, hydrogen is present as the NEGATIVE hydride ion H-.
  Negative ions migrate to the positive electrode (the anode):

       2 H-   ->   H2   +   2 e-      (at the ANODE)
       Na+ + e- -> Na                 (at the cathode)

  This experiment PROVES that the H- ion exists.
```

## Topic: Water

### Q A23
Give the bond angle and the shape of a water molecule in the gas phase.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SHAPE      :  BENT (angular, V-shaped)
  BOND ANGLE :  H-O-H  =  104.5 degrees
  BOND LENGTH:  O-H    =  95.7 pm
  HYBRIDISATION of O : sp3, with 2 bond pairs and 2 LONE PAIRS

  The angle is less than the ideal 109.5 degrees because the two
  LONE PAIRS repel the bond pairs more strongly than the bond pairs
  repel each other, squeezing the O-H bonds together.
```

### Q A24
Why does ice float on water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In ICE, every oxygen atom is joined TETRAHEDRALLY to FOUR others by
  hydrogen bonds. This forces the molecules into an OPEN, HEXAGONAL,
  CAGE-LIKE structure containing LARGE EMPTY SPACES.

  The same mass therefore occupies a GREATER volume as ice, so its
  DENSITY IS LOWER:
        ice   0.917 g/cm3     water  0.9998 g/cm3

  Being less dense, ice FLOATS on water.
```

---

### Q A25
At what temperature does water have its maximum density? Why is this
important for aquatic life?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Water has its MAXIMUM DENSITY at 277 K (4 degrees C).

  IMPORTANCE: in winter, water cools and sinks until the whole lake is
  at 277 K. Below that temperature it becomes LIGHTER, stays on top and
  freezes there. The floating ice acts as an INSULATING BLANKET, so
  the water beneath remains liquid at about 277 K and fish and plants
  survive the winter.
```

### Q A26
What is meant by the amphoteric nature of water? Give one equation for each
behaviour.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AMPHOTERIC means water can act BOTH as an ACID (donating a proton)
  and as a BASE (accepting a proton).

  AS AN ACID :   H2O  +  NH3   <-->   NH4+   +   OH-
  AS A BASE  :   H2O  +  H2S   <-->   H3O+   +   HS-
```

### Q A27
Why does CCl4 not undergo hydrolysis while SiCl4 does?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Carbon belongs to Period 2 and has NO VACANT d-ORBITALS, so a water
  molecule cannot attach its lone pair to the carbon atom. Hydrolysis
  therefore cannot begin, and CCl4 is unaffected by water.

  Silicon belongs to Period 3 and DOES have vacant 3d orbitals, which
  can accept the lone pair of water, so SiCl4 hydrolyses readily:

       SiCl4  +  2 H2O   ->   SiO2  +  4 HCl
```

## Topic: Hard water

### Q A28
What is temporary hardness of water? How is it caused?

**ANSWER:**
```
  TEMPORARY HARDNESS is the hardness that can be removed simply by
  BOILING the water.

  It is caused by the dissolved BICARBONATES of calcium and
  magnesium:
        Ca(HCO3)2    and    Mg(HCO3)2

  On boiling these decompose to insoluble carbonate / hydroxide:
        Ca(HCO3)2 --(boil)--> CaCO3 (down) + H2O + CO2
```

---

### Q A29
What is permanent hardness? Name the salts responsible.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PERMANENT HARDNESS is the hardness that is NOT removed by boiling.

  It is caused by the dissolved CHLORIDES and SULPHATES of calcium
  and magnesium:
        CaCl2  ,  MgCl2  ,  CaSO4  ,  MgSO4

  It is removed by washing soda, calgon, permutit or ion-exchange
  resins.
```

---

### Q A30
What is Clark's method? Write the equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CLARK'S METHOD removes TEMPORARY hardness by adding a CALCULATED
  quantity of SLAKED LIME, Ca(OH)2:

     Ca(HCO3)2  +  Ca(OH)2   ->   2 CaCO3 (down)  +  2 H2O

  The soluble bicarbonate is turned into insoluble carbonate, which
  is filtered off.

  Only the CALCULATED quantity is used, because EXCESS Ca(OH)2 would
  itself make the water hard again.
```

---

### Q A31
What is calgon? Write its formula and state its main advantage.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CALGON is SODIUM HEXAMETAPHOSPHATE.
  The name comes from "CALcium GONe".

  FORMULA:   Na6P6O18  ,  written for reactions as   Na2[Na4(PO3)6]

  It removes BOTH temporary and permanent hardness:
     2 CaSO4 + Na2[Na4(PO3)6] -> Na2[Ca2(PO3)6] + 2 Na2SO4

  MAIN ADVANTAGE: the Ca2+ ions are locked inside a SOLUBLE COMPLEX,
  so NO PRECIPITATE is formed. There is nothing to filter and no
  sludge or scale in the boiler.
```

---

### Q A32
What is permutit? How is an exhausted permutit bed regenerated?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PERMUTIT is artificial ZEOLITE - hydrated sodium aluminium silicate,
  Na2Al2Si2O8 . x H2O (written for short as Na2Z).

  It softens water by ION EXCHANGE:
       Na2Z  +  Ca2+   ->   CaZ   +   2 Na+

  REGENERATION: when all the sodium is used up, the bed is washed with
  a concentrated solution of BRINE (10% NaCl):

       CaZ  +  2 NaCl   ->   Na2Z   +   CaCl2

  The CaCl2 is washed away and the bed can be used again.
```

---

### Q A33
Why does soap not lather in hard water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Soap is sodium stearate, C17H35COONa. The calcium and magnesium ions
  of hard water replace the sodium and form INSOLUBLE stearates, which
  appear as a white curdy SCUM:

   2 C17H35COONa  +  CaCl2  ->  (C17H35COO)2Ca (scum)  +  2 NaCl

  All the soap is destroyed as scum before any lather can form.
```

## Topic: Hydrogen peroxide

### Q A34
How is hydrogen peroxide prepared in the laboratory from barium peroxide?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrated barium peroxide is added SLOWLY, in small amounts, to
  ICE-COLD DILUTE sulphuric acid:

     BaO2 . 8H2O  +  H2SO4   ->   BaSO4 (down)  +  H2O2  +  8 H2O

  The insoluble BaSO4 is filtered off, leaving a dilute solution
  of hydrogen peroxide.
```

### Q A35
Why is hydrated barium peroxide used instead of the dry (anhydrous) form?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Dry BaO2 quickly becomes COATED with a layer of insoluble BaSO4.
  This coating prevents the acid from reaching the rest of the BaO2,
  and the reaction stops.

  The HYDRATED form does not cake up like this, so the reaction
  continues to completion.
```

### Q A36
Draw the structure of hydrogen peroxide and give the dihedral angle in the
gas phase.

**ANSWER:**
```
  H2O2 is NON-PLANAR. Its shape is described as an OPEN BOOK - the
  two O-H bonds lie in two different planes, like the two pages of a
  half-open book, with the O-O bond as the spine.

           H
            \
             \                        <- page 1
              O -------------- O
              |     SPINE      |\
              |   (O-O bond)   | \
              |                |  H   <- page 2

  GAS PHASE:      dihedral angle = 111.5 deg
                  O-O = 147.5 pm , O-H = 95.0 pm , O-O-H = 94.8 deg

  SOLID (110 K):  dihedral angle =  90.2 deg
```

### Q A37
Why is hydrogen peroxide stored in dark coloured bottles?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrogen peroxide decomposes into water and oxygen:

       2 H2O2   ->   2 H2O   +   O2

  This decomposition is CATALYSED BY LIGHT. A dark coloured (brown or
  opaque) bottle keeps light out and slows the decomposition.

  For the same reason it is kept in a COOL place, in WAX-LINED glass
  or plastic (ordinary glass is slightly alkaline and rough, both of
  which catalyse decomposition), and a STABILISER such as UREA,
  PHOSPHORIC ACID, GLYCEROL or ACETANILIDE is added.
```

---

### Q A38
Define the volume strength of a hydrogen peroxide solution. What does
"10 volume H2O2" mean?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The VOLUME STRENGTH of a hydrogen peroxide solution is the volume of
  OXYGEN, measured at STP, that is liberated by the complete
  decomposition of ONE VOLUME of that solution.

       2 H2O2   ->   2 H2O   +   O2

  "10 VOLUME H2O2" therefore means that 1 mL of the solution gives
  10 mL of oxygen at STP (and 1 litre gives 10 litres of oxygen).

  Its strength = 10 x 17/5.6 = 30.4 g/L.
```

---

### Q A39
Why is hydrogen peroxide concentrated by distillation under reduced pressure?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrogen peroxide DECOMPOSES on heating:

       2 H2O2   ->   2 H2O   +   O2      delta H = -196 kJ/mol

  If it were distilled at atmospheric pressure the high temperature
  required would decompose it, and it could even explode.

  Under REDUCED PRESSURE the liquid boils at a much LOWER temperature,
  so the water evaporates off and the H2O2 survives.
```

### Q A40
Write any two uses of hydrogen peroxide.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. As a mild ANTISEPTIC and DISINFECTANT for washing wounds, and as
     an antiseptic mouthwash.
  2. As a BLEACHING AGENT for delicate materials such as hair, silk,
     wool, ivory and feathers, which chlorine would damage.

  (also: restoring old oil paintings, PbS + 4H2O2 -> PbSO4 + 4H2O ;

   pollution control)
```

### Q A41
Why is hydrogen peroxide able to act both as an oxidising agent and as a
reducing agent?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In H2O2 the oxidation state of oxygen is -1, which is the MIDDLE
  of its range.

        H2O (-2)   <--   H2O2 (-1)   -->   O2 (0)

  It can be REDUCED to -2 (in water), in which case H2O2 is the
  OXIDISING agent; or it can be OXIDISED to 0 (as O2 gas), in which
  case H2O2 is the REDUCING agent.

  RULE: whenever OXYGEN GAS appears among the products, H2O2 has
  acted as a REDUCING agent.
```

### Q A42
What is the energy released per gram by dihydrogen, and how does it compare
with petrol?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIHYDROGEN releases about  150 kJ per gram.
  PETROL     releases about   45 kJ per gram.

  Dihydrogen therefore gives about THREE TIMES as much energy per gram
  as petrol, and its only combustion product is WATER - no CO2, CO,
  SO2, oxides of nitrogen or soot. It is the cleanest fuel known.
```

### Q B1
Justify the position of hydrogen in the periodic table on the basis of its
electronic configuration.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PART 1 - RESEMBLANCE TO ALKALI METALS (Group 1)

     1. Hydrogen has the configuration 1s1, and alkali metals have
        ns1 - both have ONE electron in the outermost shell.
     2. Both show a valency of ONE and an oxidation state of +1.
     3. Both form UNIPOSITIVE ions:
              H -> H+ + e-        Na -> Na+ + e-
     4. Both are good REDUCING agents.
     5. Both form halides, oxides and sulphides of similar type:
              HCl / NaCl ,  H2O / Na2O ,  H2S / Na2S
     6. Both are liberated at the CATHODE during electrolysis.

  PART 2 - RESEMBLANCE TO HALOGENS (Group 17)

     1. Like the halogens, hydrogen is ONE electron short of the
        nearest noble gas configuration (He, 1s2).
     2. Both exist as DIATOMIC molecules: H2 and X2.
     3. Both form UNINEGATIVE ions: H- and X-.
     4. Both show the -1 oxidation state with metals:
              NaH (Na+ H-)   and   NaCl (Na+ Cl-)
     5. The ionisation enthalpy of hydrogen (1312 kJ/mol) is HIGH,
        like a halogen (F 1680, Cl 1255) and quite unlike an alkali
        metal (Li 520, Na 496).
     6. Both are non-metals and both form covalent compounds with
        other non-metals.

  PART 3 - CONCLUSION

     Because hydrogen resembles BOTH families but belongs FULLY to
     NEITHER, its position is described as ANOMALOUS. By convention
     it is placed at the top of Group 1, but only as a convenience.
```

### Q B2
Compare the three isotopes of hydrogen.

**ANSWER:**
```
  +--------------+-------------+---------------+----------------+
  |  PROPERTY    |  PROTIUM    |  DEUTERIUM    |  TRITIUM       |
  |              |    1H1      |   1H2  (D)    |   1H3  (T)     |
  +--------------+-------------+---------------+----------------+
  |  Protons     |     1       |      1        |      1         |
  |  Neutrons    |     0       |      1        |      2         |
  |  Mass number |     1       |      2        |      3         |
  |  Atomic mass |   1.008     |    2.014      |    3.016       |
  |  Abundance   |  99.985 %   |   0.0156 %    |  1 in 10^18    |
  |  Radioactive |    NO       |     NO        |     YES        |
  |  Radiation   |     -       |      -        |  beta (b-)     |
  |  Half-life   |     -       |      -        |  12.33 years   |
  |  Molecule    |    H2       |     D2        |     T2         |
  |  Oxide       |   H2O       |   D2O         |    T2O         |
  |              |  (water)    | (heavy water) |                |
  |  Boiling pt  |  20.4 K     |   23.5 K      |   25.0 K       |
  |  of molecule |             |               |                |
  +--------------+-------------+---------------+----------------+

  Chemically all three behave the SAME (each has one electron).
  They differ only PHYSICALLY, because they differ in MASS. The
  mass ratio of D to H is 2:1, the largest for any pair of isotopes
  in the periodic table, so D2 and D2O react noticeably more slowly
  than H2 and H2O. This is the KINETIC ISOTOPE EFFECT.

  USES:  protium - fuel, ammonia manufacture
         deuterium - moderator in reactors, tracer
         tritium - radioactive tracer, fusion bombs, luminous paint
```

### Q B3
Describe the laboratory preparation of dihydrogen. Why is granulated zinc
used, and why is dilute sulphuric acid used?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE: an active metal displaces hydrogen from a dilute acid.

  METHOD 1 - metal with a dilute acid

     Granulated ZINC is placed in a flask fitted with a thistle
     funnel and a delivery tube. DILUTE SULPHURIC ACID is poured in
     through the funnel.

          Zn  +  dil H2SO4   ->   ZnSO4  +  H2
          Zn  +  2 HCl       ->   ZnCl2  +  H2

     The gas is collected by DOWNWARD DISPLACEMENT OF WATER.

  METHOD 2 - metal with an alkali (for amphoteric metals)

          Zn   +  2 NaOH             ->  Na2ZnO2  +  H2
          2 Al +  2 NaOH  +  2 H2O   -> 2 NaAlO2  + 3 H2

  WHY GRANULATED ZINC?
     Zinc reacts at a convenient SPEED - not violently like sodium,
     not too slowly like iron - and it is cheap. GRANULES (not dust
     or powder) are used so that the reaction can be controlled.

  WHY DILUTE SULPHURIC ACID, AND NOT NITRIC OR CONCENTRATED ACID?

     formed, giving SO2 or oxides of nitrogen instead of H2.

  FOR VERY PURE HYDROGEN:
          CaH2  +  2 H2O   ->  Ca(OH)2  +  2 H2       ("hydrolith")
     or electrolysis of warm Ba(OH)2 solution with Ni electrodes.
```

Describe the chemical properties of dihydrogen.

### Q B6
Explain ortho and para hydrogen. How does temperature affect their ratio?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A dihydrogen molecule contains TWO protons, and every proton SPINS.
  A spinning charge acts as a tiny magnet, so the two nuclear spins
  can be arranged in only two ways.

  +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
  |      ORTHO HYDROGEN         |      PARA HYDROGEN          |
  +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+
  |    H --------- H            |    H --------- H            |
  |    ^           ^            |    ^           v            |
  |                             |                             |
  |  nuclear spins PARALLEL     |  nuclear spins ANTI-PARALLEL|
  |  HIGHER energy              |  LOWER energy, more stable  |
  +-----------------------------+-----------------------------+

  PROPERTIES

     Their CHEMICAL properties are IDENTICAL.
     Their PHYSICAL properties differ slightly:

         PROPERTY               ORTHO       PARA
         melting point         13.95 K     13.83 K
         boiling point         20.39 K     20.26 K
         thermal conductivity   lower      HIGHER (about 50% more)
         vapour pressure        lower      higher

  EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE

     1. At ROOM TEMPERATURE ordinary hydrogen is 75% ORTHO and
        25% PARA, that is a ratio of 3 : 1.
     2. As the temperature is LOWERED the proportion of PARA
        INCREASES, because para is the lower-energy form.
     3. At ABSOLUTE ZERO (0 K), hydrogen is 100% PARA.
     4. However HIGH the temperature, the ortho form can NEVER
        exceed 75%.

     Pure PARA hydrogen can be obtained by cooling H2 to about 20 K
     with ACTIVATED CHARCOAL as catalyst.
     Pure ORTHO hydrogen CANNOT be obtained.
```

### Q B7
Classify hydrides. Give the characteristics and two examples of each type.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrides are binary compounds of hydrogen with another element.
  They are of THREE types, decided by WHERE the partner element sits.

  1. IONIC (SALINE) HYDRIDES
       Formed by: s-block metals - Group 1 (all) and Group 2
                  (Ca, Sr, Ba). NOT Be and Mg.
       Bonding  : ionic, M+ H- ; hydrogen is in the -1 state

       Conduct  : only when MOLTEN, and give H2 at the ANODE
       React with water violently:
                  NaH + H2O -> NaOH + H2
                  CaH2 + 2 H2O -> Ca(OH)2 + 2 H2
       EXAMPLES : NaH , CaH2

  2. COVALENT (MOLECULAR) HYDRIDES
       Formed by: p-block non-metals (Groups 13 to 17), plus Be, Mg
       Bonding  : covalent, discrete molecules held by weak
                  van der Waals forces
       Nature   : gases or volatile liquids, low m.p. and b.p.
       Conduct  : non-conductors
       Sub-types: ELECTRON DEFICIENT (Group 13, B2H6 - Lewis acids)
                  ELECTRON PRECISE  (Group 14, CH4 - tetrahedral)
                  ELECTRON RICH     (Groups 15-17, NH3, H2O, HF -
                                     they have lone pairs and are
                                     Lewis bases)
       EXAMPLES : CH4 , NH3 (also H2O, HF, B2H6)

                  number ratio; they CONDUCT electricity; they are
                  LESS dense than the parent metal
       Use      : hydrogen STORAGE
       EXAMPLES : TiH1.73 , LaH2.87  (also PdH0.6, ZrH1.30)

  THE HYDRIDE GAP: groups 7, 8 and 9 of the d-block (Mn, Fe, Co and
  their families) form NO hydrides at all.
```

### Q B8
Describe the structure of ice and explain why ice is less dense than water.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STRUCTURE OF ICE

     Ice has a highly ordered, OPEN, three-dimensional HEXAGONAL
     CAGE structure held together entirely by HYDROGEN BONDS.

     Every oxygen atom is surrounded TETRAHEDRALLY by FOUR others:
        - joined to TWO hydrogens by COVALENT bonds (about 101 pm)
        - joined to TWO hydrogens by HYDROGEN bonds (about 175 pm)

     Each water molecule can make FOUR hydrogen bonds, because it
     has TWO hydrogen atoms to donate and TWO lone pairs to accept.

                    O . . . . . . O
                   /                \
                  .                  .
                 /       EMPTY        \
                O        SPACE         O
                 \      (a HOLE)      /
                  .                  .
                   \                /
                    O . . . . . . O

       (dotted line = hydrogen bond)
       Note the LARGE HOLE at the centre of every hexagon.

  WHY ICE IS LESS DENSE

     1. The four hydrogen bonds LOCK every molecule into a rigid
        tetrahedral position.
     2. This forces an OPEN CAGE with large EMPTY SPACES; the
        molecules are held FURTHER APART than they need to be.
     3. The SAME MASS therefore occupies a GREATER VOLUME, so the
        density FALLS:
              ice   at 273 K  =  0.917 g/cm3
              water at 273 K  =  0.9998 g/cm3
     4. On melting, about 15% of the hydrogen bonds break, the cage
        COLLAPSES and molecules fall into the holes, so LIQUID water
        packs more closely and is DENSER.

     Therefore ICE FLOATS ON WATER, about nine-tenths submerged.

  SIGNIFICANCE
     A floating ice layer INSULATES the lake beneath, so the water
     stays liquid at about 277 K and aquatic life survives the winter.
     If ice sank, lakes would freeze solid from the bottom up and
     every living thing in them would die.
```

### Q B10
What is hard water? Explain the causes and the disadvantages of hard water.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
     HARD WATER is water that does not lather easily with soap and
     forms a white curdy SCUM instead.
     SOFT WATER lathers easily (rain water, distilled water).

  CAUSE
     Dissolved salts of CALCIUM and MAGNESIUM.

     TEMPORARY HARDNESS - caused by the BICARBONATES
          Ca(HCO3)2  ,  Mg(HCO3)2       (removed by boiling)

     PERMANENT HARDNESS - caused by the CHLORIDES and SULPHATES
          CaCl2 , MgCl2 , CaSO4 , MgSO4  (NOT removed by boiling)

  WHY SOAP FAILS
     Soap is sodium stearate, C17H35COONa. The Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions
     replace the Na+ and form INSOLUBLE stearates:

       2 C17H35COONa + CaCl2 -> (C17H35COO)2Ca (scum) + 2 NaCl
       2 C17H35COONa + MgSO4 -> (C17H35COO)2Mg (scum) + Na2SO4

     All the soap is wasted making scum before any lather forms.

  DISADVANTAGES

     DOMESTIC
        - wastes a large quantity of soap
        - the sticky scum spoils fabrics and sticks to skin and hair
        - pulses and dal do not cook properly; more fuel is needed
        - the water tastes unpleasant

     IN BOILERS  (the most serious)
        - a hard crust of CaCO3, CaSO4 and Mg(OH)2 called SCALE or
          FUR forms on the inside
        - scale is a BAD CONDUCTOR of heat, so much more fuel is
          needed - WASTE OF FUEL
        - scale blocks pipes and valves
        - uneven heating under a thick scale can crack the boiler
          plate and cause a BOILER EXPLOSION

     IN INDUSTRY
        - useless for dyeing, tanning, paper, sugar and textile work;
          the Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions spoil the dyes and the products
        - laundry output looks grey and dull
```

### Q B11
Explain any THREE methods of removing the permanent hardness of water.

**ANSWER:**
```
  METHOD 1 - WASHING SODA (sodium carbonate) METHOD

     Na2CO3.10H2O is added. It throws the calcium and magnesium out
     as insoluble carbonates, which are filtered off.

          CaCl2  +  Na2CO3   ->   CaCO3 (down)  +  2 NaCl
          CaSO4  +  Na2CO3   ->   CaCO3 (down)  +  Na2SO4
          MgCl2  +  Na2CO3   ->   MgCO3 (down)  +  2 NaCl
          MgSO4  +  Na2CO3   ->   MgCO3 (down)  +  Na2SO4

     Drawback: leaves the water full of sodium salts and a
     precipitate has to be filtered off.

  METHOD 2 - THE CALGON METHOD

     CALGON is SODIUM HEXAMETAPHOSPHATE, Na6P6O18, written for
     reactions as Na2[Na4(PO3)6]. The name means "CALcium GONe".

     In water:  Na2[Na4(PO3)6]  ->  2 Na+  +  [Na4(PO3)6] 2-

     The complex anion traps the Ca2+ and Mg2+:

       2 CaSO4 + Na2[Na4(PO3)6] -> Na2[Ca2(PO3)6] + 2 Na2SO4
       2 MgSO4 + Na2[Na4(PO3)6] -> Na2[Mg2(PO3)6] + 2 Na2SO4

     ADVANTAGE: the Ca2+ is locked inside a SOLUBLE COMPLEX, so
     NO PRECIPITATE forms at all. Nothing to filter and no boiler
     scale. This is the best treatment for boiler water.

  METHOD 3 - THE PERMUTIT / ZEOLITE ION-EXCHANGE METHOD

     PERMUTIT is artificial ZEOLITE, hydrated sodium aluminium
     silicate, Na2Al2Si2O8.xH2O, written for short as Na2Z.

     Hard water is passed down a tank packed with permutit granules,
     which EXCHANGE their sodium for the calcium and magnesium:

          Na2Z  +  Ca2+   ->   CaZ   +   2 Na+
          Na2Z  +  Mg2+   ->   MgZ   +   2 Na+

     REGENERATION: when the bed is exhausted it is washed with
     concentrated BRINE (10% NaCl):

          CaZ  +  2 NaCl   ->   Na2Z   +   CaCl2

     The CaCl2 is washed away and the bed is ready to use again.
     Drawback: the softened water still contains Na+ ions, so it is
     soft but not de-ionised.
```

### Q B12
Explain the ion-exchange (synthetic resin) method of obtaining de-ionised
water.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Two columns of synthetic resin are used, one after the other. This
  method removes ALL the ions, not just Ca2+ and Mg2+, and gives
  water as pure as distilled water.

  COLUMN 1 - CATION EXCHANGE RESIN

     A giant organic resin carrying acidic -SO3H or -COOH groups,
     written as RSO3H. It swaps its H+ for every cation in the water:

        2 RSO3H  +  Ca2+   ->   (RSO3)2Ca   +   2 H+
        2 RSO3H  +  Mg2+   ->   (RSO3)2Mg   +   2 H+
          RSO3H  +  Na+    ->     RSO3Na    +     H+

     The water leaving column 1 is ACIDIC (it contains H+).

  COLUMN 2 - ANION EXCHANGE RESIN

     A resin carrying basic -NH2 or quaternary ammonium groups,
     written as RNH3OH. It swaps its OH- for every anion:

        RNH2  +  H2O    ->   RNH3+  +  OH-
        RNH3+OH-  +  Cl-       ->  RNH3+Cl-     +  OH-
        2 RNH3+OH- + SO4 2-    ->  (RNH3)2SO4   + 2 OH-

  FINAL STEP

     The H+ from column 1 and the OH- from column 2 neutralise
     each other:
                 H+   +   OH-   ->   H2O

     The result is DE-MINERALISED (DE-IONISED) water.

  FLOW DIAGRAM

     hard water
         |
         v
   +-------------+   acidic water   +-------------+
   |   CATION    |   (contains H+)  |   ANION     |
   |  EXCHANGER  | ---------------> |  EXCHANGER  | ---> DE-IONISED
   |   RSO3H     |                  |   RNH3OH    |        WATER
   +-------------+                  +-------------+

  REGENERATION
     cation resin - washed with dilute H2SO4 or HCl (restores H+)
     anion resin  - washed with dilute NaOH        (restores OH-)
```

### Q B13
Explain the structure of hydrogen peroxide in the gas phase and in the solid
phase.

**ANSWER:**
```
  H2O2 is a NON-PLANAR molecule. Its shape is described as an
  OPEN BOOK: the two O-H bonds lie in TWO DIFFERENT PLANES, like the
  two pages of a half-open book, with the O-O bond as the SPINE.

           H
            \
             \                        <- page 1
              O -------------- O
              |     SPINE      |\
              |   (O-O bond)   | \
              |                |  H   <- page 2

  Looking down the O-O bond, the angle between the two "pages" is
  the DIHEDRAL (torsion) angle.

  +---------------------------+---------------+------------------+
  |  PARAMETER                |  GAS PHASE    |  SOLID (110 K)   |
  +---------------------------+---------------+------------------+
  |  O - O bond length        |   147.5 pm    |    145.8 pm      |
  |  O - H bond length        |    95.0 pm    |     98.8 pm      |
  |  O - O - H bond angle     |    94.8 deg   |    101.9 deg     |
  |  DIHEDRAL angle           |   111.5 deg   |     90.2 deg     |
  +---------------------------+---------------+------------------+

  WHY THE VALUES DIFFER IN THE SOLID
     In the solid state the molecules are HYDROGEN BONDED to one
     another. Those hydrogen bonds pull the O-H groups round and
     change both the angles and the bond lengths, squeezing the
     "book" from 111.5 degrees to 90.2 degrees.

  The oxidation state of oxygen in H2O2 is -1, halfway between -2
  in water and 0 in O2. This is exactly why H2O2 can act BOTH as
  an oxidising and as a reducing agent.
```

### Q B14
Explain the action of hydrogen peroxide as an oxidising agent in acidic and
in basic medium, with equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  When H2O2 acts as an OXIDISING agent it is itself REDUCED - the
  oxygen goes from -1 down to -2.

  IN ACIDIC MEDIUM the half reaction is

          H2O2  +  2 H+  +  2 e-   ->   2 H2O

     Examples:

     1.  2 Fe2+  +  2 H+  +  H2O2   ->   2 Fe3+  +  2 H2O
         (green ferrous is oxidised to yellow ferric)

     2.  2 I-  +  2 H+  +  H2O2   ->   I2  +  2 H2O
         (iodide is oxidised to iodine, which turns starch blue)

     3.  PbS  +  4 H2O2   ->   PbSO4  +  4 H2O
         BLACK                 WHITE
         (this is the reaction used to RESTORE OLD OIL PAINTINGS)

     4.  H2S  +  H2O2   ->   S  +  2 H2O

  IN BASIC MEDIUM the half reaction is

          H2O2  +  2 e-   ->   2 OH-

     Examples:

     1.  2 Fe2+  +  H2O2   ->   2 Fe3+  +  2 OH-

     2.  Mn2+  +  H2O2   ->   Mn4+  +  2 OH-
         (manganous is oxidised; MnO2 precipitates)

     3.  CrCl3 + 3 H2O2 + 10 KOH -> 2 K2CrO4 + 3 KCl + 8 H2O
         (green chromic is oxidised to yellow chromate)

  NOTE: in EVERY one of these reactions, NO oxygen gas is released -
  that is how you know H2O2 was the OXIDISING agent.
```

### Q B18
What is the hydrogen economy? Explain the advantages and the problems of
using hydrogen as a fuel.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE HYDROGEN ECONOMY is the idea of TRANSPORTING and STORING energy
  in the form of LIQUID or GASEOUS DIHYDROGEN instead of as
  electricity, so that energy can be moved and used with minimum
  loss and with WATER as the only waste product.

  Note carefully: hydrogen is NOT a source of energy. It is an
  energy CARRIER - you must spend energy to make it.

  ADVANTAGES

     1. HIGHEST ENERGY PER GRAM of any common fuel:
              dihydrogen  about 150 kJ/g
              CNG         about  55 kJ/g
              LPG         about  50 kJ/g
              petrol      about  45 kJ/g
              coal        about  30 kJ/g
        Hydrogen gives about THREE times the energy of petrol.

     2. The only combustion product is WATER:
              2 H2 + O2 -> 2 H2O    dH = -285.9 kJ per mole of H2
        No CO2, CO, SO2, oxides of nitrogen, soot or unburnt
        hydrocarbons - so NO air pollution and NO greenhouse gas.

     3. It can be used in a FUEL CELL, which converts chemical
        energy DIRECTLY into electricity at 60-70% efficiency,
        against 25-30% for a petrol engine.

  PROBLEMS

     1. STORAGE AND TRANSPORT - hydrogen has a very low density, so
        it must be compressed to very high pressure or liquefied
        at 20 K, both difficult and expensive.
     2. SAFETY - hydrogen-air mixtures are explosive from 4% to 75%
        hydrogen, and hydrogen burns with an almost invisible flame.
     3. COST - electrolysis needs a great deal of electricity;
        making H2 from natural gas still produces CO2.
     4. METAL EMBRITTLEMENT - hydrogen diffuses into steel and makes
        it brittle, so special containers are needed.

  PRESENT SOLUTIONS
     Storage as INTERSTITIAL METAL HYDRIDES (LaNi5, FeTi, Mg2Ni),
     which absorb hydrogen and release it on heating; and HCNG,
     which is 5% dihydrogen blended into CNG for buses.
```

```
      +----------------------+-----------+------------------------+

      |                      |           | LEWIS BASES            |

          H2O 373 K but H2S 213 K ; NH3 240 K but PH3 185 K

     The elements of GROUPS 7, 8 and 9 of the d-block -
     Mn, Tc, Re ; Fe, Ru, Os ; Co, Rh, Ir - do NOT form hydrides
```

```
  PART 1 - THE WATER MOLECULE (GAS PHASE)

     The oxygen atom is sp3 hybridised with 2 BOND PAIRS and
     2 LONE PAIRS, so the electron geometry is tetrahedral but the
     SHAPE is BENT (angular, V-shaped).

                lone pair    lone pair
                     \        /
                      ( O )
                      /     \
             95.7 pm /       \ 95.7 pm
                    H         H
                   <-- 104.5 deg -->

        H-O-H bond angle  =  104.5 degrees
        O-H bond length   =  95.7 pm
        dipole moment     =  1.84 D

     The angle is less than the ideal 109.5 degrees because
     lone pair - lone pair repulsion > lone pair - bond pair >
     bond pair - bond pair, so the two lone pairs squeeze the
     O-H bonds closer together.

     Water is POLAR: the O-H dipoles do NOT cancel, because the
     molecule is bent.

  PART 2 - THE STRUCTURE OF ICE

     Each water molecule has TWO hydrogens to donate and TWO lone
     pairs to accept, so it can make FOUR hydrogen bonds.

     In ICE, every oxygen is surrounded TETRAHEDRALLY by FOUR
     others - two by covalent O-H bonds (about 101 pm) and two by
     hydrogen bonds (about 175 pm). The result is a rigid,
     three-dimensional, OPEN HEXAGONAL CAGE.

                    O . . . . . . O
                   /                \
                  .                  .
                 /       EMPTY        \
                O        SPACE         O
                 \      (a HOLE)      /
                  .                  .
                   \                /
                    O . . . . . . O

     WHY ICE IS LESS DENSE THAN WATER
       1. Four hydrogen bonds lock each molecule in place.
       2. This forces an OPEN CAGE with large EMPTY SPACES, so the
          molecules are held further apart than they need to be.
       3. The same mass occupies more volume, so density falls:
              ice 0.917 g/cm3  ;  water 0.9998 g/cm3
       4. On melting, about 15% of the H-bonds break, the cage
          collapses, molecules fall into the holes, and the liquid
          becomes DENSER.
       Therefore ICE FLOATS.

     MAXIMUM DENSITY AT 277 K: below 277 K the collapse of the
     ice-like cage dominates (density rises on warming); above
     277 K ordinary thermal expansion dominates (density falls).
     The two balance at 277 K.

     SIGNIFICANCE: the floating ice layer INSULATES the water below,
     which stays at about 277 K, so fish and plants survive winter.
     If ice sank, lakes would freeze solid and all life in them
     would die.

  PART 3 - ANOMALOUS PHYSICAL PROPERTIES

     +---------------------------+------------+------------------+
     |  PROPERTY                 |  VALUE     |  COMPARE         |
     +---------------------------+------------+------------------+
     |  melting point            |  273.0 K   |  H2S  187 K      |
     |  boiling point            |  373.0 K   |  H2S  213 K      |
     |  density of ice           | 0.917 g/cc |  solid < liquid  |
     |  maximum density at       |  277 K     |  not at 273 K    |
     |  specific heat capacity   | 4.18 J/g/K |  among highest   |
     |  enthalpy of vaporisation | 40.7 kJ/mol|  very high       |
     |  surface tension (293 K)  | 72.75      |  very high       |
     |  dielectric constant      |  78.4      |  very high       |
     +---------------------------+------------+------------------+

     EVERY ONE of these is caused by HYDROGEN BONDING - four
     hydrogen bonds per molecule, all of which must be broken
     before the molecules can separate.

     The very high dielectric constant is why water is the
     UNIVERSAL SOLVENT for ionic compounds: it weakens the
     attraction between the ions about 78-fold and then HYDRATES
     each ion.

  PART 4 - CHEMICAL PROPERTIES

     (a) AMPHOTERIC
            H2O + NH3 <--> NH4+ + OH-      water acts as an ACID
            H2O + H2S <--> H3O+ + HS-      water acts as a BASE

     (b) REDOX
            2 Na + 2 H2O -> 2 NaOH + H2    water is the OXIDANT
            3 Fe + 4 H2O -> Fe3O4 + 4 H2
            2 F2 + 2 H2O -> 4 HF  + O2     water is the REDUCTANT

     (c) HYDROLYSIS
            SO3   + H2O    -> H2SO4        (acid from oxide)
            CaO   + H2O    -> Ca(OH)2      (base from oxide)
            SiCl4 + 2 H2O  -> SiO2 + 4 HCl
            CaC2  + 2 H2O  -> C2H2 + Ca(OH)2
            CCl4 does NOT hydrolyse - carbon has no vacant
            d-orbitals for water to attack.

     (d) HYDRATE FORMATION
            coordinated water     [Cr(H2O)6]3+
            interstitial water    BaCl2.2H2O
            hydrogen-bonded water CuSO4.5H2O = [Cu(H2O)4]SO4.H2O
```
