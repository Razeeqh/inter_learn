# p-Block Group 13 (Boron Family) — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

This chapter has **no numericals at all**. Every pattern is a
*trend + reason*, a *structure + reason*, or a *reaction + condition*.
Once you can name the pattern, you already know the first line of the answer.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — CONFIGURATION, MEMBERS AND CLASSIFICATION           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the general electronic configuration of group 13",
            "Name the members of the boron family", "Which member is
            a non-metal?"
  METHOD    1. General configuration = ns2 np1 .
            2. List: B , Al , Ga , In , Tl .
            3. Classify:
                  B  = the ONLY NON-METAL
                  Al , Ga , In , Tl = METALS
            4. If room, give the individual configurations, e.g.
                  B  [He] 2s2 2p1 ,  Tl [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p1 .
  TRAP      Do NOT write ns2 np2 (that is group 14). And group 13 has
            NO metalloids in this syllabus - do not copy the Si/Ge
            answer from chapter 11.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — OCCURRENCE, ORES AND ABUNDANCE                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which is the most abundant metal in the crust?",
            "Name two ores of boron / of aluminium."
  METHOD    1. ALUMINIUM is 8.3 % of the crust - the MOST ABUNDANT
               METAL and the THIRD most abundant ELEMENT (after
               oxygen 46 % and silicon 27.7 %).
            2. Ores of Al : BAUXITE Al2O3.2H2O (main) , cryolite
               Na3AlF6 , corundum Al2O3 , feldspar , mica , clay.
            3. Boron is only 0.0001 % and NEVER occurs free.
               Ores: BORAX Na2B4O7.10H2O , COLEMANITE Ca2B6O11.5H2O ,
               kernite Na2B4O7.4H2O , orthoboric acid H3BO3 .
            4. Isotopes of boron: 10B (19 %) , 11B (81 %).
  TRAP      "MOST ABUNDANT METAL" but "THIRD MOST ABUNDANT ELEMENT".
            Read which one is asked - this exact trick is used every
            year in EAPCET.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — TREND + REASON DOWN THE GROUP                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Discuss the variation of ... down group 13",
            "How do atomic radii / ionisation enthalpy /
            electronegativity / melting point vary?"
  METHOD    Answer in THREE columns every single time:
                 PROPERTY  |  DIRECTION  |  REASON
            Standard reasons to reuse:
                 size UP        -> a new shell each period
                 IE DOWN        -> outer electron farther and shielded
                 EN DOWN then UP-> B is tiny ; then poor d/f shielding
                 m.p. DOWN then UP -> giant covalent B, then metals ;
                                   Ga is anomalous
                 density UP     -> mass rises faster than volume
                 metallic UP    -> IE falls, electrons lost easily
            ALWAYS add the exceptions (see Pattern 4).
  TRAP      NEVER write only the direction - half the marks are for
            the REASON. And in THIS group most trends are IRREGULAR;
            use the word "irregular" and then explain why.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — THE FOUR EXCEPTIONS IN THE TRENDS                   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the atomic radius of Ga less than Al?",
            "Why is the IE of Ga higher than Al / of Tl higher than
            In?", "Why does gallium have such a low melting point?",
            "Why does electronegativity increase from Al to Tl?"
  METHOD    1. SIZE : Al 143 , Ga 135 pm. REASON = the ten 3d
                 electrons of Ga SHIELD POORLY, so the outer
                 electrons feel a bigger effective nuclear charge and
                 are pulled in (D-BLOCK CONTRACTION).
            2. IE : B 801 > Tl 589 > Ga 579 > Al 577 > In 558.
                 REASON = POOR SHIELDING by 3d10 (Ga) and by 4f14 +
                 5d10 (Tl, the LANTHANOID CONTRACTION).
            3. m.p. OF GALLIUM : 303 K. REASON = solid gallium exists
                 as Ga2 DIATOMIC MOLECULES held by WEAK forces.
                 b.p. is 2676 K because boiling must break the strong
                 Ga-Ga bond -> widest liquid range -> high-temperature
                 thermometers.
            4. EN : 2.0 -> 1.5 -> 1.6 -> 1.7 -> 1.8 . Falls then
                 RISES, again because of poor d and f shielding.
  TRAP      Do NOT use "3d shielding" to explain gallium's MELTING
            POINT. Shielding explains SIZE and IE. The melting point
            is explained by Ga2 MOLECULES. Two different answers.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WHY BORON NEVER FORMS B3+                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does boron form only covalent compounds?",
            "Why does the B3+ ion not exist?"
  METHOD    1. Quote the numbers:
                 IE1 + IE2 + IE3 = 801 + 2427 + 3659 = 6887 kJ/mol .
            2. Say that no lattice energy or hydration energy can
               repay that much.
            3. Conclude: boron cannot lose three electrons, so it
               SHARES them instead - ALL boron compounds are COVALENT.
            4. Add: boron is also very small and very electronegative
               (2.0), which favours covalency.
  TRAP      "It has no d orbitals" is NOT the reason here. That is the
            reason for its MAXIMUM COVALENCY OF FOUR. Keep them apart.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — MAXIMUM COVALENCY OF FOUR                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the maximum covalency of boron four?",
            "Why does [BF4]- exist but not [BF6]3- ?",
            "Why can aluminium form [AlF6]3- ?"
  METHOD    1. Boron's valence shell is the SECOND shell: one 2s and
               three 2p orbitals = FOUR orbitals, and NO d ORBITALS.
            2. Four orbitals -> at most FOUR bonds -> [BF4]- is the
               limit.
            3. Aluminium's valence shell is the THIRD shell, which has
               VACANT 3d ORBITALS, so it can expand its octet to a
               covalency of SIX: [AlF6]3- , [Al(H2O)6]3+ .
  TRAP      Do not say "boron is too small". The reason is the NUMBER
            OF ORBITALS AVAILABLE and the ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — INERT PAIR EFFECT                              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the inert pair effect?", "Why does the +1 state
            become more stable down the group?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: the reluctance of the ns2 electron pair to
               take part in bonding, increasing down a p-block group.
            2. CAUSE in four steps:
                  (a) filled d (3d10,4d10,5d10) and f (4f14) subshells
                      come in between
                  (b) d and f orbitals SHIELD POORLY
                  (c) so the ns2 pair feels a large effective nuclear
                      charge and is held tightly
                  (d) promoting an ns electron to np costs more than
                      the two extra long weak bonds give back
            3. RESULT: +1 stability RISES , +3 stability FALLS:
                  +1 :  Al  <  Ga  <  In  <  Tl
                  +3 :  Al  >  Ga  >  In  >  Tl
            4. Give one example: Tl(I) is the stable state of thallium.
  TRAP      The cause is POOR SHIELDING BY d AND f ELECTRONS. Do not
            write "the atom is big" and stop.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — OXIDISING CHARACTER OF M(III) AT THE BOTTOM     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is Tl(I) more stable than Tl(III)?",
            "Why are Tl(III) compounds strong oxidising agents?"
  METHOD    1. State the inert pair effect in one line.
            2. Identify the oxidation state in the compound.
            3. Apply the rule: an UNSTABLE HIGH state at the bottom of
               the group GAINS electrons to drop to the stable LOW
               state -> it is an OXIDISING AGENT.
            4. Write the equation:
                  TlCl3  --(heat)-->  TlCl  +  Cl2
                  Tl2O3  --(heat)-->  Tl2O  +  O2
            5. Add the order:  Al3+ < Ga3+ < In3+ << Tl3+ for
               oxidising power.
  TRAP      Students reverse it. BOTTOM of the group + HIGH state =
            OXIDISER. (Compare PbO2 in group 14 - same logic.)
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — WHY TlI3 IS NOT THALLIUM(III) IODIDE                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does TlI3 not exist as Tl(III) iodide?",
            "What is the actual structure of TlI3 ?"
  METHOD    1. Tl3+ is a strong OXIDISING agent (unstable +3 state).
            2. I- is a good REDUCING agent.
            3. Tl3+ would instantly oxidise I- to I2 and be reduced
               to Tl+.
            4. So the real compound is THALLIUM(I) TRI-IODIDE,
                  Tl+ (I3)- .
            5. Compare: PbI4 does not exist at all, for the same
               reason.
  TRAP      Do not say "TlI3 does not exist". It DOES exist - but as
            Tl+(I3)-, not as Tl(III)I3. Say it properly.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — DISPROPORTIONATION OF Ga(I) AND In(I)              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens to Ga+ in solution?", "Explain
            3 Ga+ -> 2 Ga + Ga3+ ."
  METHOD    1. Define DISPROPORTIONATION: the same species is
               simultaneously OXIDISED and REDUCED.
            2. Write the equation:  3 Ga+  -->  2 Ga  +  Ga3+ .
            3. Reason: in the MIDDLE of the group neither +1 nor +3 is
               comfortable, so the +1 state splits into the element
               and the +3 state.
            4. The same happens for In(I), but not for Tl(I), which is
               genuinely stable.
  TRAP      Do not confuse disproportionation with decomposition. Give
            both the oxidation and the reduction half in words.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF BORON                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the anomalous behaviour of boron", "How does
            boron differ from the other members of its group?"
  METHOD    1. Start with the CAUSE - FIVE points:
                  small size (85 pm) , high IE (801 ; sum 6887) ,
                  high EN (2.0) , only FOUR orbitals , NO d orbitals.
            2. Then list the DIFFERENCES, one line each:
                  (i)   B is a NON-METAL, the rest are metals
                  (ii)  B forms ONLY COVALENT compounds, no B3+
                  (iii) max covalency 4 vs 6 for Al
                  (iv)  BX3 monomeric planar vs Al2Cl6 dimer
                  (v)   B(OH)3 and B2O3 ACIDIC vs Al amphoteric,
                        Tl basic
                  (vi)  B forms stable electron-deficient BORANES
                  (vii) boron halides COMPLETELY hydrolysed
                  (viii) B shows only +3, the rest show +3 and +1
            3. Aim for five or six differences with a reason each.
  TRAP      Do not just list differences with no cause. The five
            causes carry half the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP BORON AND SILICON            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a diagonal relationship?", "Give the points of
            similarity between boron and silicon."
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: a second-period element resembles the
               element DIAGONALLY BELOW AND TO THE RIGHT of it.
               The pairs are Li-Mg , Be-Al , B-Si .
            2. REASON: across a period size falls and EN rises; down a
               group size rises and EN falls; along a diagonal the two
               effects CANCEL, so size, EN and charge/radius ratio
               match.  B 85 pm EN 2.0 ; Si 118 pm EN 1.8 .
            3. SIMILARITIES - give four, WITH EQUATIONS:
                  (i)   both non-metallic semiconductors, only
                        COVALENT compounds
                  (ii)  volatile flammable hydrides, hydrolysed:
                        B2H6 + 6H2O -> 2H3BO3 + 6H2
                        SiH4 + 4H2O -> Si(OH)4 + 4H2
                  (iii) halides completely hydrolysed:
                        BCl3 + 3H2O -> H3BO3 + 3HCl
                        SiCl4 + 4H2O -> Si(OH)4 + 4HCl
                  (iv)  oxides ACIDIC, dissolve in alkali:
                        B2O3 + 6NaOH -> 2Na3BO3 + 3H2O
                        SiO2 + 2NaOH -> Na2SiO3 + H2O
                  (v)   polymeric BORATES and SILICATES
                  (vi)  hard borides/silicides ; B4C and SiC
  TRAP      The partner is SILICON, not carbon. Carbon is the
            HORIZONTAL neighbour. Say "one step across, one step down".
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — WHY BX3 IS ELECTRON DEFICIENT / A LEWIS ACID       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is BF3 electron deficient?", "Why is BCl3 a Lewis
            acid?", "What happens when BF3 reacts with NH3 ?"
  METHOD    1. COUNT the electrons around boron:
                  3 (its own) + 3 (one from each halogen) = 6 .
               A SEXTET, not an octet.
            2. Say it has ONE COMPLETELY EMPTY 2p ORBITAL.
            3. Therefore it ACCEPTS A LONE PAIR = LEWIS ACID.
            4. Give an equation:
                  BF3 + NH3 -> F3B <- NH3   (a COORDINATE bond;
                                             boron becomes sp3)
                  BF3 + F-  -> [BF4]-
            5. Mention the shape: sp2, TRIGONAL PLANAR, 120 degrees,
               non-polar.
  TRAP      Do not say "boron needs two more electrons to become a
            noble gas". Say SEXTET and EMPTY 2p ORBITAL - those are
            the marking words.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — THE LEWIS ACID ORDER AND BACK BONDING         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange BF3, BCl3, BBr3, BI3 in order of Lewis acid
            strength", "Why is BF3 the weakest Lewis acid?"
  METHOD    1. WRITE THE ORDER FIRST:
                  BF3  <  BCl3  <  BBr3  <  BI3
            2. Name the effect: p(pi) - p(pi) BACK BONDING (back
               donation).
            3. Explain it: a FILLED p orbital of the halogen donates
               its lone pair SIDEWAYS into boron's EMPTY 2p orbital.
               This partly fills the orbital, gives the B-X bond some
               DOUBLE BOND character and REDUCES the deficiency.
            4. Compare the overlap: effective pi overlap needs
               orbitals of SIMILAR SIZE.
                  F 2p - same size as B's 2p - BEST overlap -
                         strongest back bonding - WEAKEST acid
                  Cl 3p , Br 4p , I 5p - worse and worse
                  I 5p - almost NO back bonding - STRONGEST acid
            5. Evidence: B-F in BF3 is only 130 pm, SHORTER than a
               normal single bond.
  TRAP      *** The intuitive answer "F is most electronegative so BF3
            is the strongest acid" is WRONG. The examiner is testing
            exactly this. The magic words are BACK BONDING. ***
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — HYDROLYSIS OF THE HALIDES                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when BCl3 is added to water?",
            "Why does an aqueous solution of AlCl3 turn litmus red?"
  METHOD    1. BCl3 is COMPLETELY hydrolysed:
                  BCl3  +  3 H2O  -->  H3BO3  +  3 HCl
               because boron accepts a lone pair from water into its
               empty 2p orbital.
            2. BF3 is only PARTIALLY hydrolysed, because back bonding
               makes the B-F bond unusually strong:
                  4 BF3 + 3 H2O -> 3 H3O+ + 3 [BF4]- + B(OH)3
            3. AlCl3 hydrolyses giving an ACIDIC solution:
                  AlCl3  +  3 H2O  -->  Al(OH)3  +  3 HCl
               which is why anhydrous AlCl3 cannot be made by
               evaporating the aqueous solution.
  TRAP      Compare with CCl4, which is NOT hydrolysed at all. Boron
            has an EMPTY orbital to accept the water lone pair;
            carbon has neither an empty orbital nor d orbitals.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — REACTIVITY TOWARDS AIR AND THE NATURE OF THE OXIDES
                                                             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when boron / aluminium is heated in air?",
            "Discuss the nature of the oxides of group 13."
  METHOD    1. With DIOXYGEN:
                  2 B  + 3/2 O2 --(heat)--> B2O3
                  4 Al + 3 O2   --(heat)--> 2 Al2O3
                  general :  2 E + 3/2 O2 -> E2O3
            2. With DINITROGEN (high temperature):
                  2 B  + N2 --> 2 BN     (boron nitride)
                  2 Al + N2 --> 2 AlN
            3. NATURE OF THE OXIDES - the trend and the reason:
                  B2O3 ACIDIC -> Al2O3 , Ga2O3 AMPHOTERIC ->
                  In2O3 , Tl2O3 BASIC ; Tl2O strongly basic.
                  REASON: metallic character INCREASES down the group.
            4. Same trend for the hydroxides: B(OH)3 acidic ,
               Al(OH)3 amphoteric , TlOH as strong as NaOH.
  TRAP      Crystalline boron is nearly INERT at room temperature -
            say "on heating". And remember that BN is called
            INORGANIC GRAPHITE.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — AMPHOTERIC NATURE OF ALUMINIUM                     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Show that aluminium / Al2O3 / Al(OH)3 is amphoteric",
            "What happens when aluminium is treated with NaOH?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINE: amphoteric = reacts with BOTH acids and alkalis.
            2. THE METAL:
                  2 Al + 6 HCl --> 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2
                  2 Al + 2 NaOH + 6 H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 3 H2
            3. THE OXIDE:
                  Al2O3 + 6 HCl --> 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2O
                  Al2O3 + 2 NaOH + 3 H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4]
            4. THE HYDROXIDE:
                  Al(OH)3 + 3 HCl --> AlCl3 + 3 H2O
                  Al(OH)3 + NaOH  --> Na[Al(OH)4]
            5. Conclude: salt with acid, ALUMINATE with alkali ->
               AMPHOTERIC.
  TRAP      The gas evolved with alkali is HYDROGEN, not oxygen. And
            the product is an ALUMINATE Na[Al(OH)4] (older books:
            NaAlO2 + 2H2O), not Al(OH)3.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — PASSIVITY OF ALUMINIUM                             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why can concentrated HNO3 be transported in aluminium
            containers?", "Why does an aluminium vessel not corrode?"
  METHOD    1. Aluminium is reactive, but it instantly forms a THIN,
               HARD, CONTINUOUS, IMPERVIOUS layer of Al2O3.
            2. That layer clings to the surface and seals the metal
               off from further attack. The metal is PASSIVE.
            3. Concentrated HNO3 is a strong OXIDISER, so it builds an
               unusually thick layer and the reaction stops dead.
            4. Deliberately thickening the layer by electrolysis is
               called ANODISING.
  TRAP      Do not say "aluminium is unreactive". It is very reactive
            - it is the OXIDE LAYER that protects it.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — STRUCTURE OF BORAX                            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the structure of borax", "Write the real formula of
            borax", "How many sp3 borons are in the borax anion?"
  METHOD    1. Formulae:
                  simple  Na2B4O7 . 10 H2O
                  real    Na2 [B4O5(OH)4] . 8 H2O
               Four of the ten "waters" are -OH groups on boron.
            2. DRAW the anion and label it:
                  TWO sp2 borons - TRIGONAL PLANAR - BO3 units
                  TWO sp3 borons - TETRAHEDRAL - BO4 units,
                       carrying the 2- charge
                  units joined by SHARED (bridging) oxygens
                  each boron carries ONE -OH
                  a CENTRAL bridging oxygen joins the two sp3 borons
            3. Two Na+ balance the charge; 8 H2O are water of
               crystallisation.
  TRAP      Do not write that all four borons are the same. "TWO
            TRIANGLES AND TWO TETRAHEDRA" is the marking phrase.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — WHY BORAX SOLUTION IS ALKALINE                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is an aqueous solution of borax alkaline?",
            "Why is borax used as a buffer / cleaning agent?"
  METHOD    1. Borax is the salt of a STRONG base (NaOH) and a WEAK
               acid (H3BO3).
            2. It therefore HYDROLYSES:
                  Na2B4O7  +  7 H2O   -->   2 NaOH  +  4 H3BO3
            3. The strong base is fully ionised and the weak acid
               hardly at all, so OH- is in excess -> ALKALINE.
            4. It turns red litmus blue and acts as a buffer.
  TRAP      Write the hydrolysis EQUATION. "Because it is basic" with
            no equation earns almost nothing.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — ACTION OF HEAT ON BORAX AND THE BEAD          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when borax is heated?", "What is the
            composition of the borax bead?"
  METHOD    Three stages, with equations:
            1. About 373 K - loses water and SWELLS into a light,
               white, PUFFY, POROUS mass:
                  Na2B4O7 . 10 H2O --(heat)--> Na2B4O7 + 10 H2O
            2. Further heating - the puffy mass MELTS.
            3. Red heat (~1000 K) - decomposes; the melt cools to a
               TRANSPARENT GLASSY BEAD:
                  Na2B4O7 --(red heat)--> 2 NaBO2 + B2O3
                                          sodium     boric
                                          metaborate anhydride
            4. State clearly: THE BEAD = NaBO2 + B2O3 .
  TRAP      The bead is NOT "Na2B4O7". Name BOTH components. And
            remember the word "SWELLS / puffy mass" for stage 1 -
            it is often worth a mark on its own.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — THE BORAX BEAD TEST AND ITS COLOURS                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the borax bead test", "Give the colour of the
            borax bead with a copper / cobalt / chromium salt."
  METHOD    1. THE METHOD in five steps: platinum wire loop -> heat
               and dip in borax -> form the transparent bead ->
               touch the metal salt -> heat in the OXIDISING flame
               and then the REDUCING flame.
            2. WHY PLATINUM: it is INERT, adds no colour of its own,
               and has a very high melting point.
            3. THE CHEMISTRY:
                  B2O3 + CuO --(heat)--> Cu(BO2)2
                  B2O3 + CoO --(heat)--> Co(BO2)2
                  CuSO4 + B2O3 --(heat)--> Cu(BO2)2 + SO3
            4. THE PRINCIPLE: boric anhydride reacts with the metal
               oxide to give a coloured METAL METABORATE M(BO2)n.
            5. THE COLOUR TABLE:
                  Cu  blue-green (ox)  / red opaque (red)
                  Co  deep blue        / deep blue
                  Cr  green            / green
                  Fe  yellowish-brown  / bottle green
                  Mn  violet           / colourless
                  Ni  brown            / grey
            6. LIMITATION: only COLOURED (transition) metal ions.
  TRAP      The coloured product is a METABORATE M(BO2)n, NOT a
            borate M3(BO3)n and NOT the oxide. Get the formula right.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — PREPARATION OF BORAX AND OF BORIC ACID             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is borax prepared from colemanite?", "How is boric
            acid prepared from borax?"
  METHOD    BORAX:
                Ca2B6O11 + 2 Na2CO3 --> 2 CaCO3 + Na2B4O7 + 2 NaBO2
                4 NaBO2 + CO2 --> Na2B4O7 + Na2CO3
                4 H3BO3 + Na2CO3 --> Na2B4O7 + CO2 + 6 H2O
            BORIC ACID:
                Na2B4O7 + 2 HCl + 5 H2O --> 2 NaCl + 4 H3BO3
                Na2B4O7 + H2SO4 + 5 H2O --> Na2SO4 + 4 H3BO3
                Ca2B6O11 + 4 SO2 + 11 H2O --> 2 Ca(HSO3)2 + 6 H3BO3
                BCl3 + 3 H2O --> H3BO3 + 3 HCl
            Add the practical detail: boric acid is much less soluble
            in COLD water, so it crystallises out on cooling.
  TRAP      Balance the water. "Na2B4O7 + 2HCl -> 2NaCl + 4H3BO3" is
            NOT balanced - you need + 5 H2O on the left.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — STRUCTURE OF ORTHOBORIC ACID / WHY IT IS SOAPY 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the structure of H3BO3", "Why does boric acid feel
            soapy?", "Why is boric acid used as a lubricant?"
  METHOD    1. Each boron is sp2, TRIGONAL PLANAR, bonded to three
               -OH groups at 120 degrees.
            2. DRAW the sheet: BO3 units joined into flat
               TWO-DIMENSIONAL LAYERS by HYDROGEN BONDS between the
               -OH groups. Show the H bonds as dotted lines.
            3. The LAYERS are held to one another only by WEAK VAN
               DER WAALS FORCES.
            4. So the layers SLIDE over one another -> SOAPY,
               SLIPPERY -> used as a LUBRICANT.
            5. Compare with graphite - identical argument.
  TRAP      The HYDROGEN BONDS are WITHIN a layer; the VAN DER WAALS
            forces are BETWEEN layers. Getting these the wrong way
            round loses the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — WHY H3BO3 IS A WEAK MONOBASIC ACID            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Boric acid has three hydrogens but is monobasic - why?",
            "Is H3BO3 a Bronsted acid or a Lewis acid?"
  METHOD    1. Say what it does NOT do: it does NOT donate any of its
               own protons.
            2. Say what it DOES: boron has only SIX electrons and one
               EMPTY 2p orbital, so B(OH)3 acts as a LEWIS ACID and
               ACCEPTS a hydroxide ion from a water molecule.
            3. Write the equation:
                  B(OH)3  +  2 H2O   -->   [B(OH)4]-  +  H3O+
            4. Count: ONE H3O+ per molecule -> MONOBASIC.
               Only a tiny fraction reacts -> VERY WEAK
               (Ka about 5.8 x 10^-10).
            5. Extra mark: adding GLYCEROL or MANNITOL (a cis-diol)
               makes it strong enough to titrate against NaOH.
  TRAP      Do NOT write "only one of the three hydrogens is
            ionisable". NONE of them ionise. The phrase the examiner
            wants is "ACCEPTS OH- FROM WATER".
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — ACTION OF HEAT ON BORIC ACID                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when orthoboric acid is heated?"
  METHOD    Write the chain with the temperatures ON the arrows:
                H3BO3   --(370 K)-->    HBO2    + H2O   METABORIC acid
                4 HBO2  --(430 K)-->    H2B4O7  + H2O   TETRABORIC acid
                H2B4O7  --(red heat)--> 2 B2O3  + H2O   BORIC ANHYDRIDE
            Then summarise in one line:
                H3BO3 -> HBO2 -> H2B4O7 -> B2O3
  TRAP      Name each product. "H3BO3 gives B2O3 on heating" skips two
            stages and loses marks. And note the coefficient 4 in
            front of HBO2.
```

  PATTERN 27 — PREPARATION AND REACTIONS OF DIBORANE         4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is diborane prepared?", "Give the reactions of
            diborane with water / oxygen / ammonia."
  METHOD    PREPARATION:
                2 NaBH4 + I2 --> B2H6 + 2 NaI + H2        (laboratory)
                4 BF3 + 3 LiAlH4 --> 2 B2H6 + 3 LiAlF4
                2 BF3 + 6 NaH --(450 K)--> B2H6 + 6 NaF   (industrial)

                B2H6 + 6 H2O --> 2 H3BO3 + 6 H2           (hydrolysis)

                                                 catches fire in air)
                B2H6 + 2 NH3 --> [BH2(NH3)2]+[BH4]-
                3 [BH2(NH3)2]+[BH4]- --(473K)--> 2 B3N3H6 + 12 H2
                                                 BORAZINE
                B2H6 + 2 NMe3 --> 2 BH3.NMe3      (bridge SPLIT)
                B2H6 + 2 LiH  --> 2 LiBH4
                B2H6 + 6 CH3OH --> 2 B(OCH3)3 + 6 H2
  TRAP      With ammonia there are TWO products depending on the

```
  PATTERN 28 — STRUCTURE AND BONDING OF DIBORANE             4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the structure of diborane", "What are banana bonds?",
            "Why is B2H6 called electron deficient?"
  METHOD    1. THE ELECTRON COUNT:
                  available 2x3 + 6x1 = 12
                  needed    7 bonds x 2 = 14
                  12 < 14 -> TWO SHORT -> ELECTRON DEFICIENT
            2. DRAW it: 4 TERMINAL H (two on each B) and 2 BRIDGING H
               (one above, one below).
            3. Each boron is sp3 HYBRIDISED.
            4. The 4 B-Ht bonds are normal 2-CENTRE 2-ELECTRON bonds,
               119 pm.
            5. The 2 bridges are 3-CENTRE 2-ELECTRON (BANANA) bonds -
               ONE pair holding THREE atoms - 133 pm, longer and
               weaker.
            6. GEOMETRY: 2 B + 4 terminal H in ONE PLANE; the 2
               bridging H PERPENDICULAR to it.
            7. ANGLES: Ht-B-Ht = 121.5 deg , Hb-B-Hb = 97 deg .
  TRAP      *** THERE IS NO B-B BOND. *** Never draw a line straight
            between the two borons. This single mistake costs the
            structure mark every year.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — DIBORANE versus Al2Cl6 (the comparison)            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare the bonding in B2H6 and Al2Cl6", "Why is the
            bridge in Al2Cl6 different from that in B2H6 ?"
  METHOD    Answer in a TABLE:
                 bridging atom : H (2)          vs  Cl (2)
                 bridge bond   : 3c-2e BANANA   vs  ordinary
                                                    COORDINATE bond
                 why           : H has NO lone  vs  Cl HAS lone pairs
                                 pair to donate     and donates one
                 hybridisation : sp3 on B       vs  sp3 on Al
                 octet after   : STILL deficient vs COMPLETE
                 bond lengths  : 119 / 133 pm   vs  202 / 221 pm
            Then one summary sentence: both dimerise because the
            central atom has a SEXTET, but hydrogen cannot donate a
            lone pair, so diborane must use a three-centre bond.
  TRAP      Do not say Al2Cl6 has banana bonds. It does NOT - the
            chlorine bridge is a perfectly ordinary coordinate bond.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — WHY AlCl3 DIMERISES / STRUCTURE OF Al2Cl6     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does aluminium chloride exist as a dimer?",
            "Draw the structure of Al2Cl6."
  METHOD    1. In AlCl3 the aluminium has only SIX electrons - a
               SEXTET - so it is ELECTRON DEFICIENT and a Lewis acid.
            2. To complete its OCTET it accepts a LONE PAIR from a
               chlorine of a SECOND AlCl3 through a COORDINATE bond.
            3. Two chlorine BRIDGES form:  2 AlCl3 == Al2Cl6 .
            4. DRAW it: 4 terminal Cl (202 pm) + 2 bridging Cl
               (221 pm); each Al sp3 and TETRAHEDRAL; the two
               tetrahedra share an EDGE.
            5. The dimer exists in the VAPOUR and in NON-POLAR
               SOLVENTS; above about 1073 K it splits into planar
               monomers.
            6. Add: AlF3 is IONIC and does NOT dimerise.
  TRAP      Do not write "all aluminium halides are dimers". AlF3 is
            the exception.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — PREPARATION AND USE OF ANHYDROUS AlCl3        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is anhydrous AlCl3 prepared?", "Why can it not be
            made from the aqueous solution?", "Name its main use."
  METHOD    1. DRY methods only:
                  2 Al + 3 Cl2 --(heat)--> Al2Cl6
                  Al2O3 + 3 C + 3 Cl2 --(heat)--> 2 AlCl3 + 3 CO
                  2 Al + 6 HCl (dry gas) --(heat)--> 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2
            2. WHY NOT FROM SOLUTION: it hydrolyses,
                  AlCl3 + 3 H2O --> Al(OH)3 + 3 HCl
               so evaporating gives Al(OH)3 and HCl, not AlCl3.
            3. USE: anhydrous AlCl3 is a powerful LEWIS ACID and is
               THE FRIEDEL-CRAFTS CATALYST:
                  C6H6 + CH3Cl --(anhyd. AlCl3)--> C6H5CH3 + HCl
                  C6H6 + CH3COCl --(anhyd. AlCl3)--> C6H5COCH3 + HCl
  TRAP      Write the word ANHYDROUS and the word DRY. The whole point
            of the question is that water ruins it.
```

```
  PATTERN 32 — ALUMS                                         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are alums?", "Give the general formula of an alum",
            "Give three uses of potash alum."
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: a DOUBLE SALT of the sulphate of a
               MONOVALENT cation and the sulphate of a TRIVALENT
               cation with 24 water molecules.
            2. GENERAL FORMULA:
                  M2SO4 . M'2(SO4)3 . 24 H2O
               or M+ M'3+ (SO4)2 . 12 H2O
                  M  = Na+ , K+ , NH4+
                  M' = Al3+ , Cr3+ , Fe3+
            3. EXAMPLES: potash alum K2SO4.Al2(SO4)3.24H2O ,
               chrome alum , ferric alum , ammonium alum.
            4. DOUBLE not COMPLEX: in water it gives K+ , Al3+ and
               2 SO4 2- .
            5. USES: water purification (Al(OH)3 COAGULATES colloidal
               dirt) , MORDANT in dyeing , TANNING , STYPTIC to stop
               bleeding , sizing paper.
  TRAP      It is 24 H2O in the double formula, or 12 H2O in the
            halved formula. Do not mix the two up.
```

```
  PATTERN 33 — USES OF BORON AND ALUMINIUM                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the uses of boron", "Why is boron used in nuclear
            reactors?", "What is aluminothermy?"
  METHOD    BORON:
              1. 10B ABSORBS NEUTRONS -> CONTROL RODS in nuclear
                 reactors (boron steel, boron carbide).
              2. Boron FIBRES -> bullet-proof vests, light aircraft
                 composites.
              3. B4C and cubic BN (BORAZON) -> ABRASIVES ; BN is a
                 high-temperature lubricant.
              4. Borax and boric acid -> BOROSILICATE (Pyrex) glass,
                 enamels, antiseptics.
              5. A hardening agent in steel; a plant micronutrient.
            ALUMINIUM:
              1. Overhead POWER LINES - light and cheap, and weight
                 for weight about twice as conducting as copper.
              2. Foil, utensils, packaging.
              3. DURALUMIN (Al+Cu+Mg+Mn) for AIRCRAFT.
              4. THERMITE WELDING / ALUMINOTHERMY:
                    2 Al + Fe2O3 --> Al2O3 + 2 Fe + heat
                 also used to extract Cr and Mn.
              5. Silvery paints; anodised frames; Al2O3 as an
                 abrasive; anhydrous AlCl3 as a catalyst.
  TRAP      For "why aluminium and not copper", the reason is the
            LOW DENSITY, not a higher conductivity. Copper conducts
            better per unit VOLUME; aluminium wins per unit MASS.
```

```
  PATTERN 34 — THE p-BLOCK GENERAL INTRODUCTION              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the p-block?", "Write the general electronic
            configuration of the p-block elements", "Name the groups
            of the p-block."
  METHOD    1. The p-block is the SIX COLUMNS on the right, groups 13
               to 18. The last electron enters a p orbital.
            2. GENERAL CONFIGURATION = ns2 np(1-6) .
               Helium (1s2) is the exception - it is placed in group
               18 because of its PROPERTIES.
            3. The groups and their families:
                  13 ns2np1 boron    14 ns2np2 carbon
                  15 ns2np3 nitrogen 16 ns2np4 oxygen
                  17 ns2np5 halogen  18 ns2np6 noble gases
            4. THREE GENERAL TRENDS:
                  (a) it is the ONLY block with metals, metalloids
                      AND non-metals; metallic character rises DOWN
                      and falls ACROSS
                  (b) the FIRST member of each group is ANOMALOUS
                      (small, high EN, high IE, only four orbitals,
                      no d orbitals)
                  (c) the INERT PAIR EFFECT makes the LOWER state
                      (group number minus 2) more stable down a group
  TRAP      Do not forget helium's exception, and do not say the
            p-block "starts at group 12".
```

```
  PATTERN 35 — BORON NITRIDE                                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is boron nitride?", "Why is BN called inorganic
            graphite?"
  METHOD    1. Preparation:  2 B + N2 --(high temp)--> 2 BN .
            2. It has the SAME LAYER STRUCTURE as graphite - flat
               sheets of hexagonal rings with B and N ALTERNATING.
               A B-N unit is isoelectronic with a C-C unit.
            3. Like graphite it is soft, white and slippery -> a
               HIGH-TEMPERATURE LUBRICANT.
            4. Its CUBIC form, BORAZON, is one of the hardest
               substances known -> an abrasive.
            5. Hydrolysed by water:  BN + 3 H2O --> H3BO3 + NH3 .
  TRAP      Do not confuse BORON NITRIDE (BN, inorganic graphite)
            with BORAZINE (B3N3H6, inorganic benzene). Different
            compounds, different nicknames.
```
