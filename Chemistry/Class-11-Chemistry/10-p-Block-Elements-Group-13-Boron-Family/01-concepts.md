# TOPIC 0 — WHAT IS THE p-BLOCK? (the 2-minute introduction)

The periodic table is cut into blocks according to **which orbital the last
electron enters**.

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |   s-BLOCK   last electron enters an  s  orbital   Groups 1, 2      |
  |   p-BLOCK   last electron enters a   p  orbital   Groups 13 to 18  |
  |   d-BLOCK   last electron enters a   d  orbital   Groups 3 to 12   |
  |   f-BLOCK   last electron enters an  f  orbital   Ln and An        |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**The p-block is the six columns on the RIGHT of the periodic table.**

```
    GROUP    13        14        15        16        17        18
    CONFIG   ns2np1    ns2np2    ns2np3    ns2np4    ns2np5    ns2np6
    NAME     Boron     Carbon    Nitrogen  Oxygen    Halogen   Noble
             family    family    family    family    family    gases

             B         C         N         O         F         He/Ne
             Al        Si        P         S         Cl        Ar
             Ga        Ge        As        Se        Br        Kr
             In        Sn        Sb        Te        I         Xe
             Tl        Pb        Bi        Po        At        Rn
```

**Group 13 is the FIRST column of the p-block — the left-hand edge.** That is
where this chapter lives.

## 0.1 The general electronic configuration of the p-block

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |     GENERAL ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION OF THE p-BLOCK                |
  |                                                                    |
  |                    n s2   n p(1-6)                                 |
  |                                                                    |
  |     "n" is the period number = the outermost shell.                |
  |     The np part fills up from np1 (group 13) to np6 (group 18).    |
  |                                                                    |
  |     The ONLY exception is HELIUM, 1s2, which sits in group 18      |
  |     because of its PROPERTIES (a full valence shell), not its      |
  |     configuration.                                                 |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 0.2 Five general facts about the p-block worth a mark anywhere

```
  1.  General configuration = ns2 np(1-6) . Helium (1s2) is the exception.

  2.  The p-block contains METALS, METALLOIDS and NON-METALS all together.
      It is the ONLY block that does. Metallic character INCREASES down
      every p-block group and DECREASES across a period.

  3.  The FIRST member of every p-block group behaves ABNORMALLY compared
      with the rest of its own group, because it is very small, has a high
      electronegativity, a high ionisation enthalpy and NO d ORBITALS.
          B is odd in group 13 . C is odd in group 14 . N is odd in group 15.

  4.  Because the first member has only FOUR orbitals available (one s and
      three p), its MAXIMUM COVALENCY IS FOUR. The heavier members have
      vacant d orbitals and can go up to a covalency of six.

  5.  Going DOWN a p-block group, the INERT PAIR EFFECT makes the LOWER
      oxidation state (group number minus 2) more and more stable.
          Group 13:  +3 gives way to +1
          Group 14:  +4 gives way to +2
          Group 15:  +5 gives way to +3
```

That third point is the reason "Anomalous behaviour of boron" is a question
every single year.

> **TRAP:** Do not say "the p-block starts at group 13 so it has 6 groups
> including the noble gases" and then forget helium. Helium sits in group 18
> but its configuration is `1s2`, which is an **s-block** configuration.

# TOPIC 1 — GROUP 13: THE MEMBERS, CONFIGURATION AND OCCURRENCE

## 1.1 The five members

```
  +-----------+--------+-----+---------------------------+---------------+
  | ELEMENT   | SYMBOL |  Z  |  CONFIGURATION            |  NATURE       |
  +-----------+--------+-----+---------------------------+---------------+
  | Boron     |   B    |  5  |  [He] 2s2 2p1             |  NON-METAL    |
  | Aluminium |   Al   | 13  |  [Ne] 3s2 3p1             |  METAL        |
  | Gallium   |   Ga   | 31  |  [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p1        |  METAL        |
  | Indium    |   In   | 49  |  [Kr] 4d10 5s2 5p1        |  METAL        |
  | Thallium  |   Tl   | 81  |  [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p1   |  METAL        |
  +-----------+--------+-----+---------------------------+---------------+

  GENERAL ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION  =   ns2 np1
```

**Notice the pattern of what gets added:**

```
      B  -> Al     nothing extra                  a clean, normal jump
      Al -> Ga     TEN 3d electrons arrive        -> POOR SHIELDING
      Ga -> In     TEN 4d electrons arrive        -> poor shielding
      In -> Tl     FOURTEEN 4f AND ten 5d arrive  -> VERY POOR SHIELDING

  Every "exception" in this chapter comes from that column on the right.
  Whenever you are asked "why is this trend not smooth?", the answer is
  almost always "the d and f electrons shield the nuclear charge POORLY."
```

Notice how the configuration is built:

```
      ns2 np1   means  TWO electrons in the s orbital of the outer shell
                       ONE electron in a p orbital of the outer shell
                       = THREE valence electrons in total

      Orbital picture of the ground state of boron:

              2s              2p
           +------+     +----+----+----+
           | up dn|     | up |    |    |
           +------+     +----+----+----+

      Only ONE unpaired electron -> you would expect a valency of 1.
      But boron shows a valency of THREE. Why?
      Because one 2s electron is PROMOTED to an empty 2p orbital:

              2s              2p               (EXCITED STATE)
           +------+     +----+----+----+
           |  up  |     | up | up |    |
           +------+     +----+----+----+

      Now there are THREE unpaired electrons -> valency 3.
      The three orbitals used are 2s + 2px + 2py  ->  sp2 HYBRIDISATION
      ->  BX3 molecules are TRIGONAL PLANAR with 120 degree angles,
          and the third 2p orbital (2pz) is left EMPTY.

      THAT EMPTY 2pz ORBITAL IS THE WHOLE STORY OF BORON CHEMISTRY.
```

**An everyday analogy:** think of each atom as a person with three hands.
Boron is a tiny person with three short, very strong arms **and an empty
pocket**. It holds on to three partners tightly, but that empty pocket makes
it grab at any spare lone pair that comes near — that is why boron compounds
are Lewis acids. Thallium is a huge person with three long floppy arms, two of
which it keeps folded in its lap permanently (the inert pair), so it usually
uses only one.

## 1.2 The metal / non-metal split

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |     B    is the ONLY NON-METAL in group 13.                         |
  |          It is black, extremely hard, very high melting (2453 K)    |
  |          and is a SEMICONDUCTOR. It has two allotropes:             |
  |          CRYSTALLINE boron and AMORPHOUS boron.                     |
  |                                                                     |
  |     Al , Ga , In , Tl  are all METALS - soft, shiny, good           |
  |          conductors of heat and electricity.                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |     WHY?  Metallic character INCREASES down a group because the     |
  |           ionisation enthalpy falls, so outer electrons are given   |
  |           up more easily to form the "sea of electrons".            |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **TRAP:** Group 14 has TWO metalloids (Si and Ge). Group 13 has **none** in
> the AP Inter syllabus — boron is classed as a non-metal and the other four
> as metals. Do not copy the group-14 answer here.

# TOPIC 2 — ATOMIC AND IONIC RADII (and the gallium anomaly)

## 2.1 The numbers

```
  ATOMIC (METALLIC) RADIUS in pm

      B       Al       Ga       In       Tl
      85  ->  143  ->  135  ->  167  ->  170
              ^^^^     ^^^^
              LOOK!  Ga is SMALLER than Al


  IONIC RADIUS  M3+  in pm

      B       Al       Ga       In       Tl
      27  ->  53.5 ->  62   ->  80   ->  88.5

  IONIC RADIUS  Tl+  = 140 pm   (much bigger than Tl3+ = 88.5 pm,
                                 because it has lost only ONE electron)
```

## 2.2 The general trend and why

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   GOING DOWN A GROUP, ATOMIC RADIUS INCREASES.                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   WHY?  A brand-new electron SHELL is added at every step.          |
  |         The outermost electrons sit further from the nucleus and    |
  |         are screened from it by all the inner shells.               |
  |         The extra nuclear charge cannot make up for the extra       |
  |         shell, so the atom gets BIGGER.                             |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 2.3 THE GALLIUM ANOMALY — a guaranteed 2-mark question

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   QUESTION:  Why is the atomic radius of GALLIUM (135 pm) LESS      |
  |              than that of ALUMINIUM (143 pm)?                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ANSWER (learn these four lines):                                  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   1.  Gallium is the first group-13 element to come AFTER the       |
  |       first d-block series, so it has TEN 3d ELECTRONS:             |
  |             Al   [Ne] 3s2 3p1                                       |
  |             Ga   [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p1                                  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   2.  d ORBITALS ARE DIFFUSE and SHIELD THE NUCLEAR CHARGE VERY     |
  |       POORLY compared with s and p orbitals.                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   3.  So the 4s and 4p electrons of gallium feel a much LARGER      |
  |       EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR CHARGE than expected and are pulled in      |
  |       towards the nucleus.                                          |
  |                                                                     |
  |   4.  This contraction more than cancels the increase due to the    |
  |       extra shell. Result: Ga is SMALLER than Al.                   |
  |       (This is called the D-BLOCK CONTRACTION.)                     |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**A picture of what "poor shielding" means:**

```
        GOOD SHIELDING (s and p electrons)      POOR SHIELDING (d and f)

           inner electrons form a               d electrons are spread
           tight "umbrella" over the            out and full of holes
           nucleus

              +---------------+                    +--- --- --- ---+
              |###############|                    | #   #   #   # |
              |###############|                    |   #   #   #   |
              +---------------+                    +--- --- --- ---+
                     (+)                                  (+)
                      |                                    |
              outer electron feels                 outer electron SEES
              only a small pull                    the nucleus and is
                                                   pulled in HARD
```

## 2.4 Why the In -> Tl increase is also small

```
      In (167 pm)  ->  Tl (170 pm)   -   only 3 pm !

      Between them, FOURTEEN 4f electrons and TEN 5d electrons are added.
      f orbitals shield even worse than d orbitals.
      This is the LANTHANOID CONTRACTION plus a d-block contraction.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 2**

> *Q. Arrange B, Al, Ga, In, Tl in increasing order of atomic radius and
> justify the position of gallium.*

```
  STEP 1 : Write the numbers.
              B 85 , Al 143 , Ga 135 , In 167 , Tl 170  (pm)

  STEP 2 : Sort them.
              B (85) < Ga (135) < Al (143) < In (167) < Tl (170)

  STEP 3 : Justify gallium.
              Gallium comes just after the 3d series and carries ten
              3d electrons. d orbitals shield the nucleus POORLY, so the
              outer 4s and 4p electrons of Ga feel a higher effective
              nuclear charge and are pulled in. Gallium therefore ends
              up SMALLER than aluminium, even though it is one period
              lower down.
```

> **TRAP:** The order is **not** simply B < Al < Ga < In < Tl. Gallium jumps
> in front of aluminium. If an option in a multiple-choice paper shows the
> plain increasing order, look for the option with **Ga and Al swapped**.

# TOPIC 3 — IONISATION ENTHALPY

## 3.1 The numbers

```
  IONISATION ENTHALPY in kJ mol-1

    ELEMENT      IE1        IE2        IE3
    ------------------------------------------
       B         801       2427       3659
       Al        577       1816       2744
       Ga        579       1979       2962
       In        558       1820       2704
       Tl        589       1971       2877

  THE ORDER OF IE1 :   B  >  Tl  >  Ga  >  Al  >  In
                      801   589    579    577    558
```

## 3.2 The trend is NOT smooth — and that is the question

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   THE ZIG-ZAG:                                                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |     B  801   -->  Al 577   -->  Ga 579  -->  In 558  -->  Tl 589    |
  |          BIG FALL      RISE!         FALL         RISE!             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   1.  B -> Al : a LARGE FALL, as expected. The atom gets much       |
  |       bigger and there is no d contraction to spoil it.             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   2.  Al -> Ga : a small RISE, NOT a fall.                          |
  |       REASON: the ten 3d electrons of gallium shield very poorly,   |
  |       so its outer electron is held more tightly than aluminium's.  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   3.  Ga -> In : a fall, as expected (size increases a lot).        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   4.  In -> Tl : a RISE again.                                      |
  |       REASON: the fourteen 4f and ten 5d electrons of thallium      |
  |       shield extremely poorly (LANTHANOID CONTRACTION), so the      |
  |       6s2 6p1 electrons feel a very high effective nuclear charge.  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ONE-LINE ANSWER: the decrease is NOT regular because of the POOR  |
  |   SHIELDING by the intervening d and f electrons.                   |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 3.3 IE1 < IE2 < IE3 — and why that matters

```
      For every element:   IE1  <  IE2  <  IE3

      WHY?  After removing one electron you are pulling the next electron
            out of a POSITIVE ion. The remaining electrons are held more
            tightly and the ionic radius has shrunk. So each successive
            removal costs more.

      CONSEQUENCE FOR BORON:
            IE1 + IE2 + IE3  =  801 + 2427 + 3659  =  6887 kJ/mol

            That is an ENORMOUS amount of energy. No lattice energy or
            hydration energy can pay it back.

            THEREFORE BORON NEVER FORMS A B3+ ION.
            All boron compounds are COVALENT.
```

**Compare with group 2 (a common comparison question):**

```
      IE1 of group 13 is LOWER than IE1 of the group 2 element
      of the same period:

            Be 899  >  B 801           Mg 737  >  Al 577

      REASON: in group 13 the electron being removed comes from a p
      orbital, which is HIGHER in energy and better shielded than the
      completely filled ns2 sub-shell of group 2. A full ns2 sub-shell
      is extra stable, so group 2 holds on harder.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 3**

> *Q. Ionisation enthalpy generally decreases down a group. Explain why the
> first ionisation enthalpy of gallium (579) is higher than that of aluminium
> (577), and that of thallium (589) higher than that of indium (558).*

```
  STEP 1 : State the general rule.
              IE normally falls down a group because the atom gets
              bigger and the outer electron is further away and better
              shielded.

  STEP 2 : Ga vs Al.
              Ga has ten 3d electrons that Al does not have.
              d orbitals are DIFFUSE and shield the nuclear charge
              POORLY, so Ga's outer electron feels a higher effective
              nuclear charge -> more energy needed -> IE goes UP.

  STEP 3 : Tl vs In.
              Tl has fourteen 4f electrons AND ten 5d electrons that In
              does not have. f orbitals shield even worse than d.
              Same argument, but stronger -> IE of Tl > IE of In.

  STEP 4 : Conclusion.
              The fall in IE down group 13 is therefore NOT REGULAR.
              Full order:  B > Tl > Ga > Al > In .
```

# TOPIC 5 — PHYSICAL PROPERTIES: MELTING POINT, BOILING POINT, DENSITY

## 5.1 The numbers

```
  +-----------+---------+---------+----------+
  |  ELEMENT  |  m.p./K |  b.p./K | density  |
  |           |         |         | g cm-3   |
  +-----------+---------+---------+----------+
  |    B      |   2453  |   3923  |   2.35   |
  |    Al     |    933  |   2740  |   2.70   |
  |    Ga     |  ** 303 **  2676  |   5.90   |   <- MELTS IN YOUR HAND
  |    In     |    430  |   2353  |   7.31   |
  |    Tl     |    576  |   1730  |  11.85   |
  +-----------+---------+---------+----------+
```

## 5.2 Melting point: falls, then RISES

```
      B 2453  >>  Al 933  >>  Ga 303  <  In 430  <  Tl 576

  THE FALL FROM B TO Ga
      Boron is a GIANT COVALENT solid (icosahedral B12 units) with very
      strong, short covalent bonds -> enormous melting point.
      Al and Ga are metals with much weaker metallic bonding, and the
      bonds get longer and weaker as the atoms grow.

  THE RISE FROM Ga TO Tl
      Gallium's value is abnormally LOW (see below). Once you get past
      that anomaly, indium and thallium behave more normally and their
      melting points climb again because the heavier atoms pack into
      denser, better-bonded lattices.
```

## 5.3 THE GALLIUM MELTING POINT — the famous 2-marker

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   QUESTION: Why does gallium have an unusually low melting point    |
  |             (303 K, about 30 degC) - low enough to melt in the      |
  |             palm of your hand?                                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ANSWER:                                                           |
  |                                                                     |
  |   In the SOLID state, gallium does NOT have a normal metallic       |
  |   lattice of individual atoms. Instead it exists as DISCRETE Ga2    |
  |   DIATOMIC MOLECULES.                                               |
  |                                                                     |
  |   These Ga2 molecules are held to one another only by WEAK          |
  |   INTERMOLECULAR (van der Waals type) FORCES.                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |   Very little energy is therefore needed to break the solid apart,  |
  |   so gallium melts at only 303 K.                                   |
  |                                                                     |
  |   BUT to BOIL it you must break the strong Ga-Ga covalent bond      |
  |   inside the Ga2 molecule as well, which needs a huge amount of     |
  |   energy -> b.p. = 2676 K.                                          |
  |                                                                     |
  |   RESULT:  gallium has the WIDEST LIQUID RANGE of any element       |
  |            (303 K to 2676 K, a span of about 2373 K), which is      |
  |            exactly why it is used in HIGH-TEMPERATURE THERMOMETERS. |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
        THE GALLIUM PICTURE

            SOLID  Ga                          LIQUID / GAS

        (Ga-Ga)  (Ga-Ga)  (Ga-Ga)              Ga  Ga  Ga  Ga
           :        :        :        heat
        (Ga-Ga)  (Ga-Ga)  (Ga-Ga)    ------>   Ga  Ga  Ga  Ga
           :        :        :       303 K
        (Ga-Ga)  (Ga-Ga)  (Ga-Ga)              Ga  Ga  Ga  Ga

        strong Ga-Ga bonds INSIDE each pair (shown by  -  )
        WEAK forces BETWEEN the pairs        (shown by  :  )

        Melting only breaks the WEAK ones   -> LOW melting point
        Boiling must break the STRONG ones  -> VERY HIGH boiling point
```

## 5.4 Density

```
      B 2.35  <  Al 2.70  <  Ga 5.90  <  In 7.31  <  Tl 11.85  g cm-3

      DENSITY INCREASES steadily down the group.
      REASON: the atomic MASS rises much faster than the atomic VOLUME.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 4**

> *Q. Gallium melts at 303 K but boils at 2676 K. Explain this large gap and
> state the use it leads to.*

```
  STEP 1 : Solid gallium is built from Ga2 DIATOMIC MOLECULES.

  STEP 2 : MELTING only has to overcome the WEAK forces BETWEEN the Ga2
           molecules. That costs very little energy -> m.p. = 303 K,
           low enough that the metal melts in the hand.

  STEP 3 : BOILING must also break the STRONG covalent Ga-Ga bond WITHIN
           each Ga2 molecule. That costs a great deal of energy
           -> b.p. = 2676 K.

  STEP 4 : USE. The liquid range 303 K to 2676 K is the widest of any
           element, so gallium is used in HIGH-TEMPERATURE THERMOMETERS
           (a mercury thermometer would have boiled long before).
```

# TOPIC 6 — OXIDATION STATES AND THE INERT PAIR EFFECT

## 6.1 Why +3 in the first place

```
      ns2 np1  ->  three valence electrons  ->  oxidation state +3

      To use all three, one ns electron is PROMOTED to an np orbital:

              ns             np                (EXCITED STATE)
           +------+     +----+----+----+
           |  up  |     | up | up |    |
           +------+     +----+----+----+
                       three unpaired electrons -> +3 / trivalent
```

## 6.2 Why +1 turns up as well

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |   THE INERT PAIR EFFECT                                              |
  |                                                                      |
  |   DEFINITION : the RELUCTANCE OF THE ns2 ELECTRON PAIR TO TAKE PART  |
  |                IN BOND FORMATION, which INCREASES going DOWN a       |
  |                p-block group, and which makes the LOWER oxidation    |
  |                state (here +1) more and more stable.                 |
  |                                                                      |
  |   CAUSE (four steps - write all four):                               |
  |                                                                      |
  |     (a)  Going down the group, filled d (3d10, 4d10, 5d10) and       |
  |          f (4f14) subshells come in between the nucleus and the      |
  |          valence shell.                                              |
  |                                                                      |
  |     (b)  d and f orbitals are DIFFUSE and SHIELD the nuclear         |
  |          charge VERY POORLY.                                         |
  |                                                                      |
  |     (c)  So the ns2 pair feels a LARGE EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR CHARGE      |
  |          and is held very tightly and pulled in close.               |
  |                                                                      |
  |     (d)  PROMOTING an ns electron to an np orbital now costs more    |
  |          energy than is repaid by forming two extra bonds. So the    |
  |          ns2 pair stays put, only the single np1 electron is used,   |
  |          and the element shows an oxidation state of +1.             |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
      THE ENERGY ARGUMENT IN ONE PICTURE

        AT THE TOP (B, Al)                 AT THE BOTTOM (Tl)

        promotion energy  SMALL            promotion energy  LARGE
        bond energy       LARGE            bond energy       SMALL
        (short strong bonds)               (long weak bonds)

        making 3 bonds PAYS               making 3 bonds does NOT pay
             -> +3 wins                        -> +1 wins
```

## 6.3 The consequences — this is where the marks are

| ELEMENT | STABLE STATE | UNSTABLE STATE | BEHAVIOUR |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | **+3 only** | — | Never shows +1. Always covalent |
| Al | **+3** | +1 (rare, high temperature only) | Normal trivalent metal |
| Ga | +3 | +1 | Ga(I) **disproportionates** |
| In | +3 | +1 (becoming real) | In(I) exists but is a reducing agent |
| Tl | +1 **(most stable)** | +3 | Tl(III) is a strong **oxidising agent** |

```
  STABILITY OF THE +1 STATE :     Al  <  Ga  <  In  <  Tl
  STABILITY OF THE +3 STATE :     Al  >  Ga  >  In  >  Tl

  OXIDISING POWER OF THE +3 COMPOUNDS :   Al3+ < Ga3+ < In3+ << Tl3+
        (the less stable the +3 state, the more greedily it grabs
         electrons to drop to +1, so the better an oxidiser it is)
```

## 6.4 The four facts that prove it

```
  1.  Tl(III) compounds are STRONG OXIDISING AGENTS. They fall apart
      into Tl(I) on heating:

            TlCl3   --(heat)-->   TlCl  +  Cl2
            Tl2O3   --(heat)-->   Tl2O  +  O2

  2.  TlI3 EXISTS but it is NOT thallium(III) iodide.
      It is really THALLIUM(I) TRI-IODIDE ,  Tl+ (I3)- .
      REASON: Tl3+ is such a strong oxidiser and I- such a good reducer
      that Tl3+ would immediately oxidise I- to I2. So the compound
      settles for Tl+ instead.
      (Compare: PbI4 does not exist at all, for the same reason.)

  3.  Ga(I) and In(I) DISPROPORTIONATE, because in the middle of the
      group neither state is comfortable:

            3 Ga+   -->   2 Ga  +  Ga3+

  4.  TlOH is a STRONG BASE, about as strong as NaOH, because Tl+ is a
      big, low-charge, genuinely metallic ion. Compare B(OH)3 which is
      an ACID. That is the metallic character trend in one line.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 5**

> *Q. Explain why thallium(I) is more stable than thallium(III), while
> aluminium(III) is far more stable than aluminium(I).*

```
  STEP 1 : Both elements have the configuration ns2 np1.
              Al  [Ne] 3s2 3p1        Tl  [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p1

  STEP 2 : To reach +3 an atom must UNPAIR and PROMOTE one ns electron.

  STEP 3 : In ALUMINIUM there are no d or f electrons in between. The
           3s2 pair is normally shielded, promotion is cheap, and the
           three short Al-X bonds formed release plenty of energy.
              ->  +3 is easily the more stable state.

  STEP 4 : In THALLIUM the 4f14 and 5d10 electrons shield the nucleus
           very badly. The 6s2 pair is therefore gripped extremely
           tightly (INERT PAIR EFFECT) and promotion is expensive.
           At the same time Tl is large, so the Tl-X bonds are long and
           weak and release little energy.
              ->  the 6s2 pair stays unused, only 6p1 bonds,
              ->  +1 is the more stable state.

  STEP 5 : Consequence. Tl(III) compounds readily accept two electrons
           and drop to Tl(I), so they are STRONG OXIDISING AGENTS:
                 TlCl3  --(heat)-->  TlCl  +  Cl2
```

> **TRAP:** The cause of the inert pair effect is **poor shielding by d and f
> electrons**. Do not write "because the atom is big" and stop — that gets
> half marks at best.

# TOPIC 7 — THE ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF BORON

Boron, the first member of group 13, behaves very differently from Al, Ga, In
and Tl. This is a guaranteed 4-mark question.

## 7.1 THE FIVE REASONS (learn these first)

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   WHY BORON IS THE ODD ONE OUT                                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   1.  VERY SMALL SIZE          atomic radius only 85 pm             |
  |                                (Al is 143 pm - nearly twice as big) |
  |                                                                     |
  |   2.  VERY HIGH IONISATION     IE1 = 801 kJ/mol                     |
  |       ENTHALPY                 IE1+IE2+IE3 = 6887 kJ/mol            |
  |                                                                     |
  |   3.  HIGH ELECTRONEGATIVITY   2.0 , against 1.5 for Al             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   4.  ONLY FOUR ORBITALS       2s + 2px + 2py + 2pz                 |
  |       AVAILABLE                -> MAXIMUM COVALENCY = 4             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   5.  NO d ORBITALS in its     -> it can NEVER expand its octet     |
  |       valence shell            -> no [BF6]3- , no covalency 6       |
  |                                                                     |
  |   (Points 4 and 5 are really the same idea said twice. Write both,  |
  |    the examiner counts them separately.)                            |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 7.2 THE CONSEQUENCES (each of these is a mark)

| # | HOW BORON DIFFERS | THE REST OF GROUP 13 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Boron is a **NON-METAL**; hard, black, high melting, a semiconductor | Al, Ga, In, Tl are soft **METALS** |
| 2 | Forms **ONLY COVALENT** compounds — B3+ never exists (IE too high) | Form **ionic** compounds, M3+ ions exist |
| 3 | **Maximum covalency 4** — `[BF4]-` is the limit | Covalency **6** — `[AlF6]3-`, `[Al(H2O)6]3+` |
| 4 | Its trihalides are **monomeric**, planar `BX3` | `AlCl3` is a **dimer**, `Al2Cl6` |
| 5 | `B(OH)3` (boric acid) is **ACIDIC** | `Al(OH)3` is **amphoteric**, `TlOH` is basic |
| 6 | `B2O3` is an **ACIDIC** oxide | `Al2O3`, `Ga2O3` amphoteric; `In2O3`, `Tl2O3` basic |
| 7 | Forms many **electron-deficient hydrides (boranes)** that are stable and well known | Their hydrides are unstable and poorly characterised |
| 8 | Boron halides are **completely hydrolysed** by water to `H3BO3` | `AlCl3` hydrolyses only partially and reversibly |
| 9 | Shows only the **+3** state | Show **+3 and +1**; Tl prefers +1 |
| 10 | Boron **does not react** with acids or alkalis under mild conditions | Al dissolves in both acids and alkalis |

**WORKED EXAMPLE 6**

> *Q. Give any five points in which boron differs from the other members of
> group 13, with reasons.*

```
  STEP 1 : Begin with the CAUSE in one sentence.
           "Boron is anomalous because of its very small size, high
            ionisation enthalpy, high electronegativity, the
            availability of only four orbitals and the ABSENCE of
            d orbitals."

  STEP 2 : Then list, one line each, FACT + REASON:

     (i)   Boron is a NON-METAL, the rest are metals.
           Reason: its ionisation enthalpy is far too high to release
           electrons into a metallic "sea".

     (ii)  Boron forms ONLY COVALENT compounds; B3+ is unknown.
           Reason: IE1+IE2+IE3 = 6887 kJ/mol is never repaid by
           lattice or hydration energy.

     (iii) Boron has a MAXIMUM COVALENCY OF FOUR ([BF4]-), while
           aluminium reaches six ([AlF6]3-).
           Reason: boron has only four orbitals (2s + three 2p) and
           NO d orbitals to expand its octet.

     (iv)  Boric acid B(OH)3 is ACIDIC, while Al(OH)3 is AMPHOTERIC
           and TlOH is strongly BASIC.
           Reason: metallic character increases down the group.

     (v)   Boron trihalides are MONOMERIC and planar, while AlCl3 is
           a DIMER Al2Cl6.
           Reason: boron is too small for a chlorine atom to bridge
           two boron atoms, and back bonding already relieves some of
           boron's electron deficiency.
```

# TOPIC 8 — THE DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP: BORON AND SILICON

## 8.1 What a diagonal relationship is

```
       GROUP    1      2      13     14

       Li  ---> Be ---> B ---> C
        \        \       \
         \        \       \
       Na  ---> Mg ---> Al ---> Si
                                 ^
       THE THREE FAMOUS DIAGONAL PAIRS:

              Li  and  Mg
              Be  and  Al
              B   and  Si     <---  THIS CHAPTER
```

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   WHY DOES IT HAPPEN?                                               |
  |                                                                     |
  |   Going ACROSS a period, size DECREASES and electronegativity       |
  |   INCREASES.                                                        |
  |   Going DOWN a group, size INCREASES and electronegativity          |
  |   DECREASES.                                                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   Move one step ACROSS and one step DOWN and the two changes        |
  |   CANCEL OUT. The diagonal neighbour therefore ends up with an      |
  |   almost identical SIZE, ELECTRONEGATIVITY and CHARGE-TO-RADIUS     |
  |   RATIO - and so behaves chemically like a twin.                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |        B   radius 85 pm   EN 2.0                                    |
  |        Si  radius 118 pm  EN 1.8      <- very close                 |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 8.2 THE POINTS OF SIMILARITY (learn six, write six)

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |  1.  BOTH ARE NON-METALLIC SEMICONDUCTORS.                          |
  |      Boron is a non-metal, silicon is a metalloid; both are hard,   |
  |      high-melting, poor conductors that behave as semiconductors.   |
  |                                                                     |
  |  2.  BOTH FORM ONLY COVALENT COMPOUNDS.                             |
  |      B3+ and Si4+ ions never exist in ordinary chemistry.           |
  |                                                                     |
  |  3.  BOTH FORM VOLATILE, FLAMMABLE, EASILY HYDROLYSED HYDRIDES.     |
  |      BORANES (B2H6, B4H10 ...) and SILANES (SiH4, Si2H6 ...) are    |
  |      both gaseous, both catch fire in air and both are attacked     |
  |      by water:                                                      |
  |          B2H6  +  6 H2O  -->  2 H3BO3  +  6 H2                      |
  |          SiH4  +  4 H2O  -->  Si(OH)4  +  4 H2                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |  4.  BOTH HALIDES ARE COMPLETELY HYDROLYSED BY WATER.               |
  |          BCl3   +  3 H2O  -->  H3BO3    +  3 HCl                    |
  |          SiCl4  +  4 H2O  -->  Si(OH)4  +  4 HCl                    |
  |      (Note that AlCl3 is only partially hydrolysed - so this        |
  |       really is a BORON-SILICON likeness, not a group likeness.)    |
  |                                                                     |
  |  5.  BOTH OXIDES ARE ACIDIC AND FORM GLASSES.                       |
  |      B2O3 and SiO2 are both weakly acidic, both dissolve in hot     |
  |      alkali to give borates and silicates, and both form glassy     |
  |      networks:                                                      |
  |          B2O3   +  6 NaOH  -->  2 Na3BO3   +  3 H2O                 |
  |          SiO2   +  2 NaOH  -->  Na2SiO3    +  H2O                   |
  |                                                                     |
  |  6.  BOTH FORM POLYMERIC, CHAIN AND RING STRUCTURES.                |
  |      BORATES and SILICATES both build up from small units (BO3      |
  |      triangles / BO4 tetrahedra and SiO4 tetrahedra) sharing        |
  |      oxygen atoms into rings, chains and sheets.                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |  7.  BOTH FORM HARD, HIGH-MELTING BINARY COMPOUNDS WITH METALS      |
  |      (BORIDES and SILICIDES) and extremely hard CARBIDES            |
  |      (B4C and SiC, "carborundum") used as abrasives.                |
  |                                                                     |
  |  8.  BOTH BORIC ACID AND SILICIC ACID ARE VERY WEAK ACIDS.          |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 7**

> *Q. What is a diagonal relationship? Give four points of similarity between
> boron and silicon.*

```
  STEP 1 : DEFINITION.
           "Certain elements of the second period show a close
            resemblance to the element lying DIAGONALLY BELOW AND TO
            THE RIGHT of them in the next period. This is called the
            diagonal relationship. The three pairs are Li-Mg, Be-Al
            and B-Si."

  STEP 2 : REASON.
           Moving across a period decreases size and increases
           electronegativity; moving down a group does the opposite.
           Along a diagonal the two effects CANCEL, so the pair have
           nearly the same size, electronegativity and charge/radius
           ratio.  B = 85 pm, EN 2.0 ; Si = 118 pm, EN 1.8.

  STEP 3 : FOUR SIMILARITIES (with an equation each where possible).
           (i)   Both are non-metallic semiconductors that form only
                 COVALENT compounds.
           (ii)  Both form volatile, flammable hydrides (boranes and
                 silanes) that are hydrolysed by water.
           (iii) Both halides are completely hydrolysed:
                     BCl3 + 3H2O -> H3BO3 + 3HCl
                     SiCl4 + 4H2O -> Si(OH)4 + 4HCl
           (iv)  Both oxides B2O3 and SiO2 are ACIDIC and dissolve in
                 alkali to give borates and silicates, and both build
                 up polymeric structures.
```

> **TRAP:** The diagonal partner of boron is **silicon**, not carbon. Carbon is
> the horizontal neighbour. Write "one step across, one step down".

# TOPIC 9 — REACTIVITY TOWARDS AIR, WATER, ACIDS AND ALKALIS

## 9.1 Towards air (dioxygen and dinitrogen)

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   CRYSTALLINE boron is almost INERT at ordinary temperature.        |
  |   AMORPHOUS boron and all the metals burn when heated.              |
  |                                                                     |
  |   WITH DIOXYGEN (heated, forms the trioxide):                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |       2 B   +   3/2 O2   --(heat)-->   B2O3                         |
  |       4 Al  +   3 O2     --(heat)-->   2 Al2O3                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |       In general:   2 E  +  3/2 O2  -->  E2O3                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |   WITH DINITROGEN (very high temperature, forms the nitride):       |
  |                                                                     |
  |       2 B   +   N2   --(high temp)-->   2 BN                        |
  |       2 Al  +   N2   --(high temp)-->   2 AlN                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |       BN = BORON NITRIDE, "INORGANIC GRAPHITE", a slippery white    |
  |       solid isoelectronic with graphite. Its cubic form (BORAZON)   |
  |       is one of the hardest substances known.                       |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Why aluminium does not corrode away** — a very common 2-marker:

```
      Aluminium is a highly reactive metal, yet an aluminium vessel
      lasts for years. WHY?

      As soon as aluminium meets air it forms a THIN, HARD, CONTINUOUS,
      IMPERVIOUS LAYER OF Al2O3 that sticks tightly to the surface.
      This layer seals the metal off from further attack.
      The metal is then said to be PASSIVE / PROTECTED.

      Deliberately thickening this layer by electrolysis is called
      ANODISING - that is how coloured aluminium window frames are made.
```

## 9.2 Nature of the oxides — the trend that carries marks

```
      B2O3      Al2O3      Ga2O3      In2O3      Tl2O3
      ACIDIC    AMPHO-     AMPHO-     BASIC      BASIC
                TERIC      TERIC

      Tl2O is STRONGLY BASIC.  TlOH is as strong a base as NaOH.

      REASON: metallic character INCREASES down the group; a metallic
      element gives a basic oxide, a non-metallic element an acidic one.

      SAME TREND FOR THE HYDROXIDES:
          B(OH)3      ACIDIC      (it is boric acid!)
          Al(OH)3     AMPHOTERIC
          Ga(OH)3     AMPHOTERIC
          In(OH)3     BASIC
          Tl(OH)3 / TlOH   BASIC / STRONGLY BASIC
```

## 9.3 Towards acids

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   BORON does NOT react with dilute acids at all (it is a            |
  |   non-metal - it has no electrons to give up cheaply).              |
  |                                                                     |
  |   But HOT CONCENTRATED NITRIC ACID, being an oxidiser, attacks it:  |
  |                                                                     |
  |       B  +  3 HNO3 (conc.)  -->  H3BO3  +  3 NO2                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ALUMINIUM dissolves readily in dilute mineral acids, giving       |
  |   HYDROGEN:                                                         |
  |                                                                     |
  |       2 Al  +  6 HCl        -->  2 AlCl3      +  3 H2               |
  |       2 Al  +  3 H2SO4(dil) -->  Al2(SO4)3    +  3 H2               |
  |                                                                     |
  |   BUT CONCENTRATED NITRIC ACID makes aluminium PASSIVE.             |
  |   It instantly builds an unusually thick, tight Al2O3 layer that    |
  |   stops the reaction dead. THIS IS WHY CONCENTRATED HNO3 CAN BE     |
  |   TRANSPORTED IN ALUMINIUM CONTAINERS.                              |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 9.4 Towards alkalis — the AMPHOTERIC behaviour

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   AMPHOTERIC means: reacts with ACIDS as a base AND with            |
  |                     ALKALIS as an acid.                             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ALUMINIUM METAL dissolves in hot aqueous alkali giving HYDROGEN   |

  |                                                                     |
  |   BORON does not react with alkali solutions, but FUSED alkali      |

  |                                                                     |
  |       2 B  +  6 NaOH  --(fused)-->  2 Na3BO3  +  3 H2               |
  |                                     sodium borate                   |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 9.5 THE FOUR AMPHOTERIC EQUATIONS — learn these word for word

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   AMPHOTERIC NATURE OF ALUMINIUM METAL                              |
  |                                                                     |
  |   with ACID  :  2 Al + 6 HCl  -->  2 AlCl3 + 3 H2                   |
  |   with ALKALI:  2 Al + 2 NaOH + 6 H2O  -->  2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 3 H2    |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ------------------------------------------------------------      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   AMPHOTERIC NATURE OF ALUMINIUM OXIDE Al2O3                        |
  |                                                                     |
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 8**

> *Q. Show with equations that aluminium and its oxide are amphoteric. Why is
> concentrated nitric acid transported in aluminium containers?*

```
  STEP 1 : Define. AMPHOTERIC = reacts with BOTH acids and bases.

  STEP 2 : Aluminium metal.
              acid   :  2 Al + 6 HCl --> 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2
              alkali :  2 Al + 2 NaOH + 6 H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 3 H2

  STEP 3 : Aluminium oxide.
              acid   :  Al2O3 + 6 HCl --> 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2O
              alkali :  Al2O3 + 2 NaOH + 3 H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4]

  STEP 4 : Both give a salt with acid AND a soluble aluminate with

  STEP 5 : Concentrated HNO3 is a powerful OXIDISING agent. It oxidises
           the surface of the aluminium instantly to a THICK, TIGHT,
           IMPERVIOUS layer of Al2O3 which then protects the metal
           underneath. The metal is rendered PASSIVE, so the acid can
           safely be stored and carried in aluminium containers.
```

# TOPIC 10 — REACTIVITY TOWARDS HALOGENS; THE TRIHALIDES

## 10.1 Formation

## 10.2 The shape of BX3 — trigonal planar, sp2

```
                      X
                      |
                      |
                      B                    sp2 HYBRIDISED
                    /   \                  TRIGONAL PLANAR
                   /     \                 ALL ANGLES = 120 degrees
                  X       X                MOLECULE IS NON-POLAR
                                           (the three bond dipoles cancel)

     AND, sticking up and down at right angles to that plane,
     ONE COMPLETELY EMPTY 2pz ORBITAL:

                      (empty 2pz lobe)
                        _

                        |
                   X -- B -- X          the plane of the molecule
                        |
                        X
                        |
                       ( )
                        -
                      (empty 2pz lobe)
```

## 10.3 WHY BX3 IS ELECTRON DEFICIENT — count the electrons

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   ELECTRON COUNT AROUND BORON IN BF3                                |
  |                                                                     |
  |       boron's own valence electrons          =  3                   |
  |       one electron from each of 3 fluorines  =  3                   |
  |                                                 ---                 |
  |       total electrons around boron           =  6                   |
  |                                                                     |
  |       An octet needs 8. Boron has only SIX - a SEXTET.              |
  |                                                                     |
  |       Boron therefore has ONE COMPLETELY EMPTY 2p ORBITAL and       |
  |       is TWO electrons SHORT.  It is ELECTRON DEFICIENT.            |
  |                                                                     |
  |       An electron-deficient molecule ACCEPTS A LONE PAIR.           |
  |       A substance that accepts a lone pair is a LEWIS ACID.         |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**The two reactions that prove it:**

```
   (a)  WITH AMMONIA (a lone pair on nitrogen):

               F         H                    F   H
               |         |                    |   |
          F -- B    +   :N -- H    -->   F -- B <- N -- H
               |         |                    |   |
               F         H                    F   H

          BF3  +  NH3  -->  F3B <- NH3
          A COORDINATE (dative) bond is formed. Boron is now sp3 and
          the adduct is TETRAHEDRAL about boron.

   (b)  WITH A FLUORIDE ION:

          BF3  +  F-  -->  [BF4]-        TETRAHEDRAL, sp3

          NOTE: it stops at FOUR. [BF6]3- can NEVER form, because
          boron has only four orbitals and NO d orbitals.
          Aluminium, which has vacant 3d orbitals, happily forms
          [AlF6]3- with a covalency of SIX.
```

## 10.4 THE LEWIS ACID ORDER — the single most-asked "reason" question

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |          BF3   <   BCl3   <   BBr3   <   BI3                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |               LEWIS ACID STRENGTH INCREASES                         |
  |                                                                     |
  |   You would expect the OPPOSITE, because fluorine is the most       |
  |   electronegative halogen and should leave boron the most           |
  |   electron-starved. The observed order is the reverse. Why?         |
  |                                                                     |
  |                    BECAUSE OF BACK BONDING                          |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**What back bonding is:**

```
       Each halogen has FILLED p orbitals holding LONE PAIRS.
       Boron has ONE EMPTY 2p orbital.
       A halogen lone pair can slide SIDEWAYS into that empty orbital.
       This is  p(pi) - p(pi)  BACK BONDING  (also called back donation).


                        FILLED 2p of F
                             _
                            ( )
                             |          the lone pair leans over
                             v          into boron's empty 2p
                             _
                            ( )   <-- EMPTY 2p of B
                             |
                   F ------- B ------- F
                             |
                             F

       RESULT: the empty orbital is PARTLY FILLED, the B-X bond gets
       some DOUBLE BOND character, and boron becomes LESS hungry
       for an outside lone pair - i.e. a WEAKER Lewis acid.
```

**Why the effect dies away down the halogen group:**

```
  +----------+-----------------+---------------------+------------------+
  | MOLECULE | HALOGEN ORBITAL | OVERLAP WITH B's 2p | LEWIS ACIDITY    |
  +----------+-----------------+---------------------+------------------+
  |   BF3    |      2p         |  SAME SIZE - BEST   |  WEAKEST         |
  |   BCl3   |      3p         |  poorer             |   |              |
  |   BBr3   |      4p         |  poorer still       |   |              |
  |   BI3    |      5p         |  WORST - almost     |   v              |
  |          |                 |  none               |  STRONGEST       |
  +----------+-----------------+---------------------+------------------+

  THE ONE-LINE RULE:
      Effective sideways (pi) overlap needs orbitals of SIMILAR SIZE.
      Fluorine's 2p is the same size as boron's 2p, so back bonding is
      most effective in BF3, boron's deficiency is most relieved, and
      BF3 is the WEAKEST Lewis acid.
      Iodine's 5p is far too big and diffuse to overlap with a 2p, so
      there is essentially no back bonding in BI3, boron stays fully
      electron deficient, and BI3 is the STRONGEST Lewis acid.
```

```
  SUPPORTING EVIDENCE (write this if you want the extra mark):
      The measured B-F bond length in BF3 is 130 pm, SHORTER than a
      normal single B-F bond, and the bond is stronger than expected.
      That extra shortening is the double-bond character produced by
      back bonding. There is no such shortening in BI3.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 9**

> *Q. Arrange BF3, BCl3, BBr3 and BI3 in increasing order of Lewis acid
> strength and explain the order.*

```
  STEP 1 : Write the order.
              BF3  <  BCl3  <  BBr3  <  BI3

  STEP 2 : Say why they are Lewis acids at all.
              In BX3 boron has only SIX electrons around it (a sextet)
              and one EMPTY 2p orbital, so it accepts a lone pair.

  STEP 3 : Introduce back bonding.
              A filled p orbital of the halogen donates its lone pair
              sideways into boron's empty 2p orbital -
              p(pi)-p(pi) BACK BONDING. This partly fills the empty
              orbital and REDUCES the electron deficiency.

  STEP 4 : Compare the overlap.
              F uses a 2p orbital - the SAME SIZE as boron's 2p, so the
              overlap is BEST and back bonding is STRONGEST.
              Cl (3p), Br (4p), I (5p) get bigger and bigger; overlap
              gets worse and worse.

  STEP 5 : Conclude.
              Most back bonding  -> least deficient -> weakest acid = BF3
              Least back bonding -> most deficient  -> strongest acid = BI3
              Hence  BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3 < BI3 .
```

> **TRAP:** Do NOT answer "fluorine is most electronegative so BF3 is the
> strongest Lewis acid". That is the intuitive answer and it is **wrong**.
> The magic word the examiner wants is **BACK BONDING**.

## 10.5 Hydrolysis of the trihalides

```
      BCl3  +  3 H2O   -->   H3BO3  +  3 HCl        COMPLETE hydrolysis

      BF3 is different - it only hydrolyses PARTIALLY, because the
      strong back bonding makes the B-F bond unusually strong:

          4 BF3  +  3 H2O  -->  3 H3O+  +  3 [BF4]-  +  B(OH)3

      AlCl3 hydrolyses too, giving an acidic solution:

          AlCl3  +  3 H2O   -->   Al(OH)3  +  3 HCl

      (This is why an aqueous solution of AlCl3 turns blue litmus red,
       and why ANHYDROUS AlCl3 cannot be made by evaporating the
       aqueous solution.)
```

# TOPIC 11 — BORAX, Na2B4O7.10H2O

## 11.1 What it is

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   BORAX  =  SODIUM TETRABORATE DECAHYDRATE                          |
  |                                                                     |
  |   Simple formula   :   Na2B4O7 . 10 H2O                             |
  |   Real formula     :   Na2 [B4O5(OH)4] . 8 H2O                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   Because FOUR of the ten "water" molecules are actually present    |
  |   as -OH groups bonded to boron. Only EIGHT are true water of       |
  |   crystallisation.                                                  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   It is the most important compound of boron and the commonest      |
  |   ore. Found naturally as TINCAL in dried-up lake beds.             |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 11.2 THE STRUCTURE OF THE BORAX ANION — draw this

```
                    STRUCTURE OF  [B4O5(OH)4]2-
                                                                    2-
                              O H
                               |
                               B                 <--  sp2 BORON
                             /   \                    TRIGONAL PLANAR
                            /     \                   bonded to 3 O
                           O       O
                          /         \
              H O ---- B             B ---- O H  <--  sp3 BORONS
                          \         /                 TETRAHEDRAL
                           \       /                  bonded to 4 O
                            O     O                   these two carry
                             \   /                    the 2- CHARGE
                               B                 <--  sp2 BORON
                               |                      TRIGONAL PLANAR
                              O H

              PLUS ONE CENTRAL BRIDGING OXYGEN joining the two
              tetrahedral (sp3) borons across the middle:

                          B(sp3) --- O --- B(sp3)
```

```
  THE FACTS TO WRITE UNDER THE DIAGRAM (each is a mark):

    *  The anion is  [B4O5(OH)4]2-  and contains FOUR boron atoms.

    *  TWO borons are sp2 hybridised, TRIGONAL PLANAR, bonded to
       THREE oxygens each  ->  BO3 units.

    *  TWO borons are sp3 hybridised, TETRAHEDRAL, bonded to
       FOUR oxygens each   ->  BO4 units.

    *  The units are joined by SHARED (bridging) OXYGEN atoms.

    *  Each boron also carries ONE terminal -OH group (4 in all).

    *  The two negative charges sit on the two TETRAHEDRAL borons and
       are balanced by the two Na+ ions.

    *  The remaining EIGHT water molecules are water of crystallisation.
```

## 11.3 Preparation of borax

```
   (a)  FROM COLEMANITE (the standard industrial route):

        Finely powdered colemanite is boiled with sodium carbonate
        solution:

           Ca2B6O11  +  2 Na2CO3   -->   2 CaCO3  +  Na2B4O7  +  2 NaBO2

        The calcium carbonate is filtered off; on concentrating, borax
        crystallises. The sodium metaborate left in the mother liquor
        is converted to borax by passing CO2 through it:

           4 NaBO2  +  CO2   -->   Na2B4O7  +  Na2CO3


   (b)  FROM BORIC ACID:

           4 H3BO3  +  Na2CO3   -->   Na2B4O7  +  CO2  +  6 H2O


   (c)  FROM TINCAL (native borax): simply dissolve in hot water,
        filter off the mud and recrystallise.
```

## 11.4 Physical and chemical properties

```
  1.  A WHITE CRYSTALLINE SOLID, sparingly soluble in cold water but
      freely soluble in HOT water.

  2.  ITS AQUEOUS SOLUTION IS ALKALINE.
      Borax is the salt of a STRONG base (NaOH) and a WEAK acid
      (H3BO3), so it HYDROLYSES:

           Na2B4O7  +  7 H2O   -->   2 NaOH  +  4 H3BO3
                                     strong      weak
                                     base        acid

      The excess of NaOH makes the solution BASIC - it turns red
      litmus blue and it is used as a BUFFER and as a cleaning agent.

  3.  IT REACTS WITH ACIDS to give boric acid - the standard
      laboratory preparation of H3BO3:

           Na2B4O7  +  2 HCl  +  5 H2O   -->   2 NaCl   +  4 H3BO3
           Na2B4O7  +  H2SO4  +  5 H2O   -->   Na2SO4   +  4 H3BO3
```

## 11.5 ACTION OF HEAT ON BORAX — three stages

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   STAGE 1  (around 373 K)                                           |
  |       Borax loses its water of crystallisation and SWELLS UP into   |
  |       a light, white, PUFFY / POROUS mass:                          |
  |                                                                     |
  |          Na2B4O7 . 10 H2O   --(heat)-->   Na2B4O7  +  10 H2O        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   STAGE 2  (higher temperature)                                     |
  |       The puffy anhydrous mass MELTS to a clear liquid.             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   STAGE 3  (red heat, about 1000 K)                                 |
  |       The melt solidifies on cooling into a TRANSPARENT GLASSY      |
  |       BEAD which is a mixture of SODIUM METABORATE and BORIC        |
  |       ANHYDRIDE:                                                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |          Na2B4O7   --(red heat)-->   2 NaBO2   +   B2O3             |
  |                                     sodium       boric              |
  |                                     metaborate   anhydride          |
  |                                                                     |
  |                    T H A T   I S   T H E   B E A D                  |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 11.6 THE BORAX BEAD TEST

```
  HOW IT IS DONE (write these five steps)

    1.  Make a small loop at the end of a clean PLATINUM WIRE.
    2.  Heat the loop red hot, dip it in powdered borax and heat again
        until a clear, colourless, TRANSPARENT GLASSY BEAD of
        NaBO2 + B2O3 is formed.
    3.  Touch the hot bead to a trace of the coloured metal salt to be
        tested.
    4.  Heat the bead again in the OXIDISING FLAME (the outer, blue,
        non-luminous part) and note the colour hot and cold.
    5.  Repeat in the REDUCING FLAME (the inner, luminous part) and
        note the colour again.

  THE CHEMISTRY (this is the equation the examiner wants)

        B2O3  +  CuO   --(heat)-->   Cu(BO2)2
                                     copper metaborate - BLUE-GREEN

        B2O3  +  CoO   --(heat)-->   Co(BO2)2
                                     cobalt metaborate - DEEP BLUE

    or, starting from the sulphate:

        CuSO4  +  B2O3  --(heat)-->  Cu(BO2)2  +  SO3

  THE PRINCIPLE IN ONE SENTENCE:
        BORIC ANHYDRIDE B2O3 IN THE HOT BEAD REACTS WITH THE METAL
        OXIDE TO GIVE A METAL METABORATE M(BO2)n THAT HAS A
        CHARACTERISTIC COLOUR, AND SO IDENTIFIES THE METAL.
```

**THE COLOUR TABLE — memorise the top four**

| METAL ION | COLOUR IN THE OXIDISING FLAME | COLOUR IN THE REDUCING FLAME |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper, Cu(II)** | **blue-green** (green hot, blue when cold) | **reddish-brown, opaque** (copper) |
| **Cobalt, Co(II)** | **deep blue** | **deep blue** (blue in both) |
| **Chromium, Cr(III)** | **green** | **green** (green in both) |
| **Iron, Fe(III)** | **yellowish-brown** | **bottle green / pale green** |
| Manganese, Mn(II) | violet / amethyst (purple) | colourless |
| Nickel, Ni(II) | brown | grey / colourless |

```
  MEMORY LINE:   "COBALT and CHROMIUM don't change - blue and green
                  in BOTH flames. COPPER and IRON do change."

  WHY A PLATINUM WIRE?  Because platinum is INERT - it does not react
  with the hot bead and it does not add a colour of its own. It also
  has a very high melting point.
```

## 11.7 Uses of borax

```
   1.  In the BORAX BEAD TEST, to identify coloured metal ions in
       qualitative analysis.
   2.  As a FLUX in soldering, brazing and welding - the molten borax
       dissolves away the metal oxide film and gives a clean surface.
   3.  In making BOROSILICATE (Pyrex) glass, which resists heat and
       sudden temperature changes; also optical and hard glasses.
   4.  In making GLAZES and ENAMELS for pottery and tiles.
   5.  As a WATER SOFTENER and a component of washing powders.
   6.  As a mild ANTISEPTIC and PRESERVATIVE.
   7.  To stiffen candle wicks and in the leather and paper industries.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 10**

> *Q. What happens when borax is heated strongly? Explain the borax bead test
> with equations. Give the colours obtained with copper and cobalt salts.*

```
  STEP 1 : Heating, stage by stage.
              Na2B4O7.10H2O --(heat)--> Na2B4O7 + 10 H2O
              (it swells into a white puffy mass)
              Na2B4O7 --(red heat)--> 2 NaBO2 + B2O3
              (this melt cools to a TRANSPARENT GLASSY BEAD)

  STEP 2 : The bead is a mixture of SODIUM METABORATE and BORIC
           ANHYDRIDE.

  STEP 3 : Touch the hot bead to a coloured metal salt and heat again.
           The B2O3 reacts with the metal oxide to form a coloured
           METAL METABORATE:
              CuO + B2O3 --(heat)--> Cu(BO2)2      BLUE-GREEN
              CoO + B2O3 --(heat)--> Co(BO2)2      DEEP BLUE

  STEP 4 : The colour identifies the metal.
              Copper : blue-green in the oxidising flame,
                       reddish-brown and opaque in the reducing flame.
              Cobalt : deep blue in BOTH flames.

  STEP 5 : Note the limitation. The test works only for COLOURED
           (transition) metal ions - it is useless for Na+, K+, Ca2+.
```

> **TRAP:** The bead is **NaBO2 + B2O3**, not "Na2B4O7". And the coloured
> product is a **METABORATE**, `M(BO2)n`, not a borate `M3(BO3)n`.

# TOPIC 12 — ORTHOBORIC ACID, H3BO3

## 12.1 What it is

```
      ORTHOBORIC ACID  =  BORIC ACID  =  H3BO3  =  B(OH)3

      A WHITE, SOFT, SHINY, needle-like crystalline solid.
      SOAPY / SLIPPERY TO THE TOUCH.
      Sparingly soluble in COLD water, much more soluble in HOT water.
      Occurs naturally as SASSOLITE, and in the hot springs of Tuscany.
```

## 12.2 Preparation

```
   (a)  FROM BORAX (the laboratory method - learn this one):

           Na2B4O7  +  2 HCl  +  5 H2O   -->   2 NaCl  +  4 H3BO3
           Na2B4O7  +  H2SO4 +  5 H2O   -->   Na2SO4  +  4 H3BO3

        On cooling the hot solution, H3BO3 (which is much less soluble
        in cold water) crystallises out.

   (b)  FROM COLEMANITE (industrial): pass SO2 through a hot
        suspension of powdered colemanite:

           Ca2B6O11  +  4 SO2  +  11 H2O  -->  2 Ca(HSO3)2  +  6 H3BO3

   (c)  BY HYDROLYSIS of boron compounds:

           BCl3  +  3 H2O   -->   H3BO3  +  3 HCl
           B2H6  +  6 H2O   -->   2 H3BO3  +  6 H2
           BN    +  3 H2O   -->   H3BO3  +  NH3
```

## 12.3 THE STRUCTURE — planar sheets held by hydrogen bonds

```
     ONE MOLECULE:  a PLANAR, TRIGONAL BO3 unit,  sp2, 120 degrees

                             H
                             |
                             O
                             |
                             B
                           /   \
                          O     O
                          |     |
                          H     H
```

```
     THE LAYER:  molecules joined into a flat, two-dimensional sheet
                 by HYDROGEN BONDS (shown as  ....  )

        H            H            H            H
         \            \            \            \
          O            O            O            O
          |            |            |            |
          B            B            B            B
        /   \        /   \        /   \        /   \
       O     O      O     O      O     O      O     O
       |     |      |     |      |     |      |     |
       H     H      H     H      H     H      H     H
       :     :      :     :      :     :      :     :        <- HYDROGEN
       :     :      :     :      :     :      :     :           BONDS
       O     O      O     O      O     O      O     O
       |     |      |     |      |     |      |     |
        \   /        \   /        \   /        \   /
          B            B            B            B
          |            |            |            |
          O            O            O            O
         /            /            /            /
        H            H            H            H

     Every -OH group hydrogen bonds to the oxygen of a neighbour, so
     each layer builds up as a two-dimensional network of six-membered
     rings, rather like a sheet of chicken wire.
```

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   THE THREE POINTS TO WRITE UNDER THE DIAGRAM:                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   1.  Each boron is sp2 hybridised and TRIGONAL PLANAR, bonded to   |
  |       three -OH groups at 120 degrees.                              |
  |                                                                     |
  |   2.  The BO3 units are linked into flat, two-dimensional LAYERS    |
  |       by HYDROGEN BONDS between the -OH groups.                     |
  |                                                                     |
  |   3.  The LAYERS are held to one another only by WEAK VAN DER       |
  |       WAALS FORCES, so they SLIDE over each other.                  |
  |       *** THAT IS WHY BORIC ACID FEELS SOAPY AND SLIPPERY AND       |
  |           CAN BE USED AS A LUBRICANT ***                            |
  |       (Exactly the same argument as for graphite.)                  |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 12.4 WHY IT IS A WEAK MONOBASIC ACID — the classic 4-marker

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   H3BO3 has THREE hydrogen atoms - so you would expect it to be     |
  |   TRIBASIC. It is NOT. It is MONOBASIC. And it is very WEAK         |
  |   (Ka is only about 5.8 x 10^-10).                                  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   WHY?                                                              |
  |                                                                     |
  |   BECAUSE IT DOES NOT DONATE ITS OWN PROTONS AT ALL.                |
  |   It acts as a LEWIS ACID: it ACCEPTS A HYDROXIDE ION FROM WATER,   |
  |   using the empty 2p orbital on boron, and this SETS FREE one       |
  |   H3O+ ion:                                                         |
  |                                                                     |
  |       B(OH)3   +   2 H2O    -->    [B(OH)4]-    +    H3O+           |
  |                                    tetrahydroxo-                    |
  |                                    borate(III)                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ONE molecule of B(OH)3 releases exactly ONE H3O+ ion, so it is    |
  |   MONOBASIC.                                                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   It is WEAK because it depends on this indirect route and only a   |
  |   tiny fraction of the molecules react.                             |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
      THE PICTURE

                OH                                          OH        -
                |                                           |
         HO --- B          +   :OH- (taken from water)  -->  HO -- B -- OH
                |                                           |
                OH                                          OH

         boron with only 6 electrons                boron now has 8
         and one EMPTY 2p orbital                   (a full octet)
         = LEWIS ACID                               TETRAHEDRAL, sp3

         and the H+ left over from the water becomes H3O+
```

```
  A USEFUL EXTRA (worth an extra mark):

      Boric acid is such a weak acid that it CANNOT be titrated
      directly against NaOH with an ordinary indicator.
      Adding a POLYHYDROXY compound such as GLYCEROL, MANNITOL or
      CIS-DIOL makes it a much STRONGER acid (the diol chelates the
      boron and stabilises the [B(OH)4]- form), and it can then be
      titrated sharply against NaOH with phenolphthalein.
```

```

   2.  As a FOOD PRESERVATIVE (milk, butter, tinned foods) - in tiny,

   3.  In making BOROSILICATE (Pyrex) glass, glazes and ENAMELS for
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 11**

> *Q. Boric acid contains three hydrogen atoms yet it is monobasic. Explain.
> What happens when it is heated?*

```
  STEP 1 : State what it does NOT do.
              H3BO3 does NOT ionise by giving away its own H atoms.
              The O-H bonds in B(OH)3 are not acidic in the ordinary
              sense.

  STEP 2 : Say what it DOES do.
              Boron in B(OH)3 has only SIX electrons around it and one
              EMPTY 2p orbital. It therefore acts as a LEWIS ACID and
              ACCEPTS a hydroxide ion from a water molecule:

                  B(OH)3  +  2 H2O  -->  [B(OH)4]-  +  H3O+

  STEP 3 : Count the protons released.
              Each molecule of boric acid sets free exactly ONE H3O+.
              Therefore it is MONOBASIC.
              Because only a small fraction reacts, it is a WEAK acid
              (Ka about 5.8 x 10^-10).

  STEP 4 : Action of heat.
                  H3BO3   --(370 K)-->   HBO2    + H2O   metaboric acid
                  4 HBO2  --(430 K)-->   H2B4O7  + H2O   tetraboric acid
                  H2B4O7  --(red heat)--> 2 B2O3 + H2O   boric anhydride
```

> **TRAP:** Do not write "H3BO3 is monobasic because only one of its three
> hydrogens is ionisable". None of them ionise. The acidity comes from
> **accepting OH- from water**, and that is what the examiner wants to see.

# TOPIC 13 — DIBORANE, B2H6, AND THE BORANES

## 13.1 What boranes are

```
      The hydrides of boron are called BORANES.
      Boron does NOT form a simple BH3 - it always makes an
      ELECTRON-DEFICIENT, bridged, cluster hydride instead.

      TWO SERIES:
          NIDO-boranes     Bn H(n+4)     B2H6 , B5H9 , B6H10
          ARACHNO-boranes  Bn H(n+6)     B4H10 , B5H11 , B9H15

      DIBORANE B2H6 is the simplest and the most important.
```

## 13.2 Preparation of diborane

```
   (a)  LABORATORY - oxidation of sodium borohydride with iodine:

           2 NaBH4   +   I2    -->   B2H6   +   2 NaI   +   H2

   (b)  From boron trifluoride and lithium aluminium hydride:

           4 BF3   +   3 LiAlH4   -->   2 B2H6   +   3 LiAlF4

   (c)  From boron trichloride:

           4 BCl3  +  3 LiAlH4  -->  2 B2H6  +  3 LiCl  +  3 AlCl3

   (d)  INDUSTRIAL - boron trifluoride with sodium hydride at 450 K:

           2 BF3   +   6 NaH   --(450 K)-->   B2H6   +   6 NaF
```

## 13.3 Physical properties

```
      *  COLOURLESS gas at room temperature.
      *  Boiling point 180 K - it condenses easily.
      *  HIGHLY TOXIC.
      *  Has a repulsive, sickly sweet smell.
      *  CATCHES FIRE SPONTANEOUSLY in air.
```

## 13.4 THE STRUCTURE — BANANA BONDS (an 8-mark favourite)

**First, the electron count that creates the problem:**

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   ELECTRONS AVAILABLE IN B2H6                                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |       2 boron atoms   x 3 valence electrons  =  6                   |
  |       6 hydrogen atoms x 1 valence electron  =  6                   |
  |                                                 ---                 |
  |       TOTAL AVAILABLE                        = 12 electrons         |
  |                                                                     |
  |   ELECTRONS NEEDED for a normal structure                           |
  |                                                                     |
  |       6 B-H bonds  +  1 B-B bond  =  7 bonds                        |
  |       7 bonds x 2 electrons each  = 14 electrons                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |       12 available  <  14 needed  ->  TWO ELECTRONS SHORT           |
  |                                                                     |
  |       B2H6 IS THEREFORE AN ELECTRON-DEFICIENT MOLECULE.             |
  |                                                                     |
  |   NATURE'S SOLUTION: two of the hydrogen atoms are made to BRIDGE   |
  |   the two borons, each bridge holding THREE atoms together with     |
  |   only TWO electrons - a THREE-CENTRE TWO-ELECTRON (3c-2e) BOND,    |
  |   nicknamed a BANANA BOND because of its curved shape.              |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**The structure:**

```
              THE STRUCTURE OF DIBORANE   B2H6


        Ht                                          Ht
          \                                       /
           \                                     /
            \                Hb                 /
             \             /    \              /
              \          /        \           /
               B                             B
              /          \        /           \
             /             \    /              \
            /                Hb                 \
           /                                     \
          /                                       \
        Ht                                          Ht


        Ht  =  TERMINAL hydrogen   (there are FOUR)
        Hb  =  BRIDGING hydrogen   (there are TWO)
```

```
  THE FACTS TO WRITE UNDER THE DIAGRAM (each is worth a mark):

   1.  Each boron atom is sp3 HYBRIDISED.

   2.  The FOUR TERMINAL hydrogens are bound by ordinary
       TWO-CENTRE TWO-ELECTRON (2c-2e) bonds.
       B-Ht bond length = 119 pm.

   3.  The TWO BRIDGING hydrogens are bound by
       THREE-CENTRE TWO-ELECTRON (3c-2e) BANANA BONDS.
       Each such bond spreads ONE pair of electrons over
       THREE atoms:  B ... H ... B .
       B-Hb bond length = 133 pm - LONGER and WEAKER than B-Ht.

   4.  GEOMETRY: the two boron atoms and the FOUR TERMINAL hydrogens
       all lie in ONE PLANE. The TWO BRIDGING hydrogens lie in a
       plane at RIGHT ANGLES (perpendicular) to it - one above and
       one below.

   5.  BOND ANGLES:  Ht - B - Ht  =  121.5 degrees
                     Hb - B - Hb  =  97 degrees

   6.  B2H6 is ELECTRON DEFICIENT: 12 valence electrons available
       against the 14 needed for seven normal bonds.
```

**A side view that makes the geometry obvious:**

```
       LOOKING ALONG THE B ... B AXIS

                      Hb        <- ABOVE the plane
                       |
                       |
        Ht ---- B ----(:)---- B ---- Ht      <- THE PLANE
        Ht ----'               `---- Ht         (2 B + 4 terminal H)
                       |
                       |
                      Hb        <- BELOW the plane

       The two bridge hydrogens sit in a plane PERPENDICULAR to the
       plane of the four terminal hydrogens.
```

**What a 3c-2e bond actually looks like:**

```
                       H
                      / \                   ONE PAIR of electrons,
                     /   \                  spread over THREE atoms.
                    /     \                 The bonding region is
                   B       B                CURVED - hence "BANANA".

        Compare a NORMAL 2c-2e bond:

                   B ----- H                ONE PAIR of electrons,
                                            shared between TWO atoms.
```

# TOPIC 14 — ALUMINIUM CHLORIDE AND THE DIMER Al2Cl6

## 14.1 Preparation of anhydrous AlCl3

```
      (a)  Pass DRY chlorine over heated aluminium:

               2 Al   +   3 Cl2   --(heat)-->   Al2Cl6

      (b)  Pass dry chlorine over a heated mixture of alumina and coke:

               Al2O3  +  3 C  +  3 Cl2  --(heat)-->  2 AlCl3  +  3 CO

      (c)  Pass DRY HCl gas over heated aluminium:

               2 Al   +   6 HCl   --(heat)-->   2 AlCl3   +   3 H2

  *** WHY YOU CANNOT MAKE IT FROM THE AQUEOUS SOLUTION ***

      Because AlCl3 HYDROLYSES in water:

               AlCl3  +  3 H2O   -->   Al(OH)3  +  3 HCl

      Evaporating the solution just gives Al(OH)3 / Al2O3 and HCl gas.
      Anhydrous AlCl3 must always be made by a DRY method.
```

## 14.2 WHY IT DIMERISES — the 2-mark answer

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   In a single AlCl3 molecule the aluminium atom is surrounded by    |
  |   only SIX electrons - a SEXTET, not an octet. AlCl3 is therefore   |
  |   ELECTRON DEFICIENT and is a strong LEWIS ACID.                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |   To complete its octet, aluminium accepts a LONE PAIR from a       |
  |   CHLORINE ATOM OF A SECOND AlCl3 MOLECULE.                         |
  |                                                                     |
  |   Two AlCl3 molecules therefore join through TWO CHLORINE BRIDGES,  |
  |   each bridging chlorine donating a lone pair by a COORDINATE       |
  |   bond. Both aluminium atoms now have EIGHT electrons.              |
  |                                                                     |
  |            2 AlCl3    ==    Al2Cl6                                  |
  |                                                                     |
  |   The dimer exists in the VAPOUR at moderate temperature and in     |
  |   NON-POLAR SOLVENTS such as benzene. Above about 1073 K it         |
  |   dissociates back into planar AlCl3 monomers.                      |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 14.3 The structure

```
                      STRUCTURE OF  Al2Cl6


          Cl                    Cl                    Cl
             \                /    \                /
               \            /        \            /
                 \        /            \        /
                   Al                    Al
                 /        \            /        \
               /            \        /            \
             /                \    /                \
          Cl                    Cl                    Cl


       TERMINAL chlorines : FOUR  (two on each Al)
       BRIDGING chlorines : TWO   (they hold the two halves together)

       Each Al is sp3 HYBRIDISED and TETRAHEDRAL.
       The two tetrahedra share an EDGE (the two bridging Cl atoms).

       BOND LENGTHS:   Al - Cl (terminal)  =  202 pm
                       Al - Cl (bridging)  =  221 pm   (LONGER, WEAKER)
```

```
  +----------------+---------------------------+----------------------+
  |                |  B2H6                     |  Al2Cl6              |
  +----------------+---------------------------+----------------------+
  | Bridging atom  |  HYDROGEN                 |  CHLORINE            |
  | Type of bridge |  3-CENTRE 2-ELECTRON      |  ordinary COORDINATE |
  |                |  (BANANA) bond            |  (dative) bond       |
  | Why?           |  H has NO lone pair to    |  Cl HAS lone pairs   |
  |                |  donate, so the pair must |  and can donate one  |
  |                |  be shared over 3 atoms   |  normally            |
  | Hybridisation  |  sp3 on each B            |  sp3 on each Al      |
  | Electron count |  DEFICIENT even as a dimer|  octet COMPLETE in   |

  +----------------+---------------------------+----------------------+
```

## 14.4 Properties and uses of AlCl3

```
      *  A white solid when pure (usually yellowish from FeCl3
         impurity), which SUBLIMES on heating.

      *  It FUMES in moist air, because of hydrolysis:
             AlCl3  +  3 H2O   -->   Al(OH)3  +  3 HCl

      *  Its aqueous solution is ACIDIC (turns blue litmus red) for the
         same reason.

      *  It is a powerful LEWIS ACID, and this is its great use:
         *** ANHYDROUS AlCl3 IS THE CATALYST FOR FRIEDEL-CRAFTS
             ALKYLATION AND ACYLATION ***

             C6H6  +  CH3Cl   --(anhyd. AlCl3)-->   C6H5CH3  +  HCl
             C6H6  +  CH3COCl --(anhyd. AlCl3)-->   C6H5COCH3 + HCl

      *  Also used in cracking petroleum and in making many organic
         compounds.
```

> **TRAP:** `AlF3` is **ionic** and has a high melting point; it does **not**
> dimerise, because fluorine is too small to bridge and the Al-F bond is
> strongly ionic. Only the chloride, bromide and iodide form the `Al2X6`
> dimer. Do not write "all aluminium halides are dimers".

# TOPIC 15 — ALUMS

## 15.1 What an alum is

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   DEFINITION: an ALUM is a DOUBLE SALT made of the sulphate of a    |
  |   MONOVALENT cation and the sulphate of a TRIVALENT cation,         |
  |   crystallising together with 24 molecules of water.                |
  |                                                                     |
  |   GENERAL FORMULA                                                   |
  |                                                                     |
  |       M2SO4 . M'2(SO4)3 . 24 H2O                                    |
  |                                                                     |
  |   or, equivalently                                                  |
  |                                                                     |
  |       M+ M'3+ (SO4)2 . 12 H2O                                       |
  |                                                                     |
  |   where   M   = a MONOVALENT cation : Na+, K+, NH4+                 |
  |           M'  = a TRIVALENT cation  : Al3+, Cr3+, Fe3+             |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 15.2 The four you must know

| ALUM | FORMULA | THE TRIVALENT ION |
|---|---|---|
| **Potash alum** (the ordinary "alum", phitkari) | `K2SO4 . Al2(SO4)3 . 24 H2O` = `KAl(SO4)2 . 12 H2O` | Al3+ |
| **Chrome alum** | `K2SO4 . Cr2(SO4)3 . 24 H2O` = `KCr(SO4)2 . 12 H2O` | Cr3+ |
| **Ferric alum** | `(NH4)2SO4 . Fe2(SO4)3 . 24 H2O` = `NH4Fe(SO4)2 . 12 H2O` | Fe3+ |
| **Ammonium alum** | `(NH4)2SO4 . Al2(SO4)3 . 24 H2O` | Al3+ |

## 15.3 Why alums are DOUBLE salts, not complex salts

```
      A DOUBLE SALT dissolves in water to give ALL the ions of BOTH
      its parent salts. Alums do exactly this:

          KAl(SO4)2 . 12 H2O   ---(water)--->   K+  +  Al3+  +  2 SO4 2-

      A COMPLEX SALT such as K4[Fe(CN)6] does NOT release its
      component ions - it gives the intact complex ion [Fe(CN)6]4-.

      SO: alums are DOUBLE SALTS. They exist only in the SOLID state
      as a single crystal; in solution they are just a mixture.
```

## 15.4 Uses of alums

```
   1.  PURIFICATION OF WATER. The Al3+ ion hydrolyses to gelatinous
       Al(OH)3, which COAGULATES the negatively charged colloidal
       clay and dirt particles and drags them down as a sludge.

   2.  As a MORDANT in dyeing. The Al(OH)3 formed on the cloth fixes
       the dye so that it does not wash out.

   3.  In TANNING of leather.

   4.  As a STYPTIC - a lump of potash alum rubbed on a shaving cut
       stops the bleeding, because it coagulates the blood proteins.

   5.  In SIZING PAPER, in making lakes and pigments, and in
       fire-proofing fabrics.

   6.  In baking powders and as a food additive (in tiny amounts).
```

# TOPIC 16 — USES OF BORON AND ALUMINIUM

## 16.1 Uses of boron and its compounds

```
   1.  *** NUCLEAR REACTORS. The 10B isotope is an excellent NEUTRON
       ABSORBER, so boron steel and boron carbide are used to make
       the CONTROL RODS that regulate a nuclear reactor. ***

   2.  BORON FIBRES are extremely strong and very light, so they are
       used in BULLET-PROOF VESTS and in light composite materials
       for aircraft and sports goods.

   3.  AMORPHOUS BORON is used in rocket-fuel igniters and in
       pyrotechnics (it burns with a green flame).

   4.  BORON CARBIDE (B4C) and BORON NITRIDE (BN, especially the
       cubic form BORAZON) are extremely hard ABRASIVES and cutting
       materials. BN is also a high-temperature lubricant.

   5.  Small amounts of boron are added to STEEL and to COPPER alloys
       as a HARDENING agent and a de-gasser.

   6.  BORAX and BORIC ACID are used to make heat-resistant
       BOROSILICATE (Pyrex) GLASS, glazes and enamels.

   7.  Boric acid is a mild ANTISEPTIC; borax is used in soaps,
       detergents and water softeners.

   8.  Boron is an ESSENTIAL MICRONUTRIENT for plants.
```

## 16.2 Uses of aluminium and its compounds

```
   1.  ELECTRICAL TRANSMISSION LINES. Aluminium is a good conductor
       and it is very LIGHT; weight for weight it conducts about
       TWICE as well as copper, and it is far cheaper.

   2.  UTENSILS AND COOKWARE - light, non-toxic and a good conductor
       of heat.

   3.  ALUMINIUM FOIL for wrapping food, chocolates and medicines.

   4.  ALLOYS:
           DURALUMIN  (Al + Cu + Mg + Mn) - light and strong,
                       used for AIRCRAFT bodies
           MAGNALIUM  (Al + Mg)
           ALNICO     (Al + Ni + Co + Fe) - permanent magnets

   5.  *** THERMITE WELDING / ALUMINOTHERMY. ***
       Aluminium powder is a powerful reducing agent and the reaction
       is enormously exothermic:

           2 Al  +  Fe2O3   -->   Al2O3  +  2 Fe   +   heat

       The molten iron produced is used to weld cracked rails and
       machine parts on the spot. Aluminium is also used to extract
       chromium and manganese from their oxides:

           2 Al  +  Cr2O3   -->   Al2O3  +  2 Cr

   6.  ALUMINIUM PAINTS. Aluminium powder mixed with oil gives the
       silvery paint used on radiators, tanks and railings.

   7.  ANODISED aluminium for window frames, doors and decorative
       fittings.

   8.  Al2O3 (alumina) is used as an ABRASIVE, as a catalyst support
       and as the packing in chromatography columns; corundum and
       emery are natural forms.

   9.  Aluminium foil-lined packaging, aluminium bronze coins, and
       aluminium sulphate / alums in water purification.
```

**WORKED EXAMPLE 13**

> *Q. Write the reaction of aluminothermy and give two uses of it. Why is
> aluminium preferred to copper for long-distance power lines?*

```
  STEP 1 : The reaction.
              2 Al  +  Fe2O3   -->   Al2O3  +  2 Fe  ,  hugely exothermic
              (aluminium is a powerful reducing agent because Al2O3 is
               very stable, so its formation releases a lot of energy)

  STEP 2 : Uses of the thermite reaction.
              (i)  THERMITE WELDING of cracked railway lines and heavy
                   machine parts - the molten iron produced fills and
                   welds the crack on the spot.
              (ii) EXTRACTION of metals such as chromium and manganese
                   from their oxides:  2 Al + Cr2O3 --> Al2O3 + 2 Cr .

              Copper is the better conductor per unit VOLUME, but
              aluminium is only about ONE THIRD as dense. Weight for
              weight, an aluminium cable conducts about TWICE as well
              as a copper one, it is much cheaper, and it puts far
              less load on the pylons. So overhead transmission lines
              are aluminium (usually reinforced with a steel core).
```
