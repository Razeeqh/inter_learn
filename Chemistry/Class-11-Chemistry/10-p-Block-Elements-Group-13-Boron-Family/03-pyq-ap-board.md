```
  GENERAL CONFIGURATION  =  ns2 np1

      B   Boron       -   NON-METAL   [He] 2s2 2p1
```

## Topic: Trends and their exceptions

### Q A4
The atomic radius of gallium is less than that of aluminium. Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Al = 143 pm  ,  Ga = 135 pm

  Gallium is the first group-13 element to come AFTER the 3d series,
  so it carries TEN 3d ELECTRONS which aluminium does not have.

      Al   [Ne] 3s2 3p1
      Ga   [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p1

  d ORBITALS ARE DIFFUSE AND SHIELD THE NUCLEAR CHARGE VERY POORLY.
  The 4s and 4p electrons of gallium therefore feel a much larger
  EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR CHARGE and are pulled in towards the nucleus.

  This contraction (the D-BLOCK CONTRACTION) more than cancels the
  increase due to the extra shell, so Ga ends up SMALLER than Al.
```

### Q A5
Ionisation enthalpy decreases down a group, yet in group 13 the decrease is
not regular. Explain.

**ANSWER:**
```
  IE1 / kJ mol-1 :  B 801 , Al 577 , Ga 579 , In 558 , Tl 589

  ORDER :  B  >  Tl  >  Ga  >  Al  >  In

  1.  B -> Al : a LARGE FALL, as expected (much bigger atom).

  2.  Al -> Ga : a small RISE. The ten 3d electrons of gallium shield
      the nucleus POORLY, so gallium's outer electron is held MORE
      tightly than aluminium's.

  3.  In -> Tl : a RISE again. The fourteen 4f and ten 5d electrons of
      thallium shield even more poorly (lanthanoid contraction).

  ONE-LINE REASON: POOR SHIELDING BY THE INTERVENING d AND f ELECTRONS.
```

### Q A6
Why does gallium have an abnormally low melting point?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Melting point of gallium = 303 K (about 30 degC) - it MELTS IN THE
  PALM OF YOUR HAND.

  REASON: in the SOLID state gallium does not form a normal metallic
  lattice of separate atoms. It exists as DISCRETE Ga2 DIATOMIC
  MOLECULES, which are held to one another only by WEAK
  INTERMOLECULAR FORCES.

  Melting has to break only those weak forces, so very little energy
  is needed.

  (Its boiling point is 2676 K, because boiling must break the STRONG
   Ga-Ga covalent bond inside each Ga2 molecule. Gallium therefore has
   the WIDEST LIQUID RANGE of any element and is used in
   HIGH-TEMPERATURE THERMOMETERS.)
```

### Q A7
How does electronegativity vary down group 13?

**ANSWER:**
```
  B 2.0  ->  Al 1.5  ->  Ga 1.6  ->  In 1.7  ->  Tl 1.8

  The variation is IRREGULAR:
      it first DECREASES sharply from B to Al,
      then INCREASES slightly from Al to Tl.

  REASONS:
      B -> Al  : boron is very small (85 pm) so it pulls the shared
                 electron pair very strongly; aluminium is much
                 bigger and pulls far less.
      Al -> Tl : the poor shielding by the intervening d and f
                 electrons raises the effective nuclear charge again.
```

## Topic: Oxidation states and the inert pair effect

### Q A8
What is the inert pair effect?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE INERT PAIR EFFECT is the RELUCTANCE OF THE ns2 ELECTRON PAIR TO
  TAKE PART IN BOND FORMATION, which increases as we go DOWN a
  p-block group.

  CAUSE: the filled d (3d10, 4d10, 5d10) and f (4f14) subshells shield
  the nuclear charge POORLY, so the ns2 pair is held very tightly and
  cannot be promoted to a p orbital.

  RESULT in group 13: the +1 oxidation state becomes MORE stable and
  the +3 state LESS stable as we go down.
      Stability of +1 :  Al < Ga < In < Tl
  Tl(I) is the stable state of thallium.
```

### Q A9
Why is thallium(I) more stable than thallium(III)?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Thallium is  [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p1 .

  Because of the INERT PAIR EFFECT, the 6s2 pair is gripped very
  tightly by a nucleus that is badly shielded by the 4f14 and 5d10
  electrons. Promoting a 6s electron costs more energy than the two
  extra long, weak Tl-X bonds give back.

  So thallium uses only its single 6p1 electron and shows +1.

  CONSEQUENCE: Tl(III) compounds are STRONG OXIDISING AGENTS and
  break down to Tl(I) on heating:
        TlCl3   --(heat)-->   TlCl  +  Cl2
```

### Q A10
Why does TlI3 not exist as thallium(III) iodide?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because Tl3+ is a very strong OXIDISING agent (the +3 state of
  thallium is unstable) and I- is a good REDUCING agent.

  If Tl3+ and I- were put together, Tl3+ would immediately oxidise
  I- to I2 and be reduced to Tl+.

  The compound that actually exists is THALLIUM(I) TRI-IODIDE:

        TlI3   is really   Tl+ (I3)-

  (Compare PbI4 in group 14, which does not exist for the same reason.)
```

### Q A11
Why does boron show only the +3 oxidation state?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Boron has the configuration [He] 2s2 2p1 - three valence electrons.

  1.  It has NO intervening d or f electrons, so there is no poor
      shielding and NO INERT PAIR EFFECT. The 2s2 pair is normally
      available for bonding, so boron always uses all THREE electrons
      and shows +3.

  2.  It never forms B3+ either, because
          IE1 + IE2 + IE3 = 801 + 2427 + 3659 = 6887 kJ mol-1
      which is far too large to be repaid by lattice or hydration
      energy. All boron compounds are therefore COVALENT.
```

## Topic: Electron deficiency and Lewis acids

### Q A12
Why is BF3 called an electron-deficient compound? Why is it a Lewis acid?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRON COUNT AROUND BORON IN BF3
      boron's own valence electrons          =  3
      one electron from each of 3 fluorines  =  3
                                               ---
      total around boron                     =  6

  Boron has only SIX electrons - a SEXTET, not an octet - and ONE
  COMPLETELY EMPTY 2p ORBITAL. It is therefore ELECTRON DEFICIENT.

  To complete its octet it ACCEPTS A LONE PAIR of electrons. A species
  that accepts a lone pair is a LEWIS ACID.

        BF3  +  NH3   -->   F3B <- NH3
        BF3  +  F-    -->   [BF4]-
```

### Q A13
Arrange BF3, BCl3, BBr3 and BI3 in the increasing order of Lewis acid
strength.

**ANSWER:**
```
        BF3   <   BCl3   <   BBr3   <   BI3

  REASON: p(pi) - p(pi) BACK BONDING.

  A filled p orbital of the halogen donates its lone pair sideways
  into boron's EMPTY 2p orbital, which partly relieves the electron
  deficiency.

  Fluorine uses a 2p orbital - the SAME SIZE as boron's 2p - so the
  overlap is BEST, back bonding is STRONGEST, and BF3 is the WEAKEST
  Lewis acid.
  Iodine uses a 5p orbital, far too large for good overlap, so there
  is almost NO back bonding in BI3, boron stays fully deficient, and
  BI3 is the STRONGEST Lewis acid.
```

### Q A14
Why does boron have a maximum covalency of four, while aluminium can show
six?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BORON  : its valence shell is the SECOND shell, which contains only
           ONE 2s and THREE 2p orbitals - FOUR orbitals in all - and
           NO d ORBITALS. It can therefore form at most FOUR bonds.
                 [BF4]-  EXISTS      [BF6]3-  NEVER EXISTS

  ALUMINIUM : its valence shell is the THIRD shell, which has VACANT
           3d ORBITALS. Aluminium can expand its octet and reach a
           covalency of SIX.
                 [AlF6]3-  and  [Al(H2O)6]3+  both exist
```

Show that aluminium is amphoteric, with equations.

**ANSWER:**

### Q A16
Why can concentrated nitric acid be transported in aluminium containers?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Concentrated HNO3 is a powerful OXIDISING AGENT.

  It instantly oxidises the surface of the aluminium to a THICK,
  HARD, CONTINUOUS, IMPERVIOUS layer of Al2O3 which sticks tightly to
  the metal and seals it off from further attack.

  The metal is then said to be PASSIVE, and the reaction stops.
  Concentrated nitric acid can therefore be safely stored and carried
  in aluminium containers.
```

### Q A21
Why is orthoboric acid a monobasic acid?

**ANSWER:**
```
  H3BO3 contains three hydrogen atoms but it is MONOBASIC, because it
  does NOT donate any of its own protons.

  Boron in B(OH)3 has only SIX electrons and one EMPTY 2p orbital, so
  it acts as a LEWIS ACID and ACCEPTS a hydroxide ion from a water
  molecule. This sets free exactly ONE H3O+ ion:

        B(OH)3  +  2 H2O   -->   [B(OH)4]-  +  H3O+

  ONE H3O+ per molecule  ->  MONOBASIC.
  Only a tiny fraction of the molecules react  ->  it is a VERY WEAK
  acid (Ka about 5.8 x 10^-10).
```

### Q A22
Why does boric acid feel soapy to the touch?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In solid H3BO3 the boron atoms are sp2 hybridised and TRIGONAL
  PLANAR. The BO3 units are linked into flat TWO-DIMENSIONAL LAYERS
  by HYDROGEN BONDS between the -OH groups.

  These LAYERS are held to one another only by WEAK VAN DER WAALS
  FORCES, so they SLIDE over one another very easily.

  That sliding of layers makes the solid feel SOAPY and SLIPPERY, and
  is why boric acid can be used as a LUBRICANT.
  (Exactly the same argument as for graphite.)
```

### Q A24
What are banana bonds? In which molecule are they found?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A BANANA BOND is a THREE-CENTRE TWO-ELECTRON (3c-2e) bond, in which
  a SINGLE PAIR of electrons holds THREE atoms together. The electron
  cloud is curved, which is why it is nicknamed a "banana" bond.

  They are found in DIBORANE, B2H6.

  In B2H6 the TWO BRIDGING hydrogen atoms each form a B - H - B
  banana bond. The four TERMINAL hydrogens are held by ordinary
  two-centre two-electron bonds.

  Diborane needs them because it is ELECTRON DEFICIENT: only 12
  valence electrons are available where 14 would be needed for seven
  normal bonds.
```

### Q A25
Why does aluminium chloride exist as a dimer?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In a single AlCl3 molecule the aluminium atom has only SIX
  electrons around it - a SEXTET - so AlCl3 is ELECTRON DEFICIENT.

  To complete its octet, aluminium ACCEPTS A LONE PAIR from a
  chlorine atom of a SECOND AlCl3 molecule through a COORDINATE
  (dative) bond.

  Two such chlorine BRIDGES form, and both aluminium atoms then have
  EIGHT electrons:

        2 AlCl3   ==   Al2Cl6

  The dimer exists in the vapour state and in non-polar solvents such
  as benzene. Each aluminium is sp3 hybridised and tetrahedral.
```

### Q A28
Why is boron used in nuclear reactors?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Boron has two isotopes, 10B (19 %) and 11B (81 %).

  The 10B ISOTOPE IS AN EXCELLENT ABSORBER OF NEUTRONS.

  Boron steel and boron carbide are therefore used to make the
  CONTROL RODS of a nuclear reactor. Pushing the rods in absorbs
  neutrons and slows the chain reaction down; pulling them out speeds
  it up. This is how the reactor is regulated.
```

### Q A29
Which diagonal relationship does boron show, and why do diagonal
relationships arise?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BORON shows a DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP with SILICON  (B <-> Si).
  (The other two pairs are Li-Mg and Be-Al.)

  REASON: going ACROSS a period the size decreases and the
  electronegativity increases; going DOWN a group the size increases
  and the electronegativity decreases. Moving one step ACROSS and one
  step DOWN, the two effects CANCEL.

  So the diagonal pair end up with nearly the same SIZE,
  ELECTRONEGATIVITY and CHARGE-TO-RADIUS RATIO, and behave alike.
        B   85 pm  , EN 2.0
        Si  118 pm , EN 1.8
```

### Q A30
What is boron nitride? Why is it called inorganic graphite?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BORON NITRIDE, BN, is made by heating boron in dinitrogen at high
  temperature:
        2 B  +  N2   --(high temp)-->   2 BN

  It is called INORGANIC GRAPHITE because it has the SAME LAYER

```

### Q B1
Discuss the variation of atomic radius, ionisation enthalpy,
electronegativity and melting point down group 13, giving reasons.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  ATOMIC RADIUS - increases down the group, but IRREGULARLY.
          B 85 , Al 143 , Ga 135 , In 167 , Tl 170  (pm)
      REASON for the increase : a new shell is added each period.
      THE EXCEPTION : Ga (135) is SMALLER than Al (143), because the
      ten 3d electrons of gallium shield the nuclear charge POORLY
      (d-block contraction), so its outer electrons are pulled in.

  2.  IONISATION ENTHALPY - decreases, but NOT smoothly.
          B 801 , Al 577 , Ga 579 , In 558 , Tl 589  kJ/mol
          ORDER :  B > Tl > Ga > Al > In
      REASON for the general fall : bigger atom, outer electron
      farther away and better shielded.
      THE EXCEPTIONS : Ga > Al and Tl > In, because of POOR SHIELDING
      by the 3d10 electrons (Ga) and by the 4f14 and 5d10 electrons
      (Tl).

  3.  ELECTRONEGATIVITY - falls, then rises again.
          B 2.0 , Al 1.5 , Ga 1.6 , In 1.7 , Tl 1.8
      REASON : boron is very small and pulls the shared pair strongly;
      aluminium is much larger so it drops sharply. After Al the poor
      shielding by d and f electrons raises the effective nuclear
      charge again, so the value creeps back up.

  4.  MELTING POINT - falls from B to Ga, then rises again.
          B 2453 , Al 933 , Ga 303 , In 430 , Tl 576  K
      REASON : boron is a GIANT COVALENT solid with very strong short
      bonds; the metals have much weaker metallic bonding that gets
      weaker as the atoms grow.
      THE EXCEPTION : gallium's value is abnormally LOW because solid
      gallium consists of Ga2 DIATOMIC MOLECULES held by weak forces.
      After that anomaly the values climb again for In and Tl.
```

### Q B2
Explain the inert pair effect. How does it affect the oxidation states of
group 13 elements?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
      The INERT PAIR EFFECT is the RELUCTANCE OF THE ns2 ELECTRON PAIR
      TO TAKE PART IN BOND FORMATION, an effect that INCREASES going
      DOWN a p-block group and makes the LOWER oxidation state more
      and more stable.

  CAUSE (four steps)
      (a) Going down the group, filled d (3d10, 4d10, 5d10) and
          f (4f14) subshells come in between the nucleus and the
          valence shell.
      (b) These d and f orbitals are DIFFUSE and SHIELD the nuclear
          charge VERY POORLY.
      (c) The ns2 pair therefore feels a LARGE EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR
          CHARGE and is held very tightly.
      (d) PROMOTING an ns electron to an np orbital now costs more
          energy than is repaid by the two extra bonds - which are
          long and weak in a large atom. So the ns2 pair stays put.

  EFFECT ON THE OXIDATION STATES
      Only the single np1 electron is used, giving the +1 state.

          Stability of +1 :   Al  <  Ga  <  In  <  Tl
          Stability of +3 :   Al  >  Ga  >  In  >  Tl

      B and Al show +3 almost exclusively.
      Tl shows +1 as its STABLE state.

  CONSEQUENCES (give at least two)
      1.  Tl(III) compounds are STRONG OXIDISING AGENTS:
              TlCl3  --(heat)-->  TlCl  +  Cl2
      2.  TlI3 is not Tl(III) iodide but Tl+(I3)-, because Tl3+ would
          oxidise I- to I2.
      3.  Ga(I) and In(I) DISPROPORTIONATE:  3 Ga+ -> 2 Ga + Ga3+
      4.  TlOH is a strong base, comparable with NaOH.
```

### Q B3
Explain the anomalous behaviour of boron. Give any five points.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE CAUSE (write this first)
      Boron differs from the rest of group 13 because of
        (i)   its VERY SMALL SIZE (85 pm, against 143 pm for Al)
        (ii)  its VERY HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPY (801; sum of the
              first three = 6887 kJ/mol)
        (iii) its HIGH ELECTRONEGATIVITY (2.0, against 1.5 for Al)
        (iv)  the availability of ONLY FOUR ORBITALS (2s + three 2p)
        (v)   the complete ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS in its valence shell.

  THE DIFFERENCES

  1.  Boron is a NON-METAL - hard, black, very high melting and a
      semiconductor. Al, Ga, In and Tl are all soft METALS.
      Reason: boron's ionisation enthalpy is far too high to release
      electrons into a metallic "sea".

  2.  Boron forms ONLY COVALENT compounds; the B3+ ion never exists.
      Reason: IE1+IE2+IE3 = 6887 kJ/mol is never repaid by lattice or
      hydration energy. Aluminium and the rest form ionic compounds.

  3.  Boron has a MAXIMUM COVALENCY OF FOUR ([BF4]-), while aluminium
      reaches SIX ([AlF6]3-, [Al(H2O)6]3+).
      Reason: boron has only four orbitals and NO d orbitals, so it
      can never expand its octet.

  4.  Boron trihalides are MONOMERIC and trigonal planar, whereas
      AlCl3 is a DIMER, Al2Cl6.

  5.  B(OH)3 is ACIDIC and B2O3 is an ACIDIC oxide, whereas Al(OH)3
      and Al2O3 are AMPHOTERIC and Tl2O3 / TlOH are BASIC.
      Reason: metallic character increases down the group.

  6.  Boron forms many stable ELECTRON-DEFICIENT hydrides (the
      BORANES, e.g. B2H6), while the hydrides of the other members
      are unstable and poorly characterised.

  7.  Boron halides are COMPLETELY hydrolysed by water
      (BCl3 + 3H2O -> H3BO3 + 3HCl), whereas AlCl3 hydrolyses only
      partially.

  8.  Boron shows only the +3 state; the others show +3 AND +1.
```

### Q B4
What is a diagonal relationship? Explain the diagonal relationship between
boron and silicon with any four points of similarity.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
      Some elements of the second period resemble the element lying
      DIAGONALLY BELOW AND TO THE RIGHT of them in the next period
      more than they resemble their own group members. This is the
      DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP.
      The three pairs are  Li-Mg ,  Be-Al  and  B-Si .

  REASON
      Across a period, size DECREASES and electronegativity INCREASES.
      Down a group, size INCREASES and electronegativity DECREASES.
      Along a diagonal the two changes CANCEL, so the pair have nearly
      the same SIZE, ELECTRONEGATIVITY and CHARGE-TO-RADIUS RATIO.
            B   85 pm  , EN 2.0
            Si  118 pm , EN 1.8

  POINTS OF SIMILARITY BETWEEN B AND Si

  1.  Both are non-metallic SEMICONDUCTORS - hard, high melting and
      poor conductors. Both form ONLY COVALENT compounds; B3+ and
      Si4+ do not exist.

  2.  Both form VOLATILE, SPONTANEOUSLY FLAMMABLE HYDRIDES that are
      readily HYDROLYSED - the BORANES and the SILANES:
            B2H6  +  6 H2O   -->   2 H3BO3   +  6 H2
            SiH4  +  4 H2O   -->   Si(OH)4   +  4 H2

  3.  Both halides are COMPLETELY HYDROLYSED by water:
            BCl3   +  3 H2O  -->   H3BO3     +  3 HCl
            SiCl4  +  4 H2O  -->   Si(OH)4   +  4 HCl

  4.  Both oxides are ACIDIC and dissolve in alkali, and both form
      glassy networks:
            B2O3  +  6 NaOH  -->   2 Na3BO3  +  3 H2O
            SiO2  +  2 NaOH  -->   Na2SiO3   +  H2O

  5.  Both form POLYMERIC ring, chain and sheet structures - the
      BORATES and the SILICATES - built from small units sharing
      oxygen atoms.

  6.  Both form hard, refractory BORIDES / SILICIDES with metals, and
      both carbides (B4C and SiC) are extremely hard abrasives.
```

### Q B5
Why are boron trihalides Lewis acids? Explain the order
BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3 < BI3.

**ANSWER:**
```
  WHY THEY ARE LEWIS ACIDS

      In BX3 the boron atom is sp2 hybridised and the molecule is
      TRIGONAL PLANAR. Around boron there are only

            3 (its own)  +  3 (one from each halogen)  =  6 electrons

      A SEXTET, not an octet, plus ONE COMPLETELY EMPTY 2p ORBITAL.
      BX3 is therefore ELECTRON DEFICIENT and readily ACCEPTS A LONE
      PAIR - which is the definition of a LEWIS ACID.

            BF3  +  NH3  -->  F3B <- NH3
            BF3  +  F-   -->  [BF4]-

  THE ORDER AND ITS REASON

      OBSERVED ORDER :   BF3  <  BCl3  <  BBr3  <  BI3

      This is the OPPOSITE of what electronegativity alone would
      predict. The reason is p(pi) - p(pi) BACK BONDING.

      Each halogen has FILLED p orbitals carrying lone pairs. One of
      these lone pairs can be donated SIDEWAYS into boron's EMPTY 2p
      orbital. This BACK DONATION partly fills the empty orbital,
      gives the B-X bond some double-bond character, and REDUCES
      boron's electron deficiency.

      Effective sideways overlap needs orbitals of SIMILAR SIZE:

          BF3   F uses 2p  - SAME SIZE as B's 2p - BEST overlap
                          - STRONGEST back bonding - WEAKEST acid
          BCl3  Cl uses 3p - poorer overlap
          BBr3  Br uses 4p - poorer still
          BI3   I uses 5p  - far too large - almost NO back bonding
                          - boron stays fully deficient
                          - STRONGEST acid

      EVIDENCE: the B-F bond in BF3 measures only 130 pm, SHORTER
      than a normal B-F single bond, showing the partial double-bond
      character produced by back bonding.
```

### Q B8
How is orthoboric acid prepared? Describe its structure and explain why it
is a weak monobasic acid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PREPARATION

    (a) FROM BORAX (the laboratory method) - add a mineral acid to a
        hot concentrated solution of borax and cool:

            Na2B4O7  +  2 HCl  +  5 H2O  -->  2 NaCl  +  4 H3BO3
            Na2B4O7  +  H2SO4 +  5 H2O  -->  Na2SO4  +  4 H3BO3

        H3BO3 is much less soluble in cold water, so it crystallises.

    (b) FROM COLEMANITE (industrial) - pass SO2 through a hot
        suspension of powdered colemanite:

            Ca2B6O11 + 4 SO2 + 11 H2O --> 2 Ca(HSO3)2 + 6 H3BO3

    (c) BY HYDROLYSIS:   BCl3 + 3 H2O --> H3BO3 + 3 HCl

  STRUCTURE

        H            H            H
         \            \            \
          O            O            O
          |            |            |
          B            B            B
        /   \        /   \        /   \
       O     O      O     O      O     O
       |     |      |     |      |     |
       H     H      H     H      H     H
       :     :      :     :      :     :        <- HYDROGEN BONDS
       O     O      O     O      O     O
       |     |      |     |      |     |
        \   /        \   /        \   /
          B            B            B
          |            |            |
          O            O            O
         /            /            /
        H            H            H

      *  Each boron is sp2 hybridised, TRIGONAL PLANAR, bonded to
         three -OH groups at 120 degrees.
      *  The BO3 units are linked into flat TWO-DIMENSIONAL LAYERS by
         HYDROGEN BONDS between the -OH groups.
      *  The LAYERS are held together only by WEAK VAN DER WAALS
         FORCES, so they SLIDE over one another - which is why boric
         acid feels SOAPY and can be used as a LUBRICANT.

  WHY IT IS A WEAK MONOBASIC ACID

      H3BO3 has three hydrogens but does NOT donate any of them.
      Boron has only SIX electrons and one EMPTY 2p orbital, so
      B(OH)3 acts as a LEWIS ACID: it ACCEPTS a hydroxide ion from a
      water molecule, and this sets free exactly ONE H3O+ ion.

            B(OH)3  +  2 H2O   -->   [B(OH)4]-  +  H3O+

      One H3O+ per molecule  ->  MONOBASIC.
      Only a very small fraction of the molecules react  ->  it is a
      VERY WEAK acid, Ka about 5.8 x 10^-10 .

      It cannot be titrated directly against NaOH. Adding GLYCEROL or
      MANNITOL (a cis-diol) makes it a much stronger acid so it can
      then be titrated sharply.
```

### Q B10
Draw and explain the structure of diborane. Why is it called an
electron-deficient molecule?

**ANSWER:**
```
  WHY IT IS ELECTRON DEFICIENT - count the electrons

      AVAILABLE :   2 B x 3 electrons  =  6
                    6 H x 1 electron   =  6
                                         ---
                                         12 electrons

      NEEDED for a normal structure :
                    6 B-H bonds + 1 B-B bond = 7 bonds
                    7 x 2 = 14 electrons

      12 available  <  14 needed  ->  TWO ELECTRONS SHORT
      *** B2H6 IS THEREFORE AN ELECTRON-DEFICIENT MOLECULE. ***

  THE STRUCTURE

        Ht                                          Ht
          \                                       /
           \                Hb                   /
            \             /    \                /
             \          /        \             /
              B                                B
             /          \        /              \
            /             \    /                 \
           /                Hb                    \
          /                                        \
        Ht                                          Ht

        Ht = TERMINAL hydrogen  (FOUR)
        Hb = BRIDGING hydrogen  (TWO)

  EXPLANATION

    1.  Each boron atom is sp3 HYBRIDISED.

    2.  The FOUR TERMINAL hydrogens are held by ordinary TWO-CENTRE
        TWO-ELECTRON (2c-2e) bonds.   B-Ht = 119 pm.

    3.  The TWO BRIDGING hydrogens are held by THREE-CENTRE
        TWO-ELECTRON (3c-2e) BANANA BONDS: a SINGLE electron pair
        holds THREE atoms (B ... H ... B) together. The electron
        cloud is curved, hence "banana".   B-Hb = 133 pm, LONGER and
        WEAKER than B-Ht.

    4.  GEOMETRY: the two boron atoms and the FOUR TERMINAL hydrogens
        all lie in ONE PLANE. The TWO BRIDGING hydrogens lie in a
        plane PERPENDICULAR to it, one above and one below.

    5.  BOND ANGLES:  Ht - B - Ht = 121.5 degrees
                      Hb - B - Hb = 97 degrees

    6.  *** THERE IS NO DIRECT B-B BOND. *** The two borons are held
        together only through the two hydrogen bridges.
```

Explain the anomalous behaviour of boron, its diagonal relationship with

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown)**

```

  ================================================================

      THE FIVE CAUSES
          (i)   very small size (85 pm; Al is 143 pm)
          (ii)  very high ionisation enthalpy (801; sum of the first

          (iii) high electronegativity (2.0; Al is 1.5)

          (v)   NO d orbitals in the valence shell

       1. Boron is a NON-METAL (hard, black, semiconductor); the rest
          are soft METALS.
       2. Boron forms ONLY COVALENT compounds; B3+ never exists.
       3. Maximum covalency of boron is FOUR ([BF4]-); aluminium
          reaches SIX ([AlF6]3-) using its vacant 3d orbitals.
       4. Boron trihalides are MONOMERIC and planar; AlCl3 is the
          DIMER Al2Cl6.
       5. B(OH)3 and B2O3 are ACIDIC; Al(OH)3 and Al2O3 are
          AMPHOTERIC and TlOH is strongly BASIC.
       6. Boron forms stable ELECTRON-DEFICIENT BORANES; the hydrides
          of the others are unstable.
       7. Boron halides are COMPLETELY hydrolysed; AlCl3 only
          partially.
       8. Boron shows only +3; the others show +3 and +1.

  ================================================================
  PART 2 - DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP WITH SILICON                (3 M)
  ================================================================

      WHAT IT IS
          A second-period element resembles the element lying
          DIAGONALLY BELOW AND TO THE RIGHT of it. The three pairs
          are Li-Mg, Be-Al and B-Si.

      WHY IT HAPPENS
          Across a period size falls and EN rises; down a group size
          rises and EN falls. Along a diagonal the two effects
          CANCEL, so the pair have nearly the same SIZE,
          ELECTRONEGATIVITY and CHARGE-TO-RADIUS RATIO.
              B  85 pm , EN 2.0    Si 118 pm , EN 1.8

      THE SIMILARITIES
       1. Both are non-metallic SEMICONDUCTORS forming only COVALENT
          compounds.
       2. Both form volatile, spontaneously flammable, easily
          hydrolysed hydrides - BORANES and SILANES:
              B2H6 + 6H2O --> 2H3BO3 + 6H2
              SiH4 + 4H2O --> Si(OH)4 + 4H2
       3. Both halides are COMPLETELY hydrolysed:
              BCl3 + 3H2O --> H3BO3 + 3HCl
              SiCl4 + 4H2O --> Si(OH)4 + 4HCl
       4. Both oxides are ACIDIC and dissolve in alkali:
              B2O3 + 6NaOH --> 2Na3BO3 + 3H2O
              SiO2 + 2NaOH --> Na2SiO3 + H2O
       5. Both form POLYMERIC borates and silicates built from small
          units sharing oxygens.
       6. Both form hard BORIDES / SILICIDES with metals and
          extremely hard carbides B4C and SiC.

  ================================================================
  PART 3 - THE LEWIS ACID ORDER OF THE TRIHALIDES            (2 M)
  ================================================================

      WHY THEY ARE LEWIS ACIDS
          BX3 is sp2, trigonal planar. Boron has only
              3 (own) + 3 (one from each X) = 6 electrons
          A SEXTET, plus one COMPLETELY EMPTY 2p orbital. BX3 is
          ELECTRON DEFICIENT and accepts a lone pair:
              BF3 + NH3 --> F3B <- NH3
              BF3 + F-  --> [BF4]-

      THE ORDER
              BF3  <  BCl3  <  BBr3  <  BI3

          SIDEWAYS into boron's empty 2p orbital. This back donation
          partly fills the orbital and REDUCES the deficiency.
          Effective overlap needs orbitals of SIMILAR SIZE:
              F uses 2p  - same size as B's 2p - BEST overlap
                         - strongest back bonding - WEAKEST acid
              Cl 3p , Br 4p , I 5p - overlap gets worse and worse
              I  uses 5p - almost NO back bonding
                         - boron stays fully deficient
                         - STRONGEST acid
          EVIDENCE: B-F in BF3 is only 130 pm, shorter than a normal
          single bond, showing partial double-bond character.

          *** NEVER answer "F is most electronegative so BF3 is the
              strongest". That is the trap. ***
```

### Q C6
Discuss the position of the p-block in the periodic table and give a full
account of the reactivity of group 13 elements towards air, water, acids,
alkalis and halogens.

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown)**

```
  ================================================================
  PART 1 - THE p-BLOCK                                       (2 M)
  ================================================================

      The p-block is the SIX COLUMNS on the right of the periodic
      table, groups 13 to 18. The last electron enters a p orbital.

      GENERAL ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION  =  ns2 np(1-6)
      (Helium, 1s2, is the exception; it sits in group 18 because of
       its properties, not its configuration.)

          GROUP    13      14      15      16      17      18
          CONFIG   ns2np1  ns2np2  ns2np3  ns2np4  ns2np5  ns2np6
          NAME     Boron   Carbon  Nitro-  Oxygen  Halo-   Noble
                   family  family  gen     family  gen     gases

      THREE GENERAL POINTS
       1.  The p-block is the ONLY block containing METALS,
           METALLOIDS and NON-METALS together. Metallic character
           increases DOWN a group and decreases ACROSS a period.
       2.  The FIRST member of every group is ANOMALOUS: small size,
           high EN, high IE, only four orbitals, no d orbitals.
       3.  Going down a group the INERT PAIR EFFECT makes the LOWER
           oxidation state (group number minus 2) more stable.

      GROUP 13 is the first column of the p-block: B, Al, Ga, In, Tl,
      configuration ns2 np1, oxidation states +3 and +1.

  ================================================================
  PART 2 - REACTIVITY TOWARDS AIR                            (2 M)
  ================================================================

      Crystalline boron is nearly INERT at ordinary temperature.
      Amorphous boron and all the metals burn when heated:

          2 B   +  3/2 O2  --(heat)-->  B2O3
          4 Al  +  3 O2    --(heat)-->  2 Al2O3
          general :  2 E + 3/2 O2 --> E2O3

      At very high temperature they react with DINITROGEN:

          2 B   +  N2  --(high temp)-->  2 BN   (boron nitride)
          2 Al  +  N2  --(high temp)-->  2 AlN

      BN is called INORGANIC GRAPHITE - flat sheets of alternating B
      and N atoms; its cubic form BORAZON is extremely hard.

      Aluminium builds a thin, hard, impervious Al2O3 layer that
      protects the metal (PASSIVITY); thickening it deliberately is
      called ANODISING.

      NATURE OF THE OXIDES
          B2O3 ACIDIC -> Al2O3, Ga2O3 AMPHOTERIC -> In2O3, Tl2O3
          BASIC (Tl2O strongly basic), reflecting the rise in
          metallic character.

  ================================================================
  PART 3 - TOWARDS WATER, ACIDS AND ALKALIS                  (2 M)
  ================================================================

      WATER : boron does not react. Aluminium reacts with steam:
          2 Al + 3 H2O --(steam)--> Al2O3 + 3 H2

      ACIDS : boron does not react with dilute acids, but hot
      concentrated nitric acid oxidises it:
          B + 3 HNO3 (conc.) --> H3BO3 + 3 NO2
      Aluminium dissolves in dilute acids liberating hydrogen:
          2 Al + 6 HCl --> 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2
          2 Al + 3 H2SO4 (dil) --> Al2(SO4)3 + 3 H2
      Concentrated HNO3 renders aluminium PASSIVE.

      ALKALIS : aluminium is AMPHOTERIC and dissolves in hot alkali:
          2 Al + 2 NaOH + 6 H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 3 H2
      Boron reacts only with FUSED alkali:
          2 B + 6 NaOH --(fused)--> 2 Na3BO3 + 3 H2

  ================================================================
  PART 4 - TOWARDS HALOGENS                                  (2 M)
  ================================================================

      All the elements form trihalides:
          2 E  +  3 X2   -->   2 EX3
          2 B  +  3 Cl2  -->   2 BCl3      (monomeric, planar)
          2 Al +  3 Cl2  -->   Al2Cl6      (DIMER)

      THE EXCEPTION: TlI3 is not thallium(III) iodide but
      THALLIUM(I) TRI-IODIDE, Tl+(I3)-, because Tl3+ would
      immediately oxidise I- to I2.

      BX3 is sp2, TRIGONAL PLANAR, 120 degrees, NON-POLAR, and
      ELECTRON DEFICIENT (only a SEXTET on boron plus an empty 2p
      orbital), so it is a LEWIS ACID:
          BF3 + NH3 --> F3B <- NH3
          BF3 + F-  --> [BF4]-
      The order of Lewis acid strength is
          BF3 < BCl3 < BBr3 < BI3
      because p(pi)-p(pi) BACK BONDING is strongest in BF3 (2p-2p
      overlap) and negligible in BI3.

      Boron halides are COMPLETELY hydrolysed:
          BCl3 + 3 H2O --> H3BO3 + 3 HCl
      while AlCl3 hydrolyses only partially:
          AlCl3 + 3 H2O --> Al(OH)3 + 3 HCl
```
