# TOPIC 1 — Why do atoms combine at all?

Think of an atom as a person carrying an awkward, unbalanced bag.
A **noble gas** (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe) is a person whose bag is perfectly packed —
they are happy alone, they react with nothing.

Every other atom is trying to get that same "perfectly packed bag".
The perfectly packed bag = **8 electrons in the outermost shell** (2 for helium).

```
  THE ONE SENTENCE:

     Atoms combine because the combined form has LOWER ENERGY
     than the separate atoms.

     Lower energy  =  more stable  =  what nature always wants.
```

## Kossel-Lewis approach (1916)

Two scientists, same year, two halves of the same idea.

```
  +-------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
  |  KOSSEL           |  Atoms TRANSFER electrons.                       |
  |                   |  A metal loses electrons  -> positive ion        |
  |                   |  A non-metal gains them   -> negative ion        |
  |                   |  The two opposite ions attract  -> IONIC BOND    |
  +-------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
  |  LEWIS            |  Atoms SHARE electrons in pairs.                 |
  |                   |  Each shared pair = one COVALENT BOND            |
  |                   |  He drew the atom as a cube with electrons at    |
  |                   |  the corners (8 corners = 8 electrons = octet)   |
  +-------------------+--------------------------------------------------+
```

## The octet rule

```
  OCTET RULE:  atoms gain, lose or share electrons so that each atom
               ends up with EIGHT electrons in its valence shell.

  DUET RULE:   hydrogen, lithium and beryllium aim for TWO (like helium).
```

## Lewis dot symbols

Put one dot for each valence electron around the symbol.

```
        .              .            .            . .
     Na .           . Mg .       . B .        . C .
                                   .            .

     Na = 1 valence e-    Mg = 2      B = 3       C = 4


      . .          . .          . .         . .
     . N .       . O :       : F :       : Ne :
      .            . .         . .         . .

     N = 5                O = 6      F = 7       Ne = 8
```

> **TRAP:** Only the **valence** (outermost) electrons get dots.
> Sodium has 11 electrons but only **one** dot.

# TOPIC 2 — How to draw a Lewis structure (the 5-step method)

This is a machine. Follow the steps and you cannot go wrong.

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  STEP 1   Count the TOTAL valence electrons of all atoms.            |
  |           For a NEGATIVE ion, ADD the charge.                        |
  |           For a POSITIVE ion, SUBTRACT the charge.                   |
  |                                                                      |
  |  STEP 2   Pick the CENTRAL atom - usually the LEAST electronegative  |
  |           one. Hydrogen is NEVER central. Carbon is usually central. |
  |                                                                      |
  |  STEP 3   Join the central atom to each outer atom with ONE single   |
  |           bond. Each bond uses 2 electrons.                          |
  |                                                                      |
  |  STEP 4   Spread the LEFT-OVER electrons as LONE PAIRS on the OUTER  |
  |           atoms first, until each has an octet. Any that remain go   |
  |           on the central atom.                                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |  STEP 5   If the central atom is still short of an octet, pull a     |
  |           lone pair from an outer atom to make a DOUBLE or TRIPLE    |
  |           bond.                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## Worked example 1 — CO2

```
  STEP 1   C = 4 , O = 6 , O = 6      total = 16 electrons  (8 pairs)

  STEP 2   Carbon is central.

  STEP 3   O - C - O          uses 4 electrons , 12 left

  STEP 4   Put 12 on the two oxygens:

              . .        . .
            : O - C - O :        Oxygens have octets.
              . .        . .     Carbon has only 4 electrons.  NOT OK.

  STEP 5   Pull one lone pair from EACH oxygen into a double bond:

              : O = C = O :

           Now:  C has 8 , each O has 8.   DONE.
```

## Worked example 2 — the ammonia molecule NH3

```
  STEP 1   N = 5 , 3 H = 3        total = 8 electrons  (4 pairs)

  STEP 2   N is central (H is never central).

  STEP 3-5

                  ..
                  N            The dots on top are the LONE PAIR.
                / | \          Three bond pairs, one lone pair.
               H  H  H
```

## Worked example 3 — the carbonate ion CO3 2-

```
  STEP 1   C = 4 , 3 O = 18 , charge 2- adds 2   ->  total 24 electrons

  STEP 2-5

                     : O :
                       |
                       C                 2-
                     //  \
                  : O     O :
                            (the two extra electrons give the 2- charge)
```

> **TRAP:** For an ion, always put the whole structure in square brackets
> in your answer paper and write the charge outside. Examiners look for it.

# TOPIC 3 — Formal charge

Sometimes you can draw a molecule in more than one way. Formal charge is the
referee that tells you **which drawing is the correct one**.

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |     FORMAL   =   valence     -   lone-pair    -   1  x  bonding      |
  |     CHARGE       electrons       electrons        -     electrons    |
  |                  of the free                      2                  |
  |                  atom                                                |
  |                                                                      |
  |     Short form:      FC   =   V   -   L   -   B / 2                  |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  RULE:  The BEST Lewis structure is the one where the formal charges are
         as close to ZERO as possible, and any negative formal charge sits
         on the MOST electronegative atom.
```

## Worked example — ozone O3

```
                  O
                 / \\           label them:  O(1) - O(2) = O(3)
              O     O                        left   centre   right
             (1)   (2)  (3)

  CENTRAL oxygen O(2):
        V = 6 , lone-pair electrons L = 2 , bonding electrons B = 6
        FC = 6 - 2 - 6/2  =  6 - 2 - 3  =  +1

  LEFT oxygen O(1)  (single bond, 3 lone pairs):
        V = 6 , L = 6 , B = 2
        FC = 6 - 6 - 1  =  -1

  RIGHT oxygen O(3)  (double bond, 2 lone pairs):
        V = 6 , L = 4 , B = 4
        FC = 6 - 4 - 2  =   0

  CHECK:  (+1) + (-1) + 0  =  0   =  charge on the molecule.  Correct.
```

> **TRAP:** The sum of all formal charges MUST equal the overall charge of the
> species (zero for a neutral molecule). If it does not, you made an
> arithmetic mistake — go back and recount.

# TOPIC 4 — Limitations of the octet rule

The octet rule is a good servant but a bad master. It breaks in three ways.

```
  +-----------------------+---------------------------------------------+
  |  (1) INCOMPLETE OCTET |  Central atom has FEWER than 8 electrons.   |
  |      (electron        |                                             |
  |       deficient)      |  LiCl (Li has 2) , BeCl2 (Be has 4)         |
  |                       |  BF3 , BCl3 , AlCl3  (B and Al have 6)      |
  +-----------------------+---------------------------------------------+
  |  (2) EXPANDED OCTET   |  Central atom has MORE than 8 electrons.    |
  |      (super-octet)    |  Only possible from period 3 onwards        |
  |                       |  because vacant d orbitals are available.   |
  |                       |                                             |
  |                       |  PCl5 (10) , SF6 (12) , IF7 (14)            |
  |                       |  H2SO4 (12) , SF4 (10)                      |
  +-----------------------+---------------------------------------------+
  |  (3) ODD-ELECTRON     |  Total electrons is an ODD number, so       |
  |      MOLECULES        |  pairing everything is impossible.          |
  |                       |                                             |
  |                       |  NO (11 valence e-) , NO2 (17) , ClO2       |
  |                       |  These are PARAMAGNETIC.                    |
  +-----------------------+---------------------------------------------+
```

Other honest failures of the octet idea:

```
  - It says NOTHING about the SHAPE of a molecule.
  - It says NOTHING about the ENERGY / stability of the bond.
  - The noble gases were supposed to be inert, yet XeF2, XeF4, XeOF2 exist.
```

> **TRAP:** Nitrogen can NEVER expand its octet — it is in period 2 and has
> no d orbitals. That is why NCl5 does not exist but PCl5 does.

# TOPIC 5 — The ionic (electrovalent) bond

```
  Sodium has 1 electron too many.   Chlorine is 1 electron short.
  Sodium hands it over. Both end up with a noble-gas arrangement.

     Na  .    +   : Cl :        ->      Na+   [ : Cl : ]-
                    . .                          . .

     Na  ->  Na+  +  e-           (2,8,1 -> 2,8   like Ne)
     Cl  +  e-  ->  Cl-           (2,8,7 -> 2,8,8 like Ar)

  The + and the - now pull on each other. That electrostatic pull IS the
  ionic bond.
```

```
  IMPORTANT:  there is no such thing as an "NaCl molecule".
              Solid NaCl is a giant 3-D LATTICE. Every Na+ is surrounded
              by 6 Cl- and every Cl- by 6 Na+.

              +---+---+---+
              |Na |Cl |Na |
              +---+---+---+
              |Cl |Na |Cl |          (a slice through the lattice)
              +---+---+---+
              |Na |Cl |Na |
              +---+---+---+
```

## Lattice enthalpy

```
  LATTICE ENTHALPY  =  the energy REQUIRED to completely separate one mole
                       of a solid ionic compound into gaseous ions.

     NaCl (s)   ->   Na+ (g)  +  Cl- (g)        lattice enthalpy = +788 kJ/mol

  A LARGE lattice enthalpy means a VERY stable ionic solid
  (high melting point, hard, brittle).
```

```
  LATTICE ENTHALPY GOES UP WHEN:

      charge on the ions goes UP        (MgO  >>  NaCl)
      size of the ions goes DOWN        (LiF  >  LiI)

  This is just Coulomb's law:

        Lattice energy  proportional to   ( q1 x q2 ) / ( r+  +  r- )
```

## The Born-Haber cycle (the idea, not the arithmetic)

Lattice enthalpy cannot be measured directly. So we go the long way round
and use Hess's law: **energy is a state function, so both routes must add up
to the same total.**

```
                             DIRECT ROUTE
        Na (s) + 1/2 Cl2 (g) --------------------> NaCl (s)
             |                  (formation, dH f)      ^
             |                                         |
     sublimation (S)                                   | lattice
             v                                         | enthalpy (U)
          Na (g)                                       |
             |                                         |
     ionisation (IE)                                   |
             v                                         |
          Na+ (g)  +  Cl- (g)  <---------------  Cl (g)  <--- 1/2 bond
                                electron affinity            dissociation
                                     (EA)                      (1/2 D)

     dH f  =  S  +  IE  +  (1/2) D  +  EA  +  U

     Everything except U can be measured, so U is found by difference.
```

## Factors favouring the formation of an ionic bond

```
  1. LOW ionisation enthalpy of the metal      (easy to lose electrons)
                                                -> Group 1 and 2 metals

  2. HIGH electron gain enthalpy of the        (eager to accept electrons)
     non-metal                                  -> halogens, oxygen

  3. HIGH lattice enthalpy of the product      (small, highly charged ions)

  4. LARGE electronegativity difference        (about 1.7 or more)
     between the two atoms
```

> **TRAP:** Ionisation enthalpy is always ENDOthermic (energy in). It is the
> huge lattice enthalpy released afterwards that pays that bill and makes the
> whole process worth it. Say that sentence in the 4-mark answer.

# TOPIC 6 — Covalent bond and the bond parameters

When neither atom is willing to hand over electrons, they **share**.

```
   H .  +  . H     ->     H : H       or      H - H

   Each hydrogen now "feels" 2 electrons  =  helium arrangement.
```

```
  Single bond   -  one shared pair    -   H - H       ,  Cl - Cl
  Double bond   -  two shared pairs   -   O = O       ,  H2C = CH2
  Triple bond   -  three shared pairs -   N # N       ,  HC # CH
```

## The four bond parameters

```
  +------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
  |  BOND LENGTH     |  Distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms. |
  |                  |  Measured in picometres (pm) or angstrom.         |
  |                  |  MORE bonds -> SHORTER length.                    |
  |                  |     C-C 154 pm > C=C 134 pm > C#C 120 pm          |
  +------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
  |  BOND ANGLE      |  The angle between two bonds at the central atom. |
  |                  |     CH4 109.5 , NH3 107 , H2O 104.5 degrees       |
  +------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
  |  BOND ENTHALPY   |  Energy needed to BREAK one mole of bonds in the  |
  |  (bond energy)   |  gas phase. Always POSITIVE.                      |
  |                  |     H-H 435 , O=O 498 , N#N 946 kJ/mol            |
  |                  |  MORE bonds -> HIGHER enthalpy.                   |
  +------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
  |  BOND ORDER      |  Number of bonds between the two atoms.           |
  |                  |     H2 = 1 , O2 = 2 , N2 = 3                      |
  |                  |  HIGHER order -> shorter and stronger bond.       |
  +------------------+---------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  THE MASTER RELATION - remember this one line and you can answer
  a dozen different questions:

     BOND ORDER  up   ->   BOND LENGTH  DOWN   ->   BOND ENTHALPY  UP
                                                    STABILITY      UP
```

## Worked example

```
  Q. Arrange O2 , O2+ , O2- , O2 2-  in order of increasing bond length.

  Bond orders (from MOT, see Topic 17):
        O2+   = 2.5
        O2    = 2.0
        O2-   = 1.5
        O2 2- = 1.0

  Higher bond order = shorter bond, so:

        O2+  <  O2  <  O2-  <  O2 2-        (increasing bond length)
```

# TOPIC 7 — Resonance

Sometimes ONE Lewis structure is not enough. The real molecule is a **blend**.

Think of a mule. You describe it as "part horse, part donkey" — but the mule
is not flipping between the two. It is permanently one single in-between thing.

```
  OZONE  O3   -  experiment says BOTH O-O bonds are the SAME length (128 pm)

  Structure I              Structure II

     O                        O
    / \\                    // \
   O    O                  O    O

  Neither is real. The REAL ozone is the resonance HYBRID, drawn with a
  dotted "one-and-a-half" bond:

                 O
                / \
               O - O          both bonds identical, order 1.5
              (dotted, partial double character on BOTH sides)
```

```
  CARBONATE ION  CO3 2-   -  all THREE C-O bonds are equal (129 pm)

      : O :            : O :            : O
        ||               |                |
        C        <->     C        <->     C
       /  \             / \\             // \
    : O    O :       : O    O :       : O    O :

  Real bond order of each C-O bond  =  4 bonds / 3 positions  =  1.33
```

```
  RESONANCE ENERGY  =  (energy of the actual molecule)
                          -  (energy of the most stable single structure)

  It is the EXTRA stability the molecule gains from resonance.
  Benzene's resonance energy is about 150 kJ/mol - that is why benzene
  refuses to behave like a normal alkene.
```

```
  CONDITIONS for valid resonance structures:
     1. Same positions of all the ATOMS - only ELECTRONS move.
     2. Same total number of electrons and same overall charge.
     3. Roughly similar energies - and the more stable structures
        contribute more to the hybrid.
```

> **TRAP:** Resonance structures are NOT in equilibrium. Never draw a
> double-headed equilibrium arrow. Use a single double-headed arrow `<->`.
> The molecule does not "switch" between them.

# TOPIC 8 — Polarity of bonds and dipole moment

```
  If the two atoms are IDENTICAL   ->  the pair is shared equally
                                        -> NON-POLAR covalent  (H-H , Cl-Cl)

  If they are DIFFERENT            ->  the more electronegative atom pulls
                                        harder -> POLAR covalent

              d+       d-
              H  ----> Cl         (the arrow points to the negative end)
```

## Dipole moment

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |      DIPOLE MOMENT      mu   =   q   x   d                           |
  |                                                                      |
  |      q = magnitude of the charge (coulomb)                           |
  |      d = distance between the charges (metre)                        |
  |                                                                      |
  |      UNIT:   debye (D)        1 D  =  3.336 x 10^-30  C m            |
  |                                                                      |
  |      Dipole moment is a VECTOR - it has direction as well as size.   |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**This is the whole secret:** a molecule's dipole moment is the **vector sum**
of all its bond dipoles. Symmetry can make them cancel to zero.

## The two classic comparisons (learn these word for word)

```
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  CASE 1:   CO2   versus   H2O
  ------------------------------------------------------------------

     CO2 :   O = C = O            LINEAR , angle 180

             <---  o  --->        Two equal dipoles pointing in
                                  EXACTLY OPPOSITE directions.
             mu  =  0             THEY CANCEL.  NON-POLAR.


     H2O :        O               BENT , angle 104.5
                 / \
                H   H             The two dipoles do NOT cancel.
                                  They ADD to a resultant pointing
             mu  =  1.85 D        up through the oxygen.  POLAR.
```

```
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  CASE 2:   BF3   versus   NH3
  ------------------------------------------------------------------

     BF3 :        F               TRIGONAL PLANAR , angle 120
                  |               Symmetrical - the three B-F dipoles
                  B               are at 120 degrees and cancel exactly.
                 / \
                F   F             mu  =  0     NON-POLAR
                                  (even though each B-F bond is very polar)


     NH3 :       ..               PYRAMIDAL , angle 107
                 N                The three N-H dipoles do NOT lie in a
               / | \              plane, AND the LONE PAIR adds its own
              H  H  H             dipole in the same direction.

                                  mu  =  1.47 D    POLAR
```

```
  NH3   mu = 1.47 D          NF3   mu = 0.24 D          WHY THE DIFFERENCE?

     In NH3, the lone-pair dipole and the three bond dipoles point the
     SAME way, so they ADD.
     In NF3, fluorine is more electronegative than nitrogen, so the bond
     dipoles point AWAY from N, OPPOSITE to the lone-pair dipole.
     They partly CANCEL  ->  small dipole moment.
```

## Percentage ionic character

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                    observed dipole moment            |
  |   % IONIC CHARACTER   =   100  x  --------------------------------   |
  |                                    dipole moment calculated for      |
  |                                    100 % ionic character             |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Worked example**

```
  Q. The dipole moment of HCl is 1.03 D and the H-Cl bond length is 1.27 A.
     Find the percentage ionic character.

  For a 100 % ionic H+ Cl- :
        mu = q x d = (4.8 x 10^-10 esu) x (1.27 x 10^-8 cm) = 6.10 D

                            1.03
  % ionic character =  100 x -------  =  16.9 %
                            6.10
```

> **TRAP:** "Polar bonds" and "polar molecule" are different things.
> CO2 has polar bonds but is a NON-POLAR molecule. Never mix them up —
> examiners set this trap every single year.

# TOPIC 9 — Fajans' rules (when does an ionic bond turn covalent?)

No bond is 100 % ionic. A small, highly charged **cation** can pull
(polarise) the electron cloud of the **anion** towards itself, which starts
to look like sharing — that is covalent character.

```
                 BEFORE                       AFTER POLARISATION

          (+)      ( - - - )              (+)     ( - -  )
          small     big soft              small   distorted, electron
          cation    anion                 cation  cloud pulled between
                                                  the two  =  COVALENT
                                                  CHARACTER
```

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  FAJANS' RULES - covalent character INCREASES when:                  |
  |                                                                      |
  |   1.  the CATION is SMALL                    (high polarising power) |
  |   2.  the ANION is LARGE                     (easily polarised)      |
  |   3.  the CHARGE on either ion is HIGH                               |
  |   4.  the cation has a PSEUDO NOBLE-GAS configuration                |
  |       ( 18-electron outer shell, e.g. Cu+, Ag+, Zn2+, Cd2+ )         |
  |       rather than a noble-gas one (Na+, K+)                          |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  POLARISING POWER of a cation  goes with   charge / size
  POLARISABILITY of an anion    goes with   size and charge
```

**Worked examples**

```
  LiCl  is more covalent than  NaCl
       -> Li+ is smaller, so it polarises Cl- more strongly.

  AlCl3 is more covalent than  MgCl2 which is more covalent than NaCl
       -> charge on the cation rises 1+ , 2+ , 3+

  LiI  >  LiBr  >  LiCl  >  LiF     (covalent character)
       -> the anion gets bigger and softer down the group.

  CuCl is more covalent than  NaCl
       -> Cu+ has an 18-electron pseudo noble-gas shell which shields
          poorly, so its effective pull is stronger than Na+.
```

Consequence: more covalent character means **lower melting point, lower
solubility in water, higher solubility in organic solvents**.

# TOPIC 10 — VSEPR theory (Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion)

The single most examinable topic in this chapter.

**The idea in one picture:** think of balloons tied together at one knot.
They automatically push apart until they are as far from each other as
possible. Electron pairs do exactly the same.

```
  2 balloons  ->  opposite ends           LINEAR
  3 balloons  ->  flat triangle           TRIGONAL PLANAR
  4 balloons  ->  a pyramid-like spread   TETRAHEDRAL
  5 balloons  ->  triangle + top + bottom TRIGONAL BIPYRAMIDAL
  6 balloons  ->  a plus sign in 3-D      OCTAHEDRAL
```

## Postulates of VSEPR theory

```
  1. The shape of a molecule depends on the number of electron pairs
     (bonding AND non-bonding) around the CENTRAL atom.

  2. Electron pairs repel one another, so they arrange themselves as far
     apart as possible to minimise repulsion.

  3. The valence shell is treated as a sphere with the pairs sitting on
     its surface at maximum separation.

  4. A LONE PAIR occupies more space than a BOND PAIR (a lone pair is
     held by only ONE nucleus, so it spreads out more).

  5. Repulsion order:

           lone pair - lone pair  >  lone pair - bond pair
                                  >  bond pair - bond pair

  6. A MULTIPLE bond is treated as ONE single "super" electron pair for
     shape purposes, but it repels a little more than a single bond.
```

## The shapes with NO lone pairs

```
  2 pairs   LINEAR                    Cl - Be - Cl        180 degrees
                                      BeCl2 , CO2 , C2H2 , HgCl2


  3 pairs   TRIGONAL PLANAR                 F             120 degrees
                                            |
                                            B
                                           / \
                                          F   F
                                      BF3 , BCl3 , CO3 2- , NO3 -


  4 pairs   TETRAHEDRAL                     H             109.5 degrees
                                            |
                                            C
                                          / | \
                                         H  H  H
                                      CH4 , CCl4 , NH4+ , SO4 2-


  5 pairs   TRIGONAL BIPYRAMIDAL         Cl               90 and 120
                                          |                degrees
                                     Cl   |
                                       \  |
                                        \ |
                                         P ---- Cl
                                        / |
                                       /  |
                                     Cl   |
                                          Cl
                                      PCl5 , PF5


  6 pairs   OCTAHEDRAL                 F   F              all 90 degrees
                                        \  |
                                         \ |
                                    F ---- S ---- F
                                          /|
                                         / |
                                        F  F
                                      SF6 , PF6 -
```

# TOPIC 11 — How lone pairs bend the shape (where the marks are)

## The famous ladder: CH4 -> NH3 -> H2O

```
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   METHANE  CH4      4 bond pairs , 0 lone pairs
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------

                 H
                 |
                 C                TETRAHEDRAL
               / | \              bond angle  109.5 degrees
              H  H  H             Perfect, nothing squashes it.


  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   AMMONIA  NH3      3 bond pairs , 1 lone pair
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------

                 : :   <-- lone pair pushes down harder
                  N
                / | \             TRIGONAL PYRAMIDAL
               H  H  H            bond angle  107 degrees

           The lone pair takes more room, squeezing the three N-H bonds
           closer together.  109.5  ->  107


  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   WATER  H2O        2 bond pairs , 2 lone pairs
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------

               : :  : :   <-- TWO lone pairs pushing down
                  O
                 / \                BENT / ANGULAR (V-shaped)
                H   H               bond angle  104.5 degrees

           Two lone pairs squeeze even harder.  107  ->  104.5
```

```
  THE SENTENCE THE EXAMINER WANTS:

     "All three have four electron pairs and are based on a TETRAHEDRAL
      arrangement, but as the number of lone pairs increases from 0 to 1
      to 2, the extra lone-pair repulsion pushes the bond pairs closer,
      so the bond angle falls from 109.5 to 107 to 104.5 degrees."
```

## Shapes with lone pairs — the full picture

```
  3 PAIRS , 1 LONE PAIR       ->  BENT (angular) , angle slightly under 120

              : :
               S                       SO2  (119.5) , SnCl2 , O3
              / \\
             O    O


  5 PAIRS , 1 LONE PAIR       ->  SEE-SAW

                  F
                  |
             ::-- S -- F               SF4
                  | \
                  F   F

           (the lone pair always takes an EQUATORIAL position)


  5 PAIRS , 2 LONE PAIRS      ->  T-SHAPED

                  F
                  |
             ::-- Cl --::              ClF3
                  |
                  F
                  |
                  F   (drawn flat: three F in a T)


  5 PAIRS , 3 LONE PAIRS      ->  LINEAR

                  F
                  |
           :: -- Xe -- ::              XeF2 , I3 -
                  |
                  F


  6 PAIRS , 1 LONE PAIR       ->  SQUARE PYRAMIDAL

                  F
                  |
             F -- Br -- F              BrF5
                 /|
                F |
                  ::


  6 PAIRS , 2 LONE PAIRS      ->  SQUARE PLANAR

                  ::
                  |
             F -- Xe -- F              XeF4
                / |  \
              F   |    F
                  ::
        (the two lone pairs go OPPOSITE each other, above and below)
```

> **TRAP:** In a trigonal bipyramid, lone pairs ALWAYS go to the
> **equatorial** positions, never axial. An equatorial position has only
> 2 neighbours at 90 degrees; an axial one has 3. Fewer close neighbours
> = less repulsion.

# TOPIC 12 — Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

VSEPR tells you the shape but never says **why** a bond forms.
Valence Bond Theory (Heitler, London, Pauling, Slater) answers that.

```
  THE IDEA:   A covalent bond forms when a HALF-FILLED atomic orbital of
              one atom OVERLAPS with a HALF-FILLED atomic orbital of
              another atom, and the two electrons have OPPOSITE spins.

              The greater the overlap, the stronger the bond.
```

```
  THE ENERGY PICTURE for H2 :

   ENERGY
     ^
     |   \
     |    \                        As the two H atoms come closer,
     |     \                       attraction wins, energy FALLS.
     |      \
   0 |-------\--------------------------  (separated atoms)
     |        \        ______
     |         \      /
     |          \____/
     |            |
     |         -435 kJ/mol   <---- MINIMUM = the stable bond
     |            |
     |         74 pm  =  bond length
     +------------------------------------------>  distance between nuclei

   Push them still closer and nucleus-nucleus repulsion sends the
   energy shooting back up.
```

## Sigma and pi bonds

```
  SIGMA (head-on overlap)              PI (sideways overlap)

     ( )----( )                            ( )
      s     s                               |         two p orbitals
                                            |         side by side
     ( )-- ==                              (===)
      s    p                                |
                                            |
     ==-- ==                                ( )
      p    p    (end to end)

  The overlap is ALONG the             The overlap is ABOVE and BELOW
  line joining the nuclei.             the internuclear axis.
```

```
  +----------------------------+---------------------------------------+
  |  SIGMA BOND                |  PI BOND                              |
  +----------------------------+---------------------------------------+
  |  Head-on (axial) overlap   |  Sideways (lateral) overlap           |
  |  STRONGER (more overlap)   |  WEAKER (less overlap)                |
  |  Can rotate freely         |  NO free rotation                     |
  |  Formed FIRST              |  Formed only AFTER a sigma bond       |
  |  Exists alone              |  Never exists alone                   |
  |  s-s , s-p , p-p possible  |  p-p , p-d only                       |
  +----------------------------+---------------------------------------+

  COUNTING RULE:
     single bond  =  1 sigma
     double bond  =  1 sigma + 1 pi
     triple bond  =  1 sigma + 2 pi
```

**Worked example**

```
  Q. Count the sigma and pi bonds in  CH2 = CH - C # N.

     C-H bonds        3 sigma
     C = C            1 sigma + 1 pi
     C - C            1 sigma
     C # N            1 sigma + 2 pi
     ------------------------------------
     TOTAL            6 sigma  and  3 pi
```

# TOPIC 13 — Hybridisation

Carbon's real configuration is 1s2 2s2 2p2 — only **two** unpaired electrons.
So carbon should form 2 bonds. But methane is CH4, with **four identical**
bonds. Something is wrong with the simple picture.

**Hybridisation is the fix.** Orbitals of nearly equal energy mix together
and produce a NEW set of identical orbitals.

```
  DEFINITION:  Hybridisation is the intermixing of atomic orbitals of
               slightly different energies belonging to the SAME atom to
               produce a NEW set of orbitals that are EQUAL in energy,
               IDENTICAL in shape, and oriented in a definite direction.
```

```
  CARBON, step by step:

   GROUND STATE       2s [^v]    2p [^][^][ ]        only 2 unpaired

   EXCITED STATE      2s [^ ]    2p [^][^][^]        now 4 unpaired
                          (one 2s electron promoted to 2p)

   HYBRIDISED         four sp3 orbitals
                      [^][^][^][^]   all identical, pointing to the
                                     corners of a tetrahedron
```

## The five hybridisations you must know

```
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   sp    =  1 s  +  1 p    ->  2 hybrid orbitals    LINEAR , 180 degrees
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                    50 % s character

         <===O====>  C  <====O===>       BeCl2 , BeF2 , C2H2 , CO2 , HgCl2

         Cl -------- Be -------- Cl


  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   sp2   =  1 s  +  2 p    ->  3 hybrid orbitals    TRIGONAL PLANAR
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                    120 degrees , 33 % s

                     F
                     |
                     B                  BF3 , BCl3 , AlCl3 , C2H4 ,
                    / \                 CO3 2- , NO3 - , SO3 , graphite
                   F   F


  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   sp3   =  1 s  +  3 p    ->  4 hybrid orbitals    TETRAHEDRAL
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                    109.5 degrees , 25 % s

                     H
                     |
                     C                  CH4 , CCl4 , NH3 , H2O , NH4+ ,
                   / | \                SO4 2- , diamond
                  H  H  H


  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   sp3d  =  1 s + 3 p + 1 d ->  5 hybrid orbitals   TRIGONAL BIPYRAMIDAL
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                    90 and 120 degrees

                    Cl                  PCl5 , PF5 , SF4 , ClF3 , XeF2
                     |
                Cl   |
                  \  |
                   P ---- Cl
                  / |
                Cl  |
                    Cl


  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
   sp3d2 = 1 s + 3 p + 2 d ->  6 hybrid orbitals    OCTAHEDRAL
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
                                                    all 90 degrees

                 F   F                  SF6 , PF6 - , XeF4 , BrF5 ,
                  \  |                  SiF6 2-
                   \ |
              F ---- S ---- F
                    /|
                   / |
                  F  F
```

## The fast way to find hybridisation (use this in every exam)

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                        1                                             |
  |     STERIC NUMBER  =  ---  [  V  +  M  -  C  +  A  ]                 |
  |            X           2                                             |
  |                                                                      |
  |     V = valence electrons of the CENTRAL atom                        |
  |     M = number of MONOVALENT atoms attached (H, F, Cl, Br, I)        |
  |     C = charge, if the species is a CATION  (subtract)               |
  |     A = charge, if the species is an ANION  (add)                    |
  |                                                                      |
  |     Oxygen and sulphur attached by DOUBLE bonds contribute ZERO.     |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

     X = 2  ->  sp        X = 5  ->  sp3d
     X = 3  ->  sp2       X = 6  ->  sp3d2
     X = 4  ->  sp3       X = 7  ->  sp3d3
```

**Worked examples with the trick**

```
  NH3     X = (1/2)[ 5 + 3 - 0 + 0 ]  =  4   ->  sp3
  H2O     X = (1/2)[ 6 + 2 - 0 + 0 ]  =  4   ->  sp3
  CH4     X = (1/2)[ 4 + 4 - 0 + 0 ]  =  4   ->  sp3
  BF3     X = (1/2)[ 3 + 3 - 0 + 0 ]  =  3   ->  sp2
  NH4+    X = (1/2)[ 5 + 4 - 1 + 0 ]  =  4   ->  sp3
  PCl5    X = (1/2)[ 5 + 5 - 0 + 0 ]  =  5   ->  sp3d
  SF6     X = (1/2)[ 6 + 6 - 0 + 0 ]  =  6   ->  sp3d2
  XeF4    X = (1/2)[ 8 + 4 - 0 + 0 ]  =  6   ->  sp3d2  (square planar)
  SO4 2-  X = (1/2)[ 6 + 0 - 0 + 2 ]  =  4   ->  sp3   (S=O count 0)
  CO3 2-  X = (1/2)[ 4 + 0 - 0 + 2 ]  =  3   ->  sp2
  ClF3    X = (1/2)[ 7 + 3 - 0 + 0 ]  =  5   ->  sp3d  (T-shaped)
  I3 -    X = (1/2)[ 7 + 2 - 0 + 1 ]  =  5   ->  sp3d  (linear)
```

> **TRAP:** Steric number gives the ELECTRON-PAIR geometry, not always the
> molecular SHAPE. XeF4 has sp3d2 (octahedral electron geometry) but its
> SHAPE is square planar because two positions hold lone pairs.

## Hybridisation and bond strength

```
  MORE s character  ->  the orbital is held CLOSER to the nucleus
                    ->  SHORTER, STRONGER bond and HIGHER electronegativity

     sp  (50 % s)  >  sp2 (33 % s)  >  sp3 (25 % s)

     Bond length:    C(sp)-H  <  C(sp2)-H  <  C(sp3)-H
     Acidity:        HC#CH  >  H2C=CH2  >  H3C-CH3
```

# TOPIC 14 — Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT): the idea

Valence Bond Theory says electrons stay in their own atoms' orbitals.
MOT says something bolder: **when atoms combine, their atomic orbitals
disappear and become NEW orbitals that belong to the whole molecule.**

```
  LCAO  =  Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals

     Two atomic orbitals combine in TWO ways:

     ADD them      ->  BONDING molecular orbital       (lower energy)
                       electron density BETWEEN the nuclei -> glue

     SUBTRACT them ->  ANTIBONDING molecular orbital   (higher energy)
                       a NODE between the nuclei -> pushes atoms apart
                       marked with a star:  sigma* , pi*
```

```
    ATOM A        MOLECULE         ATOM B

                  sigma*1s
                  --------          <-- ANTIBONDING (higher energy)
                 /        \
                /          \
    __1s__ -----            ----- __1s__
                \          /
                 \        /
                  --------          <-- BONDING (lower energy)
                  sigma 1s

    The bonding MO is lowered by roughly the same amount that the
    antibonding one is raised (in fact the antibonding is raised
    slightly MORE).
```

```
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  BONDING MO               |  ANTIBONDING MO                    |
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  Formed by ADDITION       |  Formed by SUBTRACTION             |
  |  LOW energy , stable      |  HIGH energy , unstable            |
  |  High electron density    |  NODE (zero density) between the   |
  |  between the nuclei       |  nuclei                            |
  |  Favours bond formation   |  Opposes bond formation            |
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
```

```
  CONDITIONS for atomic orbitals to combine:
     1. They must have comparable ENERGY.
     2. They must have the same SYMMETRY about the molecular axis.
     3. They must OVERLAP to a significant extent.
```

## The energy order — and the change after N2

```
  ORDER A  -  for  Li2 , Be2 , B2 , C2 , N2      (up to Z total = 14)

     sigma1s < sigma*1s < sigma2s < sigma*2s
              < ( pi2px = pi2py ) < sigma2pz
              < ( pi*2px = pi*2py ) < sigma*2pz


  ORDER B  -  for  O2 , F2 , Ne2                 (Z total 15 and above)

     sigma1s < sigma*1s < sigma2s < sigma*2s
              < sigma2pz
              < ( pi2px = pi2py )
              < ( pi*2px = pi*2py ) < sigma*2pz


  THE ONLY DIFFERENCE:  sigma2pz sits BELOW the pi orbitals for O2 and F2,
                        but ABOVE them for N2 and lighter.

  REASON:  in the lighter molecules, 2s and 2p energies are close, so they
           mix (s-p mixing) and push sigma2pz up above the pi orbitals.
```

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                          Nb  -  Na                                   |
  |        BOND ORDER  =  --------------                                 |
  |                              2                                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |    Nb = electrons in BONDING MOs , Na = electrons in ANTIBONDING MOs |
  |                                                                      |
  |    B.O. = 0  ->  molecule does NOT exist                             |
  |    B.O. 1 , 2 , 3  ->  single , double , triple bond                 |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  MAGNETISM:
      any UNPAIRED electron   ->  PARAMAGNETIC (attracted by a magnet)
      all electrons PAIRED    ->  DIAMAGNETIC  (weakly repelled)
```

## H2 (2 electrons)

```
   ENERGY
     ^
     |              _______              sigma*1s      (empty)
     |             /       \
     |   __^__ ---           --- __^__     1s      1s
     |             \       /
     |              __^v___              sigma 1s
     |
     H                H2                  H

   Configuration:  sigma1s2
   Nb = 2 , Na = 0     ->   B.O. = (2 - 0)/2 = 1
   All paired          ->   DIAMAGNETIC
   H2 EXISTS, with a single bond.
```

## He2 (4 electrons) — why helium is a loner

```
   ENERGY
     ^
     |              __^v___              sigma*1s
     |             /       \
     |   __^v__ --           -- __^v__     1s     1s
     |             \       /
     |              __^v___              sigma 1s
     |
     He               He2                He

   Configuration:  sigma1s2  sigma*1s2
   Nb = 2 , Na = 2     ->   B.O. = (2 - 2)/2 = 0

   BOND ORDER ZERO  ->  He2 DOES NOT EXIST.
   The antibonding pair completely cancels the bonding pair.
```

## Li2 (6 electrons) and Be2 (8 electrons)

```
   Li2 :  sigma1s2  sigma*1s2  sigma2s2
          Nb = 4 , Na = 2   ->   B.O. = 1        Li2 EXISTS , diamagnetic

   Be2 :  sigma1s2  sigma*1s2  sigma2s2  sigma*2s2
          Nb = 4 , Na = 4   ->   B.O. = 0        Be2 DOES NOT EXIST
```

## B2 (10 electrons) — paramagnetic, and MOT proves it

```
   ENERGY
     ^
     |     ________  ________               sigma*2pz    (empty)
     |
     |     ________  ________               pi*2px   pi*2py   (empty)
     |
     |     ___^____  ___^____               pi 2px   pi 2py
     |                                      ONE electron in each
     |                                      (Hund's rule)
     |     ________                         sigma 2pz    (empty)
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma*2s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 2s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma*1s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 1s

   Nb = 6 , Na = 4   ->   B.O. = (6 - 4)/2 = 1
   TWO UNPAIRED electrons  ->  PARAMAGNETIC
```

## C2 (12 electrons)

```
   Add two more electrons to B2, filling the pi orbitals:

     sigma1s2 sigma*1s2 sigma2s2 sigma*2s2 ( pi2px2 = pi2py2 )

   Nb = 8 , Na = 4   ->   B.O. = (8 - 4)/2 = 2
   All paired        ->   DIAMAGNETIC

   INTERESTING:  C2 has a DOUBLE bond made of TWO PI BONDS and no
                 sigma bond from the 2p set. MOT predicts this;
                 VBT never could.
```

## N2 (14 electrons) — the strongest common bond

```
   ENERGY
     ^
     |     ________                         sigma*2pz   (empty)
     |
     |     ________  ________               pi*2px  pi*2py  (empty)
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 2pz
     |
     |     ___^v___  ___^v___               pi 2px   pi 2py
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma*2s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 2s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma*1s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 1s

   Configuration:
     sigma1s2 sigma*1s2 sigma2s2 sigma*2s2 (pi2px2 = pi2py2) sigma2pz2

   Nb = 10 , Na = 4   ->   B.O. = (10 - 4)/2 = 3       TRIPLE BOND
   All paired         ->   DIAMAGNETIC

   Bond enthalpy 946 kJ/mol - that is why nitrogen gas is so unreactive.
```

## O2 (16 electrons) — THE star question of the chapter

```
   ENERGY
     ^
     |     ________                         sigma*2pz   (empty)
     |
     |     ___^____  ___^____               pi*2px   pi*2py
     |                                      ONE electron each, SAME spin
     |     ___^v___  ___^v___               pi 2px   pi 2py
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 2pz
     |                                      (note: BELOW the pi orbitals)
     |     ___^v___                         sigma*2s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 2s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma*1s
     |
     |     ___^v___                         sigma 1s

   Configuration:
     sigma1s2 sigma*1s2 sigma2s2 sigma*2s2 sigma2pz2
                  (pi2px2 = pi2py2) (pi*2px1 = pi*2py1)

   Nb = 10 , Na = 6   ->   B.O. = (10 - 6)/2 = 2       DOUBLE BOND

   TWO UNPAIRED ELECTRONS in pi*2px and pi*2py
                      ->  O2 IS PARAMAGNETIC
```

```
  WHY THIS IS FAMOUS:

     The simple Lewis structure  : O = O :  shows every electron paired,
     so it predicts O2 is DIAMAGNETIC.

     But liquid oxygen sticks to the poles of a magnet - it is clearly
     PARAMAGNETIC.

     Only MOT explains this, by putting one electron each into the two
     degenerate pi* orbitals (Hund's rule).

     ***THIS IS THE BIGGEST SINGLE TRIUMPH OF MOT AND A GUARANTEED
        EXAM QUESTION.***
```

## F2 (18 electrons)

```
   Two more electrons than O2, so the pi* orbitals are now FULL:

     ... sigma2pz2 (pi2px2 = pi2py2) (pi*2px2 = pi*2py2)

   Nb = 10 , Na = 8   ->   B.O. = (10 - 8)/2 = 1       SINGLE BOND
   All paired         ->   DIAMAGNETIC

   Ne2 would add two more into sigma*2pz  ->  B.O. = 0  ->  DOES NOT EXIST.
```

## The oxygen family of ions (asked constantly)

```
  O2 +     15 electrons   pi* has 1 electron     B.O. = 2.5   paramagnetic
  O2       16 electrons   pi* has 2 electrons    B.O. = 2.0   paramagnetic
  O2 -     17 electrons   pi* has 3 electrons    B.O. = 1.5   paramagnetic
  O2 2-    18 electrons   pi* has 4 electrons    B.O. = 1.0   DIAmagnetic

  Bond length order:      O2+  <  O2  <  O2-  <  O2 2-
  Stability order:        O2+  >  O2  >  O2-  >  O2 2-
```

> **TRAP:** When you remove an electron from O2 you take it from the
> HIGHEST occupied orbital, which is pi*, an ANTIBONDING orbital.
> Removing an antibonding electron makes the bond STRONGER, not weaker.
> That is why O2+ has a higher bond order than O2.

# TOPIC 16 — Hydrogen bonding

```
  A HYDROGEN BOND forms when hydrogen is attached to a SMALL, HIGHLY
  ELECTRONEGATIVE atom - only  F , O  or  N.

  The H becomes strongly d+ and is attracted to a lone pair on the
  F, O or N of a NEIGHBOURING molecule.

           d-    d+          d-    d+
            O --- H  . . . .  O --- H
           /                 /
          H                 H
                (the dotted line is the hydrogen bond)

  STRENGTH:  about 10 to 40 kJ/mol
             much weaker than a covalent bond (about 400 kJ/mol)
             but much stronger than ordinary van der Waals forces.
```

## Two types

```
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  INTERMOLECULAR                    |  INTRAMOLECULAR
  (between DIFFERENT molecules)     |  (within the SAME molecule)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Molecules get linked into a       |  A ring closes inside the molecule.
  big associated cluster.           |  Molecules stay separate.
                                    |
  RAISES boiling point              |  LOWERS boiling point (compared with
  RAISES melting point              |  the intermolecular case)
  INCREASES solubility in water     |  DECREASES solubility in water
                                    |
  H2O , HF , NH3 , alcohols ,       |  o-nitrophenol , salicylaldehyde ,
  carboxylic acids , p-nitrophenol  |  o-hydroxybenzoic acid
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
```

```
  ORTHO versus PARA NITROPHENOL - the classic question

     o-nitrophenol                     p-nitrophenol

          O - H                             O - H
         /    :                            /
        C      :   INTRAmolecular         C
       / \     :   H-bond closes a       / \
      C   C - N     ring inside the     C   C  . . . H-bond to the NEXT
          |  // \\  molecule                |       molecule
          |  O   O                          |       (INTERmolecular)
                                        C - NO2

     o-nitrophenol : molecules stay single  -> LOW boiling point,
                     steam volatile, less soluble in water
     p-nitrophenol : molecules link up      -> HIGH boiling point,
                     not steam volatile, more soluble in water
```

## What hydrogen bonding explains

```
  1. H2O is a LIQUID at room temperature but H2S is a GAS,
     even though H2S is heavier.
        -> water has hydrogen bonding, H2S does not (S is not
           electronegative enough).

  2. HF has a much higher boiling point than HCl.

  3. ICE FLOATS on water. In ice, hydrogen bonds hold the molecules in an
     open cage-like structure with lots of empty space, so ice is LESS
     dense than liquid water.

  4. Alcohols and carboxylic acids dissolve in water; hydrocarbons do not.

  5. The two strands of DNA are held together by hydrogen bonds.
```

> **TRAP:** HCl does NOT have hydrogen bonding. Chlorine is electronegative
> but it is too BIG, so its charge is spread out and the attraction is weak.
> Only F, O and N qualify.

# TOPIC 17 — Metallic bonding (short but examinable)

```
  ELECTRON-SEA MODEL:

     Metal atoms lose their valence electrons, leaving positive "kernels"
     sitting in a regular lattice. The freed electrons are DELOCALISED -
     they belong to the whole crystal and move about freely.

        e-        e-         e-
           (+)       (+)        (+)
        e-        e-         e-
           (+)       (+)        (+)
        e-        e-         e-

     "Positive kernels immersed in a sea of mobile electrons."
```

```
  IT EXPLAINS:

   ELECTRICAL CONDUCTIVITY  -  free electrons drift when a voltage
                               is applied
   THERMAL CONDUCTIVITY     -  free electrons carry kinetic energy
   METALLIC LUSTRE          -  free electrons absorb and re-emit light
   MALLEABILITY / DUCTILITY -  layers of kernels can slide over one
                               another without breaking the bonding,
                               because the electron sea flows with them
   HIGH MELTING POINT       -  strong attraction between kernels and
                               the electron sea
```

```
  Metallic bond strength INCREASES with:
      more valence electrons per atom      (Na < Mg < Al)
      smaller size of the kernel
```
