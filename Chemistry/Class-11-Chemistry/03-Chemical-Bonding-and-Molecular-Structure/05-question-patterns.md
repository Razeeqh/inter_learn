# Chemical Bonding — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The molecules change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — STATE THE OCTET RULE AND ITS LIMITATIONS            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the octet rule?" / "Give the limitations of the
            octet rule."
  METHOD    1. Definition: atoms gain, lose or share electrons to get
               EIGHT electrons in the valence shell (a noble-gas
               arrangement).
            2. Then the THREE limitations, each with an example:
                  INCOMPLETE octet  -  BeCl2 (4) , BF3 (6)
                  EXPANDED octet    -  PCl5 (10) , SF6 (12)
                  ODD electron      -  NO (11) , NO2 (17)
            3. Add: it says nothing about SHAPE or ENERGY.
  TRAP      An example is worth a mark. Never write the limitation
            without naming a molecule.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — DRAW A LEWIS DOT STRUCTURE                       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the Lewis structure of ..." for CO2, NH3, H2O, SO2,
            HNO3, CO3 2-, NO3 -, O3, SO4 2-.
  METHOD    1. Count TOTAL valence electrons.
                 negative ion -> ADD the charge
                 positive ion -> SUBTRACT the charge
            2. Central atom = LEAST electronegative. H is NEVER central.
            3. Draw single bonds to every outer atom.
            4. Fill lone pairs on the OUTER atoms first.
            5. Short of an octet on the centre? Make a double or triple
               bond by pulling a lone pair in.
  TRAP      For an ION, draw square brackets round the whole structure
            and write the charge outside. Marks are lost for skipping it.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — CALCULATE THE FORMAL CHARGE                         2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the formal charge on ..." / "Which structure is
            more stable?"
  METHOD              FC  =  V  -  L  -  ( B / 2 )

               V = valence electrons of the FREE atom
               L = lone-pair electrons on that atom
               B = electrons in the bonds to that atom
  CHECK     The sum of all formal charges must equal the overall charge
            (zero for a neutral molecule).
  TRAP      B is the TOTAL bonding electrons, not the number of bonds.
            A double bond gives B = 4, not 2.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — IONIC BOND FORMATION AND FAVOURING FACTORS      4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the formation of an ionic bond with an example" /
            "What factors favour the formation of an ionic bond?"
  METHOD    1. Show the electron transfer with configurations
                  Na (2,8,1) -> Na+ (2,8) + e-
                  Cl (2,8,7) + e- -> Cl- (2,8,8)
            2. Say the ions are held by ELECTROSTATIC attraction.
            3. Mention the 3-D LATTICE, not a molecule.
            4. The FOUR factors:
                  low ionisation enthalpy of the metal
                  high negative electron gain enthalpy of the non-metal
                  high lattice enthalpy of the product
                  large electronegativity difference (about 1.7+)
  TRAP      Never say "an NaCl molecule". Solid NaCl is a giant lattice.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — LATTICE ENTHALPY AND THE BORN-HABER CYCLE       4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define lattice enthalpy" / "Explain the Born-Haber cycle
            for NaCl."
  METHOD    1. Definition: energy needed to separate one mole of a solid
               ionic compound into GASEOUS IONS.
            2. State the trend:  higher charge and smaller ions -> higher
               lattice enthalpy.   MgO > NaF > NaCl > NaBr > NaI
            3. Draw the cycle box and write

                dH f  =  S  +  IE  +  (1/2) D  +  EA  +  U

            4. Say WHY we need it: lattice enthalpy cannot be measured
               directly, and enthalpy is a state function (Hess's law).
  TRAP      Watch the SIGNS. S, IE and D are positive (endothermic);
            EA and the lattice-formation term are negative.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — BOND PARAMETERS                                     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain bond length, bond angle, bond enthalpy and bond
            order."
  METHOD    Define all four with a number for each:
                length   C-C 154 , C=C 134 , C#C 120 pm
                angle    CH4 109.5 , NH3 107 , H2O 104.5
                enthalpy H-H 435 , O=O 498 , N#N 946 kJ/mol
                order    H2 1 , O2 2 , N2 3
            Finish with the master line:
                bond order UP -> length DOWN -> enthalpy UP
  TRAP      Bond enthalpy is always POSITIVE (energy to BREAK a bond).
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — RESONANCE STRUCTURES AND RESONANCE ENERGY           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the resonance structures of ..." (O3, CO3 2-, NO3 -,
            SO2, SO3, benzene) or "Why are all the bond lengths equal?"
  METHOD    1. Draw ALL the equivalent structures joined by  <->
            2. Say the real molecule is the resonance HYBRID.
            3. Compute the fractional bond order:
                  bond order = total bonds / number of positions
                  CO3 2- -> 4/3 = 1.33 ,  O3 -> 3/2 = 1.5
            4. Define resonance energy = extra stability from
               delocalisation.
  TRAP      Only ELECTRONS move between resonance structures. The atoms
            stay exactly where they are. Never draw an equilibrium arrow.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — WHY IS THIS MOLECULE POLAR / NON-POLAR?             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Although the B-F bond is polar, BF3 has zero dipole moment.
            Why?" / "Why is CO2 non-polar but H2O polar?"
  METHOD    1. Give the SHAPE first (linear, planar, bent, pyramidal).
            2. Say whether the bond dipoles are symmetric.
            3. Symmetric -> vector sum ZERO -> non-polar.
               Not symmetric -> dipoles ADD -> polar.
            4. Quote the numbers: CO2 = 0 , H2O = 1.85 D ,
               BF3 = 0 , NH3 = 1.47 D
  TRAP      The question is about SYMMETRY, not electronegativity.
            Writing "B-F is polar so BF3 is polar" scores zero.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — DIPOLE MOMENT NUMERICAL / % IONIC CHARACTER      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A dipole moment in debye and a bond length in angstrom.
  METHOD    1. mu(100 % ionic) = e x d
                 use e = 4.8 x 10^-10 esu and d in cm
                 result divided by 10^-18 gives debye directly
            2.                     mu (observed)
               % ionic = 100 x  --------------------
                                 mu (100 % ionic)
  TRAP      Convert the bond length: 1 angstrom = 10^-8 cm.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — FAJANS' RULES / ORDER OF COVALENT CHARACTER     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Fajans' rules" / "Why is LiCl more covalent than
            NaCl?" / "Arrange in order of covalent character."
  METHOD    Four rules - covalent character rises when
                 cation SMALL , anion LARGE , charge HIGH ,
                 cation has an 18-electron pseudo noble-gas shell
            Then apply the ONE that changes across the given series.
            Ready-made orders:
                 LiI > LiBr > LiCl > LiF          (anion size)
                 LiCl > NaCl > KCl                (cation size)
                 AlCl3 > MgCl2 > NaCl             (cation charge)
                 CuCl > NaCl , AgCl > KCl         (18-electron shell)
  TRAP      Say which rule you are using. "Because of Fajans' rules"
            alone does not score.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — STATE THE POSTULATES OF VSEPR THEORY        4 or *** 8 ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the postulates of VSEPR theory."
  METHOD    Write all of these as numbered points:
              1. shape depends on the number of valence electron pairs
              2. pairs repel and go as far apart as possible
              3. valence shell treated as a sphere
              4. a LONE pair takes more space than a bond pair
              5. lp-lp > lp-bp > bp-bp
              6. a multiple bond counts as ONE pair
              7. electronegativity of outer atoms affects the angle
  NOTE      Almost always the first half of the 8-mark question.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — PREDICT THE SHAPE USING VSEPR              2 , 4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Predict the shape of ..." for anything at all.
  METHOD    1. Count total electron pairs on the central atom.
                  X = (1/2)[V + M - C + A]
            2. lone pairs = X - (number of atoms attached)
            3. Read off the table:
                  2 pairs  linear 180
                  3 pairs  0 lp trigonal planar 120 , 1 lp bent
                  4 pairs  0 lp tetrahedral 109.5 , 1 lp pyramidal 107 ,
                           2 lp bent 104.5
                  5 pairs  0 lp trigonal bipyramidal , 1 lp see-saw ,
                           2 lp T-shaped , 3 lp linear
                  6 pairs  0 lp octahedral , 1 lp square pyramidal ,
                           2 lp square planar
            4. DRAW the shape and write the bond angle.
  TRAP      The shape is named from the ATOMS only, never from the lone
            pairs. XeF4 is "square planar", not "octahedral".
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — WHY 109.5 -> 107 -> 104.5 ?                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the bond angle of NH3 less than that of CH4?" /
            "Explain the decreasing bond angle in CH4, NH3, H2O."
  METHOD    1. All three have FOUR electron pairs -> tetrahedral base.
            2. Count lone pairs: 0 , 1 , 2.
            3. Quote lp-lp > lp-bp > bp-bp.
            4. Each lone pair squeezes the bond pairs, cutting the angle
               by about 2.5 degrees.
  TRAP      Do not say "NH3 is not tetrahedral". Its ELECTRON GEOMETRY
            IS tetrahedral; only the SHAPE is pyramidal.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — ORDER OF BOND ANGLES DOWN A GROUP               objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange NH3, PH3, AsH3, SbH3 in order of bond angle."
  METHOD    Down a group the central atom gets BIGGER and LESS
            electronegative, so bond pairs sit further out and repel
            less  ->  the angle SHRINKS.
                NH3 107 > PH3 93.5 > AsH3 92 > SbH3 91
                H2O 104.5 > H2S 92 > H2Se 91 > H2Te 90
            More electronegative outer atom also SHRINKS the angle:
                NH3 107 > NF3 102
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — SIGMA VERSUS PI BOND                               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between sigma and pi bonds."
  METHOD    Table with at least FIVE rows:
                overlap type (head-on vs sideways)
                strength (sigma stronger)
                rotation (free vs none)
                independent existence (sigma yes, pi never alone)
                which orbitals can do it
            Add the counting rule:
                single 1 sigma , double 1 sigma + 1 pi ,
                triple 1 sigma + 2 pi
  TRAP      Answer in TABLE form. A paragraph loses presentation marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — COUNT THE SIGMA AND PI BONDS                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many sigma and pi bonds are present in ...?"
  METHOD    Draw the structure, then
                sigma = number of bond LINES between atoms
                pi    = (double bonds) + 2 x (triple bonds)
            Ready answers: benzene 12 sigma 3 pi , C2H4 5 and 1 ,
            C2H2 3 and 2 , CO2 2 and 2 , N2 1 and 2.
  TRAP      Do not forget the C-H sigma bonds. They are the ones people
            miss in benzene.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — WHAT IS HYBRIDISATION? EXPLAIN THE TYPES   *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is hybridisation? Explain sp, sp2, sp3, sp3d and sp3d2
            with examples."
  METHOD    1. Definition + characteristics of hybrid orbitals.
            2. A TABLE with five rows:
                  type | orbitals mixed | number | shape | angle | example
            3. DRAW all five shapes.
            4. Add the steric-number shortcut as a bonus line.
  NOTE      *** ONE OF THE THREE LONG ANSWERS THIS CHAPTER SUPPLIES ***
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — FIND THE HYBRIDISATION OF A CENTRAL ATOM        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the hybridisation of ... in ...?" - the single most
            common objective question in the chapter.
  METHOD              1
              X  =  ---  [  V  +  M  -  C  +  A  ]
                     2
              V = valence electrons of the CENTRAL atom
              M = monovalent atoms attached (H, F, Cl, Br, I)
              C = charge if a CATION (subtract)
              A = charge if an ANION (add)
              double-bonded O or S contributes ZERO

              2 sp , 3 sp2 , 4 sp3 , 5 sp3d , 6 sp3d2 , 7 sp3d3
  TRAP      This gives the ELECTRON geometry. To name the SHAPE you must
            still subtract the lone pairs.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — s CHARACTER, BOND LENGTH AND ACIDITY            objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange in order of C-H bond length / acidity / bond
            strength" for ethane, ethene, ethyne.
  METHOD    s character:  sp 50 % > sp2 33 % > sp3 25 %
            MORE s  ->  electrons held CLOSER to the nucleus
                    ->  SHORTER, STRONGER bond, HIGHER electronegativity,
                        MORE acidic hydrogen
            Bond length:  C2H2 < C2H4 < C2H6
            Acidity:      HC#CH > H2C=CH2 > H3C-CH3
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — EXPLAIN MOLECULAR ORBITAL THEORY           *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the molecular orbital theory" / "What are bonding
            and antibonding molecular orbitals?"
  METHOD    1. LCAO: atomic orbitals combine into MOLECULAR orbitals
               belonging to the whole molecule.
            2. Addition -> BONDING (low energy, density between nuclei)
               Subtraction -> ANTIBONDING (high energy, node between)
            3. Number of MOs = number of AOs combined.
            4. Conditions: comparable energy, same symmetry, proper
               overlap.
            5. Filling: Aufbau, Pauli, Hund.
            6. Add the bonding vs antibonding comparison table.
  NOTE      Usually joined to Pattern 21 to make the full 8 marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — DRAW AN MO DIAGRAM AND GET THE BOND ORDER  4 or *** 8 ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the MO diagram of O2 / N2 and calculate the bond
            order" / "Explain the paramagnetism of O2."
  METHOD    1. Count the TOTAL electrons.
            2. Pick the energy order:
                 up to N2 (14 e-)   ->  pi2p BELOW sigma2pz
                 O2 and beyond      ->  sigma2pz BELOW pi2p
            3. Fill from the bottom, using Hund's rule in the pi levels.
            4. Write the configuration.
            5. B.O. = (Nb - Na)/2
            6. State the magnetic behaviour:
                 unpaired electron -> PARAMAGNETIC
                 all paired        -> DIAMAGNETIC
  TRAP      The order SWAP at O2 is the single most common mistake in
            the chapter. Write down which order you are using first.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — BOND ORDER OF O2 / N2 AND THEIR IONS               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the bond order of O2, O2+, O2-, O2 2-" /
            "Arrange them in order of bond length or stability."
  METHOD    Start from O2 (B.O. 2) and step by 0.5:
                 O2 +   2.5      O2     2.0
                 O2 -   1.5      O2 2-  1.0
            Bond length:  O2+ < O2 < O2- < O2 2-
            Stability:    O2+ > O2 > O2- > O2 2-
            Only O2 2- is DIAMAGNETIC.
  TRAP      Removing an electron from O2 takes it out of an ANTIBONDING
            pi* orbital, so the bond gets STRONGER, not weaker.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — WHICH SPECIES DOES NOT EXIST?                   objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does He2 not exist?" / "Which of these does not exist?"
  METHOD    Compute B.O. = (Nb - Na)/2.
            B.O. = 0  ->  the molecule does NOT exist.
            The three standard cases: He2 , Be2 , Ne2.
            (But He2+ has B.O. 0.5 and DOES exist, weakly.)
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — WHICH IS PARAMAGNETIC?                          objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which of the following is paramagnetic?"
  METHOD    Write the MO configuration and look for unpaired electrons.
            In the second period only  B2  and  O2  are paramagnetic.
            Also paramagnetic: NO , NO2 , O2+ , O2- , H2+ , He2+ .
            Diamagnetic: H2 , Li2 , C2 , N2 , F2 , CO , CN - , NO + ,
            O2 2- .
  TRAP      A simple Lewis structure wrongly predicts O2 to be
            diamagnetic. Only MOT gets it right - say so, it earns a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — HETERONUCLEAR DIATOMICS (CO, NO, CN-, NO+)      objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The bond order of CO is ..." / "Which pair has the same
            bond order?"
  METHOD    Count the TOTAL electrons and use the homonuclear diagram
            for that electron count.
                 14 electrons  ->  N2 , CO , CN - , NO +  ->  B.O. 3
                                   all DIAMAGNETIC
                 15 electrons  ->  NO , O2 +  ->  B.O. 2.5 , paramagnetic
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — HYDROGEN BONDING: TYPES AND EFFECTS             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is hydrogen bonding?" / "Explain intermolecular and
            intramolecular hydrogen bonding."
  METHOD    1. Definition: H attached to F, O or N is attracted to a
               lone pair on another F, O or N.
            2. Draw the dotted-line picture.
            3. Two types, with examples and effects:
                 INTERmolecular -> raises boiling point, raises
                    solubility in water (H2O, HF, NH3, alcohols,
                    p-nitrophenol)
                 INTRAmolecular -> lowers boiling point, lowers
                    solubility (o-nitrophenol, salicylaldehyde)
  TRAP      Only F, O and N qualify. HCl has NO hydrogen bonding because
            chlorine, though electronegative, is too large.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — GIVE REASONS (the hydrogen-bonding one-liners)  2 marks each
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give reasons: H2O is a liquid but H2S is a gas ..."
  METHOD    Ready-made answers:
              H2O liquid, H2S gas    -> H-bonding in water, none in H2S
              HF boils above HCl     -> H-bonding in HF
              Ice floats             -> open cage structure, less dense
              o- vs p-nitrophenol    -> intra vs inter H-bonding
              Alcohols dissolve in   -> H-bond with water molecules
                water
              Boiling point order    -> H2O > HF > NH3 > H2S
  TRAP      Always NAME hydrogen bonding explicitly. "Because of strong
            forces" earns nothing.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — METALLIC BONDING / ELECTRON-SEA MODEL              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the metallic bond" / "Why are metals malleable and
            good conductors?"
  METHOD    1. Positive KERNELS in a lattice, valence electrons
               DELOCALISED as a "sea".
            2. Draw the kernel-and-electron picture.
            3. Explain conductivity, thermal conduction, lustre,
               malleability, ductility, high melting point.
            4. Strength rises with more valence electrons and smaller
               kernels:  Na < Mg < Al
```
