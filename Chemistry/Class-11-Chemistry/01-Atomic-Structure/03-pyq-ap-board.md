## Topic: Particles, isotopes, isobars

### Q A1
What are isotopes and isobars? Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ISOTOPES  -  atoms of the SAME element with the same atomic number Z
               but DIFFERENT mass number A.
               Example:  H-1 , H-2 , H-3      (or Cl-35 and Cl-37)

  ISOBARS   -  atoms of DIFFERENT elements having the SAME mass number A
               but different atomic number Z.
               Example:  Ar-40 , K-40 , Ca-40
```

---

### Q A2
Calculate the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in `35 Cl 17` (A=35, Z=17).

**ANSWER:**
```
  Protons   =  Z       =  17
  Electrons =  Z       =  17     (neutral atom)
  Neutrons  =  A - Z   =  35 - 17  =  18
```

### Q A3
An ion carries a charge of 2- and has 18 electrons and mass number 32.
Find its atomic number and identify it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Charge 2-  means it GAINED 2 electrons.

  Z  =  electrons  -  2  =  18 - 2  =  16       ->  SULPHUR

  So the ion is  S2-  , with 32 - 16 = 16 neutrons.
```

### Q A6

**ANSWER:**

### Q A10
Why is the de Broglie wavelength of a moving cricket ball not observable?

**ANSWER:** Because the ball's mass is very large, `lambda = h / mv` comes out
around 10^-34 m — far too small to be detected by any instrument.
The wave nature becomes significant only for very light particles like electrons.

### Q A14
State the Pauli exclusion principle.

**ANSWER:** No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers the
same. As a result, an orbital can hold at most **two** electrons, and they must
have **opposite spins**.

---

### Q A15
State Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.

**ANSWER:** Pairing of electrons in the orbitals of the same subshell does not
take place until each orbital of that subshell has got **one electron each**,
all with **parallel spins**.

### Q A29
What is the significance of the negative sign in the expression
`E(n) = -13.6 Z^2 / n^2 eV` ?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The energy of a FREE electron, infinitely far from the nucleus, is
  taken as ZERO. When the electron is pulled in by the nucleus, energy
  is RELEASED, so a bound electron has LESS than zero energy.

  The negative sign therefore shows the electron is BOUND to the nucleus.
  The more negative the value, the more tightly bound and more stable
  the electron is.
```

### Q B1
Explain the significance of the four quantum numbers.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  PRINCIPAL  n  =  1, 2, 3, ...
      Gives the SIZE of the orbital and the main ENERGY level (shell).
      Maximum electrons in the shell = 2 n^2.

  2.  AZIMUTHAL  l  =  0 to (n - 1)
      Gives the SHAPE of the orbital (the subshell).
      l = 0 -> s ,  1 -> p ,  2 -> d ,  3 -> f

  3.  MAGNETIC  m  =  -l to +l
      Gives the ORIENTATION of the orbital in space.
      Number of orbitals in a subshell = (2l + 1).

  4.  SPIN  s  =  +1/2 or -1/2
      Gives the direction of SPIN of the electron.
```

Explain the shapes of s, p and d orbitals.

**ANSWER:**
```
  s ORBITAL  ( l = 0 )
        SPHERICAL and symmetrical about the nucleus.
        Only ONE orientation, so only ONE s orbital per shell.
        The probability is the same in every direction.

            ....
         ..      ..
        .    ()    .
        .          .
         ..      ..
            ....
```

### Q B3
Explain Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle and Hund's rule.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AUFBAU PRINCIPLE
     Electrons are filled into orbitals in order of INCREASING energy,
     lowest first. The order is decided by the (n + l) rule:
        lower (n + l) fills first
        if (n + l) is equal, the lower n fills first.
     Order:  1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s ...

  PAULI EXCLUSION PRINCIPLE
     No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers alike.
     Therefore one orbital holds a maximum of TWO electrons, and they must
     have OPPOSITE spins.

  HUND'S RULE OF MAXIMUM MULTIPLICITY
     Electron pairing in orbitals of the same subshell does not start until
     every orbital of that subshell has one electron, all with parallel spins.

        RIGHT:   +---+ +---+ +---+       WRONG:  +----+ +---+ +---+
                 | ^ | | ^ | | ^ |               | ^v | | ^ | |   |
                 +---+ +---+ +---+               +----+ +---+ +---+
```

### Q B4
What are the drawbacks of Rutherford's atomic model?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  STABILITY.  According to classical electromagnetic theory, a charged
      particle moving in a circular path must radiate energy continuously.
      The electron would therefore lose energy, spiral inward and fall into
      the nucleus within about 10^-8 seconds. The atom would collapse.
      But atoms ARE stable, so the model is wrong.

  2.  SPECTRUM.  Continuous loss of energy would produce a CONTINUOUS
      spectrum. In reality atoms give a LINE spectrum.

  3.  It gave no information about the ARRANGEMENT of electrons around
      the nucleus.
```

### Q B5
Explain the photoelectric effect and Einstein's explanation of it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  OBSERVATIONS:
    1.  Electrons are ejected instantly, with no time delay.
    2.  Below a threshold frequency nu(0), no electrons are ejected however
        intense the light is.
    3.  The kinetic energy of the ejected electrons depends only on the
        FREQUENCY, not on the intensity.
    4.  The NUMBER of electrons ejected depends on the INTENSITY.

  EINSTEIN'S EXPLANATION:
    Light travels as packets called PHOTONS, each carrying energy h nu.
    ONE photon is absorbed by ONE electron. Part of that energy is used to
    escape the surface (the work function), and the rest becomes kinetic
    energy.

           h nu   =   h nu(0)   +   (1/2) m v^2
```

### Q B8
Why do Cr and Cu have unexpected electronic configurations?

**ANSWER:**
```
  EXPECTED             ACTUAL
  Cr :  3d4 4s2   ->   3d5  4s1
  Cu :  3d9 4s2   ->   3d10 4s1

  REASONS  (write BOTH for full marks):

  1.  SYMMETRY
      Exactly half-filled (d5) and completely filled (d10) subshells have a
      symmetrical distribution of electrons, and symmetry gives extra
      stability.

  2.  EXCHANGE ENERGY
      Electrons with the same spin lying in different orbitals of the same
      subshell can exchange their positions. Every such exchange releases
      energy. The greater the number of possible exchanges, the greater the
      exchange energy released, and the more stable the atom.
      The d5 and d10 arrangements allow the maximum number of exchanges.
```

### Q B12
Describe the cathode ray discharge tube experiment. What are the properties
of cathode rays and what do they prove?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE EXPERIMENT

     A sealed glass tube has two metal electrodes. The air is pumped out
     until the pressure is about 0.01 mm of mercury, and a potential of
     about 10000 V is applied. A stream of invisible rays then travels
     from the CATHODE to the ANODE. These are CATHODE RAYS.

    +------------------------------------------------+
    |  CATHODE                              ANODE     |
    |   (-)                                   (+)     |
    |  ####  ~~~~~~ CATHODE RAYS ~~~~~~~~~>  ####     |
    +-----|-----------------------------------|-------+
          +---------------| |-----------------+
                     HIGH VOLTAGE

  PROPERTIES

     1.  They travel in STRAIGHT LINES - a solid object placed in the
         path casts a sharp shadow.
     2.  They consist of MATERIAL PARTICLES - they rotate a light
         paddle wheel placed in their path.
     3.  They are NEGATIVELY CHARGED - in an electric field they are
         deflected TOWARDS the POSITIVE plate.
     4.  They cause FLUORESCENCE on the glass and on a ZnS screen.
     5.  They produce X-rays when they strike a heavy metal target.
     6.  Their nature is INDEPENDENT of the gas in the tube and of the
         material of the cathode.

  CONCLUSION

     Because property 6 holds for every gas and every electrode metal,
     the same negatively charged particle must be present in ALL matter.
     That particle is the ELECTRON.

     Thomson measured its charge-to-mass ratio:
         e / m  =  1.758 x 10^11  C / kg
```

### Q B13
Describe Millikan's oil drop experiment and state its conclusion.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE SET-UP

    +-------------------------------------------+
    |   ####################################### |  <- UPPER PLATE  (+)
    |            . <- charged oil drop          |
    |            |  electric force UP  (q E)    |
    |            v  gravity DOWN     (m g)      |
    |   ####################################### |  <- LOWER PLATE  (-)
    +-------------------------------------------+
       X-rays ionise the air so the drop picks
       up a whole number of electrons

  THE METHOD

     1.  Oil is sprayed as a fine mist; one drop falls through a hole.
     2.  With the field OFF the drop falls at a steady terminal speed;
         from that speed its MASS is calculated.
     3.  X-rays ionise the air and the drop picks up n electrons,
         so it now carries charge q = n e.
     4.  The field E is adjusted until the drop HANGS STILL. Then

                q E  =  m g          so       q  =  m g / E

  THE RESULT

     Every charge measured on thousands of drops came out as a WHOLE
     NUMBER multiple of one basic value:

         1.602x10^-19 , 3.204x10^-19 , 4.806x10^-19 , ...   never 1.5 e

  CONCLUSION

     Charge is QUANTISED:   q = n e   with   e = 1.602 x 10^-19 C .
     Combining this with Thomson's e/m gives the mass of the electron:

         m  =  1.602x10^-19 / 1.758x10^11  =  9.11 x 10^-31  kg
```

### Q B15
The work function of a metal is 2.5 eV. Light of wavelength 350 nm falls on
it. Calculate the kinetic energy and the velocity of the emitted electron.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1   Energy of the incident photon.
           350 nm = 3500 A

                     12400        12400
           E(eV)  = --------  =  --------  =  3.543  eV
                     lambda        3500

  STEP 2   Kinetic energy

           KE  =  E  -  W0  =  3.543  -  2.5  =  1.043  eV
```

### Q C1  *** ASKED MOST OFTEN ***
State the postulates of Bohr's model of the atom. Explain how it accounts for
the hydrogen spectrum. What are its limitations?

**COMPLETE MODEL ANSWER**

```
  PART 1 — THE POSTULATES                                    [ 3 marks ]

  1.  The electron revolves around the nucleus only in certain fixed
      circular paths of definite radius and definite energy. These are
      called ORBITS, SHELLS or STATIONARY STATES.

  2.  As long as the electron stays in a stationary state it does NOT
      radiate energy. Its energy stays constant, so the atom is stable.
      (This is the postulate that repairs Rutherford's collapse problem.)

  3.  The angular momentum of the electron is QUANTISED. It can only take
      values that are whole-number multiples of h / 2 pi :

                                n h
                m v r    =    --------          n = 1, 2, 3, ...
                                2 pi

      Here m = mass of the electron, v = its velocity, r = the radius of
      the orbit, and n is called the principal quantum number.

  4.  Energy is emitted or absorbed only when the electron JUMPS from one
      stationary state to another. The energy of the photon involved is

                delta E   =   E2  -  E1   =   h nu


  PART 2 — THE RESULTS THAT FOLLOW                           [ 1 mark  ]

      +--------------------------------------------------------------+
      |                  0.529 n^2                                   |
      |   RADIUS  r(n) = ------------  angstrom                      |
      |                       Z                                      |
      |                                                              |
      |                  - 13.6 Z^2            - 2.18 x 10^-18 Z^2   |
      |   ENERGY  E(n) = ------------  eV  =  ---------------------- J
      |                       n^2                     n^2            |
      |                                                              |
      |                                    Z                         |
      |   VELOCITY v(n) = 2.188 x 10^6 x  ---  m/s                   |
      |                                    n                         |
      +--------------------------------------------------------------+

      The energy is NEGATIVE because a free electron at infinity is
      taken to have zero energy, and a bound electron has less than that.


  PART 3 — HOW IT EXPLAINS THE HYDROGEN SPECTRUM             [ 2 marks ]

      1.  When energy is supplied (heat or electric discharge), the
          electron ABSORBS a quantum and jumps to a higher orbit. The
          atom is now in an EXCITED state.

      2.  The excited state is unstable, so within about 10^-8 s the
          electron falls back to a lower orbit and EMITS a photon of

                h nu   =   E(higher)  -  E(lower)

      3.  Because the orbits have FIXED energies, only certain FIXED
          differences are possible, so only certain FIXED frequencies
          can be emitted. That is exactly why hydrogen gives a LINE
          spectrum and not a continuous band.

      4.  Different landing levels give the different SERIES:

              falls to n = 1  ->  LYMAN      (ultraviolet)
              falls to n = 2  ->  BALMER     (visible)
              falls to n = 3  ->  PASCHEN    (infrared)
              falls to n = 4  ->  BRACKETT   (infrared)
              falls to n = 5  ->  PFUND      (far infrared)

      5.  Combining E(n) with delta E = h nu reproduces the Rydberg
          equation, which had previously been only an empirical fit:

                 1              +-   1         1   -+
              --------  =  R Z^2|  ------  -  ----- |
               lambda           +-  n1^2       n2^2-+

          DRAW THE ENERGY LEVEL DIAGRAM - it carries marks:

             n = inf  ..............  E = 0
             n = 5    --------------  E = -0.54 eV
             n = 4    --------------  E = -0.85 eV
             n = 3    --------------  E = -1.51 eV
             n = 2    --------------  E = -3.40 eV
                        |  |  |
                        v  v  v
             n = 1    --------------  E = -13.6 eV   (ground state)


  PART 4 — LIMITATIONS                                       [ 2 marks ]

      1.  It works only for HYDROGEN and hydrogen-like one-electron
          species (He+, Li2+, Be3+). It fails completely for every
          multi-electron atom, because it ignores electron-electron
          repulsion.

      2.  It cannot explain the FINE STRUCTURE - each line, viewed with
          a better spectrometer, turns out to be several close lines.

      3.  It cannot explain the ZEEMAN EFFECT (splitting of lines in a
          magnetic field) or the STARK EFFECT (splitting in an electric
          field).

      4.  It cannot explain the shapes of molecules or how chemical
          bonds form.

      5.  It VIOLATES the Heisenberg uncertainty principle, because it
          claims to know the exact path AND the exact speed of the
          electron at the same time.

      6.  It treats the electron purely as a particle and completely
          ignores its WAVE nature, which de Broglie later established.

      7.  It gives no reason for the quantisation of angular momentum -
          it simply assumes it.
```

### Q C2  *** ASKED MOST OFTEN ***
What are quantum numbers? Explain each of them and describe the shapes of
s, p and d orbitals.

**COMPLETE MODEL ANSWER**

```
  PART 1 — WHAT QUANTUM NUMBERS ARE                          [ 1 mark  ]

      Quantum numbers are a set of FOUR numbers that completely describe
      the size, shape, orientation and spin of an electron in an atom -
      in other words they give the complete "address" of an electron.
      They come out of the solution of the Schrodinger wave equation.


  PART 2 — THE FOUR QUANTUM NUMBERS                          [ 4 marks ]

  1.  PRINCIPAL QUANTUM NUMBER  ( n )

      VALUES     n = 1, 2, 3, 4, ...   (never zero, never fractional)
      TELLS YOU  the SIZE of the orbital and the main ENERGY level
      SHELLS     n = 1 -> K , 2 -> L , 3 -> M , 4 -> N
      COUNTING   maximum electrons in a shell  =  2 n^2
                 number of orbitals in a shell =  n^2
                 e.g. n = 3  ->  9 orbitals, 18 electrons


  2.  AZIMUTHAL (ANGULAR MOMENTUM) QUANTUM NUMBER  ( l )

      VALUES     l = 0 to (n - 1) , so there are n values for each n
      TELLS YOU  the SHAPE of the orbital, i.e. which SUBSHELL

           +-----+-----------+--------------------+-----------------+
           |  l  | SUBSHELL  |  SHAPE             |  MAX ELECTRONS  |
           +-----+-----------+--------------------+-----------------+
           |  0  |    s      |  spherical         |        2        |
           |  1  |    p      |  dumb-bell         |        6        |
           |  2  |    d      |  double dumb-bell  |       10        |
           |  3  |    f      |  complex           |       14        |
           +-----+-----------+--------------------+-----------------+

      COUNTING   orbitals in a subshell   =  ( 2 l + 1 )
                 electrons in a subshell  =  2 ( 2 l + 1 )
      EXAMPLE    n = 3  ->  l = 0, 1, 2  ->  the 3s, 3p and 3d subshells
      It also gives the orbital angular momentum,
                 = sqrt( l ( l + 1 ) ) x h / 2 pi


  3.  MAGNETIC QUANTUM NUMBER  ( m )

      VALUES     m = -l , ... , 0 , ... , +l   -  that is (2l + 1) values
      TELLS YOU  the ORIENTATION of the orbital in space

           +-----+-----------------------+-----------+----------------+
           |  l  |  VALUES OF m          | ORBITALS  |  NAMES         |
           +-----+-----------------------+-----------+----------------+
           |  0  |  0                    |     1     |  s             |
           |  1  |  -1 , 0 , +1          |     3     |  px , py , pz  |
           |  2  |  -2,-1, 0,+1,+2       |     5     |  the five d    |
           |  3  |  -3 to +3             |     7     |  the seven f   |
           +-----+-----------------------+-----------+----------------+


  4.  SPIN QUANTUM NUMBER  ( s )

      VALUES     s = +1/2  or  -1/2 , and nothing else
      TELLS YOU  the direction in which the electron spins on its own axis
                 +1/2 is drawn as an UP arrow, -1/2 as a DOWN arrow
      It is the only quantum number that does not come from the
      Schrodinger equation - it was needed to explain spectral doubling.


  PART 3 — SHAPES OF THE ORBITALS                            [ 3 marks ]

  s ORBITAL  ( l = 0 )

      SPHERICAL and completely symmetrical about the nucleus. The
      probability of finding the electron is the same in every direction
      at a given distance. There is only ONE possible orientation, so
      there is only ONE s orbital in any shell.

               ....
            ..      ..
           .    ()    .          ()  = nucleus
           .          .
            ..      ..
               ....

      The size grows with n:  1s < 2s < 3s .

  p ORBITALS  ( l = 1 )

      DUMB-BELL shaped, with two lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus.
      There are THREE of them - px, py and pz - directed along the three
      mutually perpendicular axes.

             pz   |              px                 py    /
                 ( )                                    ( )
                  |         ---( )---(  )---            /
                 ( )                                  ( )
                  |                                   /

      Each p orbital has ONE NODAL PLANE passing through the nucleus, on
      which the probability of finding the electron is ZERO. For pz that
      plane is the xy plane.
      All three have the same energy - they are DEGENERATE.

  d ORBITALS  ( l = 2 )

      There are FIVE of them:  dxy , dyz , dzx , dx2-y2 , dz2 .

      The first FOUR are DOUBLE DUMB-BELL (clover-leaf) shaped, with four
      lobes lying in a plane, and each has TWO nodal planes.

                 \   /
                  \ /                 four lobes, in the xy plane
                  ( )                 for dxy
                  / \
                 /   \

      The fifth, dz2 , is different: a dumb-bell along the z axis with a
      doughnut-shaped RING of electron density around its middle.

                  |
                 ( )
              ==(  )==      <- the ring
                 ( )
                  |

      All five d orbitals are degenerate in a free atom.


  A LINE WORTH ADDING AT THE END (it often earns the last mark):

      NODES:   radial nodes  = n - l - 1 ,   angular nodes = l ,
               total nodes   = n - 1
      e.g. a 3p orbital has 1 radial node and 1 angular node.
```

### Q C3
Explain the photoelectric effect. Derive Einstein's photoelectric equation
and explain the significance of the work function and the threshold
frequency. Solve: a metal of work function 2.0 eV is illuminated with light
of wavelength 300 nm; find the kinetic energy and the velocity of the
photoelectron.

**COMPLETE MODEL ANSWER**

```
  PART 1 — WHAT THE EFFECT IS                                [ 1 mark  ]

      When light of sufficiently high frequency falls on the surface of
      a metal, electrons are ejected from the surface. This is the
      PHOTOELECTRIC EFFECT and the electrons are called PHOTOELECTRONS.

          incoming photon
          (energy = h nu)
                \                 ejected electron
                 \               /   (kinetic energy = 1/2 m v^2)
                  \             /
        ===========\===========/==================
        ############ METAL SURFACE ###############
        ==========================================


  PART 2 — THE EXPERIMENTAL OBSERVATIONS                     [ 2 marks ]

      1.  Electrons are emitted INSTANTLY, with no measurable time delay,
          however weak the light is.

      2.  For each metal there is a certain minimum frequency, the
          THRESHOLD FREQUENCY nu(0). Below it NO electrons are emitted,
          no matter how intense the light or how long it shines.

      3.  Above the threshold, the KINETIC ENERGY of the emitted
          electrons depends only on the FREQUENCY of the light, and not
          at all on its intensity.

      4.  The NUMBER of electrons emitted per second depends only on the
          INTENSITY (brightness) of the light.

      Classical wave theory could explain NONE of these, because it
      predicted that any frequency should work if you waited long enough
      and made the light bright enough.


  PART 3 — EINSTEIN'S EXPLANATION AND EQUATION               [ 2 marks ]

      Einstein applied Planck's quantum idea to light itself. Light
      travels as PHOTONS, each carrying energy h nu, and ONE photon is
      absorbed by ONE electron - all or nothing.

      Of the energy h nu that arrives:
          - part is used to overcome the attraction of the metal and
            escape the surface - this part is the WORK FUNCTION W0 ;
          - the remainder appears as the kinetic energy of the electron.

      By conservation of energy:

      +------------------------------------------------------------+
      |                                                            |
      |     h nu    =    h nu(0)    +    (1/2) m v^2               |
      |       |             |                  |                   |
      |  energy of      WORK FUNCTION      kinetic energy of        |
      |  the photon     (energy just       the photoelectron        |
      |  that arrives   to escape)                                  |
      |                                                            |
      |     so   KE  =  h nu  -  h nu(0)                           |
      |                                                            |
      |                    hc          hc                          |
      |              =  --------  -  ----------                    |
      |                  lambda       lambda(0)                    |
      |                                                            |
      +------------------------------------------------------------+

      This single equation explains all four observations:
        - a photon is absorbed in one go, so emission is INSTANT ;
        - if h nu < W0 the electron cannot escape at all, which is the
          THRESHOLD ;
        - KE depends on nu only, since W0 is fixed for a given metal ;
        - a brighter beam simply means MORE photons, so more electrons.


  PART 4 — SIGNIFICANCE OF W0 AND nu(0), AND THE GRAPH       [ 1 mark  ]

      WORK FUNCTION  W0 = h nu(0)  is the minimum energy needed just to
      pull an electron out of that particular metal. It is a property of
      the metal alone. Caesium has a low work function (1.9 eV) so it is
      used in photocells; platinum has a high one.

      THRESHOLD FREQUENCY nu(0) is the frequency of a photon carrying
      exactly that minimum energy.

          nu  <  nu(0)   ->  no emission at all
          nu  =  nu(0)   ->  electron just escapes, with KE = 0
          nu  >  nu(0)   ->  electron escapes with KE = h nu - h nu(0)

      GRAPH of kinetic energy against frequency:

          KE  ^
              |          /
              |         /        slope of the line  =  h
              |        /         x-intercept        =  nu(0)
              |       /          y-intercept        =  -W0
              +------*---------------->  frequency
                   nu(0)


  PART 5 — THE NUMERICAL                                     [ 2 marks ]

      GIVEN   W0 = 2.0 eV ,  lambda = 300 nm = 3000 A

      STEP 1  Energy of the incoming photon

                       hc        1.9878 x 10^-25
              E   =  --------  = -----------------  =  6.626 x 10^-19 J
                      lambda        3 x 10^-7

              In eV:  6.626 x 10^-19 / 1.602 x 10^-19  =  4.14 eV

              (Check with the shortcut: 12400 / 3000 = 4.13 eV.  OK)

      STEP 2  Kinetic energy

              KE  =  4.14  -  2.0  =  2.14  eV

              In joules:  2.14 x 1.602 x 10^-19  =  3.428 x 10^-19 J

      STEP 3  Velocity, from  KE = (1/2) m v^2

                     2 KE       2 x 3.428 x 10^-19
              v^2 = ------  =  --------------------
                       m          9.11 x 10^-31

                     6.856 x 10^-19
                  = ----------------  =  7.526 x 10^11  =  75.26 x 10^10
                     9.11  x 10^-31

              v  =  sqrt(75.26) x 10^5  =  8.68 x 10^5  m/s

      ANSWERS:   KE = 2.14 eV = 3.43 x 10^-19 J ,   v = 8.68 x 10^5 m/s
```

```
      and fast, so only something massive can deflect them; ZnS because
      it flashes visibly; a vacuum so that air does not scatter the beam.


  PART 2 — OBSERVATIONS AND THE CONCLUSION FROM EACH         [ 3 marks ]

  +-------------------------------------+------------------------------+
  |  OBSERVATION                        |  CONCLUSION                  |
  +-------------------------------------+------------------------------+
  |  Most of the alpha particles passed |  The atom is MOSTLY EMPTY    |

  |  through small angles               |  POSITIVE charge inside that |
  |                                     |  repels the positive alpha   |
  |                                     |  particles                   |
  +-------------------------------------+------------------------------+
  |  A very few (about 1 in 20000)      |  That positive charge, and   |
  |  were deflected through more than   |  almost all the mass, is     |
  |  90 degrees, some bouncing straight |  packed into an extremely    |
  |  back                               |  TINY, DENSE centre - the    |
  |                                     |  NUCLEUS                     |
  +-------------------------------------+------------------------------+

      Rutherford himself said this last result was "as incredible as if
      you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of tissue paper and it came
      back and hit you". It destroyed Thomson's plum pudding model,
      in which the positive charge was spread thinly and could never
      have pushed an alpha particle backwards.


  PART 3 — FEATURES OF THE NUCLEAR MODEL                     [ 1 mark  ]

      1.  Nearly all the mass and the ENTIRE positive charge of the atom
          are concentrated in a very small central region called the
          NUCLEUS.

      2.  The nucleus is about 10^-15 m across while the atom is about
          10^-10 m across, so the atom is roughly 100000 times wider
          than its nucleus, and about 10^15 times larger in volume.

      3.  The electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular paths at
          very high speed, like planets around the sun. The electrostatic
          attraction supplies the centripetal force.

      4.  The atom as a whole is electrically neutral: the number of
          electrons equals the number of protons.


  PART 4 — DRAWBACKS                                         [ 2 marks ]

      1.  STABILITY OF THE ATOM.  By classical electromagnetic theory,
          any charged particle moving in a circular path is accelerating
          and must radiate energy continuously. The electron would
          therefore lose energy, spiral inwards and crash into the
          nucleus within about 10^-8 seconds. The atom would collapse.
          But atoms are stable, so the model must be wrong.

                     electron spiralling in
                        .-'''-.

                      \   `-'   /
                        `-...-'

      2.  NATURE OF THE SPECTRUM.  A continuous loss of energy would
```

### Q C5
How was the electron discovered? Describe the cathode ray experiment and
Millikan's oil drop experiment, give the properties of the fundamental
particles, and define isotopes and isobars with examples.

**COMPLETE MODEL ANSWER**

```
  PART 1 — THE CATHODE RAY EXPERIMENT                        [ 3 marks ]

      A glass discharge tube fitted with two metal electrodes is
      evacuated to about 0.01 mm of mercury and a potential of roughly
      10000 volts is applied. A stream of invisible rays then passes
      from the cathode to the anode. These are CATHODE RAYS.

        +------------------------------------------------+
        |  CATHODE                              ANODE     |
        |   (-)                                   (+)     |
        |  ####  ~~~~~~ CATHODE RAYS ~~~~~~~~~>  ####     |
        +-----|-----------------------------------|-------+
              +---------------| |-----------------+
                         HIGH VOLTAGE

      PROPERTIES OF CATHODE RAYS

      1.  They travel in straight lines - an obstacle casts a sharp
          shadow on the far wall of the tube.
      2.  They consist of material particles - they can turn a small
          paddle wheel placed in their path, so they carry momentum.
      3.  They are negatively charged - in an electric field they bend
          TOWARDS the POSITIVE plate, and a magnetic field also deflects
          them.
      4.  They cause fluorescence in the glass and in a ZnS screen.
      5.  They produce X-rays when they strike a heavy metal target.
      6.  Their properties are the SAME whatever gas is in the tube and
          whatever metal the electrodes are made of.

      CONCLUSION.  Since property 6 holds universally, the same
      negatively charged particle must be present in all matter. It was
      named the ELECTRON. Thomson measured its charge-to-mass ratio by
      balancing an electric against a magnetic deflection:

              e / m   =   1.758 x 10^11   C / kg


  PART 2 — MILLIKAN'S OIL DROP EXPERIMENT                    [ 2 marks ]

      Fine oil droplets are sprayed into a chamber between two charged
      horizontal plates. One droplet enters through a hole and is watched
      through a microscope.

        +-------------------------------------------+
        |   ####################################### |  UPPER PLATE (+)
        |            . <- charged drop              |
        |            |   electric force UP  ( q E ) |
        |            v   gravity DOWN      ( m g )  |
        |   ####################################### |  LOWER PLATE (-)
        +-------------------------------------------+

      1.  With no field the drop falls at a constant terminal velocity;
          from that velocity its MASS is found.
      2.  X-rays ionise the gas and the drop picks up a whole number of
          electrons, acquiring a charge q.
      3.  The field is adjusted until the drop is STATIONARY. Then the
          upward electric force balances the weight:

                q E  =  m g          so     q  =  m g / E

      RESULT.  On thousands of drops, every charge came out as a whole
      number multiple of one basic value - 1.602x10^-19, 3.204x10^-19,
      4.806x10^-19 C and so on, never a fraction of it. Therefore

                q  =  n e     with    e  =  1.602 x 10^-19  C

      i.e. CHARGE IS QUANTISED. Combining with Thomson's ratio gives

                m  =  e / (e/m)  =  1.602x10^-19 / 1.758x10^11
                   =  9.11 x 10^-31  kg


  PART 3 — THE THREE FUNDAMENTAL PARTICLES                   [ 1 mark  ]

  +------------+--------+-------------------+--------------+-------------+
  | PARTICLE   | SYMBOL |  CHARGE (C)       |  MASS (kg)   | DISCOVERER  |
  +------------+--------+-------------------+--------------+-------------+
  | Electron   |   e-   |  -1.602 x 10^-19  | 9.11 x10^-31 | J J Thomson |
  | Proton     |   p+   |  +1.602 x 10^-19  | 1.67 x10^-27 | Goldstein   |
  | Neutron    |   n    |        0          | 1.675x10^-27 | Chadwick    |
  +------------+--------+-------------------+--------------+-------------+

      A proton is about 1837 times heavier than an electron, so nearly
      all the mass of an atom lies in the nucleus while nearly all the
      volume is the space in which the electrons move.
      Chadwick produced neutrons by bombarding beryllium with alpha
      particles:      Be-9  +  alpha  ->  C-12  +  neutron


  PART 4 — ISOTOPES AND ISOBARS                              [ 2 marks ]

      ATOMIC NUMBER  Z = number of protons = number of electrons in a
                         neutral atom
      MASS NUMBER    A = protons + neutrons ,  so neutrons = A - Z

      ISOTOPES are atoms of the SAME element having the same atomic
      number Z but DIFFERENT mass number A. They differ only in the
      number of neutrons, so they have identical chemical properties but
      slightly different physical properties.
          Examples:  H-1 , H-2 (deuterium) , H-3 (tritium)
                     C-12 , C-13 , C-14
                     Cl-35 , Cl-37

      ISOBARS are atoms of DIFFERENT elements having the SAME mass
      number A but different atomic numbers Z. Their chemical properties
      are completely different.
          Examples:  Ar-40 (Z=18) , K-40 (Z=19) , Ca-40 (Z=20)

      Because natural chlorine is 75 % Cl-35 and 25 % Cl-37, its average
      atomic mass is

              ( 75 x 35 + 25 x 37 ) / 100  =  3550 / 100  =  35.5 u

      which is why the periodic table shows a fractional value.
```

### Q C6
Explain the dual nature of matter. State de Broglie's equation and
Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, show how they overturned Bohr's model,
and distinguish clearly between an orbit and an orbital.

**COMPLETE MODEL ANSWER**

```
  PART 1 — DUAL NATURE AND THE DE BROGLIE EQUATION           [ 3 marks ]

      Light behaves as a WAVE in interference and diffraction, but as a
      stream of PARTICLES (photons) in the photoelectric effect. In 1924
      de Broglie argued that nature is symmetrical, so MATTER should
      show the same duality.

      Starting from the two expressions for the energy of a photon,

              E = h nu = h c / lambda    and    E = m c^2

      equating them gives  h c / lambda = m c^2 , so

      +-----------------------------------------------------------+
      |                                                           |
      |                 h            h                            |
      |     lambda  =  ----   =   -------                         |
      |                 p           m v                           |
      |                                                           |
      |     also   lambda = h / sqrt(2 m KE)                      |
      |            lambda = h / sqrt(2 m q V)                     |
      |                                                           |
      +-----------------------------------------------------------+

      WHY WE NEVER SEE IT FOR LARGE OBJECTS.  For a cricket ball of
      mass 0.15 kg moving at 30 m/s,

              lambda = 6.626x10^-34 / (0.15 x 30) = 1.47 x 10^-34 m

      which is far too small to detect. For an electron moving at
      10^6 m/s, lambda comes out near 7 x 10^-10 m - about the size of
      an atom - so the wave nature is very much observable. This is the
      basis of the electron microscope, and it was confirmed
      experimentally by the diffraction of electrons by a nickel crystal.

      IT ALSO EXPLAINS BOHR'S THIRD POSTULATE.  For the electron wave
      to fit round the orbit without cancelling itself out, a whole
      number of wavelengths must fit the circumference:

              2 pi r  =  n lambda  =  n h / (m v)

              so      m v r  =  n h / 2 pi


  PART 2 — HEISENBERG'S UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE                [ 3 marks ]

      STATEMENT.  It is impossible to determine simultaneously, and with
      complete accuracy, both the POSITION and the MOMENTUM (or
      velocity) of a small moving particle such as an electron.

      +-----------------------------------------------------------+
      |                                                           |
      |                                       h                   |
      |      (delta x)  x  (delta p)    >=   -----                |
      |                                      4 pi                 |
      |                                                           |
      |                                        h                  |
      |      (delta x)  x  (m delta v)  >=    -----               |
      |                                       4 pi                |
      |                                                           |
      +-----------------------------------------------------------+

      MEANING.  The more precisely the position is known, the less
      precisely the momentum can be known, and the other way round.
      Their product can never fall below h / 4 pi.

      PHYSICAL REASON.  To "see" an electron you must bounce a photon
      off it, and the photon inevitably changes the electron's momentum.
      The act of measuring disturbs what is being measured.

      WHY IT DOES NOT AFFECT LARGE OBJECTS.  The mass appears in the
      denominator. For a ball of mass 0.15 kg whose speed is known to
      within 10^-6 m/s,

              delta x  >=  5.273x10^-35 / (0.15 x 10^-6)
                       =   3.5 x 10^-28  m

      which is undetectable, so classical mechanics remains perfectly
      good for everyday objects.

      A WORKED CASE FOR AN ELECTRON.  If delta x = 10^-10 m (the size of
      an atom),

              delta v  >=  5.273x10^-35 / (9.11x10^-31 x 10^-10)
                       =   5.79 x 10^5  m/s

      an enormous uncertainty in the speed.

      CONSEQUENCE - THIS IS WHAT KILLED BOHR'S MODEL.  Bohr claimed to
      know the exact radius of the path AND the exact speed of the
      electron at the same instant. The uncertainty principle says that
      is impossible. So definite circular ORBITS cannot exist, and they
      must be replaced by ORBITALS - regions of space in which the
      electron is PROBABLY found.


  PART 3 — ORBIT VERSUS ORBITAL                              [ 2 marks ]

  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  ORBIT                            |  ORBITAL                        |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  A well-defined circular PATH     |  A REGION of space around the   |
  |  around the nucleus in which the  |  nucleus where the probability  |
  |  electron is said to revolve      |  of finding the electron is     |
  |                                   |  maximum (about 90 per cent)    |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  Always circular (or elliptical   |  Has definite shapes: s is      |
  |  in Sommerfeld's extension)       |  spherical, p is dumb-bell,     |
  |                                   |  d is double dumb-bell          |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  Represents the electron as a     |  Takes the WAVE nature of the   |
  |  particle only                    |  electron fully into account    |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  VIOLATES the Heisenberg          |  Completely CONSISTENT with the |
  |  uncertainty principle            |  uncertainty principle          |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  Can hold up to 2 n^2 electrons   |  Can hold a maximum of only     |
  |                                   |  TWO electrons, of opposite spin|
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  A concept of the Bohr model      |  A concept of the quantum       |
  |                                   |  mechanical model               |
  +-----------------------------------+---------------------------------+
```
