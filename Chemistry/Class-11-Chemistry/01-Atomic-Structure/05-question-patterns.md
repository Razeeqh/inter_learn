# Atomic Structure — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — COUNT PROTONS, NEUTRONS, ELECTRONS                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A symbol written with A on top and Z at the bottom, or
            "find the number of neutrons in ...".
  METHOD    protons   = Z
            electrons = Z            (for a NEUTRAL atom)
            neutrons  = A - Z
            For an ION of charge q:  electrons = Z - q
  TRAP      For a NEGATIVE ion you ADD electrons, for a POSITIVE ion you
            SUBTRACT. O2- has 8 + 2 = 10 electrons.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — ISOTOPES / ISOBARS / ISOTONES / ISOELECTRONIC       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define isotopes and isobars" or "Which of these are
            isoelectronic?"
  METHOD    isoTOPES        - same Z (P for Protons same)
            isoBARS         - same A (mass number same)
            isoTONES        - same number of NEUTRONS
            isoELECTRONIC   - same number of ELECTRONS
            Always give an EXAMPLE, it carries marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — WAVELENGTH / FREQUENCY / WAVE NUMBER / ENERGY       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any numerical giving a wavelength or a frequency.
  METHOD    c = nu x lambda
            E = h nu  =  h c / lambda
            wave number = 1 / lambda
  TRAP      Convert the units FIRST:
               1 nm = 10^-9 m ,  1 A = 10^-10 m ,  1 pm = 10^-12 m
            Wave number is usually wanted in cm^-1, so convert lambda to cm.
  SHORTCUT  E (in eV)  =  12400 / lambda (in angstrom)
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — PHOTOELECTRIC EFFECT NUMERICAL                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   The words "work function", "threshold frequency",
            "photoelectrons", "stopping potential".
  METHOD    h nu  =  h nu(0)  +  (1/2) m v^2

            KE = (energy of incoming photon) - (work function)

            Work function  W(0)  =  h nu(0)  =  hc / lambda(0)
  TRAP      Keep both terms in the SAME unit. Convert everything to eV,
            or everything to joules - never mix.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — PHOTOELECTRIC THEORY MCQ                          objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens if the intensity is doubled / frequency is
            increased ..."
  METHOD    ONE LINE ANSWERS ALL OF THEM:

               FREQUENCY  controls  the KINETIC ENERGY of each electron
               INTENSITY  controls  the NUMBER of electrons

            Below the threshold frequency, NOTHING happens at any intensity.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — RYDBERG EQUATION NUMERICAL                          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the wavelength of the ... line of the ... series."
  METHOD    1. Identify n1 from the series name:
                 Lyman 1 , Balmer 2 , Paschen 3 , Brackett 4 , Pfund 5
              2. Identify n2:
                 "first line"  -> n2 = n1 + 1
                 "second line" -> n2 = n1 + 2
                 "series limit / shortest wavelength" -> n2 = infinity
              3. Apply
                      1              +-   1        1   -+
                   --------  =  R Z^2|  -----  -  ----- |
                    lambda           +-  n1^2      n2^2-+
              4. Invert to get lambda.
  TRAP      n1 must be the LOWER level. Swapping them gives a negative answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — NUMBER OF SPECTRAL LINES                         objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many lines appear when an electron falls from level n ..."
  METHOD    Down to the ground state:      n ( n - 1 ) / 2

            From level n2 to level n1:     (n2 - n1)(n2 - n1 + 1) / 2
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — BOHR RADIUS AND ENERGY NUMERICAL                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the radius / energy of the nth orbit of ..."
  METHOD                0.529 n^2                     - 13.6 Z^2
             r(n)  =  -------------  angstrom   E(n)= -------------  eV
                            Z                              n^2

             Identify Z from the species:
                 H = 1 ,  He+ = 2 ,  Li2+ = 3 ,  Be3+ = 4
  TRAP      The ENERGY must come out NEGATIVE. A positive answer means
            you dropped the minus sign.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — IONISATION ENERGY                                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the ionisation energy of ..."
  METHOD    IE  =  E(infinity)  -  E(1)  =  0  -  E(1)  =  + 13.6 Z^2  eV

            H   -> 13.6 eV        He+  -> 54.4 eV
            Li2+-> 122.4 eV
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — BOHR'S POSTULATES AND LIMITATIONS          *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the postulates of Bohr's theory" / "explain the
            hydrogen spectrum" / "what are its limitations".
  METHOD    Write ALL FOUR parts:
              1. The 4 postulates (include mvr = nh/2pi)
              2. The radius and energy formulae
              3. How jumps between levels create the spectral series
              4. The 6 limitations
  NOTE      *** THE MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED LONG ANSWER IN THIS CHAPTER ***
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — RUTHERFORD EXPERIMENT AND DRAWBACKS         4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the gold foil experiment" / "drawbacks of
            Rutherford's model".
  METHOD    1. Draw the set-up.
            2. Give the 3 observations.
            3. Give the 3 conclusions (each observation maps to one).
            4. Give the drawbacks: collapse, continuous spectrum,
               no electron arrangement.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — DE BROGLIE NUMERICAL                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of ..."
  METHOD                h              h                  h
            lambda  =  ----   =    ---------   =    ---------------
                        p            m v             sqrt(2 m KE)
  SHORTCUT  For an electron accelerated through V volts:

                lambda  =  12.27 / sqrt(V)     angstrom
  TRAP      Mass must be in kg, velocity in m/s. The answer comes out in metres.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — WHY MACROSCOPIC OBJECTS SHOW NO WAVE NATURE      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the wave nature of a cricket ball not observed?"
  METHOD    Because m is very large, lambda = h/mv comes out around
            10^-34 m, which is too small to detect.
            The wave nature matters only for very light particles.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — HEISENBERG NUMERICAL                            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The uncertainty in position is ... Calculate the uncertainty
            in velocity / momentum."
  METHOD                            h
            (delta x)(m delta v) >= -----
                                    4 pi

                             h
            delta v   >=  ---------------------
                          4 pi  m  (delta x)
  TRAP      Use h/4pi = 5.27 x 10^-35 to save time.
            The answer is a MINIMUM value, so write ">=" not "=".
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — ORBIT VS ORBITAL                                  4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between an orbit and an orbital."
  METHOD    Give at least FOUR points of difference in a two-column table:
            path vs region · shape · uncertainty principle · capacity
            (2n^2 vs 2) · particle only vs wave nature included.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — QUANTUM NUMBERS EXPLAINED                  *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are quantum numbers? Explain each."
  METHOD    For each of n, l, m, s give:
              - the allowed VALUES
              - what it TELLS you
              - the counting formula that goes with it
            Then add the l -> subshell -> orbitals -> electrons table.
  NOTE      Usually combined with orbital shapes to make the full 8 marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — "WHICH SET OF QUANTUM NUMBERS IS NOT POSSIBLE"  objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Four sets of (n, l, m, s) are given as options.
  METHOD    Run this checklist on each option:
                n  must be a positive whole number, never 0
                l  must lie between 0 and (n - 1)
                m  must lie between -l and +l
                s  must be +1/2 or -1/2
            The first one that breaks a rule is the answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — COUNT ORBITALS / ELECTRONS                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many orbitals / electrons are possible with n = ... ?"
  METHOD    Orbitals in a SHELL      =  n^2
            Electrons in a SHELL     =  2 n^2
            Orbitals in a SUBSHELL   =  2l + 1
            Electrons in a SUBSHELL  =  2(2l + 1)
            Electrons with a GIVEN SPIN in a shell  =  n^2
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — RADIAL AND ANGULAR NODES                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many radial nodes does a 3p orbital have?"
  METHOD    Radial nodes  =  n - l - 1
            Angular nodes =  l
            Total nodes   =  n - 1
            Read n and l straight off the orbital name:
              3p  ->  n = 3 , l = 1
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — SHAPES OF ORBITALS                                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the shapes of s, p and d orbitals."
  METHOD    s  -  spherical, one orientation only
            p  -  dumb-bell, three of them (px, py, pz), mutually
                  perpendicular, each with one nodal plane
            d  -  five of them; four are clover-leaf (double dumb-bell),
                  dz2 is a dumb-bell with a ring around the middle
            DRAW them. Diagrams carry marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — AUFBAU / PAULI / HUND                             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State and explain Aufbau principle / Pauli exclusion
            principle / Hund's rule."
  METHOD    One paragraph each, and for Hund's rule DRAW the box diagram
            showing the right and the wrong filling.
            Include the (n+l) rule under Aufbau.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — WRITE THE ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATION             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the electronic configuration of ..." 
  METHOD    1. Fill in the order
                 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s ...
            2. Use the noble gas shorthand if allowed, e.g. [Ar] 3d6 4s2.
  TRAP      For an ION of a transition metal, remove the 4s electrons
            BEFORE the 3d electrons.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — WHY Cr AND Cu ARE EXCEPTIONS                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the anomalous configuration of Cr / Cu."
  METHOD    State expected vs actual, then give BOTH reasons:
               1. SYMMETRY of a half-filled (d5) or fully-filled (d10)
                  subshell
               2. Greater EXCHANGE ENERGY released
  TRAP      Giving only "half-filled is stable" without explaining
            exchange energy loses half the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — COUNT UNPAIRED ELECTRONS                       objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The number of unpaired electrons in Fe2+ is ..."
  METHOD    1. Write the configuration of the NEUTRAL atom.
            2. For a cation, remove 4s electrons FIRST.
            3. Draw the boxes for the d subshell.
            4. Apply Hund's rule and count the single arrows.
  SHORTCUT  For a d^x ion:   x = 1..5   ->  unpaired = x
                             x = 6..10  ->  unpaired = 10 - x
  EXTRA     If magnetic moment is asked:  mu = sqrt(n(n+2)) BM
            n=1 -> 1.73 , n=2 -> 2.83 , n=3 -> 3.87 ,
            n=4 -> 4.90 , n=5 -> 5.92
  TRAP      "Paramagnetic" needs at least ONE unpaired electron.
            Zn2+ (d10) and Sc3+ (d0) are DIAmagnetic.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — CATHODE RAYS AND THE DISCOVERY OF THE ELECTRON  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the cathode ray experiment" / "give the properties
            of cathode rays" / "how was the electron discovered?"
  METHOD    1. Draw the discharge tube: cathode (-), anode (+), high
               voltage, gas at about 0.01 mm Hg.
            2. Give the properties, in this order:
                 travel in straight lines (sharp shadow)
                 material particles (turn a paddle wheel)
                 negatively charged (bend TOWARDS the POSITIVE plate)
                 cause fluorescence
                 produce X-rays on a metal target
                 INDEPENDENT of the gas and of the cathode metal
            3. State the conclusion: the same negative particle exists
               in ALL matter, so it is fundamental -> the ELECTRON.
            4. Quote Thomson's result:  e/m = 1.758 x 10^11 C/kg
  TRAP      Do NOT write "they bend towards the negative plate".
            They are negative, so they go towards the POSITIVE plate.
  TRAP      Thomson measured only the RATIO e/m , not e and not m.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — MILLIKAN'S OIL DROP EXPERIMENT                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe Millikan's experiment" / "how was the charge on the
            electron measured?" / "what is meant by quantisation of
            charge?"
  METHOD    1. Draw the two horizontal plates with a charged drop between.
            2. Field OFF -> the drop falls at terminal speed -> gives
               the MASS.
            3. X-rays ionise the air -> the drop picks up n electrons.
            4. Field adjusted until the drop is STATIONARY:

                     q E  =  m g        so      q  =  m g / E

            5. Result: every q is a whole-number multiple of
               1.602 x 10^-19 C , so CHARGE IS QUANTISED,  q = n e .
            6. Combine with Thomson:  m = e / (e/m) = 9.11 x 10^-31 kg
  NUMERICAL If a charge is given, the number of electrons is
                     n  =  q / 1.602 x 10^-19
            e.g.  q = 8.01x10^-19 C  ->  n = 5
  TRAP      Charges like 2.4 x 10^-19 C are IMPOSSIBLE, because they are
            not whole multiples of e.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — COUNTING PHOTONS / ENERGY PER MOLE               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many photons ..." / "energy of one mole of photons" /
            "a 100 W bulb emits ... photons per second"
  METHOD    1. Energy of ONE photon:   E = h c / lambda
                 (use hc = 1.9878 x 10^-25 J m in one step)
            2. NUMBER of photons:      n = total energy / E
            3. PER MOLE:               multiply E by 6.022 x 10^23
            4. To kJ/mol:              divide by 1000
  SHORTCUT  eV -> kJ/mol :  multiply by 96.5
  TRAP      For a bulb, "watt" means JOULES PER SECOND, so the total
            energy for one second is just the wattage.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — PHOTOELECTRIC GRAPH IDENTIFICATION             objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A graph is drawn, or the words "slope" / "intercept" appear.
  METHOD    There are only three graphs. Learn them:

            KE against FREQUENCY    -> straight line
                 slope        =  h
                 x-intercept  =  nu(0)
                 y-intercept  =  -W0

            KE against INTENSITY    -> flat horizontal line

            NUMBER of electrons against INTENSITY
                                    -> straight line through the origin

            Stopping potential V0 against FREQUENCY
                 slope        =  h / e

  TRAP      Two metals give PARALLEL lines - same slope h (a universal
            constant), different intercepts (different work functions).
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — ORBITAL ANGULAR MOMENTUM (uses l, not n)       objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   The words "orbital angular momentum" or a named orbital
            such as "a 3d electron".
  METHOD    READ THE WORD FIRST:

            "ORBIT"   (Bohr)  ->  m v r  =  n h / 2 pi        uses n
            "ORBITAL"         ->  sqrt( l (l+1) ) x h / 2 pi  uses l

            s -> 0 ,  p -> sqrt2 ,  d -> sqrt6 ,  f -> sqrt12
  TRAP      Any s electron has ZERO orbital angular momentum - 1s, 2s,
            3s, 4s, all of them.
  TRAP      A 2p and a 4p electron have the SAME orbital angular
            momentum, because only l matters.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — COMPARING TWO HYDROGEN-LIKE SPECIES            objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which has the same radius / energy as ...", "the ratio of
            ...", "identify the ion whose ... is ...".
  METHOD    Write the three proportionalities first:

                 r  ~   n^2 / Z
                 E  ~  - Z^2 / n^2
                 v  ~    Z / n

            Then just compute the number for each option and compare.
            Z:  H = 1 , He+ = 2 , Li2+ = 3 , Be3+ = 4

            USEFUL EQUALITIES TO SPOT INSTANTLY:
                 same radius   when  n^2 / Z  is equal
                 same energy   when  n / Z    is equal
                 wavelength    scales as  1 / Z^2
  TRAP      Z is the number of PROTONS, not the charge on the ion.
            Li2+ has Z = 3 , not 2.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — QUANTUM NUMBERS OF A NAMED ELECTRON            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the four quantum numbers of the valence electron of
            sodium" / "give the quantum numbers of every electron in
            nitrogen".
  METHOD    1. Write the electronic configuration.
            2. Draw the box diagram, applying Hund's rule.
            3. Read off, for the electron asked:
                 n  =  the shell number in front of the letter
                 l  =  0 for s , 1 for p , 2 for d , 3 for f
                 m  =  the box it sits in, from -l to +l
                 s  =  +1/2 for the first arrow in a box, -1/2 for the
                       second
            4. Check that no two electrons share all four numbers.
  TRAP      Never write l = 1 for an s electron. The l value comes from
            the LETTER, not from the number.
```

```
  PATTERN 32 — AVERAGE ATOMIC MASS / PERCENTAGE ABUNDANCE       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Percentages of two or three isotopes are given, or the
            average mass is given and a percentage is asked for.
  METHOD    FORWARD:
                            (%1 x A1) + (%2 x A2) + ...
              average  =  -------------------------------
                                       100

            BACKWARD (average given, percentages wanted):
              let the first percentage be x , the second (100 - x) ,
              write the same equation and solve the simple linear
              equation for x.
  EXAMPLE   Cl: 75 % of 35 and 25 % of 37 -> (2625 + 925)/100 = 35.5 u
  TRAP      Use MASS NUMBERS, not atomic numbers, in the calculation.
```
