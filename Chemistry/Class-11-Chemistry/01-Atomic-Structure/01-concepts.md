# TOPIC 1 — What is inside an atom?

An atom is made of three particles.

```
  +------------+----------+-----------------+-------------+----------------+
  | PARTICLE   | SYMBOL   | CHARGE          | MASS (kg)   | FOUND WHERE    |
  +------------+----------+-----------------+-------------+----------------+
  | Electron   |  e-      | -1.602 x 10^-19 | 9.11x10^-31 | outside the    |
  |            |          | (negative)      |             | nucleus        |
  +------------+----------+-----------------+-------------+----------------+
  | Proton     |  p+      | +1.602 x 10^-19 | 1.67x10^-27 | in the nucleus |
  |            |          | (positive)      |             |                |
  +------------+----------+-----------------+-------------+----------------+
  | Neutron    |  n       |  ZERO           | 1.67x10^-27 | in the nucleus |
  +------------+----------+-----------------+-------------+----------------+
```

```
  KEY FACT:  a proton is about 1837 times heavier than an electron.

  So virtually ALL the mass of an atom sits in the nucleus,
  but virtually ALL the VOLUME is the empty space where electrons move.
```

## Who discovered what

```
  Electron  ->  J. J. Thomson (cathode ray tube experiment)
                He measured  charge / mass  =  1.758 x 10^11  C/kg

  Charge on ->  R. A. Millikan (oil drop experiment)
  electron      He measured   e  =  1.602 x 10^-19  C

  Proton    ->  Goldstein (anode / canal rays)

  Neutron   ->  James Chadwick (bombarded beryllium with alpha particles)
```

## 1.1 THE CATHODE RAY EXPERIMENT — how the electron was found

This is asked as a 2-mark or 4-mark question. Learn the set-up, the six properties
and the conclusion.

### The apparatus

```
           to vacuum pump
                 ^
                 |
    +------------|------------------------------------+
    |            |                                    |
    |   CATHODE                              ANODE     |
    |    (-)                                  (+)      |
    |   ####  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~>  ####      |
    |   ####  ~~~~~ CATHODE RAYS ~~~~~~~~~>  ####      |
    |   ####  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~>  ####      |
    |     |                                    |       |
    +-----|------------------------------------|-------+
          |                                    |
          +------------| |----------------------+
                    HIGH VOLTAGE
                   (10000 volts)

  The tube is a sealed glass tube with two metal plates sealed in.
  The air inside is pumped out until the pressure is about 0.01 mm of
  mercury (a very good vacuum).

  Switch on 10000 V and a stream of INVISIBLE rays travels from the
  CATHODE (negative plate) to the ANODE (positive plate).
  These are the CATHODE RAYS.
```

### The six properties of cathode rays (write any four for 2 marks)

```
  1.  They travel in STRAIGHT LINES from the cathode to the anode.
      PROOF: put a solid object in the path and it casts a sharp SHADOW.

  2.  They are made of MATERIAL PARTICLES with mass and momentum.
      PROOF: they turn a tiny paddle wheel placed in their path.

  3.  They are NEGATIVELY CHARGED.
      PROOF: in an electric field they bend TOWARDS the POSITIVE plate.
      They are also deflected by a magnetic field.

  4.  They make the glass behind the anode GLOW (fluorescence), and they
      make a zinc sulphide screen shine.

  5.  They can produce X-rays when they strike a heavy metal target.

  6.  *** THE BIG ONE ***  The nature of the cathode rays does NOT depend
      on the gas in the tube, nor on the metal the cathode is made of.
      Hydrogen, oxygen, helium - it makes no difference.

      CONCLUSION:  the same negatively charged particle is present in
      EVERY kind of matter.  That particle is the ELECTRON.
```

> **TRAP:** students write "cathode rays bend towards the negative plate".
> They are negative, so they are REPELLED by negative and ATTRACTED to
> positive. They bend **towards the POSITIVE plate**.

### What Thomson actually measured

```
  Thomson applied an ELECTRIC field and a MAGNETIC field at right angles
  to the beam and adjusted them until the two deflections cancelled and
  the beam went straight again. From the field strengths he could work
  out the ratio of charge to mass:

      +-------------------------------------------------------+
      |        e                                              |
      |      -----  =  1.758  x  10^11   coulomb / kilogram    |
      |        m                                              |
      +-------------------------------------------------------+

  NOTE what he did NOT get:  he got the RATIO only, not e and not m
  separately. That is why Millikan's experiment was still needed.
```

## 1.2 MILLIKAN'S OIL DROP EXPERIMENT — how the charge was found

```
           atomiser sprays a fine mist of oil
                        |
                        v
    +-------------------------------------------+
    |            tiny hole                      |
    +---------------o---------------------------+
    |                                           |
    |   ####################################### |  <-- UPPER PLATE  (+)
    |                                           |
    |            . <- one charged oil drop      |
    |            |                              |
    |            | electric force UP (qE)       |
    |            v gravity DOWN (mg)            |
    |                                           |
    |   ####################################### |  <-- LOWER PLATE  (-)
    |                                           |
    +-------------------------------------------+
           X-rays ionise the air, and the oil
           drops pick up 1, 2, 3 ... electrons

           A microscope watches one single drop.
```

### The method, in four steps

```
  STEP 1   Spray oil into the chamber. One drop falls through the hole.

  STEP 2   With NO electric field, the drop falls under gravity and soon
           reaches a steady (terminal) speed, because air resistance
           balances gravity. From that speed Millikan calculated the
           MASS of the drop.

  STEP 3   Shine X-rays in. The air is ionised and the drop picks up a
           whole number of electrons, so it now carries a charge q.

  STEP 4   Switch on the electric field E and adjust it until the drop
           HANGS MOTIONLESS. Now the upward electric force exactly
           balances the downward weight:

           +-------------------------------------------+
           |          q E   =   m g                    |
           |                                           |
           |                     m g                   |
           |            q   =  -------                 |
           |                      E                    |
           +-------------------------------------------+
```

### The result — the whole point of the experiment

```
  Millikan repeated this on thousands of drops. Every single charge he
  measured came out as a WHOLE NUMBER MULTIPLE of one basic amount:

      1.602x10^-19 ,  3.204x10^-19 ,  4.806x10^-19 ,  6.408x10^-19 , ...
           1 e             2 e             3 e             4 e

  NEVER 1.5 e , never 2.7 e.

      +-------------------------------------------------------+
      |     q   =   n  e      where n = 1, 2, 3, ...           |
      |                                                       |
      |     e   =   1.602  x  10^-19  coulomb                  |
      +-------------------------------------------------------+

  CONCLUSION:  charge is QUANTISED. It comes in indivisible packets.
               e is the smallest amount of charge that can exist free.
```

### Combining the two experiments — this is how we know the electron's mass

```
                    charge on the electron            e
  mass of electron = --------------------------  =  -------
                    the ratio Thomson measured       (e/m)

                     1.602 x 10^-19
              m  =  ------------------  =  9.11 x 10^-31  kg
                     1.758 x 10^11
```

## WORKED EXAMPLES — TOPIC 1

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1.1  (mass of the electron)

Thomson found `e/m = 1.758 x 10^11 C/kg` and Millikan found
`e = 1.602 x 10^-19 C`. Find the mass of the electron.

```
  STEP 1   Write the relation.
                   e                            e
              -------- = 1.758 x 10^11   ->  m = ---------------
                 m                              1.758 x 10^11

  STEP 2   Substitute.

                 1.602 x 10^-19
           m  = ------------------
                 1.758 x 10^11

  STEP 3   Divide the numbers, then the powers.

           1.602 / 1.758  =  0.9113
           10^-19 / 10^11 =  10^-30

  STEP 4   m  =  0.9113 x 10^-30  =  9.11 x 10^-31  kg      ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1.2  (Millikan — how many electrons on a drop?)

An oil drop carries a charge of `8.01 x 10^-19 C`. How many electrons does
it hold?

```
  STEP 1   q  =  n e      ->      n  =  q / e

  STEP 2                 8.01 x 10^-19
              n   =   ---------------------
                       1.602 x 10^-19

  STEP 3   8.01 / 1.602  =  5     and  10^-19 / 10^-19 = 1

  STEP 4   n  =  5  electrons       ANSWER

  CHECK:   5 x 1.602 x 10^-19  =  8.01 x 10^-19    OK
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1.3  (Millikan — the balancing calculation)

An oil drop of mass `3.2 x 10^-15 kg` hangs motionless between the plates
when the electric field is `1.96 x 10^5 V/m`. Take `g = 9.8 m/s^2`.
Find the charge on the drop and the number of electrons on it.

```
  STEP 1   At balance:   q E  =  m g

                   m g
            q  =  -----
                    E

  STEP 2   Numerator:  m g  =  (3.2 x 10^-15) x 9.8  =  3.136 x 10^-14  N

  STEP 3            3.136 x 10^-14
            q  =  -------------------
                    1.96 x 10^5

           3.136 / 1.96  =  1.6
           10^-14 / 10^5 =  10^-19

            q  =  1.6 x 10^-19  C          ANSWER (part 1)

  STEP 4   Number of electrons

                 1.6 x 10^-19
           n = ------------------  =  1  electron      ANSWER (part 2)
                1.602 x 10^-19
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1.4  (how many electrons make one coulomb?)

```
  STEP 1   Each electron carries 1.602 x 10^-19 C.

  STEP 2                 1 C                 1
            n   =   ---------------   =   -------- x 10^19
                    1.602 x 10^-19          1.602

  STEP 3   1 / 1.602  =  0.6242

  STEP 4   n  =  0.6242 x 10^19  =  6.24 x 10^18  electrons     ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1.5  (charge on one mole of electrons)

```
  STEP 1   1 mole  =  6.022 x 10^23  electrons

  STEP 2   Q  =  (6.022 x 10^23) x (1.602 x 10^-19)

  STEP 3   6.022 x 1.602  =  9.6472
           10^23 x 10^-19 =  10^4

  STEP 4   Q  =  9.6472 x 10^4  =  96472  C  =  about 96500 C   ANSWER

  This number has a name - it is the FARADAY constant. You meet it
  again in Electrochemistry in 2nd year.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1.6  (how much heavier is a proton?)

```
   mass of proton      1.67 x 10^-27
  ----------------  =  ----------------
   mass of electron    9.11 x 10^-31

   1.67 / 9.11   =  0.1833
   10^-27/10^-31 =  10^4

   ratio  =  0.1833 x 10^4  =  1833   (about 1837 with exact masses)

   So one proton weighs as much as roughly 1837 electrons.
   THIS is why the electrons contribute almost nothing to the mass
   of the atom.
```

> **TRAP:** the mass of a proton and the mass of a neutron are both about
> `1.67 x 10^-27 kg`. Do not write that a neutron is much heavier - it is
> only very slightly heavier.

# TOPIC 2 — Atomic number, mass number, isotopes, isobars

```
  ATOMIC NUMBER  Z  =  number of PROTONS
                    =  number of ELECTRONS  (in a neutral atom)

  MASS NUMBER    A  =  protons  +  neutrons

  NUMBER OF NEUTRONS  =  A  -  Z
```

How an element is written:

```
        A
         X            example:      23
        Z                             Na       Z = 11 , A = 23
                                    11         so  neutrons = 23 - 11 = 12
```

```
  +------------+---------------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  TERM      |  SAME                     |  EXAMPLES                     |
  +------------+---------------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  ISOTOPES  |  same Z (same element),   |  H-1 , H-2 , H-3              |
  |            |  DIFFERENT A              |  C-12 , C-13 , C-14           |
  |            |                           |  Cl-35 , Cl-37                |
  +------------+---------------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  ISOBARS   |  same A ,                 |  Ar-40 , K-40 , Ca-40         |
  |            |  DIFFERENT Z              |                               |
  +------------+---------------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  ISOTONES  |  same number of NEUTRONS  |  C-14 , N-15 , O-16           |
  |            |                           |  (all have 8 neutrons)        |
  +------------+---------------------------+-------------------------------+
  | ISOELECTRO-|  same number of ELECTRONS |  Na+ , Mg2+ , F- , Ne         |
  |  NIC       |                           |  (all have 10 electrons)      |
  +------------+---------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

> **MEMORY HOOK:**
> isoTOPES -> **P** for **P**rotons are the same
> isoBARS -> **A** in bAr -> mass number **A** is the same

## Average atomic mass

Most elements exist as a MIXTURE of isotopes, so the atomic mass in the
periodic table is a weighted average.

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |    average       (%1 x A1) + (%2 x A2) + (%3 x A3) + ...           |
  |    atomic  =  ----------------------------------------------       |
  |    mass                            100                             |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## WORKED EXAMPLES — TOPIC 2

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.1  (straight counting)

Find the protons, neutrons and electrons in `Cl-35 (Z = 17)` and in
`Cl-37 (Z = 17)`.

```
  Cl-35 :  protons   = Z = 17
           electrons = Z = 17   (neutral)
           neutrons  = A - Z = 35 - 17 = 18

  Cl-37 :  protons   = 17
           electrons = 17
           neutrons  = 37 - 17 = 20

  Same Z , different A  ->  these two are ISOTOPES.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.2  (counting for an ION)

Find the protons, neutrons and electrons in `S2- (A = 32, Z = 16)` and in
`Al3+ (A = 27, Z = 13)`.

```
  RULE:   electrons  =  Z  -  (charge, WITH its sign)

  S2-  :   protons   =  16
           electrons =  16 - (-2)  =  16 + 2  =  18
           neutrons  =  32 - 16  =  16

  Al3+ :   protons   =  13
           electrons =  13 - (+3)  =  13 - 3  =  10
           neutrons  =  27 - 13  =  14
```

> **TRAP:** a NEGATIVE ion has GAINED electrons, so you ADD.
> A POSITIVE ion has LOST electrons, so you SUBTRACT.
> Half the marks lost in this chapter are lost on this one line.

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.3  (working backwards from the electrons)

An ion `M3+` has 23 electrons and 30 neutrons. Find Z, A and identify M.

```
  STEP 1   The ion lost 3 electrons, so the neutral atom had
              23 + 3  =  26  electrons

  STEP 2   For a neutral atom, electrons = Z , so  Z = 26.

  STEP 3   A  =  Z  +  neutrons  =  26 + 30  =  56

  STEP 4   Z = 26  ->  IRON.  The ion is  Fe3+  and the atom is Fe-56.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.4  (average atomic mass of chlorine)

Chlorine is 75% Cl-35 and 25% Cl-37. Find its average atomic mass.

```
  STEP 1              (75 x 35)  +  (25 x 37)
         average  =  --------------------------
                                100

  STEP 2   75 x 35  =  2625
           25 x 37  =   925
           sum      =  3550

  STEP 3   3550 / 100  =  35.5  u          ANSWER

  This is exactly why the periodic table says 35.5 and not a whole number.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.5  (average atomic mass of boron)

Boron is 19.6% B-10 and 80.4% B-11.

```
  STEP 1   (19.6 x 10)  =  196.0
           (80.4 x 11)  =  884.4
           sum          = 1080.4

  STEP 2   1080.4 / 100  =  10.804  u  ~  10.8 u        ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.6  (three isotopes — neon)

Neon is 90.92% Ne-20, 0.26% Ne-21 and 8.82% Ne-22.

```
  STEP 1   90.92 x 20  =  1818.40
            0.26 x 21  =     5.46
            8.82 x 22  =   194.04
           sum         =  2017.90

  STEP 2   2017.90 / 100  =  20.179  u  ~  20.18 u      ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.7  (finding the percentages — the reverse question)

Copper has two isotopes, Cu-63 and Cu-65, and an average atomic mass of
63.5. Find the percentage of each.

```
  STEP 1   Let the percentage of Cu-63 be  x .
           Then the percentage of Cu-65 is  (100 - x) .

  STEP 2         63x  +  65(100 - x)
           63.5 = ---------------------
                          100

  STEP 3   Multiply both sides by 100:

           6350  =  63x  +  6500  -  65x
           6350  =  6500  -  2x

  STEP 4     2x  =  6500 - 6350  =  150
              x  =  75

  STEP 5   Cu-63 = 75 %  ,  Cu-65 = 25 %       ANSWER

  CHECK:  (75 x 63 + 25 x 65)/100 = (4725 + 1625)/100 = 6350/100 = 63.5  OK
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2.8  (sorting a list — the classic 2-mark question)

Classify these pairs: `(Ar-40, K-40)` , `(C-12, C-13)` , `(Na-23, Mg-24)` ,
`(Ca2+, Ar)`.

```
  +-----------------+-----------------------------+------------------+
  |  PAIR           |  CHECK                      |  ANSWER          |
  +-----------------+-----------------------------+------------------+
  | Ar-40 , K-40    | A same (40), Z different    |  ISOBARS         |
  |                 | (18 and 19)                 |                  |
  +-----------------+-----------------------------+------------------+
  | C-12 , C-13     | Z same (6), A different     |  ISOTOPES        |
  +-----------------+-----------------------------+------------------+
  | Na-23 , Mg-24   | neutrons = 23-11 = 12       |  ISOTONES        |
  |                 | neutrons = 24-12 = 12  SAME |                  |
  +-----------------+-----------------------------+------------------+
  | Ca2+ , Ar       | Ca2+ = 20-2 = 18 electrons  |  ISOELECTRONIC   |
  |                 | Ar   = 18 electrons    SAME |                  |
  +-----------------+-----------------------------+------------------+
```

# TOPIC 3 — Thomson's model and Rutherford's model

## Thomson's "plum pudding" model (1898)

```
             +---------------------+
            /   -       -           \
           |        -        -       |     Positive charge spread out like
           |    -        -      -    |     a pudding, with electrons (-)
           |         -      -        |     stuck inside like plums.
            \    -        -         /
             +---------------------+

  Atom is electrically NEUTRAL overall.
```

**DRAWBACK:** it could not explain Rutherford's gold foil experiment.

## Rutherford's gold foil (alpha scattering) experiment

```
   alpha particle
   source
      |
      |  ---------------->  most pass straight through
      |  ---------------->
      |  --------\
      |           \------>  a few deflected by small angles
      |  ------X
      |         \
      |          \-------->  very few (1 in 20000) bounce straight BACK
      |
              GOLD FOIL
           (very thin sheet)
```

### What each observation means

```
  OBSERVATION                          CONCLUSION
  -----------------------------------  ------------------------------------
  Most alpha particles pass straight   The atom is MOSTLY EMPTY SPACE
  through undeflected

  A few deflected by small angles      There is a CONCENTRATED POSITIVE
                                       charge somewhere inside

  Very few bounce straight back        That positive charge is packed into
                                       a TINY, DENSE centre -> the NUCLEUS
```

### Rutherford's nuclear model

```
  1.  Almost all the mass and the entire positive charge sit in a very
      small centre called the NUCLEUS.

  2.  The nucleus is about 10^-15 m across.
      The whole atom is about 10^-10 m across.
      So the atom is about 100,000 times bigger than its nucleus.

  3.  Electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular paths, like planets
      around the sun.
```

### DRAWBACKS of Rutherford's model (very common 2-mark / part of 8-mark)

```
  1.  STABILITY PROBLEM
      A moving charged particle must continuously radiate energy.
      So the electron would lose energy, spiral inward, and crash into the
      nucleus in about 10^-8 seconds.
      -> The atom would COLLAPSE. But atoms are stable. Contradiction.

  2.  SPECTRUM PROBLEM
      Continuous loss of energy would give a CONTINUOUS spectrum.
      But atoms actually give a LINE spectrum (separate coloured lines).

  3.  It said nothing about HOW the electrons are arranged.
```

### Why gold foil, why alpha particles, why a zinc sulphide screen?

```
  GOLD             -  it is the most MALLEABLE metal, so it can be beaten
                      into a foil only about 100 atoms thick (roughly
                      0.00004 cm). A thick sheet would stop everything.

  ALPHA PARTICLES  -  they are helium nuclei (He2+): mass 4 u, charge +2.
                      Being heavy and fast (about 1.6 x 10^7 m/s), they are
                      not pushed around by the light electrons - so any
                      big deflection must come from something MASSIVE.

  ZnS SCREEN       -  it flashes with a tiny spot of light (a scintillation)
                      wherever an alpha particle lands, so the deflections
                      can literally be counted by eye in a dark room.

  VACUUM           -  air molecules would scatter the alpha particles
                      before they ever reached the foil.
```

## WORKED EXAMPLES — TOPIC 3

### WORKED EXAMPLE 3.1  (how empty is the atom?)

The radius of an atom is about `10^-10 m` and the radius of its nucleus is
about `10^-15 m`. Compare their volumes.

```
  STEP 1   Volume of a sphere is proportional to (radius)^3.

  STEP 2   volume of atom      ( 10^-10 )^3        10^-30
           ----------------  = --------------  =  ---------
           volume of nucleus   ( 10^-15 )^3        10^-45

  STEP 3   10^-30 / 10^-45  =  10^( -30 + 45 )  =  10^15

  STEP 4   The atom is 10^15 times (a thousand million million times)
           bigger in volume than its nucleus.          ANSWER

  PICTURE IT:  if the nucleus were a cricket ball at the centre of a
               stadium, the electrons would be at the far seats and
               everything in between would be EMPTY.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 3.2  (radius of a nucleus)

The radius of a nucleus is given by `R = R0 x A^(1/3)` with
`R0 = 1.2 x 10^-15 m`. Find the radius of an `Al-27` nucleus.

```
  STEP 1   A  =  27 ,  so  A^(1/3)  =  cube root of 27  =  3

  STEP 2   R  =  (1.2 x 10^-15) x 3

  STEP 3   R  =  3.6 x 10^-15  m                ANSWER

  (Compare: the whole Al atom is about 1.4 x 10^-10 m in radius,
   which is nearly 40000 times larger.)
```

# TOPIC 4 — Light: waves and energy

Light is an **electromagnetic wave**. Three quantities describe it:

```
        wavelength (lambda)
     |<------------------>|
      ____                 ____
     /    \               /    \
    /      \             /      \        AMPLITUDE = height of the wave
---/--------\-----------/--------\---
             \        /
              \______/

  FREQUENCY (nu)  =  how many waves pass a point per second.  Unit: hertz (Hz)
  WAVELENGTH (lambda) = distance between two crests.  Unit: metre (m)
```

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                              |
  |     c  =  nu  x  lambda          c = 3 x 10^8  m/s           |
  |                                    (speed of light)          |
  |                                                              |
  |                       1                                      |
  |     wave number  =  --------      unit:  cm^-1  or  m^-1     |
  |                     lambda                                   |
  |                                                              |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------+

  Note:  frequency and wavelength are INVERSELY related.
         Bigger wavelength  ->  smaller frequency  ->  LESS energy.
```

## The electromagnetic spectrum (low energy to high energy)

```
  LOW ENERGY                                                 HIGH ENERGY
  long wavelength                                        short wavelength
  <-----------------------------------------------------------------------> 

  Radio  ->  Microwave  ->  Infrared  ->  VISIBLE  ->  Ultraviolet  ->  X-ray  ->  Gamma

  VISIBLE LIGHT (400 nm to 750 nm):

      VIOLET   INDIGO   BLUE   GREEN   YELLOW   ORANGE   RED
      400 nm  ------------------------------------------>  750 nm
      highest energy                              lowest energy
```

```
  +--------------+---------------------------+--------------------------+
  |  REGION      |  WAVELENGTH               |  TYPICAL FREQUENCY (Hz)  |
  +--------------+---------------------------+--------------------------+
  |  Radio       |  more than 0.1 m          |  less than 3 x 10^9      |
  |  Microwave   |  1 mm  to  0.1 m          |  3 x 10^9  - 3 x 10^11   |
  |  Infrared    |  750 nm  to  1 mm         |  3 x 10^11 - 4 x 10^14   |
  |  VISIBLE     |  400 nm  to  750 nm       |  4 x 10^14 - 7.5 x 10^14 |
  |  Ultraviolet |  10 nm   to  400 nm       |  7.5 x 10^14 - 3 x 10^16 |
  |  X-ray       |  0.01 nm to  10 nm        |  3 x 10^16 - 3 x 10^19   |
  |  Gamma ray   |  less than 0.01 nm        |  more than 3 x 10^19     |
  +--------------+---------------------------+--------------------------+
```

> **TRAP:** the FIRST thing to do in every light numerical is convert the

>    to get wave number in cm^-1 -> lambda must be in CENTIMETRES
>
>    1 nm = 10^-9 m = 10^-7 cm
>    1 A  = 10^-10 m = 10^-8 cm
>    1 pm = 10^-12 m
> ```

# TOPIC 5 — Planck's quantum theory

Classical physics failed to explain **black body radiation**. Planck fixed it
with one bold idea.

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  PLANCK'S IDEA:                                                      |
  |                                                                      |
  |  Energy is NOT given out continuously. It is emitted or absorbed     |
  |  only in small PACKETS called QUANTA (one packet = one "quantum").   |
  |                                                                      |
  |  A quantum of LIGHT is called a PHOTON.                              |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |            E  =  h  x  nu                                            |
  |                                                                      |
  |                     h  x  c                                          |
  |            E  =  ------------                                        |
  |                    lambda                                            |
  |                                                                      |
  |     h  =  Planck's constant  =  6.626 x 10^-34  J s                  |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  For n quanta:     E  =  n  h  nu       where n is a whole number
```

> **Analogy:** you cannot buy 1.5 eggs. You buy 1 egg or 2 eggs. Energy is
> like eggs, not like water.

## The three ways an energy question can be asked

```
  +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  ASKED FOR                     |  WHAT TO DO                    |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  Energy of ONE photon, in J    |  E = h c / lambda              |
  |                                |    (lambda in METRES)          |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  Energy of ONE photon, in eV   |  work it out in J, then        |
  |                                |  DIVIDE by 1.602 x 10^-19      |
  |                                |  SHORTCUT: E(eV) = 12400/A     |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  Energy of ONE MOLE of photons |  work out E for one photon,    |
  |  (a "mole of photons" is       |  then MULTIPLY by              |
  |   called an EINSTEIN)          |  6.022 x 10^23                 |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  NUMBER of photons in a given  |  n = (total energy) /          |
  |  total energy                  |      (energy of one photon)    |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
```

# TOPIC 6 — Photoelectric effect

Shine light on a metal surface. If the light has enough energy, **electrons
are ejected**. Those electrons are called photoelectrons.

```
      incoming photon
      (energy = h nu)
            \
             \                 ejected electron
              \               /     (kinetic energy = 1/2 m v^2)
               \             /
    ============\===========/=================
    #############METAL SURFACE###############
    ==========================================
```

## The four experimental facts

```
  1.  Electrons come out INSTANTLY - no time delay at all.

  2.  Below a certain frequency (the THRESHOLD FREQUENCY, nu-zero),
      NO electrons come out - no matter how bright the light is.

  3.  Above the threshold, the KINETIC ENERGY of the ejected electrons
      depends only on the FREQUENCY of the light, not on its brightness.

  4.  The NUMBER of electrons ejected depends on the BRIGHTNESS (intensity).
```

## Einstein's equation

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |      h nu     =     h nu(0)     +     (1/2) m v^2                    |
  |        |               |                    |                        |
  |    energy of      WORK FUNCTION        kinetic energy of             |
  |    the photon     (energy needed       the ejected electron          |
  |    that arrives   just to escape)                                    |
  |                                                                      |
  |      WORK FUNCTION   W(0)  =  h nu(0)                                |
  |                                                                      |
  |      So:      (1/2) m v^2  =  h nu  -  W(0)                          |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **Why brightness does not help below the threshold:**
> One electron absorbs exactly ONE photon. If that single photon is too weak,
> the electron cannot escape. Adding more weak photons does not help, just as
> a hundred people each pushing with 1 newton cannot lift a car if each must
> lift it alone.

### Worked example

The work function of a metal is 2.0 eV. Light of wavelength 300 nm falls on it.
Find the kinetic energy of the ejected electron.

```
  STEP 1   Energy of the photon

              h c        (6.626x10^-34)(3x10^8)
     E  =  ---------  =  ------------------------  =  6.626 x 10^-19 J
             lambda            300 x 10^-9

  STEP 2   Convert to electron volts   (1 eV = 1.602 x 10^-19 J)

             6.626 x 10^-19
     E  =  ------------------  =  4.14 eV
             1.602 x 10^-19

  STEP 3   Kinetic energy

     KE  =  4.14  -  2.0  =  2.14 eV
```

## The three graphs you may be asked to draw

```
  GRAPH 1   Kinetic energy against FREQUENCY

     KE  ^
         |            /  metal B (bigger W0)
         |          /   /
         |        /   /   metal A (smaller W0)
         |      /   /
         +----*---*------------------->  frequency nu
              |   |
           nu0(A) nu0(B)

     Both lines have the SAME SLOPE = h  (Planck's constant).
     The intercept on the frequency axis is the THRESHOLD FREQUENCY.
     The intercept on the KE axis (extended down) is  -W0 .


  GRAPH 2   Kinetic energy against INTENSITY (frequency fixed)

     KE  ^
         |
         |------------------------------   FLAT LINE
         |
         +------------------------------>  intensity

     Brighter light does NOT give faster electrons.


  GRAPH 3   NUMBER of photoelectrons against INTENSITY

   number ^                    /
     of   |                 /
  electrons|             /       STRAIGHT LINE THROUGH THE ORIGIN
         |          /
         +-------/---------------------->  intensity

     Twice as bright  ->  twice as many electrons.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 6.2  (all four parts — caesium)

The work function of caesium is 1.9 eV. Find
(a) the threshold wavelength, (b) the threshold frequency,
(c) the kinetic energy of the photoelectron when 500 nm light is used,
(d) the velocity of that photoelectron.

```
  (a) THRESHOLD WAVELENGTH

  STEP 1   Use the shortcut  E(eV) = 12400 / lambda(A) , rearranged:

                        12400        12400
           lambda(A) = --------  =  --------  =  6526 A
                        E(eV)          1.9

           lambda(0)  =  6526 A  =  652.6 nm           ANSWER (a)

  CHECK the long way:
           W0  =  1.9 x 1.602 x 10^-19  =  3.044 x 10^-19  J
                        hc        1.9878 x 10^-25
           lambda(0) = -----  =  ------------------  =  6.53 x 10^-7 m
                        W0        3.044 x 10^-19
           = 653 nm     OK, matches.

  (b) THRESHOLD FREQUENCY

                  W0          3.044 x 10^-19
           nu0 = -----  =  --------------------
                   h         6.626 x 10^-34

           3.044 / 6.626  =  0.4594 ;   10^-19/10^-34 = 10^15

           nu0  =  0.4594 x 10^15  =  4.59 x 10^14  Hz     ANSWER (b)

  (c) KINETIC ENERGY WITH 500 nm LIGHT

  STEP 1   Energy of the incoming photon:
                     12400
           E(eV)  = --------  =  2.48 eV        (500 nm = 5000 A)
                     5000

  STEP 2   KE  =  E  -  W0  =  2.48  -  1.9  =  0.58 eV     ANSWER (c)

  STEP 3   In joules:
           KE  =  0.58 x 1.602 x 10^-19  =  9.29 x 10^-20  J

  (d) VELOCITY

  STEP 1   KE  =  (1/2) m v^2      ->      v = sqrt( 2 KE / m )

  STEP 2         2 x 9.29 x 10^-20        1.858 x 10^-19
           v^2 = -------------------  =  ------------------
                    9.11 x 10^-31          9.11 x 10^-31

           1.858 / 9.11    =  0.2040
           10^-19 / 10^-31 =  10^12

           v^2  =  0.2040 x 10^12  =  2.040 x 10^11  m^2/s^2

  STEP 3   v  =  sqrt( 2.040 x 10^11 )  =  sqrt( 20.40 x 10^10 )
              =  4.52 x 10^5   m/s                     ANSWER (d)
```

> **HOW TO TAKE A SQUARE ROOT OF A POWER OF TEN:** first make the power
> EVEN. `2.040 x 10^11` is awkward, so rewrite it as `20.40 x 10^10`.
> Now `sqrt(20.40) = 4.52` and `sqrt(10^10) = 10^5`. Answer `4.52 x 10^5`.

The threshold frequency of a metal is `4.4 x 10^14 Hz`. Light of frequency

               =  3.976 x 10^-19  -  2.915 x 10^-19
               =  (3.976 - 2.915) x 10^-19

                  2 KE       2 x 1.061 x 10^-19      2.122 x 10^-19

## The hydrogen spectrum and the Rydberg equation

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |      1            +-    1          1     -+                          |
  |   --------  =  R  |  --------  -  ------- |                          |
  |    lambda         +-  n1^2         n2^2  -+                          |
  |                                                                      |
  |   R  =  RYDBERG CONSTANT  =  109677  cm^-1                           |
  |                                                                      |
  |   n1  =  the LOWER shell    n2  =  the HIGHER shell    (n2 > n1)     |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## The five spectral series — LEARN THIS TABLE

```
  +-----------+------+------------+---------------------+------------------+
  |  SERIES   |  n1  |     n2     |  REGION             |  MEMORY          |
  +-----------+------+------------+---------------------+------------------+
  |  LYMAN    |  1   |  2,3,4...  |  ULTRAVIOLET        |  L = Lowest = UV |
  |  BALMER   |  2   |  3,4,5...  |  VISIBLE            |  B = the only    |
  |           |      |            |                     |  one you can SEE |
  |  PASCHEN  |  3   |  4,5,6...  |  INFRARED           |                  |
  |  BRACKETT |  4   |  5,6,7...  |  INFRARED           |                  |
  |  PFUND    |  5   |  6,7,8...  |  FAR INFRARED       |                  |
  +-----------+------+------------+---------------------+------------------+

  MEMORY SENTENCE:   Lyman  Balmer  Paschen  Brackett  Pfund
                       1       2       3        4        5
                     "Little   Boys   Prefer   Bread   Plain"
```

```
  ENERGY LEVEL PICTURE

   n=6  ------------------------------
   n=5  ------------------------------  <-- Pfund starts here (down to n=5)
   n=4  ------------------------------  <-- Brackett ends at n=4
   n=3  ------------------------------  <-- Paschen ends at n=3
   n=2  ------------------------------  <-- Balmer ends at n=2   (VISIBLE)
                    |  |  |
                    v  v  v
   n=1  ------------------------------  <-- Lyman ends at n=1    (UV)
```

## The three phrases that decide n2 — read this before every Rydberg sum

```
  +---------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
  |  THE QUESTION SAYS ...          |  YOU PUT ...                      |
  +---------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
  |  "first line"                   |  n2 = n1 + 1                      |
  |  "second line"                  |  n2 = n1 + 2                      |
  |  "third line"                   |  n2 = n1 + 3                      |
  |  "LONGEST wavelength"           |  n2 = n1 + 1   (same as 1st line) |
  |  "lowest energy line"           |  n2 = n1 + 1                      |
  |  "SERIES LIMIT"                 |  n2 = infinity, so 1/n2^2 = 0     |
  |  "SHORTEST wavelength"          |  n2 = infinity                    |
  |  "highest energy line"          |  n2 = infinity                    |
  +---------------------------------+-----------------------------------+

  WHY:  a SMALL gap between levels -> small energy -> LONG wavelength.
        A HUGE gap (all the way to infinity) -> big energy -> SHORT
        wavelength. Longest and shortest are at OPPOSITE ends.
```

# TOPIC 8 — Bohr's model of the atom  *** THE 8-MARK QUESTION ***

## The postulates (learn these word for word)

```
  POSTULATE 1
  The electron revolves around the nucleus only in certain fixed circular
  paths called ORBITS, SHELLS or STATIONARY STATES.

  POSTULATE 2
  While in a stationary state, the electron does NOT lose energy.
  (This is what fixes Rutherford's collapse problem.)

  POSTULATE 3
  The angular momentum of the electron is QUANTISED:

                                n h
                m v r   =    ---------          n = 1, 2, 3, ...
                                2 pi

  POSTULATE 4
  Energy is emitted or absorbed only when the electron JUMPS from one
  orbit to another:

                delta E  =  E2  -  E1  =  h nu
```

## The two results you must be able to write

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |                       0.529  n^2                                    |
  |    RADIUS    r(n)  = -------------  angstrom     (1 A = 10^-10 m)   |
  |                            Z                                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |                                                                     |
  |                       - 13.6  Z^2                                   |
  |    ENERGY    E(n)  = -------------  eV                              |
  |                            n^2                                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |                       - 2.18 x 10^-18  Z^2                          |
  |               or   = ----------------------  joule                  |
  |                              n^2                                    |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Why is the energy NEGATIVE?

```
  The zero of energy is taken as the electron being INFINITELY far away
  and free. Bringing it close to the nucleus RELEASES energy, so the
  bound electron has LESS than zero energy.

  More negative  =  more tightly bound  =  more stable.
```

### The energy levels of hydrogen (Z = 1)

```
   n = infinity  ....  E = 0            <-- electron is FREE (ionised)
                        |
   n = 5   -------------------  E = -0.54 eV
   n = 4   -------------------  E = -0.85 eV
   n = 3   -------------------  E = -1.51 eV
   n = 2   -------------------  E = -3.40 eV
                        |
   n = 1   -------------------  E = -13.6 eV      <-- GROUND STATE
                                                      most stable

  IONISATION ENERGY of hydrogen  =  0 - (-13.6)  =  13.6 eV
  (the energy needed to pull the electron completely out)
```

## DERIVING THE RADIUS — step by step, not just quoted

Board papers sometimes say "derive an expression for the radius of the nth
orbit". Here is the whole thing, one line at a time. There are only two
physical ideas: **a force balance** and **Bohr's third postulate**.

```
  THE PICTURE

              electron  (charge -e , mass m)
                  o
                 /|\  <- moving with speed v around the circle
                / | \
               /  |  \
              /   |   \        r = radius of the orbit
             |    v    |
             |   (+Ze) |   <- nucleus, charge +Ze
              \       /
               \_____/

  The electron does not fly away and does not fall in, so the ELECTRIC
  PULL of the nucleus must be exactly the CENTRIPETAL force needed to
  keep it moving in a circle.
```

```
  STEP 1   FORCE BALANCE

           electrostatic pull   =   centripetal force needed

                 k Z e^2              m v^2
                ---------      =     -------
                   r^2                  r

           where  k = 1 / (4 pi eps0) = 9 x 10^9  N m^2 / C^2


  STEP 2   Multiply both sides by r . One r cancels on each side:

                 k Z e^2
                ---------      =     m v^2                ...... (1)
                    r


  STEP 3   BOHR'S THIRD POSTULATE (quantised angular momentum)

                            n h
                m v r  =  --------
                            2 pi

           Rearrange for v :

                            n h
                    v  =  ----------                     ...... (2)
                          2 pi m r


  STEP 4   Put (2) into (1). First square v :

                            n^2 h^2
                  v^2  =  --------------
                          4 pi^2 m^2 r^2

           So (1) becomes

                 k Z e^2                 n^2 h^2
                ---------   =   m  x  --------------
                    r                 4 pi^2 m^2 r^2

                 k Z e^2               n^2 h^2
                ---------   =      --------------
                    r              4 pi^2 m r^2


  STEP 5   Multiply both sides by r^2 :

                                        n^2 h^2
                k Z e^2  r     =     -------------
                                      4 pi^2 m


  STEP 6   Divide both sides by  k Z e^2 :

           +--------------------------------------------------+
           |                                                  |
           |                    n^2  h^2                      |
           |        r(n)  =  ------------------                |
           |                  4 pi^2 m k Z e^2                 |
           |                                                  |
           +--------------------------------------------------+

           Everything except n^2 and Z is a constant, so

                                          n^2
                r(n)   is proportional to  -----
                                            Z
```

```
  STEP 7   PUT THE NUMBERS IN, for n = 1 and Z = 1

           h^2      =  (6.626 x 10^-34)^2  =  4.390 x 10^-67
           4 pi^2   =  4 x 9.8696          =  39.478
           m        =  9.11 x 10^-31
           k        =  8.99 x 10^9
           e^2      =  (1.602 x 10^-19)^2  =  2.5664 x 10^-38

           DENOMINATOR, built up one multiplication at a time:

              39.478 x 9.11 x 10^-31             =  3.5965 x 10^-29
              3.5965 x 10^-29  x  8.99 x 10^9    =  3.2333 x 10^-19
              3.2333 x 10^-19  x  2.5664 x 10^-38 = 8.2978 x 10^-57

                        4.390 x 10^-67
           r(1)  =  ----------------------  =  5.29 x 10^-11  m
                       8.2978 x 10^-57

                 =  0.529 x 10^-10 m  =  0.529 angstrom

           THAT is where the famous 0.529 comes from. It is called the
           BOHR RADIUS, written a0 .

           So the working formula is

           +--------------------------------------------------+
           |             0.529  n^2                           |
           |   r(n)  =  ------------  angstrom                |
           |                 Z                                |
           +--------------------------------------------------+
```

## DERIVING THE VELOCITY

```
  STEP 1   From (2):   v  =  n h / (2 pi m r)

  STEP 2   Substitute the r we just found:

                     n h            4 pi^2 m k Z e^2
           v  =  ----------  x  --------------------
                  2 pi m              n^2 h^2

  STEP 3   Cancel one n , one h , one m , and 2 pi into 4 pi^2 :

           +--------------------------------------------------+
           |                                                  |
           |                 2 pi k Z e^2                     |
           |       v(n)  =  ---------------                   |
           |                      n h                         |
           |                                                  |
           +--------------------------------------------------+

                                         Z
           v(n)   is proportional to    -----
                                         n

  STEP 4   NUMBERS, for n = 1 , Z = 1

           2 pi                     =  6.2832
           6.2832 x 8.99 x 10^9     =  5.6486 x 10^10
           x 2.5664 x 10^-38        =  1.4497 x 10^-27
           divide by h = 6.626 x 10^-34 :

                    1.4497 x 10^-27
           v(1) = --------------------  =  2.188 x 10^6  m/s
                    6.626 x 10^-34

           +--------------------------------------------------+
           |                              Z                   |
           |   v(n)  =  2.188 x 10^6  x  ---   m/s            |
           |                              n                   |
           +--------------------------------------------------+

           That is about 1/137 of the speed of light - fast, but not
           fast enough to need relativity.
```

## DERIVING THE ENERGY

```
  STEP 1   KINETIC ENERGY

           From equation (1),   m v^2  =  k Z e^2 / r , so

                    1              k Z e^2
           KE  =  ---  m v^2  =   -----------
                    2                2 r


  STEP 2   POTENTIAL ENERGY

           The electrostatic potential energy of charges +Ze and -e a
           distance r apart is

                       k (Z e)(-e)            k Z e^2
           PE   =    ----------------  =   -  ---------
                            r                     r

           It is NEGATIVE because the two charges ATTRACT.


  STEP 3   TOTAL ENERGY  =  KE  +  PE

                  k Z e^2         k Z e^2
           E  =  ---------   -   ---------
                    2 r              r

           Common denominator 2r:

                  k Z e^2      2 k Z e^2            k Z e^2
           E  =  ---------  -  ----------   =   -  ---------
                    2 r           2 r                 2 r

           +--------------------------------------------------+
           |                     k Z e^2                      |
           |         E   =   -  ---------                     |
           |                       2 r                        |
           +--------------------------------------------------+

           NOTICE:   E = - KE    and    E = PE / 2 .   Handy facts.


  STEP 4   Substitute the r found above:

                    k Z e^2       4 pi^2 m k Z e^2
           E  =  -  ---------  x  ------------------
                        2              n^2 h^2

           +--------------------------------------------------+
           |                                                  |
           |                  2 pi^2 m k^2 Z^2 e^4            |
           |      E(n)  =  -  ----------------------          |
           |                        n^2 h^2                   |
           |                                                  |
           +--------------------------------------------------+

                                             Z^2
           E(n)  is proportional to    -    -----
                                             n^2


  STEP 5   NUMBERS, for n = 1 , Z = 1

           2 pi^2                             =  19.739
           19.739 x 9.11 x 10^-31             =  1.7982 x 10^-29
           k^2 = (8.99 x 10^9)^2              =  8.082  x 10^19
           1.7982 x 10^-29 x 8.082 x 10^19    =  1.4533 x 10^-9
           e^4 = (2.5664 x 10^-38)^2          =  6.5864 x 10^-76
           1.4533 x 10^-9 x 6.5864 x 10^-76   =  9.5722 x 10^-85
           h^2                                =  4.390  x 10^-67

                      9.5722 x 10^-85
           E(1) = -  ------------------  =  - 2.18 x 10^-18  J
                      4.390  x 10^-67

           In electron volts:

                      2.18 x 10^-18
           E(1) =  - ----------------  =  - 13.6  eV
                     1.602 x 10^-19

           +----------------------------------------------------+
           |          - 13.6 Z^2          - 2.18 x 10^-18 Z^2   |
           |  E(n) = ------------- eV  = -------------------- J |
           |              n^2                     n^2           |
           +----------------------------------------------------+
```

> **HOW MUCH OF THIS DO I HAVE TO WRITE IN THE EXAM?**

> spectrum explanation + the limitations are enough. Write the derivation
> only if the paper actually says "derive". But reading it once makes the

> `Z / n^2`.

## LIMITATIONS of Bohr's model (guaranteed part of the 8-mark question)

```
  1.  It works ONLY for hydrogen and hydrogen-like species
      (He+, Li2+, Be3+ - anything with exactly ONE electron).
      It FAILS for every multi-electron atom.

  2.  It cannot explain the FINE STRUCTURE - the fact that each spectral
      line, viewed with a better instrument, is actually several close lines.

  3.  It cannot explain the ZEEMAN EFFECT (splitting of lines in a magnetic
      field) or the STARK EFFECT (splitting in an electric field).

  4.  It cannot explain the SHAPES of molecules or how chemical bonds form.

  5.  It violates the HEISENBERG UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE, because it claims
      to know the exact path AND the exact speed of the electron at the
      same time.

  6.  It treats the electron only as a particle, ignoring its WAVE nature.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.7  (ionisation energy of hydrogen-like ions)

```
  Formula:   IE  =  13.6 Z^2  eV     (from the GROUND state, n = 1)

     H     Z = 1  ->  IE  =  13.6 x 1   =   13.6  eV
     He+   Z = 2  ->  IE  =  13.6 x 4   =   54.4  eV
     Li2+  Z = 3  ->  IE  =  13.6 x 9   =  122.4  eV
     Be3+  Z = 4  ->  IE  =  13.6 x 16  =  217.6  eV

> **TRAP:** "ionisation energy" always means the energy needed to remove the
> electron **completely**, i.e. to take it to `n = infinity` where `E = 0`.
> It is a POSITIVE number. Never write `-13.6 eV` as an ionisation energy.

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.8  (energy of a transition, and the photon it emits)

           E(2)  =  -13.6 / 4  =  -3.400 eV

                    =  -1.511  +  3.400
                    =   1.889  eV                          ANSWER (a)

  STEP 3   In joules:  1.889 x 1.602 x 10^-19 = 3.026 x 10^-19 J

  (b) FREQUENCY

                delta E        3.026 x 10^-19
           nu = ---------  =  -------------------
                   h           6.626 x 10^-34

           3.026 / 6.626  =  0.4567 ;   10^-19 / 10^-34 = 10^15

           nu  =  4.567 x 10^14  Hz                        ANSWER (b)

  (c) WAVELENGTH

                     12400        12400
           lambda = --------  =  --------  =  6564 A  =  656.4 nm
                     E(eV)        1.889
                                                           ANSWER (c)

  CHECK against Worked Example 7.4, where the Rydberg equation gave
  6564.7 A for the first Balmer line. Same answer by a different route.
  THAT is exactly how Bohr's model EXPLAINS the hydrogen spectrum.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.9  (n = 2 to n = 1, checked against Lyman)

```
  STEP 1   E(2) = -3.40 eV ,    E(1) = -13.60 eV

  STEP 2   delta E  =  -3.40  -  ( -13.60 )  =  10.20  eV

  STEP 3   lambda  =  12400 / 10.20  =  1215.7  A

  This matches Worked Example 7.1 (first Lyman line, 1215.7 A) exactly.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.10  (energy needed to EXCITE, not to emit)

How much energy must be supplied to move the electron of a hydrogen atom
from n = 1 to n = 3?

```
  STEP 1   E(1)  =  -13.60 eV
           E(3)  =  - 1.51 eV

  STEP 2   Energy needed  =  E(final)  -  E(initial)
                          =  ( -1.51 )  -  ( -13.60 )
                          =  12.09  eV                      ANSWER

  A POSITIVE answer means energy is ABSORBED. Going the other way
  (3 -> 1) gives -12.09 eV, meaning that much is RELEASED.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.11  (velocity in an orbit)

```
  Formula:   v(n)  =  2.188 x 10^6  x  Z / n     m/s

  (a)  H , n = 1 :    v  =  2.188 x 10^6 x 1/1  =  2.188 x 10^6  m/s

  (b)  H , n = 3 :    v  =  2.188 x 10^6 x 1/3  =  7.29  x 10^5  m/s

  (c)  He+ , n = 1 :  v  =  2.188 x 10^6 x 2/1  =  4.376 x 10^6  m/s

  RULE OF THUMB:   higher n -> SLOWER ;   higher Z -> FASTER.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.12  (angular momentum)

Find the angular momentum of an electron in the 4th Bohr orbit.

```
  STEP 1              n h          4 x 6.626 x 10^-34
           m v r  =  ------  =  -------------------------
                      2 pi           2 x 3.1416

  STEP 2   Numerator   :  4 x 6.626 x 10^-34  =  2.6504 x 10^-33
           Denominator :  6.2832

  STEP 3   2.6504 / 6.2832  =  0.4218

           m v r  =  0.4218 x 10^-33  =  4.218 x 10^-34  kg m^2 / s
                                                             ANSWER

  QUICK VERSION:  h / (2 pi)  =  1.0546 x 10^-34 , so just multiply that
  by n. For n = 4 :  4 x 1.0546 x 10^-34 = 4.218 x 10^-34.  Same answer.
```

> **TRAP:** angular momentum depends **only on n**. It contains no Z.
> The angular momentum in the 2nd orbit of He+ equals that in the 2nd
> orbit of H.

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.13  (ratios — the fastest kind of MCQ)

```
  (a) r(H, n=3) : r(H, n=1)
         r is proportional to n^2   ->   3^2 : 1^2  =  9 : 1

  (b) r(He+, n=2) : r(H, n=1)
         r is proportional to n^2 / Z
         He+ :  4/2 = 2        H :  1/1 = 1       ->  2 : 1

  (c) E(He+, n=1) : E(H, n=1)
         E is proportional to Z^2 / n^2
         He+ :  4/1 = 4        H :  1/1 = 1       ->  4 : 1

  (d) v(H, n=1) : v(H, n=2)
         v is proportional to 1/n   ->   1/1 : 1/2  =  2 : 1
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8.14  (identify the ion from its energy)

The energy of an electron in the 2nd orbit of a hydrogen-like species is
-13.6 eV. Identify the species.

```
  STEP 1            - 13.6 Z^2
           -13.6 = -------------
                       2^2

  STEP 2   -13.6  =  - 13.6 Z^2 / 4

  STEP 3   Multiply both sides by 4, then divide by -13.6 :

               4  =  Z^2      ->      Z  =  2

  STEP 4   Z = 2 with one electron  ->  He+                  ANSWER
```

# TOPIC 9 — de Broglie: matter behaves like a wave

Louis de Broglie said: if light can behave as both a wave and a particle,
then matter should too.

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |               h            h                                         |
  |   lambda  =  ----   =   -------                                      |
  |               p           m v                                        |
  |                                                                      |
  |   lambda = wavelength   h = Planck's constant                        |
  |   m = mass              v = velocity        p = momentum             |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Why don't we see a cricket ball behaving like a wave?

```
  For a cricket ball:  m = 0.15 kg , v = 30 m/s

               6.626 x 10^-34
   lambda  =  ----------------  =  1.5 x 10^-34 m
                0.15  x  30

  That is unimaginably smaller than the ball itself, so the wave nature
  is completely undetectable.

  For an electron:  m = 9.11 x 10^-31 kg , v = 10^6 m/s

   lambda  is about  7 x 10^-10 m  -  comparable to the size of an atom.
   THAT is why the wave nature matters for electrons but not for balls.
```

> **KEY RULE:** the wave nature is significant only for VERY LIGHT particles.

## Three forms of the same formula — pick the one that matches the data

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |  GIVEN mass and VELOCITY:           lambda  =  h / (m v)            |
  |                                                                     |
  |  GIVEN mass and KINETIC ENERGY:     lambda  =  h / sqrt(2 m KE)     |
  |                                                                     |
  |  GIVEN an ACCELERATING VOLTAGE V                                    |
  |  for a particle of charge q:        lambda  =  h / sqrt(2 m q V)    |
  |                                                                     |
  |  For an ELECTRON that last one becomes the exam shortcut            |
  |                                                                     |
  |          lambda  =  12.27 / sqrt(V)     angstrom                    |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+

  WHERE THE SECOND FORM COMES FROM:
        KE = (1/2) m v^2   ->   v = sqrt(2 KE / m)
        m v = m sqrt(2 KE/m) = sqrt(2 m KE)
        so   lambda = h / sqrt(2 m KE)
```

## de Broglie EXPLAINS Bohr's third postulate — a lovely 4-mark point

```
  Bohr just ASSERTED that  m v r = n h / 2 pi .  de Broglie showed WHY.

  For the electron wave not to cancel itself out, the circumference of
  the orbit must hold a WHOLE NUMBER of wavelengths - a standing wave:

              2 pi r   =   n  lambda

  Put in lambda = h / (m v) :

                              n h
              2 pi r   =   ---------
                              m v

  Rearranging:              n h
              m v r    =   --------      <- exactly Bohr's postulate
                            2 pi

  PICTURE (n = 4, four whole waves fitted round the ring):

              _   _   _   _
             / \ / \ / \ / \        ALLOWED - the wave joins up
            |   V   V   V   |       neatly with itself
             \ _   _   _   /
                V   V   V

              _   _   _  __
             / \ / \ / \/            NOT ALLOWED - the wave arrives
            |   V   V   |            out of step and cancels itself
             \ _   _  __/            out. That orbit cannot exist.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.1  (mass and velocity given)

Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at `2.05 x 10^7 m/s`.

```
  STEP 1   Denominator first:
           m v  =  (9.11 x 10^-31)(2.05 x 10^7)

           9.11 x 2.05    =  18.676
           10^-31 x 10^7  =  10^-24

           m v  =  18.676 x 10^-24  =  1.8676 x 10^-23  kg m/s

  STEP 2               6.626 x 10^-34
           lambda  =  ------------------
                       1.8676 x 10^-23

           6.626 / 1.8676  =  3.548
           10^-34 / 10^-23 =  10^-11

  STEP 3   lambda  =  3.548 x 10^-11  m  =  0.0355 nm  =  0.355 A
                                                              ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.2  (kinetic energy given)

An electron has kinetic energy `3.0 x 10^-25 J`. Find its wavelength.

```
  STEP 1   Use  lambda = h / sqrt( 2 m KE )

  STEP 2   2 m KE  =  2 x (9.11 x 10^-31) x (3.0 x 10^-25)

           2 x 9.11 x 3.0  =  54.66
           10^-31 x 10^-25 =  10^-56

           2 m KE  =  54.66 x 10^-56

  STEP 3   Take the square root. The power 10^-56 is EVEN, which is
           exactly what we want:

           sqrt(54.66)   =  7.393
           sqrt(10^-56)  =  10^-28

           sqrt(2 m KE)  =  7.393 x 10^-28

  STEP 4               6.626 x 10^-34
           lambda  =  ------------------  =  8.963 x 10^-7  m
                       7.393 x 10^-28

                    =  896.3 nm                            ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.3  (accelerating voltage given)

Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through
(a) 100 V, (b) 1000 V.

```
  (a) THE SHORTCUT WAY

                     12.27         12.27
           lambda = ----------  =  -------  =  1.227  angstrom
                    sqrt(100)        10

      THE LONG WAY, as a check:

      2 m q V  =  2 x 9.11 x 10^-31 x 1.602 x 10^-19 x 100

           2 x 9.11 x 10^-31            =  1.822 x 10^-30
           x 1.602 x 10^-19             =  2.9188 x 10^-49
           x 100                        =  2.9188 x 10^-47

      Make the exponent even:  29.188 x 10^-48
           sqrt(29.188)  =  5.4026
           sqrt(10^-48)  =  10^-24
           sqrt(2mqV)    =  5.4026 x 10^-24

                   6.626 x 10^-34
      lambda  =  ------------------  =  1.226 x 10^-10 m  =  1.226 A
                  5.4026 x 10^-24

      The shortcut and the long way agree.               ANSWER (a)

  (b)                12.27          12.27
           lambda = -----------  =  -------  =  0.388  angstrom
                    sqrt(1000)       31.62
                                                          ANSWER (b)

  Note: 10 times the voltage does NOT give 10 times the wavelength -
  it divides the wavelength by sqrt(10) = 3.16.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.4  (cricket ball — why we never see it)

```
  m = 0.15 kg ,  v = 30 m/s

                6.626 x 10^-34       6.626 x 10^-34
     lambda  =  ----------------  =  ----------------
                  0.15 x 30                4.5

             =  1.47 x 10^-34   m                        ANSWER

```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.5  (a proton, for contrast)

Find the wavelength of a proton (`m = 1.67 x 10^-27 kg`) moving at
`1.0 x 10^5 m/s`.

```
  STEP 1   m v  =  (1.67 x 10^-27)(1.0 x 10^5)  =  1.67 x 10^-22

  STEP 2               6.626 x 10^-34

                        1.67 x 10^-22

```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.6  (find the MASS — the reverse question)

A particle moving at 300 m/s has a de Broglie wavelength of
`5.6 x 10^-7 m`. Find its mass.

```
  STEP 1   Rearrange:      h                     h
                lambda = -----     ->     m = ------------
                           m v                 lambda x v

  STEP 2   Denominator:  (5.6 x 10^-7)(300)  =  1.68 x 10^-4

  STEP 3               6.626 x 10^-34
              m   =  ------------------
                       1.68 x 10^-4

           6.626 / 1.68   =  3.944
           10^-34 / 10^-4 =  10^-30

  STEP 4   m  =  3.944 x 10^-30  kg                        ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.7  (find the VELOCITY from the wavelength)

What velocity must an electron have for its de Broglie wavelength to be
1 angstrom?

```
  STEP 1              h                6.626 x 10^-34
            v  =  ------------  =  ---------------------------
                  m x lambda       (9.11 x 10^-31)(1 x 10^-10)

  STEP 2   Denominator:  9.11 x 10^-31 x 10^-10  =  9.11 x 10^-41

  STEP 3   6.626 / 9.11    =  0.7273
           10^-34 / 10^-41 =  10^7

           v  =  0.7273 x 10^7  =  7.27 x 10^6  m/s        ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9.8  (checking the standing-wave idea)

Show that exactly TWO de Broglie wavelengths fit round the 2nd Bohr orbit
of hydrogen.

```
  STEP 1   The 2nd orbit:  r = 2.116 A = 2.116 x 10^-10 m

  STEP 2   Its circumference:
           2 pi r  =  2 x 3.1416 x 2.116 x 10^-10  =  1.3296 x 10^-9  m

  STEP 3   The speed in the 2nd orbit:
           v  =  2.188 x 10^6 / 2  =  1.094 x 10^6  m/s

  STEP 4   Its de Broglie wavelength:
           m v  =  (9.11 x 10^-31)(1.094 x 10^6)  =  9.966 x 10^-25

                       6.626 x 10^-34
           lambda  =  ------------------  =  6.648 x 10^-10  m
                        9.966 x 10^-25

  STEP 5   How many fit?

            2 pi r        1.3296 x 10^-9
           ---------  =  -----------------  =  2.00      exactly TWO
            lambda        6.648 x 10^-10

  This is not a coincidence. It works for every allowed orbit, and it is
  the reason those orbits - and no others - are allowed.
```

# TOPIC 10 — Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |  It is IMPOSSIBLE to determine, at the same time and with complete   |
  |  accuracy, both the POSITION and the MOMENTUM of a small particle.   |
  |                                                                      |
  |                                     h                                |
  |         (delta x)  x  (delta p)  >=  -----                           |
  |                                     4 pi                             |
  |                                                                      |
  |                                          h                           |
  |    or   (delta x)  x  (m  delta v)  >=  -----                        |
  |                                         4 pi                         |
  |                                                                      |
  |  delta x  =  uncertainty in POSITION                                 |
  |  delta p  =  uncertainty in MOMENTUM                                 |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  WHAT IT MEANS IN PLAIN WORDS:

  The more precisely you know WHERE an electron is,
  the less precisely you can know HOW FAST it is going - and vice versa.

  CONSEQUENCE:  Bohr's neat circular "orbits" cannot be real.
                We must replace ORBITS with ORBITALS - regions where the
                electron is PROBABLY found.
```

## The two numbers that make every Heisenberg sum quick

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                  |
  |      h            6.626 x 10^-34                                 |
  |   -------  =  ---------------------  =  5.273 x 10^-35   J s     |
  |    4 pi            4 x 3.1416                                    |
  |                                                                  |
  |      h              5.273 x 10^-35                               |
  |   ---------  =  ---------------------  =  5.79 x 10^-5   m^2/s   |
  |   4 pi m           9.11 x 10^-31                                 |
  |                                                                  |
  |   The second one is for an ELECTRON only, and it means            |
  |                                                                  |
  |        (delta x) x (delta v)  >=  5.79 x 10^-5  m^2/s            |
  |                                                                  |
  |   One division and you are done.                                 |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

---

## WORKED EXAMPLES — TOPIC 10

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10.1  (uncertainty in velocity)

The uncertainty in the position of an electron is `1 x 10^-10 m`.
Find the minimum uncertainty in its velocity.

```
  STEP 1                                    h
           (delta x) x (m delta v)   >=   -----
                                          4 pi

  STEP 2   Rearrange:
                                h                 5.273 x 10^-35
           delta v   >=  ------------------  =  --------------------
                         4 pi m (delta x)        (9.11x10^-31)(10^-10)

  STEP 3   Denominator:  9.11 x 10^-31 x 1 x 10^-10  =  9.11 x 10^-41

  STEP 4   5.273 / 9.11     =  0.5788
           10^-35 / 10^-41  =  10^6

           delta v  >=  0.5788 x 10^6  =  5.79 x 10^5  m/s     ANSWER

  MEANING: if you pin the electron down to inside one atom, you no longer
  know its speed to better than about 580 kilometres per second. That is
  a colossal uncertainty - which is exactly why fixed "orbits" are
  meaningless for an electron.

  SHORTCUT CHECK:  5.79 x 10^-5 / 10^-10  =  5.79 x 10^5 .  Same.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10.2  (a tighter position)

The uncertainty in position is now `1 x 10^-11 m` (one tenth of an angstrom).

```
           delta v  >=  5.79 x 10^-5 / 10^-11  =  5.79 x 10^6  m/s
                                                              ANSWER

  Squeeze the position ten times tighter and the velocity uncertainty
  gets ten times WORSE. That is the principle in one line.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10.3  (uncertainty in POSITION, given the speed)

An electron moves with a speed of `2.2 x 10^6 m/s` measured to an accuracy
of 0.1 %. Find the uncertainty in its position.

```
  STEP 1   0.1 % of the speed:

           delta v  =  (0.1 / 100) x 2.2 x 10^6
                    =  0.001 x 2.2 x 10^6
                    =  2.2 x 10^3   m/s

  STEP 2                        h                     5.273 x 10^-35
           delta x   >=  ------------------  =  ------------------------
                         4 pi m (delta v)        (9.11x10^-31)(2.2x10^3)

  STEP 3   Denominator:  9.11 x 2.2  =  20.04 ;  10^-31 x 10^3 = 10^-28
                         =  20.04 x 10^-28  =  2.004 x 10^-27

  STEP 4   5.273 / 2.004    =  2.631
           10^-35 / 10^-27  =  10^-8

           delta x  >=  2.631 x 10^-8  m  =  26.3 nm        ANSWER

  COMPARE: an atom is only about 0.1 nm wide, so the electron's position
  is uncertain over about 260 atoms. It cannot be on a neat circle.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10.4  (uncertainty in MOMENTUM)

The position of an electron is known to within `1 x 10^-9 m`.
Find the minimum uncertainty in its momentum.

```
  STEP 1                    h            5.273 x 10^-35
           delta p  >=  -----------  =  ------------------
                        4 pi delta x        1 x 10^-9

  STEP 2   10^-35 / 10^-9  =  10^-26

           delta p  >=  5.273 x 10^-26  kg m / s          ANSWER
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10.5  (why the principle does not matter for a ball)

A cricket ball of mass 0.15 kg has its speed known to within
`1 x 10^-6 m/s`. Find the uncertainty in its position.

```
  STEP 1   Denominator:  m x delta v  =  0.15 x 10^-6  =  1.5 x 10^-7

  STEP 2                5.273 x 10^-35
           delta x  >=  ----------------
                          1.5 x 10^-7

           5.273 / 1.5      =  3.515
           10^-35 / 10^-7   =  10^-28

           delta x  >=  3.52 x 10^-28  m                    ANSWER

  That is a hundred thousand million times smaller than a nucleus.
  Completely undetectable. THIS is why the uncertainty principle never
  bothers anything you can see: the mass m sits in the denominator, so a
  big mass makes the uncertainty vanish.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10.6  (the energy-time form)

An excited atom stays in its excited state for `1 x 10^-8 s`.
Find the minimum uncertainty in its energy.

```
  STEP 1                          h
           (delta E)(delta t)  >=  ------
                                   4 pi

  STEP 2               5.273 x 10^-35
           delta E >= ------------------  =  5.273 x 10^-27  J
                          1 x 10^-8
                                                             ANSWER

  This tiny blur in energy is the reason spectral lines are never
  infinitely thin - they always have a small natural WIDTH.
```

> **TRAP:** the answer to every Heisenberg question is a MINIMUM.
> Write the sign as `>=`, not `=`. Examiners look for it.

> **TRAP:** the mass must be in kilograms. If a question gives the mass
> of a particle in grams or in atomic mass units, convert first:
> `1 g = 10^-3 kg` and `1 u = 1.66 x 10^-27 kg`.

# TOPIC 12 — Quantum numbers  *** THE OTHER 8-MARK QUESTION ***

Four numbers describe every electron completely — like a postal address.

```
  n  ->  which CITY      (which shell / energy level / size)
  l  ->  which STREET    (which subshell / shape)
  m  ->  which HOUSE     (which orbital / orientation)
  s  ->  which ROOM      (spin direction)
```

## 1. Principal quantum number  (n)

```
  VALUES:      n = 1, 2, 3, 4, ...    (never zero)

  TELLS YOU:   the SIZE of the orbital and the main ENERGY level

  SHELL NAMES: n=1 -> K ,  n=2 -> L ,  n=3 -> M ,  n=4 -> N

  Maximum electrons in a shell  =  2 n^2
     n=1 -> 2 ,  n=2 -> 8 ,  n=3 -> 18 ,  n=4 -> 32

  Number of orbitals in a shell  =  n^2
     n=1 -> 1 ,  n=2 -> 4 ,  n=3 -> 9 ,  n=4 -> 16
```

## 2. Azimuthal (angular momentum) quantum number  (l)

```
  VALUES:      l = 0  to  (n - 1)          so there are n values

  TELLS YOU:   the SHAPE of the orbital (the SUBSHELL)

  +-------+----------+-------------------+---------------------+
  |   l   | SUBSHELL |  SHAPE            |  MAX ELECTRONS      |
  +-------+----------+-------------------+---------------------+
  |   0   |    s     |  spherical        |         2           |
  |   1   |    p     |  dumb-bell        |         6           |
  |   2   |    d     |  double dumb-bell |        10           |
  |   3   |    f     |  complex          |        14           |
  +-------+----------+-------------------+---------------------+

  Max electrons in a subshell  =  2 (2 l + 1)
  Number of orbitals in a subshell  =  (2 l + 1)

  EXAMPLE:  if n = 3 , then  l = 0, 1, 2   ->  subshells 3s , 3p , 3d
```

## 3. Magnetic quantum number  (m  or  m-l)

```
  VALUES:      m = -l  ,  ... , 0 , ... , +l        so (2l + 1) values

  TELLS YOU:   the ORIENTATION of the orbital in space

  +-------+-----------------------+-------------+---------------------+
  |   l   |  VALUES OF m          | NO. OF      |  ORBITAL NAMES      |
  |       |                       | ORBITALS    |                     |
  +-------+-----------------------+-------------+---------------------+
  |   0   |  0                    |     1       |  s                  |
  |   1   |  -1 , 0 , +1          |     3       |  px , py , pz       |
  |   2   |  -2,-1, 0,+1,+2       |     5       |  dxy, dyz, dzx,     |
  |       |                       |             |  dx2-y2 , dz2       |
  |   3   |  -3 to +3             |     7       |  seven f orbitals   |
  +-------+-----------------------+-------------+---------------------+
```

## 4. Spin quantum number  (s  or  m-s)

```
  VALUES:      s = +1/2   or   -1/2       (only two values, ever)

  TELLS YOU:   which way the electron spins

               +1/2  ->  drawn as an UP arrow      (clockwise)
               -1/2  ->  drawn as a DOWN arrow     (anticlockwise)
```

## Putting it together — a worked table for n = 3

```
  +-----+-----+---------------------+-----------+----------------+
  |  n  |  l  |  m                  | SUBSHELL  |  ORBITALS      |
  +-----+-----+---------------------+-----------+----------------+
  |  3  |  0  |  0                  |    3s     |      1         |
  |  3  |  1  |  -1 , 0 , +1        |    3p     |      3         |

## THE FULL QUANTUM-NUMBER TABLE FOR EVERY ELECTRON IN AN ATOM

This is the exercise that makes quantum numbers finally click. Do it with a
pen. We take **nitrogen, Z = 7**, configuration `1s2 2s2 2p3`.

     1s          2s              2p
   +----+      +----+     +---+ +---+ +---+
   | ^v |      | ^v |     | ^ | | ^ | | ^ |
   +----+      +----+     +---+ +---+ +---+
                           m=-1  m=0  m=+1

   (Hund's rule: the three 2p electrons go in SINGLY, all spin up.)

```

## Extend it to oxygen (Z = 8) — only ONE new row

```
     1s          2s              2p
   +----+      +----+     +----+ +---+ +---+

## Same job for sodium (Z = 11) — just the outermost electron

```

      n = 3 ,  l = 0 ,  m = 0 ,  s = +1/2

# TOPIC 13 — Shapes of orbitals

## s orbital — SPHERICAL

```
              ....
           ..      ..
          .    ()    .          () = nucleus at the centre
          .          .
           ..      ..           Same probability in every direction.
              ....              Only ONE orientation, so only ONE s orbital.
```

## p orbitals — DUMB-BELL, three of them at right angles

```
        pz                px                    py
                                                 
        |                                        /
       ( )                                     ( )
        |                 ---( )---            /
       ( )                                   ( )
        |                                    /
                                            
     along z axis      along x axis       along y axis

  Each p orbital has a NODAL PLANE passing through the nucleus, where the
  probability of finding the electron is ZERO.
```

## d orbitals — DOUBLE DUMB-BELL (mostly), five of them

```
  dxy , dyz , dzx , dx2-y2     ->  four-lobed "clover leaf" shape
  dz2                          ->  dumb-bell along z with a doughnut
                                   (ring) around the middle
```

## Nodes

```
  A NODE is a place where the probability of finding the electron is ZERO.

  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |    RADIAL NODES     =   n  -  l  -  1                                |
  |                                                                      |
  |    ANGULAR NODES    =   l                                            |
  |                                                                      |
  |    TOTAL NODES      =   n  -  1                                      |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  EXAMPLE:  3p orbital  ->  n = 3 , l = 1
            radial nodes  =  3 - 1 - 1  =  1
            angular nodes =  1
            total         =  2   =   3 - 1     OK
```

## What the two kinds of node actually look like

```
  ANGULAR NODE  =  a flat PLANE (or a cone) through the nucleus where
                   the probability is zero.
                   An s orbital has none. A p orbital has one. A d
                   orbital has two.

         pz orbital, seen side-on:

                    ( + )        upper lobe
         -----------------------  <-- the xy PLANE is an angular node
                    ( - )        lower lobe

         Anywhere on that plane the electron is NEVER found.


  RADIAL NODE   =  a spherical SHELL, at a certain distance from the
                   nucleus, where the probability is zero.
                   You can only see it by cutting the orbital open.

         2s orbital, cut in half:

                 . . . . . . .
              .  outer region   .
            .   . . . . . . .    .
           .  .  ZERO HERE   .    .   <-- the radial node, a whole
           . .   (the node)   . .  .       sphere of zero probability
           .  .  ---------   .    .
            .   . inner  .        .
              .   region        .
                 . . . . . . .
```

## Radial probability curves — how many humps?

```
  The number of PEAKS in a radial probability curve  =  n - l

     1s (n-l = 1)          2s (n-l = 2)         2p (n-l = 1)

      ^                      ^                    ^
      |  /\                  | /\                 |    /\
      | /  \                 |/  \   __           |   /  \
      |/    \_____           |    \_/  \____      |  /    \____
      +------------> r       +---+---------> r    +-------------> r
                                 |
                             radial node
                             (touches zero)

  1 peak, no node       2 peaks, 1 node      1 peak, no radial node
```

# TOPIC 14 — The three filling rules

## RULE 1 — Aufbau principle

```
  Electrons fill the LOWEST energy orbital FIRST.

  Energy order is decided by the  (n + l)  RULE:

     LOWER (n + l)  ->  filled FIRST
     If (n + l) is EQUAL for two orbitals, the one with the LOWER n
     is filled first.

  EXAMPLE:  compare  3d  and  4s

     3d  ->  n + l  =  3 + 2  =  5
     4s  ->  n + l  =  4 + 0  =  4       <-- LOWER, so 4s fills FIRST

  EXAMPLE:  compare  3d  and  4p

     3d  ->  n + l  =  5        4p  ->  n + l  =  5      TIE
     3d has the smaller n , so 3d fills FIRST.
```

**Filling order:**
```
  1s  2s  2p  3s  3p  4s  3d  4p  5s  4d  5p  6s  4f  5d  6p  7s  5f  6d  7p
```

## RULE 2 — Pauli exclusion principle

```
  NO TWO ELECTRONS in an atom can have ALL FOUR quantum numbers the same.

  CONSEQUENCE:  an orbital can hold at most TWO electrons, and they must
                have OPPOSITE spins.

                     +----+
                     | ^v |      allowed  (opposite spins)
                     +----+

                     +----+
                     | ^^ |      NOT allowed (same spin)
                     +----+
```

## RULE 3 — Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity

```
  Electrons fill orbitals of the SAME subshell SINGLY first, all with the
  SAME spin, before any pairing starts.

  Filling three p orbitals with 3 electrons:

     RIGHT                          WRONG
     +---+ +---+ +---+              +----+ +---+ +---+
     | ^ | | ^ | | ^ |              | ^v | | ^ | |   |
     +---+ +---+ +---+              +----+ +---+ +---+

  WHY:  electrons repel each other. Sitting in separate orbitals keeps
        them further apart, which lowers the energy.

  ANALOGY:  people getting into an empty bus sit one per seat before
            anyone sits next to a stranger.
```

---

## THE (n + l) RULE WORKED ON EVERY PAIR THAT MATTERS

The rule has two lines and you must apply BOTH, in order:

```
  LINE 1   The orbital with the LOWER (n + l) fills FIRST.
  LINE 2   If (n + l) is EQUAL, the one with the LOWER n fills first.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 14.1 — the pairs, one at a time

```
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  |  COMPARE   |  n + l  |  n + l  |  TIE?  |  WHICH FILLS FIRST, WHY   |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 2p and 3s  | 2+1 = 3 | 3+0 = 3 |  YES   | 2p , because n=2 < n=3    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 3p and 4s  | 3+1 = 4 | 4+0 = 4 |  YES   | 3p , because n=3 < n=4    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 4s and 3d  | 4+0 = 4 | 3+2 = 5 |  no    | 4s , its sum 4 is lower   |
  |            |         |         |        | *** THE FAMOUS ONE ***    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 3d and 4p  | 3+2 = 5 | 4+1 = 5 |  YES   | 3d , because n=3 < n=4    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 4p and 5s  | 4+1 = 5 | 5+0 = 5 |  YES   | 4p , because n=4 < n=5    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 5s and 4d  | 5+0 = 5 | 4+2 = 6 |  no    | 5s , its sum 5 is lower   |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 4d and 5p  | 4+2 = 6 | 5+1 = 6 |  YES   | 4d , because n=4 < n=5    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 5p and 6s  | 5+1 = 6 | 6+0 = 6 |  YES   | 5p , because n=5 < n=6    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 6s and 4f  | 6+0 = 6 | 4+3 = 7 |  no    | 6s , its sum 6 is lower   |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 4f and 5d  | 4+3 = 7 | 5+2 = 7 |  YES   | 4f , because n=4 < n=5    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 5d and 6p  | 5+2 = 7 | 6+1 = 7 |  YES   | 5d , because n=5 < n=6    |
  +------------+---------+---------+--------+---------------------------+
  | 6p and 7s  | 6+1 = 7 | 7+0 = 7 |  YES   | 6p , because n=6 < n=7    |

  | 7s and 5f  | 7+0 = 7 | 5+3 = 8 |  no    | 7s , its sum 7 is lower   |

### WORKED EXAMPLE 14.2 — build the whole filling order from scratch

```
  Group every orbital by its (n + l) value, then inside each group put
  them in order of increasing n. Read the groups top to bottom.

  +-----------+-----------------------------+---------------------------+
  |  (n + l)  |  ORBITALS WITH THAT SUM     |  ORDER (lower n first)    |
  +-----------+-----------------------------+---------------------------+
  |     1     |  1s                         |  1s                       |
  |     2     |  2s                         |  2s                       |
  |     3     |  2p , 3s                    |  2p , 3s                  |
  |     4     |  3p , 4s                    |  3p , 4s                  |
  |     5     |  3d , 4p , 5s               |  3d , 4p , 5s             |
  |     6     |  4d , 5p , 6s               |  4d , 5p , 6s             |
  |     7     |  4f , 5d , 6p , 7s          |  4f , 5d , 6p , 7s        |
  |     8     |  5f , 6d , 7p               |  5f , 6d , 7p             |
  +-----------+-----------------------------+---------------------------+

  READ IT OFF IN ONE LINE:

  1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p 6s 4f 5d 6p 7s 5f 6d 7p

  That is the whole Aufbau order, DERIVED rather than memorised. If you
  ever forget the diagonal-arrow diagram in the exam, rebuild it this way
  in about ninety seconds.
```

> **TRAP:** the (n + l) rule tells you the order in which orbitals are
> **FILLED**. It does NOT tell you the order in which electrons are
> **REMOVED** when an ion forms. See Topic 15 for that.

## Why Cr and Cu break the rule — the stability of half-filled and fully-filled subshells

```
  EXPECTED for Cr (Z=24):   [Ar] 3d4 4s2
  ACTUAL   for Cr:          [Ar] 3d5 4s1     <-- half-filled d, half-filled s

  EXPECTED for Cu (Z=29):   [Ar] 3d9 4s2
  ACTUAL   for Cu:          [Ar] 3d10 4s1    <-- fully-filled d

  REASON (two parts - write BOTH for full marks):

  1.  SYMMETRY
      A half-filled (d5) or fully-filled (d10) subshell is perfectly
      symmetrical, and symmetrical distributions are more stable.

  2.  EXCHANGE ENERGY
      Electrons with the SAME spin in different orbitals can exchange
      places. Every exchange releases energy. The more electrons with
      parallel spin, the more exchanges are possible, and the more
      energy is released - which means greater stability.

      d5 and d10 arrangements maximise the number of exchanges.
```

### Counting the exchanges — the proof that d5 beats d4

```
  The number of exchange pairs among N electrons of the SAME spin in
  different orbitals is  N(N-1)/2 .

     d4  (four parallel electrons):   4 x 3 / 2  =   6 exchanges
     d5  (five parallel electrons):   5 x 4 / 2  =  10 exchanges

  Moving one 4s electron into the d subshell buys FOUR extra exchanges.
  The energy released by those four exchanges is greater than the small
  cost of promoting the electron, so 3d5 4s1 wins.

  DRAW IT to show the examiner:

     EXPECTED  Cr = 3d4 4s2
     3d  +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+     4s  +----+
         | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | |   |         | ^v |
         +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+         +----+

     ACTUAL    Cr = 3d5 4s1
     3d  +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+     4s  +---+
         | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ |         | ^ |
         +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+         +---+
            half-filled AND half-filled  ->  doubly stable
```

## ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATIONS OF IONS — the 4s-before-3d removal rule

This is worth a guaranteed 2 marks and it is where most students lose them.

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                     |
  |   FILLING an atom:    4s goes IN  before  3d                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   EMPTYING an ion:    4s comes OUT before 3d                        |
  |                                                                     |
  |   "LAST IN is NOT first out."                                       |
  |                                                                     |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+

  WHY: once the 3d orbitals start filling, they drop BELOW the 4s in
  energy. In the ion, 4s is now the outermost and highest-energy
  orbital, so it is the one that gets emptied first.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 15.1 — iron and its two ions, step by step

```
  Fe  (Z = 26)

  STEP 1   Fill in Aufbau order:
           1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 4s2 3d6

  STEP 2   Rewrite in SHELL order (this is how the board wants it):
           1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 3d6 4s2      or   [Ar] 3d6 4s2

  STEP 3   Fe2+  -  remove TWO electrons, and they come from 4s :

           [Ar] 3d6 4s2   ->  remove both 4s   ->   [Ar] 3d6

           WRONG ANSWER that many students give:  [Ar] 3d4 4s2

  STEP 4   Fe3+  -  remove THREE: both 4s first, then ONE 3d :

           [Ar] 3d6 4s2   ->   [Ar] 3d5

  STEP 5   Count the unpaired electrons with boxes:

     Fe2+  =  3d6
        +----+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+
        | ^v | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ |    ->  4 UNPAIRED
        +----+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+

     Fe3+  =  3d5
        +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+
        | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ | | ^ |     ->  5 UNPAIRED
        +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+ +---+
            half-filled - this is why Fe3+ is more stable than Fe2+
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 15.2 — copper and its two ions

```
  Cu (Z = 29)  =  [Ar] 3d10 4s1        (already an exception)

  Cu+   -  remove the single 4s electron    ->  [Ar] 3d10
           d10 , completely filled, ZERO unpaired electrons.
           A d10 ion is colourless and diamagnetic.

  Cu2+  -  remove 4s , then one 3d          ->  [Ar] 3d9
           d9 , ONE unpaired electron.
           This is the blue ion you see in copper sulphate solution.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 15.3 — the whole first transition series, ionised

```
  +---------+----+-------------------+-------------------+-----------+
  | ELEMENT |  Z |  ATOM             |  COMMON ION       | UNPAIRED  |
  +---------+----+-------------------+-------------------+-----------+
  |   Sc    | 21 |  [Ar] 3d1 4s2     |  Sc3+ = [Ar]      |     0     |
  |   Ti    | 22 |  [Ar] 3d2 4s2     |  Ti3+ = [Ar] 3d1  |     1     |
  |   V     | 23 |  [Ar] 3d3 4s2     |  V3+  = [Ar] 3d2  |     2     |
  |   Cr    | 24 |  [Ar] 3d5 4s1  *  |  Cr3+ = [Ar] 3d3  |     3     |
  |   Mn    | 25 |  [Ar] 3d5 4s2     |  Mn2+ = [Ar] 3d5  |     5     |
  |   Fe    | 26 |  [Ar] 3d6 4s2     |  Fe2+ = [Ar] 3d6  |     4     |
  |         |    |                   |  Fe3+ = [Ar] 3d5  |     5     |
  |   Co    | 27 |  [Ar] 3d7 4s2     |  Co2+ = [Ar] 3d7  |     3     |
  |   Ni    | 28 |  [Ar] 3d8 4s2     |  Ni2+ = [Ar] 3d8  |     2     |
  |   Cu    | 29 |  [Ar] 3d10 4s1 *  |  Cu2+ = [Ar] 3d9  |     1     |
  |   Zn    | 30 |  [Ar] 3d10 4s2    |  Zn2+ = [Ar] 3d10 |     0     |
  +---------+----+-------------------+-------------------+-----------+
           * = the two exceptions to the Aufbau order

  HOW TO COUNT THE UNPAIRED ELECTRONS IN A d^x ION IN FIVE SECONDS:

     x = 1,2,3,4,5   ->   unpaired = x        (they all go in singly)
     x = 6,7,8,9,10  ->   unpaired = 10 - x   (pairing has begun)

     Check with the table:  d6 -> 10-6 = 4  OK ,  d9 -> 10-9 = 1  OK
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 15.4 — magnetic moment from the unpaired count

```
  The magnetic moment (in Bohr magnetons, BM) is

        mu  =  sqrt( n ( n + 2 ) )      where n = number of unpaired e

     n = 0  ->  mu = 0                      DIAMAGNETIC
     n = 1  ->  sqrt(1 x 3)  = sqrt(3)  = 1.73 BM
     n = 2  ->  sqrt(2 x 4)  = sqrt(8)  = 2.83 BM
     n = 3  ->  sqrt(3 x 5)  = sqrt(15) = 3.87 BM
     n = 4  ->  sqrt(4 x 6)  = sqrt(24) = 4.90 BM
     n = 5  ->  sqrt(5 x 7)  = sqrt(35) = 5.92 BM

  So for Fe3+ (5 unpaired):  mu = 5.92 BM
     for Ni2+ (2 unpaired):  mu = 2.83 BM

  ONE UNPAIRED ELECTRON OR MORE  ->  PARAMAGNETIC (pulled into a magnet)
  ZERO UNPAIRED ELECTRONS        ->  DIAMAGNETIC  (pushed out slightly)
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 15.5 — configurations of common p-block ions

```
  For MAIN GROUP elements there is no 4s/3d complication - just add or
  remove electrons from the outermost shell.

  +---------+----------------------+-----------------------------------+
  |  ION    |  ELECTRONS           |  CONFIGURATION                    |
  +---------+----------------------+-----------------------------------+
  |  Na+    |  11 - 1 = 10         |  1s2 2s2 2p6           = [Ne]     |
  |  Mg2+   |  12 - 2 = 10         |  1s2 2s2 2p6           = [Ne]     |
  |  Al3+   |  13 - 3 = 10         |  1s2 2s2 2p6           = [Ne]     |
  |  F-     |   9 + 1 = 10         |  1s2 2s2 2p6           = [Ne]     |
  |  O2-    |   8 + 2 = 10         |  1s2 2s2 2p6           = [Ne]     |
  |  N3-    |   7 + 3 = 10         |  1s2 2s2 2p6           = [Ne]     |
  |  Cl-    |  17 + 1 = 18         |  [Ne] 3s2 3p6          = [Ar]     |
  |  K+     |  19 - 1 = 18         |  [Ne] 3s2 3p6          = [Ar]     |
  |  Ca2+   |  20 - 2 = 18         |  [Ne] 3s2 3p6          = [Ar]     |
  |  S2-    |  16 + 2 = 18         |  [Ne] 3s2 3p6          = [Ar]     |
  +---------+----------------------+-----------------------------------+

  Every ion in the top block has 10 electrons - they are ISOELECTRONIC
  with neon. Every ion in the bottom block has 18 - isoelectronic with
  argon. That is why they are all stable: they reach a noble gas
  configuration.
```

# CHECKLIST BEFORE YOU HAND IN THE PAPER

```
  [ ]  Did I write the ENERGY as NEGATIVE (E = -13.6 Z^2 / n^2) ?
  [ ]  In the Rydberg equation, is n1 the LOWER shell ?
  [ ]  Did I remember Balmer is the only VISIBLE series ?
  [ ]  Did I fill 4s BEFORE 3d ?
  [ ]  Did I apply Hund's rule - singly first, then pair ?
  [ ]  Did I mention BOTH symmetry AND exchange energy for Cr / Cu ?
  [ ]  Did I convert nm to metres  (1 nm = 10^-9 m)  in numericals ?
  [ ]  Did I convert eV to joules  (1 eV = 1.602 x 10^-19 J)  where needed ?
```

## The other eight places marks quietly disappear

```
  [ ]  For an ION, did I ADD electrons for a negative charge and
       SUBTRACT for a positive one ?   (O2- has 10 electrons, not 6)

  [ ]  When making a transition-metal CATION, did I remove the 4s
       electrons BEFORE the 3d ?   (Fe2+ is [Ar] 3d6 , never [Ar] 3d4 4s2)

  [ ]  In a Heisenberg answer, did I write ">=" and not "=" ?
       The answer is always a MINIMUM.

  [ ]  For a wave number in cm^-1, did I put lambda in CENTIMETRES ?
       (1 A = 10^-8 cm , 1 nm = 10^-7 cm)

  [ ]  In a photoelectric sum, are the photon energy and the work
       function in the SAME unit ?  Never mix eV with joules.

  [ ]  If the photon energy came out SMALLER than the work function,
       did I say "no emission" instead of writing a negative KE ?

  [ ]  Did I take the square root correctly - by first making the
       power of ten EVEN ?  (2.04 x 10^11 -> 20.4 x 10^10 -> 4.52 x 10^5)

  [ ]  For "series limit" or "shortest wavelength", did I put
       n2 = infinity so that 1/n2^2 becomes ZERO ?
```
