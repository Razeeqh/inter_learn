# TOPIC 1 — What does "s-block" even mean?

The periodic table is divided into blocks according to **which orbital the last
electron goes into**.

```
  s-BLOCK  ->  last electron enters an  s  orbital
  p-BLOCK  ->  last electron enters a   p  orbital
  d-BLOCK  ->  last electron enters a   d  orbital
  f-BLOCK  ->  last electron enters an  f  orbital
```

The s-block is only **two groups**, sitting at the extreme left of the table.

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                               |
  |   GROUP 1   ALKALI METALS          general config:  ns1       |
  |             Li  Na  K  Rb  Cs  Fr                             |
  |                                                               |
  |   GROUP 2   ALKALINE EARTH METALS  general config:  ns2       |
  |             Be  Mg  Ca  Sr  Ba  Ra                            |
  |                                                               |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## Why the names?

```
  ALKALI METALS          Their oxides and hydroxides dissolve in water and
                         give strongly ALKALINE (basic) solutions.

  ALKALINE EARTH METALS  Their oxides are also alkaline, AND they were found
                         in the "earth" (that is, in rocks and soil), and
                         those oxides do not melt or dissolve easily.
```

```
  MEMORY SENTENCE for Group 1:
      "Li Na Ki Rab se Kaho Farmaish"      ->  Li Na K Rb Cs Fr

  MEMORY SENTENCE for Group 2:
      "Beta Mange Car Scooter Bike Race"   ->  Be Mg Ca Sr Ba Ra
```

## Electronic configurations you should be able to write

```
  GROUP 1                              GROUP 2
  ------------------------------       ------------------------------
  Li  (3)   [He] 2s1                   Be  (4)   [He] 2s2
  Na  (11)  [Ne] 3s1                   Mg  (12)  [Ne] 3s2
  K   (19)  [Ar] 4s1                   Ca  (20)  [Ar] 4s2
  Rb  (37)  [Kr] 5s1                   Sr  (38)  [Kr] 5s2
  Cs  (55)  [Xe] 6s1                   Ba  (56)  [Xe] 6s2
  Fr  (87)  [Rn] 7s1                   Ra  (88)  [Rn] 7s2
```

```
  Fr (francium) and Ra (radium) are RADIOACTIVE.
  Fr is the rarest naturally occurring element - you will never be
  asked chemistry of Fr beyond "it is radioactive".
```

> **TRAP:** Hydrogen also has the configuration 1s1, but it is **NOT** an
> alkali metal. It is a non-metal gas. Do not include H in Group 1 lists.

# TOPIC 3 — Group 1 trends, and the REASON behind each one

This is the heart of the 8-mark question. Never write only the trend — always
write the **reason**.

## 3.1 Atomic and ionic radii

| PROPERTY | TREND DOWN THE GROUP | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic radius | INCREASES: Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs | A new shell is added at every step. The extra shells push the outer electron farther out, and inner electrons shield it from the nucleus. |
| Ionic radius (M+) | INCREASES: Li+ < Na+ < K+ < Rb+ < Cs+ | Same reason — more shells. |
| M+ compared with M | M+ is always SMALLER than M | Losing an electron removes a whole outer shell, and the remaining electrons feel a stronger pull from the same number of protons. |

```
  ALKALI METALS HAVE THE LARGEST ATOMS IN THEIR PERIOD.
      Reason: they have the lowest nuclear charge in that period, and
              only one electron in a brand-new outermost shell.
```

## 3.2 Ionisation enthalpy

| POINT | STATEMENT | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Value | Alkali metals have the LOWEST first ionisation enthalpy of all elements in their period | The single ns1 electron is far from the nucleus and well shielded, so very little energy is needed to pull it out. |
| Trend | IE1 DECREASES down the group: Li > Na > K > Rb > Cs | Atomic size increases, so the outer electron is held more weakly. |
| Second IE | IE2 is ENORMOUSLY higher than IE1 | After losing one electron the atom has a stable noble-gas configuration. Breaking into that is extremely difficult. |

```
  CONSEQUENCE:  alkali metals show ONLY the +1 oxidation state.
                They never form M2+ ions, because IE2 is far too large.
```

## 3.3 Hydration enthalpy

When an ion is dropped into water, water molecules crowd around it and energy
is released. That energy is the **hydration enthalpy**.

```
  RULE:   SMALLER ion  ->  higher charge density  ->  attracts water
                           molecules more strongly  ->  LARGER (more
                           negative) hydration enthalpy.

  ORDER:   Li+  >  Na+  >  K+  >  Rb+  >  Cs+
```

```
  CONSEQUENCE 1:  Li+ is the most heavily hydrated ion, so the HYDRATED
                  Li+ ion is the BIGGEST of all hydrated alkali metal ions.

  CONSEQUENCE 2:  because it drags so much water with it, hydrated Li+
                  moves SLOWLY, so LiCl solution conducts electricity
                  LESS well than CsCl solution of the same concentration.

  CONSEQUENCE 3:  Li salts are usually HYDRATED (LiCl.2H2O),
                  Na and K salts usually are not.
```

> **TRAP:** "Smallest ion" and "smallest hydrated ion" are opposites.
> Li+ is the **smallest bare ion** but the **largest hydrated ion**.

## 3.4 Physical properties

| PROPERTY | FACT | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness | Very SOFT — can be cut with a knife (Na, K especially) | Only one valence electron per atom, so the metallic bond is weak. |
| Softness trend | Softness INCREASES down the group (Li hardest, Cs softest) | The atom gets bigger, so the metallic bond gets even weaker. |
| Melting and boiling point | Very LOW, and DECREASE down the group | Weak metallic bonding, weakening further as size increases. |
| Density | LOW; increases down the group | Large atomic size with fairly low mass. Li, Na and K are LIGHTER THAN WATER. |
| Density exception | K is LESS dense than Na | K has an unusually large increase in atomic volume compared to its mass increase. |
| Lightest metal | Li is the LIGHTEST metal known (density 0.53 g/cm3) | Very small mass, reasonably large volume. |
| Conductivity | Good conductors of heat and electricity | Free mobile valence electrons. |
| Electropositive character | Highly electropositive; increases down the group | Low and decreasing ionisation enthalpy. |

```
  DENSITY VALUES WORTH REMEMBERING (g/cm3)

      Li  0.53   |   Na  0.97   |   K  0.86   |   Rb  1.53   |   Cs  1.90

      Note:  Li < Na  but  K < Na    <-- the odd one out
```

## 3.5 Flame colours — free 2 marks every year

When these metals are heated in a flame, the outer electron absorbs heat, jumps
to a higher energy level, then falls back and gives out light of a particular
colour.

```
  +----------+----------------------+
  |  METAL   |  FLAME COLOUR        |
  +----------+----------------------+
  |   Li     |  CRIMSON RED         |
  |   Na     |  GOLDEN YELLOW       |
  |   K      |  LILAC (pale violet) |
  |   Rb     |  RED VIOLET          |
  |   Cs     |  BLUE                |
  +----------+----------------------+

  WHY they give colours at all:
      Their ionisation enthalpy is LOW, so the modest energy of a bunsen
      flame is enough to excite the outer electron. When the electron
      falls back, visible light is emitted.
```

```
  MEMORY:   "Crimson Nan Golden, King Lilac, Rob Red-Violet, Cs Blue"
              Li          Na       K          Rb              Cs
```

```
  PHOTOELECTRIC USE:  Cs and K are used in PHOTOELECTRIC CELLS,
                      because their ionisation enthalpies are so low
                      that ordinary light can knock electrons out.
```

# TOPIC 4 — Chemical reactions of Group 1

Alkali metals are the **most reactive metals**. Reactivity increases down the
group.

## 4.1 Reaction with air / oxygen — the OXYGEN RULE

```
  Li      +  O2   --(burning in air)-->   Li2O     (OXIDE)
                                          plus a small amount of Li2O2

  Na      +  O2   --(burning in air)-->   Na2O2    (PEROXIDE)

  K, Rb, Cs +  O2 --(burning in air)-->   KO2 , RbO2 , CsO2   (SUPEROXIDES)
```

Balanced versions:

```
  4 Li  +  O2   -->  2 Li2O
  2 Na  +  O2   -->  Na2O2
    K   +  O2   -->  KO2
```

```
  WHY THE DIFFERENCE?   (this is the marks-carrying reason)

      O2-      oxide ion       SMALL
      O2 2-    peroxide ion    MEDIUM
      O2 -     superoxide ion  LARGE

      A large cation stabilises a large anion, because the sizes match
      and the crystal lattice packs properly.

      Li+  is very small  ->  can only stabilise the small O2- ion
      Na+  is medium      ->  stabilises the peroxide ion
      K+, Rb+, Cs+ large  ->  stabilise the large superoxide ion
```

```
  EXTRA FACT:  KO2 (and all superoxides) are PARAMAGNETIC and COLOURED,
               because the superoxide ion O2- contains ONE UNPAIRED
               ELECTRON.
               KO2 is used in space capsules and submarines to absorb
               CO2 and release oxygen.

  4 KO2  +  2 CO2   -->  2 K2CO3  +  3 O2
```

All alkali metals also **tarnish rapidly in moist air**, forming the oxide and
then the hydroxide and finally the carbonate.

```
  LITHIUM IS SPECIAL:  it is the ONLY alkali metal that reacts directly
  with nitrogen of the air.

      6 Li  +  N2  -->  2 Li3N        (lithium nitride, ruby red)
```

## 4.2 Reaction with water

```
  2 M  +  2 H2O   -->   2 MOH  +  H2         (M = Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs)

  example:  2 Na  +  2 H2O   -->   2 NaOH  +  H2
```

```
  VIOLENCE OF THE REACTION INCREASES DOWN THE GROUP:

      Li    reacts steadily, gas bubbles off gently
      Na    reacts briskly, melts into a ball, may catch fire
      K     catches fire immediately (lilac flame)
      Rb,Cs react EXPLOSIVELY
```

> **TRAP:** Li has the MOST NEGATIVE standard electrode potential
> (E = -3.04 V), yet it reacts with water the LEAST violently.
> Reason: Li is a small, hard metal with a **high melting point and high
> lattice/atomisation energy**, so the reaction is slow. Electrode potential
> tells you about the **thermodynamics**, not the **speed**.

## 4.3 Reaction with hydrogen — ionic hydrides

```
  2 M  +  H2   --(heat)-->   2 M+ H-           IONIC (saline) HYDRIDES

  example:  2 Na  +  H2  --(673 K)-->  2 NaH
```

```
  These hydrides are:
      ionic, white crystalline solids
      contain the HYDRIDE ion H-
      strong REDUCING agents
      react with water:   NaH  +  H2O  -->  NaOH  +  H2

  STABILITY:   LiH  >  NaH  >  KH  >  RbH  >  CsH
      Reason: as the cation gets bigger, the M-H bond becomes weaker.
      LiH is the most stable of the alkali metal hydrides.
```

## 4.4 Reaction with halogens

```
  2 M  +  X2   -->   2 MX          (X = F, Cl, Br, I)

  example:  2 Na  +  Cl2  -->  2 NaCl
```

```
  All alkali metal halides are IONIC, white, high melting solids,
  soluble in water.

  EXCEPTION:  LiI is the MOST COVALENT alkali metal halide.
      Reason (FAJANS' RULES):
          Li+ is very small  ->  very high polarising power
          I-  is very large  ->  very easily polarised
          Result: the electron cloud of I- is dragged towards Li+,
                  giving the bond a covalent character.
```

## 4.5 Reducing nature

```
  All alkali metals are POWERFUL REDUCING AGENTS, because they lose
  their single electron very easily.

  STANDARD ELECTRODE POTENTIALS (volts):

      Li   -3.04     <-- MOST negative
      Na   -2.71
      K    -2.93
      Rb   -2.98
      Cs   -2.99
```

**The classic question: why is Li the strongest reducing agent in aqueous
solution, even though it has the highest ionisation enthalpy of the group?**

```
  Break the process into three steps:

      STEP 1   Li(s)   -->  Li(g)          needs SUBLIMATION enthalpy
      STEP 2   Li(g)   -->  Li+(g) + e-    needs IONISATION enthalpy
      STEP 3   Li+(g)  -->  Li+(aq)        RELEASES HYDRATION enthalpy

  Steps 1 and 2 are unfavourable for Li (it costs the most energy there).

  BUT step 3 is HUGELY favourable for Li, because Li+ is the smallest
  ion and therefore has by far the LARGEST HYDRATION ENTHALPY.

  That very large hydration energy MORE THAN CANCELS the high ionisation
  enthalpy, so overall Li gives up its electron most readily in water.

      => Li has the most negative E value  =>  strongest reducing agent
         IN AQUEOUS SOLUTION.
```

> **TRAP:** In the **gas phase** (no water present), Cs is the strongest
> reducing agent, because there hydration enthalpy plays no part and only
> ionisation enthalpy matters. Always write the words "in aqueous solution"
> when you say Li is the strongest.

## 4.6 Solutions in liquid ammonia — the blue solution

All alkali metals dissolve in liquid ammonia to give **deep blue solutions**.

```
  M  +  (x + y) NH3   -->   [M(NH3)x]+   +   [e(NH3)y]-
                            ammoniated       AMMONIATED
                            cation           ELECTRON
```

| OBSERVATION | EXPLANATION |
|---|---|
| Deep BLUE colour | The ammoniated electrons absorb energy in the red region of visible light; the transmitted light appears blue. |
| PARAMAGNETIC | The ammoniated electrons are unpaired. |
| Conducts electricity very well | Both the ammoniated cation and the free ammoniated electron carry current. |
| Strong REDUCING agent | The free electrons are available to be donated. |
| On standing, colour fades | Slowly the metal reacts with ammonia to form the amide. |
| Concentrated solutions | Turn BRONZE / copper coloured and become DIAMAGNETIC (the electrons pair up into clusters). |

```
  M  +  NH3   --(slow, catalysed by iron)-->   M NH2  +  (1/2) H2
                                               amide
```

# TOPIC 5 — Anomalous behaviour of LITHIUM

Lithium, the first member of Group 1, behaves differently from the rest.

## Why lithium is different (write these three reasons first)

```
  1.  Exceptionally SMALL atomic and ionic size.
  2.  Very HIGH charge / size ratio  ->  very high POLARISING POWER,
      so Li forms compounds with much more COVALENT character.
  3.  ABSENCE of d orbitals in its valence shell.
      (Its highest ionisation enthalpy and highest electronegativity in
       the group follow from the small size.)
```

## The points of difference (learn any six)

| PROPERTY | LITHIUM | REST OF GROUP 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness | Hardest of the group | Soft, get softer down the group |
| Melting and boiling point | Highest of the group | Much lower |
| Reaction with water | Reacts slowly and quietly | Brisk to explosive |
| With oxygen | Forms only the oxide Li2O | Na gives peroxide, K/Rb/Cs give superoxides |
| With nitrogen | Reacts directly to give Li3N | Do NOT react with N2 |
| Nature of compounds | Largely covalent | Largely ionic |
| Hydrogencarbonate | LiHCO3 exists only in SOLUTION | NaHCO3, KHCO3 exist as SOLIDS |
| Carbonate on heating | Li2CO3 decomposes: Li2CO3 --(heat)--> Li2O + CO2 | Stable to heat, do not decompose |
| Hydroxide on heating | LiOH decomposes: 2 LiOH --(heat)--> Li2O + H2O | Stable, sublime without decomposing |
| Nitrate on heating | 4 LiNO3 --(heat)--> 2 Li2O + 4 NO2 + O2 | 2 NaNO3 --(heat)--> 2 NaNO2 + O2 |
| Solubility of fluoride | LiF is sparingly soluble | NaF, KF soluble |
| Solubility of carbonate | Li2CO3 sparingly soluble | Na2CO3, K2CO3 highly soluble |
| Chloride | LiCl is DELIQUESCENT and exists as LiCl.2H2O; soluble in ethanol and pyridine | NaCl is not deliquescent, not soluble in organic solvents |
| Alkyl compounds | LiC2H5 and other alkyls are stable and soluble in organic solvents | Corresponding Na compounds are less stable |

> **TRAP:** LiF is insoluble but LiI is soluble; NaF is soluble but NaI is also
> soluble. For **small anions** like F-, lattice energy dominates, so the small
> Li+ makes a very strong lattice that water cannot break apart. Do not mix
> this up with the covalent-character argument used for LiI.

# TOPIC 6 — The DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP: Li and Mg

```
      GROUP 1     GROUP 2     GROUP 13

        Li    \
               \
        Na       Mg    \
                        \
                          Al
```

```
  WHY does a diagonal relationship exist?

      Going ACROSS a period, size DECREASES and electronegativity INCREASES.
      Going DOWN a group, size INCREASES and electronegativity DECREASES.

      Move diagonally (one right and one down) and the two effects
      CANCEL OUT. So Li and Mg end up with almost the same size,
      the same electronegativity and the same charge/size ratio (the same
      POLARISING POWER). Similar polarising power = similar chemistry.
```

## Points of similarity between Li and Mg (learn any six)

| # | SIMILARITY |
|---|---|
| 1 | Both are harder and have higher melting points than the other members of their groups. |
| 2 | Both react slowly with water. |
| 3 | Both form only the normal oxide on burning: Li2O and MgO (no peroxide, no superoxide). |
| 4 | Both react directly with nitrogen to form nitrides: Li3N and Mg3N2. |
| 5 | Their carbonates decompose on heating: Li2CO3 --(heat)--> Li2O + CO2 and MgCO3 --(heat)--> MgO + CO2. |
| 6 | Their hydroxides are weak bases and decompose on heating: LiOH and Mg(OH)2. |
| 7 | LiHCO3 and Mg(HCO3)2 exist only in solution, never as solids. |
| 8 | LiCl and MgCl2 are both deliquescent and crystallise as hydrates: LiCl.2H2O and MgCl2.6H2O. |
| 9 | Both LiCl and MgCl2 are soluble in ethanol and pyridine (covalent character). |
| 10 | LiF and MgF2 are both sparingly soluble in water. |
| 11 | Both form organometallic compounds that are used in organic synthesis. |
| 12 | Neither forms a stable solid hydrogencarbonate or a stable ethynide. |

# TOPIC 7 — Sodium chloride, NaCl

```
  COMMON NAME:  common salt, table salt.  Mineral name: ROCK SALT (halite)

  SOURCE:  sea water (about 2.7 to 2.9 percent NaCl by mass) and rock salt
           deposits.

  PURIFICATION:
      Crude NaCl is dissolved in minimum water, filtered to remove
      insoluble matter, then HCl gas is passed through the saturated
      solution. Pure NaCl crystallises out because of the COMMON ION
      EFFECT (the extra Cl- from HCl pushes NaCl out of solution).
      Impurities such as MgCl2 and CaCl2 stay in solution.
```

| PROPERTY | DETAIL |
|---|---|
| Appearance | Colourless cubic crystals |
| Melting point | 1081 K |
| Solubility | 36 g per 100 g of water at 273 K (solubility hardly changes with temperature) |
| Deliquescence | Pure NaCl is NOT deliquescent; impure NaCl is, because of MgCl2 and CaCl2 impurities |

```
  USES OF NaCl
      - food seasoning and food preservation
      - raw material for NaOH, Na2CO3, NaHCO3, HCl, Cl2 and Na metal
      - freezing mixtures (ice + NaCl gives about 251 K)
      - in leather tanning and in the textile industry
```

# TOPIC 8 — Sodium hydroxide, NaOH — the CASTNER-KELLNER CELL

**Common names: caustic soda, white caustic.**

Made by the **electrolysis of brine** (a concentrated aqueous solution of NaCl)
in a Castner-Kellner mercury cathode cell.

## The cell — draw this diagram

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                    C A S T N E R  -  K E L L N E R   C E L L            |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------------+

              brine in                                    Cl2 gas out
                 |                                             ^
                 v                                             |
     +===========================================================+
     |                    ELECTROLYTIC  CHAMBER                  |
     |                                                           |
     |    [+] TITANIUM / CARBON ANODE   [+]      [+]             |
     |     ||        ||        ||        ||                      |
     |    brine  (concentrated NaCl solution)                    |
     |___________________________________________________________|
     |~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ MERCURY  ( flowing CATHODE ) ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~|
     +===========================================================+
                              |
                     sodium amalgam  Na/Hg
                              |
                              v
     +===========================================================+
     |                    D E N U D E R                          |
     |      Na/Hg amalgam  +  water  , with iron as catalyst     |
     |                                                           |
     |        gives   NaOH  solution  +  H2 gas  +  Hg           |
     +===========================================================+
              |                 |                    |
              v                 v                    |
          NaOH solution      H2 gas out              |
                                                     |
                                MERCURY RECYCLED BACK TO THE CELL
                                <------------------------------+
```

## The reactions — write ALL of these

```
  IONISATION IN SOLUTION
      NaCl        -->   Na+  +  Cl-
      H2O         -->   H+   +  OH-

  AT THE CATHODE (mercury):
      Na+  +  e-   -->   Na
      Na   +  Hg   -->   Na / Hg          (sodium amalgam)

  AT THE ANODE (carbon or titanium):
      Cl-          -->   Cl  +  e-
      2 Cl         -->   Cl2 (gas)

  IN THE DENUDER:
      2 Na/Hg  +  2 H2O   --(iron catalyst)-->  2 NaOH  +  H2  +  2 Hg

  OVERALL:
      2 NaCl  +  2 H2O   --(electrolysis)-->   2 NaOH  +  Cl2  +  H2
```

> **TRAP:** Why is a MERCURY cathode used instead of an iron one?
> Because Na+ discharged on mercury dissolves into it as an amalgam, so the
> sodium is kept **away from the water and away from the chlorine**. On an
> ordinary cathode the sodium would react with water immediately and the NaOH
> formed would mix with Cl2 to give NaOCl, ruining the product.

## Properties of NaOH

| PROPERTY | DETAIL |
|---|---|
| Appearance | White, translucent, crystalline solid; m.p. 591 K |
| Nature | Highly DELIQUESCENT — absorbs moisture from air |
| Dissolving in water | Very soluble; dissolving is strongly EXOTHERMIC |
| On the skin | Extremely corrosive, hence "caustic" soda |
| With acid | NaOH + HCl --> NaCl + H2O |
| With CO2 | 2 NaOH + CO2 --> Na2CO3 + H2O |
| With amphoteric metals | 2 Al + 2 NaOH + 6 H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 3 H2 |
| With Zn | Zn + 2 NaOH --> Na2ZnO2 + H2 |

```
  USES OF NaOH
      - manufacture of soap, paper, artificial silk (rayon)
      - petroleum refining
      - purification of bauxite in aluminium extraction
      - in the textile industry for mercerising cotton
      - as a laboratory reagent
```

# TOPIC 9 — Sodium carbonate, Na2CO3 — the SOLVAY PROCESS

**Common name: washing soda. Formula of the crystals: Na2CO3.10H2O.**
The anhydrous form is called **soda ash**.

The Solvay process is also called the **ammonia-soda process**.

## Raw materials

```
      1.  Brine            (saturated NaCl solution)
      2.  Ammonia          NH3
      3.  Carbon dioxide   CO2   (obtained by heating limestone)
      4.  Limestone        CaCO3
```

          v

     | (quick- |                    |                |
     |  lime)  |                    +--------+-------+
     +----+----+                             |
          |                                  |
          | + H2O                            |
          v                                  |
     +---------+                             |
     | Ca(OH)2 |                             |
     +----+----+                             |
          |                                  |
          |                                  v
          |            BRINE (NaCl) ---> +--------------------------+
          |            AMMONIA  NH3 ---> |   CARBONATING  TOWER     |
          |                              |  (ammoniacal brine + CO2)|
          |                              +------------+-------------+
          |                                           |
          |                    NaHCO3 (solid, less soluble) precipitates
          |                                           |

          |                        <--------------------        |
          |                                                     |
          +-----------------------> AMMONIA RECOVERY TOWER <-----+
                                          |
                            NH4Cl + Ca(OH)2 --> NH3 + CaCl2 + H2O
                                          |
                                          v
                                   NH3 RECYCLED to the brine
                                   CaCl2 is the by-product
```

## The equations — the marks are HERE

```
  1)   CaCO3     --(heat, 1200 K)-->     CaO   +   CO2

  2)   NH3  +  H2O  +  CO2      -->      NH4HCO3
                                         (ammonium hydrogencarbonate)

  3)   NH4HCO3  +  NaCl         -->      NaHCO3 (solid)  +  NH4Cl

  4)   2 NaHCO3   --(heat, 373 K)-->     Na2CO3  +  CO2  +  H2O

  AMMONIA RECOVERY:

  5)   CaO  +  H2O              -->      Ca(OH)2

  6)   2 NH4Cl  +  Ca(OH)2      -->      2 NH3  +  CaCl2  +  2 H2O

  OVERALL:

       2 NaCl  +  CaCO3   -->   Na2CO3  +  CaCl2
```

```
  KEY IDEA OF THE WHOLE PROCESS:
      NaHCO3 is only SLIGHTLY soluble in water, so it precipitates out
      of the saturated solution and can be filtered off. Everything else
      stays dissolved.

  ECONOMY OF THE PROCESS:
      NH3 is recovered and reused.
      CO2 is recovered and reused.
      The only waste product is CaCl2.
```

> **TRAP:** The Solvay process **cannot be used to make potassium carbonate,
> K2CO3**. Reason: KHCO3 is **too soluble** in water, so it never precipitates
> out and cannot be filtered off. This is a favourite 2-mark question.

# TOPIC 11 — Biological importance of sodium and potassium

```
  ROUGH AMOUNTS IN A 70 kg ADULT:
      sodium     about 90 mg per kg of body mass
      potassium  about 1.4 g per kg of body mass
```

| ION | WHERE IT SITS | WHAT IT DOES |
|---|---|---|
| Na+ | Mainly OUTSIDE the cell — in blood plasma and the fluid between cells | Controls the flow of water across cell membranes; helps transport sugars and amino acids into cells; takes part in transmission of nerve impulses |
| K+ | Mainly INSIDE the cell | Activates many enzymes; takes part in the oxidation of glucose to produce ATP; involved in nerve signal transmission |

## The sodium-potassium pump

```
  +-----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                       |
  |     OUTSIDE the cell        |  CELL MEMBRANE  |    INSIDE the cell    |
  |     high Na+ , low K+       |                 |   low Na+ , high K+   |
  |                             |                 |                       |
  |         Na+  <-- <-- <-- <--|-- P U M P  <-- <|-- Na+   (3 out)       |
  |                             |     ^           |                       |
  |         K+   --> --> --> -->|---> |  --> --> -|-> K+    (2 in)        |
  |                             |    ATP          |                       |
  |                             | hydrolysis      |                       |
  |                             | provides the    |                       |
  |                             | energy          |                       |
  +-----------------------------------------------------------------------+

  FACTS TO WRITE:
      - The pump moves 3 Na+ OUT of the cell and 2 K+ INTO the cell
        for every molecule of ATP that is hydrolysed.
      - It works AGAINST the concentration gradient, so it needs energy.
      - It consumes more than one third of the ATP used by a resting
        human being - it is the single largest energy consumer of the body.
      - It maintains the difference in ion concentration that makes
        nerve impulse transmission possible.
```

# TOPIC 12 — Group 2 trends, and the REASON behind each one

Group 2: **Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra**, configuration **ns2**.

## 12.1 The basic comparison with Group 1

| PROPERTY | GROUP 2 COMPARED WITH GROUP 1 | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic size | SMALLER | Higher nuclear charge in the same period pulls the electrons in |
| Ionisation enthalpy IE1 | HIGHER | Smaller size and greater nuclear charge |
| Ionisation enthalpy IE2 | LOWER than IE2 of Group 1 | After losing one electron the Group 2 ion still has one more valence electron, not a noble-gas core. For Group 1, IE2 means breaking a noble-gas core |
| Hardness | HARDER | Two valence electrons per atom means stronger metallic bonding |
| Melting and boiling point | HIGHER | Stronger metallic bonding |
| Density | HIGHER | Smaller atoms, closer packing |
| Reactivity | LESS reactive | Higher ionisation enthalpy |
| Hydration enthalpy | MUCH larger | M2+ is smaller and carries double the charge |

```
  WHY +2 AND NOT +1?

      Group 2 metals DO have a high IE2, but the extra lattice energy
      (in a solid) or the extra hydration energy (in solution) released
      by forming M2+ instead of M+ MORE THAN PAYS BACK that cost.
      So the +2 state is always preferred.
```

## 12.2 Trends down Group 2

| PROPERTY | TREND | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic and ionic radius | INCREASES: Be < Mg < Ca < Sr < Ba | New shell added each period |
| Ionisation enthalpy | DECREASES down the group | Outer electrons are farther out and better shielded |
| Hydration enthalpy | DECREASES: Be2+ > Mg2+ > Ca2+ > Sr2+ > Ba2+ | The ion gets bigger, so it attracts water less |
| Electropositive character | INCREASES down the group | IE decreases |
| Reactivity | INCREASES down the group | IE decreases |
| Melting/boiling points | No regular trend | Different crystal structures in different metals |
| Density | Falls from Be to Ca, then rises | Ca has the lowest density in the group |
| Basic character of M(OH)2 | INCREASES: Be(OH)2 amphoteric, Mg(OH)2 weakly basic, Ba(OH)2 strongly basic | Larger, less polarising cation holds OH- more loosely, so it is released more easily |

## 12.3 Flame colours of Group 2

```
  +----------+----------------------+
  |  METAL   |  FLAME COLOUR        |
  +----------+----------------------+
  |   Be     |  NO COLOUR           |
  |   Mg     |  NO COLOUR           |
  |   Ca     |  BRICK RED           |
  |   Sr     |  CRIMSON             |
  |   Ba     |  APPLE GREEN         |
  |   Ra     |  CRIMSON             |
  +----------+----------------------+
```

```
  WHY DO Be AND Mg GIVE NO FLAME COLOUR?   (guaranteed 2-mark question)

      Be and Mg atoms are very SMALL, so their electrons are held very
      tightly and their IONISATION ENTHALPIES are very HIGH.
      The energy of a bunsen flame is NOT ENOUGH to excite those
      electrons to a higher energy level.
      No excitation -> no light emitted -> no flame colour.
```

```
  USE:  Sr and Ba salts are used in FIREWORKS and signal flares,
        because of their bright crimson and apple green colours.
```

# TOPIC 13 — Chemical reactions of Group 2

## 13.1 With air and oxygen

```
  2 M  +  O2   --(burning)-->   2 MO         normal OXIDE

  3 M  +  N2   --(burning)-->   M3N2         NITRIDE
                                (ALL Group 2 metals form nitrides)

  example:   2 Mg  +  O2  -->  2 MgO
             3 Mg  +  N2  -->  Mg3N2
```

```
  BeO and MgO are very stable and have very high melting points.
      BeO   is AMPHOTERIC
      MgO, CaO, SrO, BaO are BASIC, and become more basic down the group

  MgO is used as a REFRACTORY material for furnace linings, because its
  melting point is extremely high.

  Peroxides:  BaO2 and SrO2 exist, but the metals do NOT form them by
              simply burning in air. Be and Mg form no peroxide at all.
              NO Group 2 metal forms a superoxide.
```

## 13.2 With water

```
  Be    +  H2O   -->   NO REACTION even with steam
                       (a protective oxide layer forms)

  Mg    +  H2O(steam, hot)  -->   MgO  +  H2
        Mg reacts only with HOT water or steam, not cold water.

  Ca, Sr, Ba  +  2 H2O  -->  M(OH)2  +  H2      with COLD water

  example:   Ca  +  2 H2O  -->  Ca(OH)2  +  H2
```

```
  REACTIVITY WITH WATER INCREASES DOWN THE GROUP:
      Be  <  Mg  <  Ca  <  Sr  <  Ba
      Reason: ionisation enthalpy falls, so the metal loses electrons
      more easily.
```

## 13.3 With hydrogen

```
  M  +  H2   --(heat)-->   MH2

  Ca, Sr, Ba give IONIC hydrides:   CaH2 , SrH2 , BaH2
  MgH2 is intermediate / polymeric.
  BeH2 is COVALENT and cannot be made by direct combination -
      it is prepared indirectly.

  CaH2 is called HYDROLITH and is used to produce hydrogen:
      CaH2  +  2 H2O   -->   Ca(OH)2  +  2 H2
```

## 13.4 With halogens

```
  M  +  X2   --(heat)-->   MX2

  All Group 2 halides are IONIC, EXCEPT BeCl2 and BeF2 which are COVALENT.

  BeCl2 is prepared from BeO:
      BeO  +  C  +  Cl2   --(870 K)-->   BeCl2  +  CO
```

## 13.5 With acids

```
  M  +  2 HCl   -->   MCl2  +  H2

  example:   Mg  +  2 HCl  -->  MgCl2  +  H2

  All Group 2 metals liberate H2 from dilute acids.
```

```
  BERYLLIUM IS SPECIAL: it also reacts with ALKALIS, liberating hydrogen.

      Be  +  2 NaOH  +  2 H2O   -->   Na2[Be(OH)4]  +  H2
                                      sodium beryllate

  No other Group 2 metal does this. It is proof that Be is AMPHOTERIC.
```

# TOPIC 14 — Solubility: lattice energy versus hydration energy

This is the hardest idea in the chapter, so go slowly. It is worth 4 marks.

## The tug of war

```
  To dissolve an ionic solid, water has to do TWO things:

     1.  PULL THE CRYSTAL APART.
         The energy needed for this is the LATTICE ENERGY.
         Lattice energy is BIG when the ions are SMALL and highly charged.
         Big lattice energy  =>  HARD to dissolve.

     2.  WRAP WATER MOLECULES AROUND THE FREED IONS.
         The energy given out here is the HYDRATION ENERGY.
         Hydration energy is BIG when the ions are SMALL.
         Big hydration energy  =>  EASY to dissolve.

  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                               |
  |    IF   hydration energy  >  lattice energy   ->  SOLUBLE     |
  |    IF   hydration energy  <  lattice energy   ->  INSOLUBLE   |
  |                                                               |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## Rule of thumb that decides which one wins

```
  When the ANION is SMALL (like OH-):
      the lattice energy is very sensitive to the size of the CATION.
      Going down the group the cation grows fast, and lattice energy
      COLLAPSES faster than the hydration energy falls.
          =>  SOLUBILITY INCREASES DOWN THE GROUP.

  When the ANION is LARGE (like SO4 2- or CO3 2-):
      the anion already dominates the lattice, so making the cation
      bigger changes the lattice energy only a LITTLE. Meanwhile the
      hydration energy falls sharply as the cation grows.
          =>  SOLUBILITY DECREASES DOWN THE GROUP.
```

## 14.1 Hydroxides — solubility INCREASES down the group

```
  Be(OH)2  <  Mg(OH)2  <  Ca(OH)2  <  Sr(OH)2  <  Ba(OH)2
  (least soluble)                                  (most soluble)

  REASON: OH- is a SMALL anion. As the cation grows from Be2+ to Ba2+
  the LATTICE ENERGY falls MUCH faster than the hydration energy falls.
  So dissolving becomes easier and easier.
```

```
  Be(OH)2  -  insoluble, AMPHOTERIC
  Mg(OH)2  -  sparingly soluble, weakly basic (milk of magnesia)
  Ca(OH)2  -  slightly soluble, called LIME WATER
  Ba(OH)2  -  fairly soluble, strong base
```

## 14.2 Sulphates — solubility DECREASES down the group

```
  BeSO4  >  MgSO4  >  CaSO4  >  SrSO4  >  BaSO4
  (very soluble)                          (insoluble)

  REASON: SO4 2- is a LARGE anion, so the lattice energy barely changes
  as the cation grows. But the HYDRATION ENERGY falls sharply from Be2+
  to Ba2+. So the driving force for dissolving disappears.
```

```
  USE:  BaSO4 is completely insoluble and opaque to X-rays, so a
        suspension of it (a "BARIUM MEAL") is swallowed before an
        X-ray of the stomach and intestines.
```

## 14.3 Carbonates

```
  All Group 2 carbonates are INSOLUBLE in water, and solubility
  DECREASES down the group (same large-anion reasoning as sulphates).

  They dissolve in water containing CO2, forming soluble
  hydrogencarbonates - this is what causes TEMPORARY HARDNESS of water:

      CaCO3  +  H2O  +  CO2   -->   Ca(HCO3)2  (soluble)
```

## 14.4 Thermal stability of carbonates — INCREASES down the group

```
  MCO3   --(heat)-->   MO  +  CO2

  DECOMPOSITION TEMPERATURE:
      BeCO3  <  MgCO3  <  CaCO3  <  SrCO3  <  BaCO3
      (easiest to decompose)         (hardest to decompose)

  BeCO3 is so unstable that it must be stored in an atmosphere of CO2.
```

```
  REASON  (this is a POLARISATION argument):

      A SMALL, highly charged cation like Be2+ has high POLARISING POWER.
      It pulls the electron cloud of the big CO3 2- ion towards itself,
      distorting it and WEAKENING one of the C-O bonds.
      A weakened C-O bond snaps easily, so CO2 leaves at a low temperature.

      A BIG cation like Ba2+ has LOW polarising power, so the carbonate
      ion stays undistorted and needs a much higher temperature to break.

      => the bigger the cation, the MORE THERMALLY STABLE the carbonate.
```

```
  THE SAME LOGIC WORKS FOR:
      thermal stability of NITRATES     - increases down the group
      thermal stability of SULPHATES    - increases down the group
      thermal stability of HYDROXIDES   - increases down the group
```

> **TRAP:** Solubility trends of hydroxides and sulphates go in **opposite**
> directions. Memory line: **"Hydroxides go UP, sulphates go DOWN."**

# TOPIC 15 — Anomalous behaviour of BERYLLIUM

## Why beryllium is different (write these reasons first)

```
  1.  Exceptionally SMALL atomic and ionic size.
  2.  Very HIGH charge / size ratio  ->  highest polarising power in
      the group  ->  forms mainly COVALENT compounds.
  3.  HIGHEST ionisation enthalpy and highest electronegativity of the group.
  4.  ABSENCE of d orbitals in the valence shell, so its MAXIMUM
      COVALENCY IS 4 (the other members can go up to 6).
```

## Points of difference

| PROPERTY | BERYLLIUM | REST OF GROUP 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Nature of compounds | Largely COVALENT | Largely IONIC |
| Flame colour | None | Ca, Sr, Ba give characteristic colours |
| Reaction with water | Does NOT react, even with steam | Mg with steam; Ca, Sr, Ba with cold water |
| Oxide and hydroxide | BeO and Be(OH)2 are AMPHOTERIC | Basic |
| Reaction with alkali | Dissolves in NaOH giving beryllate + H2 | Do not react with alkalis |
| Maximum covalency | 4, because no d orbitals | Can be 6 |
| Halide | BeCl2 is covalent, a strong LEWIS ACID, polymeric chain in the solid, Cl-bridged dimer in the vapour, soluble in organic solvents | MgCl2, CaCl2 etc. are ionic and not soluble in organic solvents |
| Carbide | Be2C reacts with water to give METHANE | CaC2 reacts with water to give ACETYLENE (ethyne) |
| Complexes | Forms many complexes, e.g. [BeF4]2- | Weak tendency to form complexes |
| Hydride | BeH2 cannot be made by direct combination | CaH2 etc. formed directly |

```
  THE TWO CARBIDE REACTIONS (a favourite comparison):

      Be2C   +  4 H2O   -->   2 Be(OH)2  +  CH4        (methane)
      CaC2   +  2 H2O   -->   Ca(OH)2    +  C2H2       (acetylene)
```

# TOPIC 20 — PLASTER OF PARIS, CaSO4.1/2H2O

**A 4-mark question almost every year. Learn all four parts: name, preparation,
setting reaction, uses.**

```
  CHEMICAL NAME:  calcium sulphate hemihydrate
  FORMULA:        CaSO4 . 1/2 H2O      (also written 2 CaSO4 . H2O)
  WHY THE NAME:   large deposits of gypsum were found near PARIS.
```

## Preparation

```
  GYPSUM  is heated to 393 K (120 degrees Celsius):

      CaSO4.2H2O   --(heat, 393 K)-->   CaSO4.(1/2)H2O   +   (3/2) H2O
      gypsum                            plaster of Paris
```

## Setting reaction

```
  When plaster of Paris is mixed with water, it forms a plastic mass
  that SETS into a hard solid in 5 to 15 minutes:

      CaSO4.(1/2)H2O   +   (3/2) H2O   -->   CaSO4.2H2O
      plaster of Paris                       gypsum (set, hard)

  The setting is slightly EXOTHERMIC.
  The mass EXPANDS slightly on setting, which is why it takes the exact
  shape of the mould - and why it is used for casts and statues.
```

## Dead burnt plaster

```
  If gypsum is heated ABOVE 393 K, ALL the water is driven off and
  ANHYDROUS calcium sulphate, CaSO4, is formed.

      CaSO4.2H2O   --(heat above 393 K)-->   CaSO4   +   2 H2O
                                             DEAD BURNT PLASTER

  Dead burnt plaster does NOT set with water. It has lost the ability
  to take up water again.
```

> **TRAP:** The setting of plaster of Paris is **not drying**. It is a chemical
> reaction in which water is taken up to reform gypsum. Never write "the water
> evaporates".

```
  USES OF PLASTER OF PARIS
      - in surgery, for plaster casts to set broken bones
      - for making statues, models, toys and decorative material
      - for taking casts of statues and busts
      - in dentistry
      - for making blackboard chalk and for smoothing wall surfaces
```

```
  THE THREE COMPOUNDS - DO NOT MIX THEM UP:

      CaSO4.2H2O        gypsum                  (the raw material)
      CaSO4.(1/2)H2O    plaster of Paris        (sets with water)
      CaSO4             dead burnt plaster      (does NOT set)
```

# TOPIC 21 — CEMENT

```
  Cement (Portland cement) was invented by JOSEPH ASPDIN in 1824.
  It was named "Portland" because on setting it looks like Portland
  rock, a stone quarried in Britain.

  RAW MATERIALS:
      1.  LIMESTONE   (supplies CaO)
      2.  CLAY        (supplies SiO2, Al2O3 and Fe2O3)
      3.  GYPSUM      (added at the end)
```

## Average composition

| CONSTITUENT | PERCENTAGE |
|---|---|
| CaO (lime) | 50 to 60 |
| SiO2 (silica) | 20 to 25 |
| Al2O3 (alumina) | 5 to 10 |
| MgO (magnesia) | 2 to 3 |
| Fe2O3 | 1 to 2 |
| SO3 | 1 to 2 |

```
  TWO QUALITY RATIOS TO REMEMBER:

      silica  :  alumina         should be between 2.5 and 4
      lime    :  total of the    should be about  2 : 1
                 oxides of Si,
                 Al and Fe

  If lime is IN EXCESS  ->  the cement CRACKS while setting.
  If lime is TOO LITTLE ->  the cement is WEAK.
```

## Manufacture — flow chart

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                        M A N U F A C T U R E                            |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------------+

     LIMESTONE  +  CLAY
            |
            v
     +---------------+
     |   GRINDING    |     ground to a fine powder and mixed
     +-------+-------+     (dry process or wet process, making a "slurry")
             |
             v
     +----------------------------------+
     |      ROTARY KILN , 1770 K        |
     |  the mixture fuses into hard      |
     |  greyish balls called CLINKER     |
     +----------------+-----------------+
                      |
                      v
     +----------------------------------+
     |   CLINKER  +  2 to 3 % GYPSUM    |
     |         ground together           |
     +----------------+-----------------+
                      |
                      v
              P O R T L A N D   C E M E N T
```

```
  WHY IS GYPSUM ADDED?
      To SLOW DOWN the setting of the cement, so that it can be
      transported and poured before it hardens.

  SETTING OF CEMENT:
      Cement + water  ->  the constituents HYDRATE  ->  the gel formed
      then HARDENS into an interlocking mass. It is a chemical process
      of HYDRATION followed by HARDENING, not simple drying.

  CONCRETE  =  cement  +  sand  +  gravel  +  water
  REINFORCED CONCRETE  =  concrete poured around steel rods

# TOPIC 22 — Biological importance of magnesium and calcium

```
  ROUGH AMOUNTS IN A 70 kg ADULT:
      magnesium   about  22 g
      calcium     about  1200 g
```

## Magnesium

```
  1.  Mg2+ is the central metal ion of CHLOROPHYLL, the green pigment
      that lets plants carry out photosynthesis. Without Mg there is
      no photosynthesis, so all life depends on it.

  2.  Mg2+ activates many ENZYMES, especially those that transfer
      phosphate groups - so it is essential in ATP chemistry.

  3.  It is also important in DNA and RNA chemistry, and in
      neuromuscular function.
```

## Calcium

```
  1.  About 99 percent of body calcium is in BONES and TEETH.
      Bone contains calcium phosphate, Ca3(PO4)2, in the form of
      APATITE. Tooth ENAMEL contains FLUORAPATITE, Ca5(PO4)3F.

  2.  Blood plasma contains about 100 mg of Ca per litre.

  3.  Ca2+ is essential for BLOOD CLOTTING.

  4.  Ca2+ is essential for MUSCLE CONTRACTION and for the working
      of the heart muscle.

  5.  Bone is not permanent. It is constantly dissolved and re-deposited.
      This balance is controlled by two hormones:
          CALCITONIN            and     PARATHYROID HORMONE
```

> **TRAP:** Chlorophyll contains **magnesium**, haemoglobin contains **iron**.
> Students swap these two every single year.
