# PATTERN 1 — Flame colours

### Q1
The metal that gives a golden yellow colour to the flame is

(a) Li &nbsp; (b) Na &nbsp; (c) K &nbsp; (d) Ba

**ANSWER: (b)** — Na gives GOLDEN YELLOW. Li is crimson red, K is lilac,
Ba is apple green.

---

### Q2
Which pair of metals gives NO colour in the flame test?

(a) Ca and Sr &nbsp; (b) Be and Mg &nbsp; (c) Li and Na &nbsp; (d) Ba and Ra

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Be and Mg atoms are very SMALL, so their IONISATION ENTHALPIES ARE VERY
  HIGH. The bunsen flame cannot excite their electrons, so no light is
  emitted and no colour appears.
```

---

### Q3
Apple green colour in the flame test indicates the presence of

(a) Ca &nbsp; (b) Sr &nbsp; (c) Ba &nbsp; (d) Cs

**ANSWER: (c)** — Ba is apple green, Ca is brick red, Sr is crimson,
Cs is blue.

> **SHORTCUT — the flame colour table, in one line each:**
> ```
>   GROUP 1                       GROUP 2
>   Li  CRIMSON RED               Be  NONE
>   Na  GOLDEN YELLOW             Mg  NONE
>   K   LILAC (violet)            Ca  BRICK RED
>   Rb  RED VIOLET                Sr  CRIMSON
>   Cs  BLUE                      Ba  APPLE GREEN
>
>   MEMORY:  "Little Crimson Nancy Goes Killing Lilacs"
>            Li-crimson , Na-golden , K-lilac
>            "CAlcium BRICK , STRontium CRIMSON , BArium APPLE"
> ```

---

### Q4
The elements used in fireworks to give red and green colours are

(a) Na and K &nbsp; (b) Sr and Ba &nbsp; (c) Be and Mg &nbsp; (d) Li and Cs

**ANSWER: (b)** — Sr gives crimson red, Ba gives apple green.

---

### Q5
Caesium is used in photoelectric cells because

(a) it is radioactive &nbsp; (b) it has the lowest ionisation enthalpy
(c) it is the densest alkali metal &nbsp; (d) it is a liquid at room temperature

**ANSWER: (b)** — its ionisation enthalpy is so low that ordinary visible
light ejects electrons from its surface.

# PATTERN 2 — Reducing power and electrode potential

### Q6
The strongest reducing agent among the alkali metals in AQUEOUS SOLUTION is

(a) Li &nbsp; (b) Na &nbsp; (c) K &nbsp; (d) Cs

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  Li+ is the SMALLEST ion  ->  the LARGEST HYDRATION ENTHALPY  ->  this
  more than compensates its high sublimation and ionisation enthalpy
  ->  the most negative electrode potential, E = -3.04 V.
```

---

### Q7
In the GAS PHASE, the strongest reducing agent among alkali metals is

(a) Li &nbsp; (b) Na &nbsp; (c) K &nbsp; (d) Cs

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
  In the gas phase there is NO WATER, so hydration enthalpy plays no part.
  Only ionisation enthalpy matters, and Cs has the lowest.
```

> **SHORTCUT:**
> ```
>   IN WATER      ->  LITHIUM is the strongest reducing agent
>   IN GAS PHASE  ->  CAESIUM is the strongest reducing agent
>   Examiners flip between these two. Read the words carefully.
> ```

---

### Q8
Lithium has the most negative electrode potential yet reacts with water least
vigorously. The reason is

(a) it is the least electropositive &nbsp; (b) its large hydration enthalpy
(c) its high melting point and large atomisation enthalpy &nbsp; (d) it is covalent

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Electrode potential = THERMODYNAMICS (how far).
  Speed of reaction   = KINETICS       (how fast).
  Li is hard, high melting, and does not melt and spread on the water, so
  the reaction is SLOW.
```

---

### Q9
The correct order of the violence of reaction with water is

(a) Li > Na > K > Cs &nbsp; (b) Cs > Rb > K > Na > Li
(c) Na > Li > K &nbsp; (d) all react equally

**ANSWER: (b)** — reactivity with water INCREASES down the group as
ionisation enthalpy falls.

# PATTERN 3 — Reaction with oxygen: oxide, peroxide, superoxide

### Q10
The product formed when sodium is burnt in excess of air is

(a) Na2O &nbsp; (b) Na2O2 &nbsp; (c) NaO2 &nbsp; (d) NaOH

**ANSWER: (b)** — `2 Na + O2 --> Na2O2`, sodium PEROXIDE.

---

### Q11
Which of the following forms a superoxide on burning in air?

(a) Li &nbsp; (b) Na &nbsp; (c) K &nbsp; (d) Be

**ANSWER: (c)** — `K + O2 --> KO2`. Rb and Cs do the same.

---

### Q12
The reason why K, Rb and Cs form superoxides while Li forms only the oxide is

(a) K is more electronegative &nbsp; (b) a large cation stabilises a large anion
(c) Li has d orbitals &nbsp; (d) superoxides are covalent

**ANSWER: (b)**

> **SHORTCUT — the oxygen rule in three lines:**
> ```
>    O2-      oxide       SMALL anion   ->  needs SMALL cation  ->  Li2O
>    O2 2-    peroxide    MEDIUM        ->  Na2O2
>    O2 -     superoxide  LARGE anion   ->  needs LARGE cation  ->  KO2, RbO2, CsO2
>
>    "SMALL WITH SMALL , BIG WITH BIG."
>    NO Group 2 metal forms a superoxide at all.
> ```

---

### Q13
KO2 is paramagnetic because

(a) K+ has unpaired electrons &nbsp; (b) O2- (superoxide) has one unpaired electron
(c) it is ionic &nbsp; (d) it is coloured

**ANSWER: (b)** — the superoxide ion carries ONE unpaired electron.
Peroxide, O2 2-, has none and is diamagnetic.

---

### Q14
KO2 is used in space capsules and submarines because it

(a) absorbs moisture &nbsp; (b) absorbs CO2 and releases O2
(c) is a reducing agent &nbsp; (d) is a fuel

**ANSWER: (b)** — `4 KO2 + 2 CO2 --> 2 K2CO3 + 3 O2`

---

### Q15
The only alkali metal that reacts directly with nitrogen of the air is

(a) Na &nbsp; (b) K &nbsp; (c) Li &nbsp; (d) Cs

**ANSWER: (c)** — `6 Li + N2 --> 2 Li3N`, ruby red lithium nitride.
In Group 2, ALL the metals form nitrides M3N2.

# PATTERN 4 — The liquid ammonia solution

### Q16
The blue colour of a dilute solution of sodium in liquid ammonia is due to

(a) Na+ ions &nbsp; (b) ammoniated electrons &nbsp; (c) NaNH2 &nbsp; (d) dissolved H2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  M  +  (x+y) NH3  -->  [M(NH3)x]+  +  [e(NH3)y]-
  The AMMONIATED ELECTRON absorbs light from the RED region, so the
  transmitted light looks BLUE.
```

---

### Q17
A dilute solution of an alkali metal in liquid ammonia is

(a) paramagnetic and blue &nbsp; (b) diamagnetic and blue
(c) paramagnetic and bronze &nbsp; (d) diamagnetic and colourless

**ANSWER: (a)** — the free ammoniated electrons are UNPAIRED.

---

### Q18
On increasing the concentration, the solution of an alkali metal in liquid
ammonia becomes

(a) deeper blue and more paramagnetic &nbsp; (b) bronze coloured and diamagnetic
(c) colourless &nbsp; (d) green and paramagnetic

**ANSWER: (b)** — the electrons pair up into clusters, so paramagnetism is lost.

> **SHORTCUT:**
> ```
>   DILUTE        ->  BLUE   ->  PARAMAGNETIC
>   CONCENTRATED  ->  BRONZE ->  DIAMAGNETIC
>   ON STANDING   ->  colour fades, amide forms:  M + NH3 --> MNH2 + 1/2 H2
> ```

# PATTERN 5 — Anomalous behaviour of Li and Be

### Q19
Which of the following is NOT true for lithium?

(a) It is the hardest alkali metal &nbsp; (b) Li2CO3 decomposes on heating
(c) LiHCO3 exists as a solid &nbsp; (d) It forms Li3N with nitrogen

**ANSWER: (c)** — LiHCO3 exists ONLY IN SOLUTION. NaHCO3 and KHCO3 are solids.

---

### Q20
The most covalent among the following halides is

(a) LiF &nbsp; (b) LiCl &nbsp; (c) LiI &nbsp; (d) NaI

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  FAJANS' RULES:  small cation + large anion  ->  most covalent.
  Li+ is the smallest cation and I- is the largest halide ion.
```

---

### Q21
On heating, LiNO3 gives

(a) LiNO2 + O2 &nbsp; (b) Li2O + NO2 + O2 &nbsp; (c) Li + NO2 &nbsp; (d) no change

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  4 LiNO3  --(heat)-->  2 Li2O  +  4 NO2  +  O2      (Li behaves like Mg)
  2 NaNO3  --(heat)-->  2 NaNO2 +  O2                (the rest of Group 1)
```

---

### Q22
Beryllium differs from the rest of Group 2 chiefly because

(a) it has d orbitals &nbsp; (b) it has a very small size and no d orbitals
(c) it is radioactive &nbsp; (d) it has a low ionisation enthalpy

**ANSWER: (b)** — small size, very high polarising power and NO d orbitals,
so its maximum covalency is 4.

---

### Q23
Which oxide is amphoteric?

(a) MgO &nbsp; (b) CaO &nbsp; (c) BeO &nbsp; (d) BaO

**ANSWER: (c)** — BeO and Be(OH)2 are AMPHOTERIC; the rest are basic.

---

### Q24
The Group 2 metal that dissolves in NaOH liberating hydrogen is

(a) Mg &nbsp; (b) Ca &nbsp; (c) Ba &nbsp; (d) Be

**ANSWER: (d)** — `Be + 2 NaOH + 2 H2O --> Na2[Be(OH)4] + H2`.
Only beryllium does this; it proves Be is amphoteric.

---

### Q25
Be2C on hydrolysis gives

(a) acetylene &nbsp; (b) methane &nbsp; (c) ethylene &nbsp; (d) hydrogen

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Be2C  +  4 H2O  -->  2 Be(OH)2  +  CH4          METHANE
  CaC2  +  2 H2O  -->  Ca(OH)2    +  C2H2         ACETYLENE

  Al4C3 also gives methane - one more Be ~ Al similarity.
```

---

### Q26
Which pair shows a diagonal relationship?

(a) Li and Na &nbsp; (b) Be and Mg &nbsp; (c) Li and Mg &nbsp; (d) Na and Ca

**ANSWER: (c)** — the two diagonal pairs asked in this chapter are
**Li ~ Mg** and **Be ~ Al**.

---

### Q27
Both Be and Al are rendered passive by

(a) dilute HCl &nbsp; (b) concentrated HNO3 &nbsp; (c) NaOH &nbsp; (d) water

**ANSWER: (b)** — a thin protective oxide film forms on the surface.

# PATTERN 6 — Solubility: lattice energy versus hydration energy

### Q28
The correct order of solubility of Group 2 hydroxides in water is

(a) Be(OH)2 > Mg(OH)2 > Ca(OH)2 > Ba(OH)2
(b) Ba(OH)2 > Sr(OH)2 > Ca(OH)2 > Mg(OH)2 > Be(OH)2
(c) all are equally soluble &nbsp; (d) all are insoluble

**ANSWER: (b)** — solubility of hydroxides INCREASES down the group,
because OH- is a small anion and the lattice energy collapses faster than
the hydration energy falls.

---

### Q29
The least soluble sulphate among the following is

(a) BeSO4 &nbsp; (b) MgSO4 &nbsp; (c) CaSO4 &nbsp; (d) BaSO4

**ANSWER: (d)** — solubility of sulphates DECREASES down the group, because
SO4 2- is a large anion, so lattice energy hardly changes while hydration
energy falls sharply.

> **SHORTCUT — the one line that answers every solubility MCQ:**
> ```
>   "HYDROXIDES GO UP , SULPHATES GO DOWN."
>
>   SMALL anion (OH-, F-)      ->  solubility INCREASES down the group
>   LARGE anion (SO4 2-, CO3 2-) ->  solubility DECREASES down the group
>
>   Decide by:  hydration energy > lattice energy  ->  SOLUBLE
> ```

---

### Q30
A "barium meal" used before an X-ray of the stomach is

(a) BaCO3 &nbsp; (b) BaSO4 &nbsp; (c) BaCl2 &nbsp; (d) Ba(OH)2

**ANSWER: (b)** — BaSO4 is completely insoluble, so it is not absorbed and is
non-toxic, and it is opaque to X-rays.

---

### Q31
LiF is sparingly soluble in water because

(a) it is covalent &nbsp; (b) its lattice energy is very high
(c) its hydration energy is very high &nbsp; (d) F- is polarisable

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Both Li+ and F- are very SMALL, so they pack into an extremely strong
  lattice. That huge lattice energy beats the hydration energy.
  Do NOT use the covalent argument here - that one is for LiI.
```

# PATTERN 7 — Thermal stability

### Q32
The correct order of thermal stability of Group 2 carbonates is

(a) BeCO3 > MgCO3 > CaCO3 > BaCO3
(b) BaCO3 > SrCO3 > CaCO3 > MgCO3 > BeCO3
(c) all are equally stable &nbsp; (d) none decomposes

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  MCO3 --(heat)--> MO + CO2
  A SMALL cation (Be2+) has HIGH polarising power, distorts the big CO3 2-
  ion, weakens a C-O bond, so CO2 leaves easily -> LEAST stable.
  A BIG cation (Ba2+) hardly distorts it -> MOST stable.
```

---

### Q33
Which carbonate must be stored under an atmosphere of CO2?

(a) BaCO3 &nbsp; (b) CaCO3 &nbsp; (c) BeCO3 &nbsp; (d) SrCO3

**ANSWER: (c)** — BeCO3 is so thermally unstable that CO2 pressure is needed
to stop it decomposing.

---

### Q34
Which of the following carbonates decomposes on heating, unlike the rest of
its group?

(a) Na2CO3 &nbsp; (b) K2CO3 &nbsp; (c) Li2CO3 &nbsp; (d) Rb2CO3

**ANSWER: (c)** — `Li2CO3 --(heat)--> Li2O + CO2`. This is part of the
Li ~ Mg diagonal relationship, since MgCO3 also decomposes.

> **SHORTCUT:**
> ```
>   THERMAL STABILITY always INCREASES DOWN a group
>       (carbonates, nitrates, sulphates, hydroxides, all of them)
>   because polarising power of the cation DECREASES down the group.
>
>   HYDRIDE stability is the OPPOSITE way round:
>       LiH > NaH > KH > RbH > CsH  (the M-H bond weakens as M+ grows)
> ```

# PATTERN 8 — Industrial processes

### Q35
In the Solvay process, the compound that precipitates in the carbonating tower is

(a) Na2CO3 &nbsp; (b) NaHCO3 &nbsp; (c) NH4Cl &nbsp; (d) CaCl2

**ANSWER: (b)** — NaHCO3 is only slightly soluble, so it comes out of solution
and can be filtered off.

---

### Q36
The Solvay process cannot be used to prepare K2CO3 because

(a) KCl is insoluble &nbsp; (b) KHCO3 is too soluble to precipitate
(c) K reacts with ammonia &nbsp; (d) K2CO3 decomposes

**ANSWER: (b)**

---

### Q37
The by-product of the Solvay process is

(a) NH3 &nbsp; (b) CO2 &nbsp; (c) CaCl2 &nbsp; (d) NaOH

**ANSWER: (c)** — NH3 and CO2 are both RECYCLED; CaCl2 is the only waste.

---

### Q38
In the Castner-Kellner cell, the cathode is

(a) carbon &nbsp; (b) titanium &nbsp; (c) mercury &nbsp; (d) iron

**ANSWER: (c)** — a flowing MERCURY cathode; the anode is carbon or titanium.

---

### Q39
Mercury is used as the cathode in the Castner-Kellner cell because

(a) it is cheap &nbsp; (b) it forms an amalgam and keeps Na away from water and Cl2
(c) it conducts better than carbon &nbsp; (d) it prevents H2 formation

**ANSWER: (b)** — otherwise the NaOH formed would mix with Cl2 to give NaOCl.

> **SHORTCUT — the two industrial processes on one card:**
> ```
>   SOLVAY  (Na2CO3)                  CASTNER-KELLNER  (NaOH)
>   raw: brine, NH3, CO2, CaCO3       raw: brine
>   NaHCO3 precipitates               cathode = MERCURY
>   2 NaHCO3 -(373K)-> Na2CO3+CO2+H2O anode   = CARBON / titanium
>   recycled: NH3 and CO2             denuder gives NaOH + H2
>   waste: CaCl2                      2NaCl+2H2O -> 2NaOH + Cl2 + H2
>   fails for K2CO3 (KHCO3 soluble)   Cl2 is the valuable by-product
> ```

# PATTERN 9 — Calcium compounds

### Q40
The formula of plaster of Paris is

(a) CaSO4.2H2O &nbsp; (b) CaSO4.1/2H2O &nbsp; (c) CaSO4 &nbsp; (d) CaCO3

**ANSWER: (b)** — calcium sulphate HEMIhydrate, made by heating gypsum
(CaSO4.2H2O) to 393 K.

---

### Q41
Plaster of Paris sets into a hard mass because it

(a) loses water &nbsp; (b) takes up water and turns back into gypsum
(c) melts &nbsp; (d) reacts with CO2

**ANSWER: (b)** — `CaSO4.1/2H2O + 3/2 H2O --> CaSO4.2H2O`, an interlocked
crystalline mass. It expands slightly on setting, so it copies the mould.

---

### Q42
Gypsum heated ABOVE 393 K gives

(a) plaster of Paris &nbsp; (b) dead burnt plaster &nbsp; (c) quicklime &nbsp; (d) slaked lime

**ANSWER: (b)** — anhydrous CaSO4, dead burnt plaster, which does NOT set
with water.

> **SHORTCUT — the three calcium sulphates:**
> ```
>   CaSO4.2H2O      GYPSUM               raw material
>   CaSO4.1/2H2O    PLASTER OF PARIS     393 K , SETS with water
>   CaSO4           DEAD BURNT PLASTER   above 393 K , does NOT set
> ```

---

### Q43
Quicklime and slaked lime are respectively

(a) CaCO3 and CaO &nbsp; (b) CaO and Ca(OH)2 &nbsp; (c) Ca(OH)2 and CaO &nbsp; (d) CaO and CaCO3

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  CaCO3 --(1070-1270 K)--> CaO (quicklime) --(+H2O)--> Ca(OH)2 (slaked lime)
  The slaking reaction is vigorous and EXOTHERMIC.
```

---

### Q44
Lime water turns milky with CO2, and the milkiness disappears with excess CO2
because

(a) CaCO3 evaporates &nbsp; (b) soluble Ca(HCO3)2 is formed
(c) Ca(OH)2 is regenerated &nbsp; (d) CaO is formed

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Ca(OH)2 + CO2       -->  CaCO3 (milky) + H2O
  CaCO3 + H2O + CO2   -->  Ca(HCO3)2 (soluble, clear)
```

---

### Q45
Gypsum is added to cement clinker in order to

(a) increase strength &nbsp; (b) slow down the setting
(c) speed up the setting &nbsp; (d) give it colour

**ANSWER: (b)** — 2 to 3 percent gypsum SLOWS DOWN the setting of cement.

# PATTERN 10 — Biological importance and uses

### Q46
The metal ion present at the centre of chlorophyll is

(a) Fe2+ &nbsp; (b) Ca2+ &nbsp; (c) Mg2+ &nbsp; (d) Na+

**ANSWER: (c)** — CHLOROPHYLL = MAGNESIUM. HAEMOGLOBIN = IRON.
This swap is the single most common trap in this pattern.

---

### Q47
In the sodium-potassium pump, for each ATP molecule hydrolysed

(a) 2 Na+ out and 3 K+ in &nbsp; (b) 3 Na+ out and 2 K+ in
(c) 1 Na+ out and 1 K+ in &nbsp; (d) 3 K+ out and 2 Na+ in

**ANSWER: (b)** — **3 OUT, 2 IN**, sodium out, potassium in.
It consumes more than one third of the ATP of a resting human.

---

### Q48
Which ion is essential for blood clotting and muscle contraction?

(a) Na+ &nbsp; (b) K+ &nbsp; (c) Mg2+ &nbsp; (d) Ca2+

**ANSWER: (d)** — about 99 percent of body calcium is in bones and teeth;
the rest does the clotting and contraction work.

---

### Q49
Na+ is found mainly

(a) inside the cell &nbsp; (b) outside the cell, in blood plasma and interstitial fluid
(c) in bones &nbsp; (d) in chlorophyll

**ANSWER: (b)** — **Na OUTSIDE, K INSIDE.**

---

### Q50
Milk of magnesia, used as an antacid, is

(a) MgO &nbsp; (b) Mg(OH)2 &nbsp; (c) MgCO3 &nbsp; (d) MgSO4

**ANSWER: (b)** — a suspension of sparingly soluble Mg(OH)2, weakly basic and
therefore safe to swallow.

### Q51
MgO is used as a refractory lining for furnaces because

(a) it is amphoteric &nbsp; (b) it has an extremely high melting point

**ANSWER: (b)**

---

### Q52
Which of the following is the lightest metal known?

(a) Na &nbsp; (b) Li &nbsp; (c) Mg &nbsp; (d) Be

**ANSWER: (b)** — Li, density 0.53 g/cm3. Li, Na and K all float on water.

---

### Q53
Among Na, K, Rb the density order is

(a) Na < K < Rb &nbsp; (b) K < Na < Rb &nbsp; (c) Rb < K < Na &nbsp; (d) K < Rb < Na

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Density generally INCREASES down Group 1, but POTASSIUM IS THE EXCEPTION:
  K is LESS dense than Na, because its atomic volume grows faster than its
  mass. Examiners ask this exception again and again.
```

### Q54
CaH2, used as a portable source of hydrogen, is known as

(a) hydrolith &nbsp; (b) carbide &nbsp; (c) quicklime &nbsp; (d) baking soda

### Q55
The common name of Na2CO3.10H2O is
