# The s-Block Elements — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

This chapter has **no numericals at all**. Every pattern is a
*trend + reason* or a *reaction + condition*. Once you can name the pattern,
you already know the first line of the answer.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — GROUP NAME, POSITION AND CONFIGURATION              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why are they called alkali / alkaline earth metals?",
            "Write the general electronic configuration of the s-block."
  METHOD    1. Group 1 = ns1 = Li Na K Rb Cs Fr .
             2. Group 2 = ns2 = Be Mg Ca Sr Ba Ra .
             3. ALKALI      -> their oxides and hydroxides give strongly
                               ALKALINE solutions in water.
             4. ALKALINE EARTH -> oxides are alkaline AND were found in the
                               "earth" (rocks), and do not melt or dissolve
                               easily.
             5. Add: Fr and Ra are RADIOACTIVE.
  TRAP      Hydrogen is also 1s1 but is NOT an alkali metal. Never put H in
            a Group 1 list.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — WHY THEY ARE NEVER FOUND FREE / STORAGE             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why do alkali metals not occur in the free state?",
            "Why are Na and K stored under kerosene?"
  METHOD    1. State the cause: VERY LOW IONISATION ENTHALPY.
            2. Therefore they lose the ns1 electron extremely easily.
            3. Therefore they react at once with air and moisture.
            4. Conclusion: they exist only as COMPOUNDS / must be kept
               under kerosene, which keeps air and water away.
  TRAP      The answer is ONE cause (low ionisation enthalpy) followed by
            its consequence. Do not just write "they are reactive" - that
            is the observation, not the reason.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — TREND + REASON DOWN A GROUP                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Discuss the variation of ... down the group",
            "How does atomic radius / ionisation enthalpy / hydration
            enthalpy vary?"
  METHOD    Answer in THREE columns every time:
                 PROPERTY  |  DIRECTION  |  REASON
            Standard reasons to reuse:
              size UP        -> a new shell is added each period
              IE  DOWN       -> outer electron farther out and better shielded
              hydration DOWN -> bigger ion, lower charge density
              m.p./hardness DOWN -> weak metallic bond gets weaker
              reactivity UP  -> IE falls
  TRAP      NEVER write only the direction. Half the marks are for the
            REASON. One sentence of reason per row is enough.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — FLAME COLOURS                                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the flame colours of ...", "Why do alkali metals impart
            colour to the flame?"
  METHOD    1. Give the table:
                 Li crimson red , Na golden yellow , K lilac ,
                 Rb red violet , Cs blue
                 Ca brick red , Sr crimson , Ba apple green
            2. Give the reason: LOW ionisation enthalpy -> the flame
               EXCITES the outer electron -> it falls back -> emits
               VISIBLE LIGHT of a definite colour.
            3. Add the use if there is room: Cs and K in PHOTOELECTRIC
               CELLS, Sr and Ba in FIREWORKS.
  TRAP      Golden yellow is Na, not K. K is LILAC.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WHY Be AND Mg GIVE NO FLAME COLOUR                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why do beryllium and magnesium not impart colour to the flame?"
  METHOD    1. Be and Mg atoms are very SMALL.
            2. Their electrons are held very tightly, so their IONISATION
               ENTHALPIES ARE VERY HIGH.
            3. The energy of a bunsen flame is NOT ENOUGH to excite them.
            4. No excitation -> no emission -> no colour.
  TRAP      Do not say "they have no electrons to excite" or "they are
            covalent". The whole answer is the HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPY.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — THE OXYGEN RULE: OXIDE / PEROXIDE / SUPEROXIDE  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when Li / Na / K is burnt in air?",
            "Why do K, Rb and Cs form superoxides?"
  METHOD    1. Write the three equations:
                  4 Li + O2 --(burn)--> 2 Li2O      oxide
                  2 Na + O2 --(burn)--> Na2O2       peroxide
                    K  + O2 --(burn)--> KO2         superoxide
            2. Give the size list:
                  O2-  small , O2 2- medium , O2 - large
            3. Give the rule: A BIG CATION STABILISES A BIG ANION, because
               similar sizes give a well-packed, high-lattice-energy crystal.
            4. Add: superoxides are PARAMAGNETIC and COLOURED (one unpaired
               electron in O2-), and 4 KO2 + 2 CO2 --> 2 K2CO3 + 3 O2.
  TRAP      NO Group 2 metal forms a superoxide. Li forms ONLY the oxide.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — LITHIUM AS THE STRONGEST REDUCING AGENT        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is Li the strongest reducing agent although its ionisation
            enthalpy is the highest in the group?"
  METHOD    Write the THREE STEPS, then the conclusion:
              STEP 1  M(s)  -> M(g)          costs SUBLIMATION enthalpy
              STEP 2  M(g)  -> M+(g) + e-    costs IONISATION enthalpy
              STEP 3  M+(g) -> M+(aq)        RELEASES HYDRATION enthalpy
            Steps 1 and 2 are worst for Li, BUT step 3 is hugely favourable
            because Li+ is the SMALLEST ion and has the LARGEST HYDRATION
            ENTHALPY. It MORE THAN COMPENSATES the other two.
            => E = -3.04 V , the most negative.
  TRAP      You MUST write the words "IN AQUEOUS SOLUTION".
            In the GAS PHASE the answer is CAESIUM.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — Li REACTS LEAST VIOLENTLY WITH WATER                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Li has the most negative E value but reacts slowly with water.
            Explain."
  METHOD    1. Electrode potential is THERMODYNAMICS (how far), not
               KINETICS (how fast).
            2. Li is the HARDEST alkali metal with the HIGHEST melting point
               and a LARGE atomisation enthalpy.
            3. It does not melt and spread over the water, so it exposes
               little surface and the reaction is SLOW.
            4. Na melts into a ball and reacts briskly; K catches fire;
               Rb and Cs explode.
  TRAP      This looks like Pattern 7 but it is the OPPOSITE question.
            Pattern 7 = thermodynamics. Pattern 8 = speed.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — THE LIQUID AMMONIA SOLUTION                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the solution of an alkali metal in liquid ammonia blue?",
            "Why is it paramagnetic / a good conductor?"
  METHOD    1. Write the equation FIRST:
                 M + (x+y) NH3 --> [M(NH3)x]+  +  [e(NH3)y]-
                 ammoniated cation      AMMONIATED ELECTRON
            2. BLUE   -> the ammoniated electron absorbs light in the RED
                         region, so blue light is transmitted.
            3. PARAMAGNETIC -> the ammoniated electrons are UNPAIRED.
            4. CONDUCTING   -> both the cation and the free electron move.
            5. REDUCING     -> free electrons are available to donate.
            6. CONCENTRATED -> BRONZE and DIAMAGNETIC (electrons pair up).
            7. ON STANDING  -> colour fades:
                 M + NH3 --(Fe catalyst)--> MNH2 + (1/2) H2
  TRAP      Dilute = blue and paramagnetic.
            Concentrated = bronze and diamagnetic. Never swap them.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF LITHIUM                     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does lithium differ from the other alkali metals?",
            "Give six points of anomalous behaviour of Li."
  METHOD    1. Give the THREE CAUSES first (they carry marks):
                 very small size ; very high charge/size ratio, i.e. high
                 POLARISING POWER giving covalent character ; NO d orbitals.
            2. Then list SIX differences, each as
                 "Li does X , while the others do Y."
               Best six: hardest and highest m.p. ; reacts slowly with water ;
               gives only Li2O ; reacts with N2 to give Li3N ; Li2CO3 and
               LiOH decompose on heating ; LiHCO3 exists only in solution.
            3. Support with equations:
                 Li2CO3 --(heat)--> Li2O + CO2
                 4 LiNO3 --(heat)--> 2 Li2O + 4 NO2 + O2
  TRAP      Give the CAUSES, not only the list. And always contrast with
            "the others", otherwise the point is not a difference.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF BERYLLIUM                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does Be differ from the rest of Group 2?"
  METHOD    1. CAUSES: smallest size ; highest charge/size ratio -> highest
               polarising power -> COVALENT compounds ; highest IE and
               electronegativity ; NO d orbitals, so MAXIMUM COVALENCY 4.
            2. SIX differences:
                 covalent compounds ; no flame colour ; no reaction with
                 water even as steam ; BeO and Be(OH)2 AMPHOTERIC ;
                 dissolves in NaOH giving Na2[Be(OH)4] + H2 ;
                 BeCl2 covalent, Lewis acid, Cl-bridged ;
                 Be2C gives METHANE with water.
  TRAP      "Maximum covalency 4" must be linked to "no d orbitals".
            Writing one without the other loses the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP Li ~ Mg                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the diagonal relationship between Li and Mg",
            "Li resembles Mg. Give six points."
  METHOD    1. Explain WHY first: across a period size falls and
               electronegativity rises; down a group size rises and
               electronegativity falls; moving DIAGONALLY the two effects
               CANCEL, so the pair have almost the same size,
               electronegativity and POLARISING POWER.
            2. Then six similarities:
                 hard, high melting ; react slowly with water ;
                 only the normal oxide Li2O and MgO ;
                 both form nitrides Li3N and Mg3N2 ;
                 Li2CO3 and MgCO3 decompose on heating ;
                 LiHCO3 and Mg(HCO3)2 exist only in solution ;
                 LiCl and MgCl2 deliquescent, hydrated, soluble in ethanol ;
                 LiF and MgF2 sparingly soluble.
  TRAP      The pairs are Li~Mg and Be~Al. Not Li~Ca, not Be~Mg.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP Be ~ Al                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the diagonal relationship between beryllium and
            aluminium."
  METHOD    Same "why" as Pattern 12, then the similarities:
              1. Both PASSIVE with concentrated HNO3.
              2. Both dissolve in alkali giving H2:
                 [Be(OH)4]2- and [Al(OH)4]-.
              3. Oxides and hydroxides AMPHOTERIC.
              4. BeCl2 and AlCl3 covalent, LEWIS ACIDS, soluble in organic
                 solvents, Cl-BRIDGED, Friedel-Crafts catalysts.
              5. Stable complexes [BeF4]2- and [AlF6]3-.
              6. Be2C and Al4C3 both give METHANE with water.
  TRAP      Do not mix in Li~Mg points. Amphoteric and passive belong to
            Be~Al ; nitride formation belongs to Li~Mg.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — SOLUBILITY: LATTICE vs HYDRATION ENERGY            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does the solubility of Group 2 hydroxides increase but that
            of the sulphates decrease down the group?"
  METHOD    1. Define the tug of war:
                 LATTICE ENERGY   - needed to pull the crystal apart;
                                    BIG lattice energy -> hard to dissolve.
                 HYDRATION ENERGY - released when water wraps the ions;
                                    BIG hydration energy -> easy to dissolve.
                 hydration > lattice -> SOLUBLE ; hydration < lattice ->
                 INSOLUBLE.
            2. Decide by the SIZE OF THE ANION:
                 SMALL anion (OH-) : lattice energy collapses faster than
                 hydration energy falls -> SOLUBILITY INCREASES down.
                 LARGE anion (SO4 2-, CO3 2-) : lattice energy hardly changes
                 but hydration energy falls sharply -> SOLUBILITY DECREASES.
            3. Write the two orders:
                 Be(OH)2 < Mg(OH)2 < Ca(OH)2 < Sr(OH)2 < Ba(OH)2
                 BeSO4 > MgSO4 > CaSO4 > SrSO4 > BaSO4
  TRAP      "HYDROXIDES GO UP , SULPHATES GO DOWN." The two trends are
            OPPOSITE - candidates lose marks by writing one trend twice.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — THERMAL STABILITY OF CARBONATES               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange the Group 2 carbonates in order of thermal stability",
            "Why is BeCO3 the least stable?"
  METHOD    1. Equation:  MCO3 --(heat)--> MO + CO2
            2. Order:  BeCO3 < MgCO3 < CaCO3 < SrCO3 < BaCO3 (stability UP)
            3. POLARISATION argument:
                 A small, doubly charged cation (Be2+) has HIGH POLARISING
                 POWER, distorts the large CO3 2- ion and WEAKENS a C-O bond,
                 so CO2 escapes at a low temperature.
                 A large cation (Ba2+) has LOW polarising power, leaves the
                 carbonate undistorted, so a higher temperature is needed.
            4. Add: BeCO3 is stored under CO2. The same argument works for
               nitrates, sulphates and hydroxides.
  TRAP      This is a POLARISATION question, not a solubility question.
            Do not bring lattice-versus-hydration energy into it.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — HYDRIDES AND THEIR STABILITY                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange the alkali metal hydrides in order of stability",
            "What is hydrolith?"
  METHOD    1. 2 M + H2 --(heat)--> 2 MH , ionic (saline) hydrides
                 containing the H- ion; strong reducing agents.
            2. Stability:  LiH > NaH > KH > RbH > CsH , because the M-H bond
               weakens as the cation grows.
            3. Group 2: M + H2 --(heat)--> MH2 . CaH2 is HYDROLITH.
                 CaH2 + 2 H2O --> Ca(OH)2 + 2 H2
            4. BeH2 is COVALENT and cannot be made by direct combination.
  TRAP      Hydride stability goes DOWN the group - it is the OPPOSITE of
            the thermal stability of carbonates. Do not generalise.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — REACTION OF Be WITH ALKALI (AMPHOTERIC PROOF)      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which alkaline earth metal dissolves in NaOH?",
            "Show that Be is amphoteric."
  METHOD    1. Be + 2 NaOH + 2 H2O --> Na2[Be(OH)4] + H2
                                       sodium beryllate
            2. Be also reacts with acid: Be + 2 HCl --> BeCl2 + H2 .
            3. Reacting with BOTH acid and alkali = AMPHOTERIC.
            4. BeO and Be(OH)2 are amphoteric too; no other Group 2 member
               behaves like this.
  TRAP      Mg, Ca, Sr and Ba do NOT dissolve in alkali. Only Be.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — SOLVAY (AMMONIA-SODA) PROCESS              *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the manufacture of sodium carbonate",
            "Explain the ammonia-soda process."
  METHOD    Write FIVE parts, in this order:
            1. RAW MATERIALS: brine (NaCl), NH3, CO2, limestone CaCO3.
            2. FLOW CHART (draw the boxes: lime kiln -> carbonating tower ->
               filtration -> heating -> ammonia recovery tower).
            3. THE SIX EQUATIONS:
                 CaCO3 --(1200 K)--> CaO + CO2
                 NH3 + H2O + CO2 --> NH4HCO3
                 NH4HCO3 + NaCl  --> NaHCO3 + NH4Cl
                 2 NaHCO3 --(373 K)--> Na2CO3 + CO2 + H2O
                 CaO + H2O --> Ca(OH)2
                 2 NH4Cl + Ca(OH)2 --> 2 NH3 + CaCl2 + 2 H2O
                 OVERALL: 2 NaCl + CaCO3 --> Na2CO3 + CaCl2
            4. KEY IDEA: NaHCO3 is only SLIGHTLY SOLUBLE, so it precipitates
               and is filtered off.
            5. ECONOMY: NH3 and CO2 are RECYCLED; CaCl2 is the only waste.
  NOTE      *** ONE OF THE TWO MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED 8-MARK ANSWERS ***
  TRAP      Add the line "K2CO3 cannot be made this way because KHCO3 is too
            soluble" - it is often a separate 2-mark question.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — CASTNER-KELLNER CELL                        *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the manufacture of NaOH",
            "Explain the electrolysis of brine in a mercury cathode cell."
  METHOD    1. PRINCIPLE: electrolysis of BRINE with a flowing MERCURY
               CATHODE and a CARBON / titanium ANODE.
            2. DRAW the cell: electrolytic chamber above, mercury below,
               amalgam flowing to the DENUDER, mercury recycled.
            3. ELECTRODE REACTIONS:
                 cathode: Na+ + e- --> Na ; Na + Hg --> Na/Hg (amalgam)
                 anode:   Cl- --> Cl + e- ; 2 Cl --> Cl2
                 denuder: 2 Na/Hg + 2 H2O --(Fe)--> 2 NaOH + H2 + 2 Hg
                 overall: 2 NaCl + 2 H2O --(electrolysis)--> 2 NaOH + Cl2 + H2
            4. WHY MERCURY: it keeps the sodium AWAY from water and from
               chlorine, so NaOH is not contaminated with NaOCl.
            5. PROPERTIES / USES of NaOH: caustic soda, deliquescent,
               soap, paper, rayon, bauxite purification, mercerising cotton.
  NOTE      *** THE OTHER MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED 8-MARK ANSWER ***
  TRAP      Cathode = MERCURY, anode = CARBON. Getting these the wrong way
            round costs several marks at once.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — PLASTER OF PARIS AND GYPSUM                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is plaster of Paris?", "Why does POP set into a hard mass?"
  METHOD    1. NAME and FORMULA: calcium sulphate hemihydrate,
               CaSO4 . 1/2 H2O .
            2. PREPARATION:
                 CaSO4.2H2O --(393 K)--> CaSO4.(1/2)H2O + (3/2) H2O
            3. SETTING:
                 CaSO4.(1/2)H2O + (3/2) H2O --> CaSO4.2H2O (hard gypsum)
                 Sets in 5 to 15 min, slightly EXOTHERMIC, EXPANDS slightly
                 so it copies the mould exactly.
            4. DEAD BURNT PLASTER: heating above 393 K gives anhydrous
               CaSO4, which does NOT set.
            5. USES: plaster casts, statues, dentistry, chalk, smoothing walls.
  TRAP      Learn the three formulae apart:
                 CaSO4.2H2O gypsum , CaSO4.1/2H2O POP , CaSO4 dead burnt.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — THE CALCIUM CHAIN: LIMESTONE, LIME, LIME WATER 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is quicklime / slaked lime?", "Why does lime water turn
            milky with CO2 and clear again with excess CO2?"
  METHOD    Write the chain as one story:
                 CaCO3 --(1070-1270 K)--> CaO (QUICKLIME) + CO2
                 CaO + H2O --> Ca(OH)2 (SLAKED LIME)   [SLAKING, exothermic]
                 Ca(OH)2 + CO2 --> CaCO3 (MILKY) + H2O
                 CaCO3 + H2O + CO2 (excess) --> Ca(HCO3)2 (SOLUBLE, clear)
                 Ca(OH)2 + Cl2 --> CaOCl2 (bleaching powder) + H2O
            Add: suspension = MILK OF LIME , clear solution = LIME WATER.
  TRAP      Milky = insoluble CaCO3. Clear again = soluble Ca(HCO3)2.
            This same pair explains TEMPORARY HARDNESS of water.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — NAME THE COMPOUND / GIVE THE FORMULA               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the chemical formula and one use of washing soda /
            baking soda / caustic soda / bleaching powder / gypsum."
  METHOD    Learn the card:
                 NaCl           common salt (rock salt)
                 NaOH           caustic soda
                 Na2CO3.10H2O   washing soda
                 Na2CO3         soda ash
                 NaHCO3         baking soda
                 CaO            quicklime
                 Ca(OH)2        slaked lime
                 CaSO4.2H2O     gypsum
                 CaSO4.1/2H2O   plaster of Paris
                 CaOCl2         bleaching powder
                 CaH2           hydrolith
                 MgSO4.7H2O     Epsom salt
                 KCl.MgCl2.6H2O carnallite
                 MgCO3.CaCO3    dolomite
            Then add ONE use, because the second mark is for the use.
  TRAP      Na2CO3.10H2O is washing soda; NaHCO3 is baking soda.
            Efflorescence: Na2CO3.10H2O --(dry air)--> Na2CO3.H2O + 9 H2O.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — BIOLOGICAL IMPORTANCE                              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the biological importance of sodium and potassium /
            magnesium and calcium."
  METHOD    Answer as a table of WHERE + WHAT IT DOES:
              Na+  OUTSIDE the cell (plasma, interstitial fluid) - controls
                   water flow across membranes, transports sugars and amino
                   acids, nerve impulse transmission.  ~90 mg per kg.
              K+   INSIDE the cell - activates enzymes, oxidation of glucose
                   to ATP, nerve impulses.  ~1.4 g per kg.
              Mg2+ CENTRAL ION OF CHLOROPHYLL ; activates phosphate-transfer
                   enzymes (ATP chemistry). ~22 g in a 70 kg adult.
              Ca2+ 99 % in BONES and TEETH (apatite; enamel is fluorapatite);
                   BLOOD CLOTTING and MUSCLE CONTRACTION. ~1200 g.
            Add the SODIUM-POTASSIUM PUMP: 3 Na+ OUT, 2 K+ IN, per ATP
            hydrolysed; works against the gradient; uses more than one third
            of a resting person's ATP.
  TRAP      CHLOROPHYLL = MAGNESIUM. HAEMOGLOBIN = IRON. Never swap.
            Na OUTSIDE, K INSIDE. Never swap.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — ODD ONE OUT / "WHY IS THIS DIFFERENT?"             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is LiI covalent?", "Why is LiF insoluble?",
            "Why is K less dense than Na?", "Why is BeCl2 soluble in organic
            solvents?"
  METHOD    Pick the right one-line reason:
              LiI covalent      -> FAJANS: small Li+, large polarisable I-.
              LiF insoluble     -> both ions tiny -> HUGE lattice energy.
              BeCl2 in organics -> it is COVALENT (Be2+ highly polarising).
              K less dense than Na -> its atomic volume grows faster than
                                      its mass (a genuine exception).
              LiHCO3 not solid  -> Li+ too small and polarising to stabilise
                                   the large HCO3- ion in a lattice.
              MgO refractory    -> extremely high melting point.
              BaSO4 barium meal -> insoluble (non-toxic) and opaque to X-rays.
  TRAP      LiF and LiI need DIFFERENT reasons. LiF = lattice energy.
            LiI = covalent character. Using one for the other loses the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — COMPARE GROUP 1 WITH GROUP 2                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare alkali metals with alkaline earth metals."
  METHOD    A three-column table, Group 2 relative to Group 1:
              size SMALLER      - higher nuclear charge
              IE1 HIGHER        - smaller size
              IE2 LOWER         - Group 2 still has a valence electron to
                                  lose, Group 1 would break a noble-gas core
              hardness, m.p., density HIGHER - two valence electrons give a
                                  stronger metallic bond
              hydration enthalpy MUCH HIGHER - smaller ion, double charge
              reactivity LOWER  - higher ionisation enthalpy
              oxidation state +2 - the extra lattice / hydration energy more
                                  than pays back the cost of IE2
  TRAP      IE1 is higher for Group 2 but IE2 is LOWER. Stating both with
            their reasons is where the marks are.
```
