## Topic: Position, configuration, occurrence

### Q A1
Why are Group 1 elements called alkali metals? Why are Group 2 elements called
alkaline earth metals?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ALKALI METALS  -  their oxides and hydroxides dissolve in water and give
                    strongly ALKALINE (basic) solutions.

  ALKALINE EARTH METALS  -  their oxides are also alkaline, AND these oxides
                    were found in the "earth" (rocks and soil) and do not
                    melt or dissolve easily.
```

---

### Q A2
Write the general electronic configuration of s-block elements. Which two
elements of the s-block are radioactive?

**ANSWER:**
```
  GROUP 1  ->  ns1          GROUP 2  ->  ns2

  RADIOACTIVE:  Francium (Fr, Z = 87)  and  Radium (Ra, Z = 88)
```

---

### Q A3
Why are alkali metals never found in the free state in nature?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Their IONISATION ENTHALPY is very LOW, so they lose their single valence
  electron extremely easily. They therefore react at once with air, moisture
  and other substances, and always exist as COMPOUNDS, never free.
```

---

### Q A4
Why are sodium and potassium stored under kerosene oil?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Na and K react VIOLENTLY with the oxygen and moisture of air, and can even
  catch fire. Kerosene does not react with them and keeps air and water away,
  so they are stored under it.
```

## Topic: Flame colours

### Q A5
Give the flame colours of Li, Na, K, Ca, Sr and Ba.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Li  -  CRIMSON RED          Ca  -  BRICK RED
  Na  -  GOLDEN YELLOW        Sr  -  CRIMSON
  K   -  LILAC (violet)       Ba  -  APPLE GREEN
```

---

### Q A6
Why do alkali metals impart colour to the flame?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Their IONISATION ENTHALPY IS VERY LOW. The heat of an ordinary bunsen flame
  is enough to EXCITE the outer electron to a higher energy level. When that
  electron falls back to its original level, it gives out the extra energy as
  VISIBLE LIGHT of a definite colour.
```

---

### Q A7
Why do Be and Mg not impart any colour to the flame?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Be and Mg atoms are very SMALL, so their electrons are held very tightly and
  their IONISATION ENTHALPIES ARE VERY HIGH. The energy of a bunsen flame is
  NOT ENOUGH to excite those electrons to a higher level.
  No excitation  ->  no light emitted  ->  no flame colour.
```

---

### Q A8
Why are Cs and K used in photoelectric cells?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Cs and K have the LOWEST IONISATION ENTHALPIES of all the metals. Ordinary
  visible light carries enough energy to knock electrons out of their surface,
  so they emit electrons readily and are ideal for photoelectric cells.
```

## Topic: Reactions with oxygen, nitrogen, water

### Q A9
What happens when Li, Na and K are burnt in air? Write the equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  4 Li  +  O2   --(burn)-->   2 Li2O      OXIDE
  2 Na  +  O2   --(burn)-->   Na2O2       PEROXIDE
    K   +  O2   --(burn)-->   KO2         SUPEROXIDE
```

---

### Q A10
Why do K, Rb and Cs form superoxides while Li forms only the oxide?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A LARGE CATION STABILISES A LARGE ANION.

      O2-   oxide       SMALL       needs a small cation  ->  Li+  gives Li2O
      O2 2- peroxide    MEDIUM                            ->  Na+  gives Na2O2
      O2 -  superoxide  LARGE       needs a large cation  ->  K+, Rb+, Cs+
                                                              give KO2 etc.

  Li+ is far too small to hold the big superoxide ion in a stable lattice.
```

---

### Q A11
Why is KO2 paramagnetic? Where is it used?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The superoxide ion O2- contains ONE UNPAIRED ELECTRON, so KO2 is
  PARAMAGNETIC (and coloured).

  USE:  in space capsules, submarines and breathing masks, because it absorbs
        CO2 and releases O2.
            4 KO2  +  2 CO2   -->   2 K2CO3  +  3 O2
```

---

### Q A12
Which alkali metal reacts directly with nitrogen? Write the equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Only LITHIUM.       6 Li  +  N2   -->   2 Li3N     (lithium nitride, ruby red)

  Reason: Li+ is very small and highly charged, so the lattice energy of Li3N
  is large enough to make the reaction favourable.
  (In Group 2, ALL the metals form nitrides, e.g. 3 Mg + N2 --> Mg3N2.)
```

---

### Q A13
Beryllium does not react with water but calcium does. Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Be has a very HIGH ionisation enthalpy and forms a thin, tough, protective
  OXIDE LAYER on its surface, so it does not react even with steam.

  Ca has a much LOWER ionisation enthalpy and no protective coat, so it reacts
  even with cold water:      Ca  +  2 H2O   -->   Ca(OH)2  +  H2
```

## Topic: Reducing power, ammonia solution

### Q A14
Why is lithium the strongest reducing agent in aqueous solution?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Li+ is the SMALLEST alkali metal ion, so it has the LARGEST HYDRATION
  ENTHALPY. The huge energy released when Li+ is hydrated MORE THAN
  COMPENSATES its high sublimation and ionisation enthalpy.
  Hence Li has the most negative electrode potential (E = -3.04 V) and is the
  strongest reducing agent IN AQUEOUS SOLUTION.
```

---

### Q A15
Why is the solution of an alkali metal in liquid ammonia blue in colour?

**ANSWER:**
```
  M  +  (x + y) NH3   -->   [M(NH3)x]+   +   [e(NH3)y]-
                            ammoniated       AMMONIATED ELECTRON
                            cation

  The AMMONIATED ELECTRON absorbs energy from the RED region of visible light.
  The light that passes through is therefore BLUE.
```

---

### Q A16
Why is the dilute solution of an alkali metal in liquid ammonia paramagnetic,
while the concentrated solution is diamagnetic?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DILUTE   -  the ammoniated electrons are FREE and UNPAIRED  ->  PARAMAGNETIC
              (the solution is deep blue)

  CONCENTRATED  -  the electrons PAIR UP into metal-ion clusters, so no
              unpaired electrons remain  ->  DIAMAGNETIC
              (the solution turns BRONZE / copper coloured)
```

## Topic: Anomalous behaviour and diagonal relationship

### Q A17
Give any two reasons for the anomalous behaviour of lithium.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  Exceptionally SMALL atomic and ionic size.
  2.  Very HIGH charge / size ratio, i.e. very high POLARISING POWER, which
      gives Li compounds a strongly COVALENT character.
  3.  ABSENCE of d orbitals in its valence shell.
```

---

### Q A18
Why is LiI more covalent than LiF?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FAJANS' RULES.
  Li+ is very small, so it has very high POLARISING POWER.
  I- is a very LARGE anion, so it is very EASILY POLARISED; F- is small and
  hardly polarised at all.
  Li+ therefore drags the electron cloud of I- towards itself, giving LiI a
  large covalent character.
```

---

### Q A19
What is a diagonal relationship? Name two pairs that show it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The first element of a group resembles the SECOND element of the NEXT group
  more than it resembles its own family. This is the DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP.

  PAIRS:   Li ~ Mg      and      Be ~ Al

  REASON:  going ACROSS a period size decreases; going DOWN a group size
           increases. Moving DIAGONALLY the two effects CANCEL, so the pair
           have nearly the same size, electronegativity and polarising power.
```

### Q A20
Give any two similarities between beryllium and aluminium.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  Both are rendered PASSIVE by concentrated HNO3.
  2.  Both dissolve in alkalis liberating H2, giving [Be(OH)4]2- and [Al(OH)4]-.
  3.  Their oxides and hydroxides are AMPHOTERIC.
  4.  BeCl2 and AlCl3 are covalent, are LEWIS ACIDS, and dissolve in organic
      solvents.
```

### Q B1
Explain the trends in atomic radius, ionisation enthalpy and hydration enthalpy
of alkali metals down the group, with reasons.

**ANSWER:**

| PROPERTY | TREND DOWN THE GROUP | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic and ionic radius | INCREASES: Li < Na < K < Rb < Cs | A new shell is added at every step, and the inner electrons shield the outer one from the nucleus. |
| Ionisation enthalpy | DECREASES: Li > Na > K > Rb > Cs | The outer electron is farther from the nucleus and better shielded, so it is removed more easily. |
| Hydration enthalpy | DECREASES: Li+ > Na+ > K+ > Rb+ > Cs+ | The ion gets bigger, so its charge density falls and it attracts water molecules less strongly. |

```
  IMPORTANT CONSEQUENCES TO WRITE:

     - Li+ is the smallest BARE ion but the LARGEST HYDRATED ion, because it
       drags the most water with it.
     - Hydrated Li+ moves slowly, so LiCl solution conducts electricity LESS
       well than CsCl solution of the same concentration.
     - IE2 is enormously larger than IE1 for all of them, because after losing
       one electron they already have a noble-gas configuration.
       Hence alkali metals show ONLY the +1 oxidation state.
```

### Q B2
Explain why alkali metals form different products with oxygen. Give the
equations and the reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
  4 Li  +  O2   --(burn)-->   2 Li2O      OXIDE           contains  O2-
  2 Na  +  O2   --(burn)-->   Na2O2       PEROXIDE        contains  O2 2-
    K   +  O2   --(burn)-->   KO2         SUPEROXIDE      contains  O2 -
    (Rb and Cs behave like K, giving RbO2 and CsO2)
```
```
  THE REASON  -  "A BIG CATION STABILISES A BIG ANION"

      O2-      SMALL anion      matched by the SMALL Li+
      O2 2-    MEDIUM anion     matched by the medium Na+
      O2 -     LARGE anion      matched by the LARGE K+, Rb+, Cs+

  When the cation and anion are of comparable size, the crystal lattice packs
  properly and the lattice energy is high, so the compound is stable.
  A tiny cation with a huge anion gives a badly packed, unstable lattice.
```
```
  EXTRA MARKS:  the superoxides KO2, RbO2, CsO2 are PARAMAGNETIC and COLOURED
                because O2- has one unpaired electron.
                4 KO2  +  2 CO2  -->  2 K2CO3  +  3 O2   (used in submarines)
```

### Q B3
Explain why lithium is the strongest reducing agent among alkali metals in
aqueous solution, although it has the highest ionisation enthalpy in the group.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Break the change  M(s) --> M+(aq) + e-  into THREE steps:

     STEP 1   M(s)    -->  M(g)              costs SUBLIMATION enthalpy
     STEP 2   M(g)    -->  M+(g) + e-        costs IONISATION enthalpy
     STEP 3   M+(g)   -->  M+(aq)            RELEASES HYDRATION enthalpy

  For Li, steps 1 and 2 cost the MOST energy in the whole group (it is a hard,
  high-melting metal with the highest ionisation enthalpy).

  BUT in step 3 Li releases FAR MORE energy than any other member, because
  Li+ is the SMALLEST ion and so has by far the LARGEST HYDRATION ENTHALPY.

  This very large hydration enthalpy MORE THAN COMPENSATES the high
  ionisation enthalpy. So overall Li gives up its electron most readily in
  water.
```
```
  ELECTRODE POTENTIALS (volts)
      Li  -3.04   <-- most negative
      Na  -2.71 ,  K  -2.93 ,  Rb  -2.98 ,  Cs  -2.99

  ALWAYS WRITE THE WORDS "IN AQUEOUS SOLUTION".
  In the GAS PHASE Cs is the strongest reducing agent, because there is no
  water and only ionisation enthalpy matters.
```

### Q B4
Although lithium has the most negative electrode potential, it reacts with
water less vigorously than sodium. Explain.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Electrode potential is a THERMODYNAMIC quantity - it tells you how far the
  reaction wants to go, NOT how fast it goes.

  Lithium is:
      the HARDEST alkali metal,
      has the HIGHEST melting point of the group,
      has a LARGE atomisation (sublimation) enthalpy.

  So its metallic lattice is difficult to break up, the metal does not melt
  and spread over the water surface, and the reaction proceeds SLOWLY and
  quietly.

  Sodium melts with the heat of the reaction, forms a ball, exposes a large
  surface and reacts briskly; K, Rb and Cs react explosively.

      2 M  +  2 H2O   -->   2 MOH  +  H2
```

### Q B10
Explain why the solubility of Group 2 hydroxides increases down the group while
that of the sulphates decreases.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE TUG OF WAR

     LATTICE ENERGY   = energy needed to PULL THE CRYSTAL APART.
                        Big lattice energy  ->  HARD to dissolve.

     HYDRATION ENERGY = energy RELEASED when water wraps round the freed ions.
                        Big hydration energy  ->  EASY to dissolve.

     hydration energy  >  lattice energy   ->   SOLUBLE
     hydration energy  <  lattice energy   ->   INSOLUBLE
```
```
  WHICH ONE WINS DEPENDS ON THE SIZE OF THE ANION.

  SMALL anion, OH- :
      the lattice energy depends strongly on the size of the cation.
      Going down the group the cation grows and the LATTICE ENERGY COLLAPSES
      FASTER than the hydration energy falls.
          =>  SOLUBILITY INCREASES DOWN THE GROUP.
      Be(OH)2 < Mg(OH)2 < Ca(OH)2 < Sr(OH)2 < Ba(OH)2

  LARGE anion, SO4 2- :
      the big anion already dominates the lattice, so growing the cation
      changes the lattice energy only a LITTLE. Meanwhile the HYDRATION
      ENERGY FALLS SHARPLY as the cation grows.
          =>  SOLUBILITY DECREASES DOWN THE GROUP.
      BeSO4 > MgSO4 > CaSO4 > SrSO4 > BaSO4
```
```
  MEMORY LINE:  "HYDROXIDES GO UP , SULPHATES GO DOWN."

  USE:  BaSO4 is completely insoluble and opaque to X-rays, so a suspension
        of it (a BARIUM MEAL) is swallowed before an X-ray of the stomach.
```

### Q B11
Explain the trend in thermal stability of Group 2 carbonates.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MCO3   --(heat)-->   MO  +  CO2

  STABILITY INCREASES DOWN THE GROUP:
      BeCO3  <  MgCO3  <  CaCO3  <  SrCO3  <  BaCO3
      (easiest to decompose)          (needs the highest temperature)
```
```
  THE REASON  -  A POLARISATION ARGUMENT

      A SMALL, doubly charged cation like Be2+ has a very HIGH POLARISING
      POWER. It pulls the electron cloud of the large CO3 2- ion towards
      itself, DISTORTS it and WEAKENS one of the C-O bonds. That weakened
      bond snaps easily, so CO2 escapes at a low temperature.

      A BIG cation like Ba2+ has LOW polarising power, leaves the carbonate
      ion undistorted, and so a much higher temperature is needed.

      => the BIGGER the cation, the MORE THERMALLY STABLE the carbonate.
```
```
  BeCO3 is so unstable that it has to be stored under an atmosphere of CO2.
  The SAME argument gives increasing thermal stability down the group for
  the NITRATES, SULPHATES and HYDROXIDES.
```

### Q B12
Write the preparation, properties and uses of plaster of Paris.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NAME     calcium sulphate hemihydrate
  FORMULA  CaSO4 . 1/2 H2O      (also written 2 CaSO4 . H2O)

  PREPARATION
      CaSO4.2H2O   --(heat, 393 K)-->   CaSO4.(1/2)H2O  +  (3/2) H2O
       gypsum                            plaster of Paris

  SETTING
      CaSO4.(1/2)H2O  +  (3/2) H2O   -->   CaSO4.2H2O
       plaster of Paris                    hard gypsum

      Sets in 5 to 15 minutes, is slightly EXOTHERMIC, and EXPANDS slightly
      on setting, so it reproduces the mould exactly.

  DEAD BURNT PLASTER
```

Discuss the trends in the physical and chemical properties of alkali metals,

```
  2.  PHYSICAL TRENDS                                             2 marks
```

| PROPERTY | TREND DOWN THE GROUP | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic and ionic radius | INCREASES | A new shell is added each period |
| Ionisation enthalpy | DECREASES | Outer electron farther out and better shielded |

| Density | Low; increases down the group, but K is LESS dense than Na | Volume grows faster than mass at potassium |
| Electropositive character and reactivity | INCREASE | Ionisation enthalpy falls |

```
  3.  FLAME COLOURS                                               1 mark

      Li crimson red , Na golden yellow , K lilac , Rb red violet , Cs blue.
```

### Q C4
Discuss the trends in the properties of alkaline earth metals, and explain the
solubility and thermal stability of their compounds.

**MODEL ANSWER:**

```
  1.  POSITION AND CONFIGURATION                                  1 mark

      Group 2: Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra  ,  general configuration  ns2 .
      They lose both electrons to give M2+ . Although IE2 is high, the extra
      lattice energy (in solids) or hydration energy (in solution) released
      by M2+ more than pays that cost, so +2 is the only state shown.
```

```
  2.  TRENDS DOWN THE GROUP                                       2 marks
```

| PROPERTY | TREND | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Atomic and ionic radius | INCREASES | A new shell each period |
| Ionisation enthalpy | DECREASES | Larger, better-shielded atoms |
| Hydration enthalpy | DECREASES: Be2+ > Mg2+ > Ca2+ > Sr2+ > Ba2+ | Bigger ion, lower charge density |
| Reactivity and electropositive character | INCREASE | Ionisation enthalpy falls |
| Melting and boiling point | No regular trend | The metals have different crystal structures |
| Density | Falls to Ca, then rises | Ca has the lowest density in the group |
| Basic character of MO and M(OH)2 | INCREASES: BeO amphoteric, MgO weakly basic, BaO strongly basic | A bigger, less polarising cation releases OH- more easily |

```
  FLAME COLOURS:  Be none , Mg none , Ca brick red , Sr crimson ,
                  Ba apple green .
  Be and Mg give none because their atoms are small and their ionisation
  enthalpies are too high for a bunsen flame to excite the electrons.
  Sr and Ba salts are used in FIREWORKS.
```

```
  3.  CHEMICAL REACTIONS                                          2 marks

      2 M  +  O2   --(burn)-->   2 MO         only the normal oxide
      3 M  +  N2   --(burn)-->   M3N2         ALL of them form nitrides
      Be   +  H2O  -->  NO REACTION even with steam
      Mg   +  H2O(steam)  -->  MgO  +  H2
      Ca   +  2 H2O  -->  Ca(OH)2  +  H2      with cold water
      M    +  H2  --(heat)-->  MH2   ;  CaH2 is HYDROLITH
      CaH2 +  2 H2O  -->  Ca(OH)2  +  2 H2
      M    +  2 HCl  -->  MCl2  +  H2
      Be   +  2 NaOH  +  2 H2O  -->  Na2[Be(OH)4]  +  H2    (Be only)
```

```
  4.  SOLUBILITY - THE LATTICE / HYDRATION ARGUMENT               2 marks

      hydration energy > lattice energy  ->  SOLUBLE
      hydration energy < lattice energy  ->  INSOLUBLE

      HYDROXIDES (small OH- anion):
          lattice energy collapses faster than hydration energy falls,
          so SOLUBILITY INCREASES down the group.
          Be(OH)2 < Mg(OH)2 < Ca(OH)2 < Sr(OH)2 < Ba(OH)2

      SULPHATES (large SO4 2- anion):
          the lattice energy hardly changes, but hydration energy falls
          sharply, so SOLUBILITY DECREASES down the group.
          BeSO4 > MgSO4 > CaSO4 > SrSO4 > BaSO4

      CARBONATES: all insoluble; solubility decreases down the group.

      "HYDROXIDES GO UP , SULPHATES GO DOWN."
```

```
  5.  THERMAL STABILITY OF CARBONATES                             1 mark

      MCO3  --(heat)-->  MO  +  CO2
      BeCO3 < MgCO3 < CaCO3 < SrCO3 < BaCO3     stability INCREASES

      A small, highly charged cation like Be2+ has high POLARISING POWER,
      distorts the large CO3 2- ion and weakens a C-O bond, so CO2 leaves
      easily. A large cation like Ba2+ hardly distorts it, so a much higher
      temperature is needed.
```
