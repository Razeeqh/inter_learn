## Topic: Carbon, hybridisation, structure

### Q A1
What is catenation? Why does carbon show it to the maximum extent?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CATENATION - the property by which atoms of an element link with atoms
  of the SAME element to form long chains and rings.

  Carbon shows it best because:
    * the C-C bond is very strong (348 kJ/mol) as carbon is a SMALL atom
    * C also forms strong bonds to H, O, N, S and the halogens
    * C can form single, double and triple bonds with itself

  (Down the group the bond gets weaker: Si-Si is only 297 kJ/mol,
   so silicon chains break easily.)
```

---

### Q A2
Why does carbon form a very large number of compounds?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. TETRAVALENCY - carbon has 4 valence electrons and forms 4 strong
     covalent bonds.
  2. CATENATION - carbon atoms link to one another in chains and rings
     of unlimited length.
  3. It forms SINGLE, DOUBLE and TRIPLE bonds, and forms stable bonds
     with H, O, N, S and halogens.
  4. Its small size gives short, strong, stable bonds.
```

**ANSWER:**

### Q A7
Define a homologous series and give its characteristics.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HOMOLOGOUS SERIES - a series of compounds with the SAME functional
  group in which successive members differ by -CH2- and which are
  represented by ONE general formula.

  CHARACTERISTICS:
    1. Same general formula (alkanes: CnH(2n+2)).
    2. Successive members differ by CH2 = 14 u.
    3. Same functional group -> SIMILAR chemical properties.
    4. Physical properties change GRADUALLY with molecular mass.
    5. All can be made by the SAME general method.
```

### Q A8
Distinguish between alicyclic and aromatic compounds with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ALICYCLIC - a carbocyclic ring that behaves like an ALIPHATIC
              compound; not conjugated; gives addition reactions.
              Example: cyclohexane, cyclopropane.

  AROMATIC  - a planar, cyclic, fully conjugated ring with (4n+2) pi
              electrons (Huckel's rule); unusually stable; gives
              SUBSTITUTION not addition.
              Example: benzene, toluene, naphthalene.
```

### Q A22
What is hyperconjugation? Why is it called no-bond resonance?

**ANSWER:**
```
  HYPERCONJUGATION - the delocalisation of the SIGMA electrons of an
  ALPHA C-H bond into an adjacent empty p orbital, half-filled p
  orbital or pi orbital.

  It is called NO-BOND RESONANCE because in the contributing
  structures the C-H bond is shown as BROKEN - the hydrogen appears
  as H+ with NO BOND drawn to the carbon.

  It explains why (CH3)3C+ (9 alpha H) is more stable than CH3+ (0).
```

## Topic: Purification and analysis

### Q A24
What is the principle of steam distillation? Name one compound purified by it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE: for two IMMISCIBLE liquids, each exerts its own vapour
  pressure independently. The mixture boils when

        p(compound) + p(water) = atmospheric pressure

  which happens BELOW 373 K, i.e. below the boiling point of both.
  So the compound distils over at a low, safe temperature.

  Example: ANILINE (normal b.p. 457 K) distils over at about 371 K.
```

---

### Q A25
Why is fractional distillation preferred over simple distillation for a
mixture of acetone and methanol?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Acetone boils at 329 K and methanol at 338 K - a difference of only
  9 K, which is far less than the ~25 K needed for simple distillation.
  A FRACTIONATING COLUMN provides many surfaces for repeated
  condensation and evaporation, and each cycle enriches the vapour in
  the more volatile acetone, giving a clean separation.
```

### Q B1
Explain the shapes of methane, ethene and ethyne on the basis of hybridisation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  METHANE - sp3
     One 2s and three 2p orbitals of the excited carbon mix to give
     FOUR identical sp3 orbitals (25% s character). They point to the
     corners of a TETRAHEDRON to minimise repulsion.
     H-C-H angle = 109 deg 28 min , C-H = 109 pm, all 4 bonds sigma.

              H
              |
              C
            / | \
          H   H   H

  ETHENE - sp2
     One 2s and two 2p mix to give THREE sp2 orbitals in one PLANE at
     120 deg (33.3% s). ONE p orbital remains unhybridised.
     Two carbons overlap head-on (sigma) and their unhybridised p
     orbitals overlap sideways (PI). The molecule is PLANAR.
     C=C = 134 pm , angle about 120 deg.

           H            H
            \          /
             C ====== C
            /          \
           H            H

  ETHYNE - sp
     One 2s and one 2p mix to give TWO sp orbitals at 180 deg (50% s).
     TWO p orbitals remain, mutually perpendicular, and form TWO PI
     bonds. The molecule is LINEAR.
     C(triple)C = 120 pm.

           H --- C (triple) C --- H
```

### Q B2
State the IUPAC rules of nomenclature and illustrate with two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  RULE 1  Select the LONGEST CONTINUOUS carbon chain. It must contain
          the functional group. If two chains are equal, choose the one
          with MORE substituents.

  RULE 2  NUMBER the chain to give the LOWEST LOCANT to:
             first  - the principal functional group
             then   - the double / triple bond
             then   - the substituents
          If still tied, use the FIRST POINT OF DIFFERENCE.

  RULE 3  Name the substituents as PREFIXES in ALPHABETICAL order.
          Ignore di, tri, tetra when alphabetising. Repeat locants:
          2,2-dimethyl (not 2-dimethyl).

  RULE 4  If more than one functional group is present, the HIGHEST
          PRIORITY one becomes the SUFFIX, the rest become PREFIXES.
             -COOH > -COOR > -CONH2 > -CN > -CHO > >C=O > -OH > -NH2
             > C=C > C(triple)C  ;  -R, -X, -NO2 are prefixes only.

  The name is built as:
     PREFIX + WORD ROOT + PRIMARY SUFFIX + SECONDARY SUFFIX


  EXAMPLE 1                 CH3
                             |
              CH3 - CH2 - CH - CH2 - CH3

       longest chain = 5 C -> pentane
       methyl is on C3 from either end
       NAME:  3-methylpentane


  EXAMPLE 2      HO - CH2 - CH2 - COOH

       -COOH beats -OH, so -COOH is the suffix and its carbon is C1;
       -OH becomes the prefix "hydroxy".
       NAME:  3-hydroxypropanoic acid
```

### Q B5
Explain the inductive effect and state its applications.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION: the PERMANENT and PARTIAL displacement of SIGMA bond
  electrons towards a more electronegative atom, transmitted along the
  chain and dying out after about 3 carbons.

       dd+     d+      d-
      CH3 --- CH2 --- Cl

  -I GROUPS (withdraw):  -NO2 > -CN > -COOH > -F > -Cl > -Br > -I
                              > -OH > -OR > -C6H5
  +I GROUPS (release) :  -C(CH3)3 > -CH(CH3)2 > -C2H5 > -CH3 > -H

  APPLICATIONS
  1. ACID STRENGTH. -I groups stabilise the RCOO- anion and increase
     acidity:
         Cl3C-COOH > Cl2CH-COOH > ClCH2-COOH > CH3COOH
     and the closer the -I group to -COOH, the stronger the acid.
     +I groups decrease acidity: HCOOH > CH3COOH > (CH3)3C-COOH.

  2. CARBOCATION STABILITY: 3 deg > 2 deg > 1 deg > CH3+
     (alkyl groups push electrons into the deficient carbon).

  3. CARBANION STABILITY (reversed): CH3- > 1 deg > 2 deg > 3 deg.

  4. BASICITY of amines in the gas phase:
     (CH3)3N > (CH3)2NH > CH3NH2 > NH3
```

### Q B6
What is the electromeric effect? Explain +E and -E with examples, and
distinguish it from the inductive effect.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTROMERIC EFFECT - the COMPLETE transfer of a PI electron pair of
  a multiple bond to one of the two bonded atoms, occurring ONLY in
  the presence of an ATTACKING REAGENT. It is TEMPORARY - the
  molecule returns to normal when the reagent is removed.

  +E EFFECT: the pi electrons move TOWARDS the atom to which the
             reagent finally attaches.

        CH2 = CH2  +  H+   -->   CH3 - CH2+
        (the pi pair went to the carbon that took the H+)

  -E EFFECT: the pi electrons move AWAY from the atom to which the
             reagent attaches.

              O                    O-
              ||                   |
        CH3 - C - CH3 + CN- --> CH3-C-CH3
                                    |
                                   CN
        (the pi pair of C=O went to OXYGEN)

  +-----------------+---------------+-------------------+
  |                 | INDUCTIVE     | ELECTROMERIC      |
  +-----------------+---------------+-------------------+
  | electrons       | SIGMA         | PI                |
  | transfer        | PARTIAL       | COMPLETE          |
  | nature          | PERMANENT     | TEMPORARY         |
  | reagent needed  | NO            | YES               |
  | multiple bond   | not needed    | ESSENTIAL         |
  +-----------------+---------------+-------------------+
```

### Q B7
What is resonance? Write the rules for writing resonance structures and
explain resonance energy taking benzene as an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  RESONANCE - when a molecule cannot be represented by a single Lewis
  structure, it is described as a HYBRID of two or more CANONICAL
  (resonance) structures. Only the HYBRID is real; the canonical
  structures are imaginary.

  RULES
   1. Only ELECTRONS move; atoms and nuclei stay put.
   2. The number of paired and unpaired electrons stays the same.
   3. All structures have the same molecular formula and same charge.
   4. All contributing atoms must be in ONE PLANE with parallel
      p orbitals (conjugation is essential).
   5. Structures with MORE covalent bonds are more stable.
   6. Structures with a COMPLETE OCTET on every atom are more stable.
   7. Structures with SEPARATED CHARGES contribute less.
   8. Like charges on adjacent atoms make a structure very unstable.
   9. Negative charge is best on the most electronegative atom.

  BENZENE
     Two Kekule structures contribute equally, so the real molecule is
     a hybrid: all six C-C bonds are IDENTICAL at 139 pm (between
     C-C 154 and C=C 134), a regular planar hexagon of sp2 carbons
     with 6 delocalised pi electrons.

  RESONANCE ENERGY
     expected heat of hydrogenation = 3 x 119.7 = 359 kJ/mol
     observed heat of hydrogenation =         208.6 kJ/mol
     RESONANCE ENERGY = 359 - 208.6 = about 150 kJ/mol

  This extra stability is why benzene prefers SUBSTITUTION over
  ADDITION - addition would destroy the delocalisation.
```

### Q B8
Explain hyperconjugation and the stability orders it accounts for.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HYPERCONJUGATION - delocalisation of the SIGMA electrons of an
  ALPHA C-H bond into an adjacent empty p orbital (carbocation),
  half-filled p orbital (free radical), or pi orbital (alkene).
  Also called NO-BOND RESONANCE or the BAKER-NATHAN EFFECT, because
  the C-H bond is shown broken in the contributing structures.

  IN THE ETHYL CATION:

        H                     H+
        |                     |
    H - C - CH2+   <-->   H - C = CH2      (and two more, one per
        |                     |             alpha hydrogen)
        H                     H

  Number of no-bond structures = number of ALPHA hydrogens.

  ORDERS EXPLAINED
   1. CARBOCATIONS:  (CH3)3C+ (9 alpha H) > (CH3)2CH+ (6)
                     > CH3CH2+ (3) > CH3+ (0)
   2. FREE RADICALS: same order, 3 deg > 2 deg > 1 deg > CH3
   3. ALKENES:  (CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 (12) > CH3CH=CHCH3 (6)
                > CH3CH=CH2 (3) > CH2=CH2 (0)
                -> this is the basis of SAYTZEFF'S RULE.
   4. -CH3 on benzene is ACTIVATING and o,p-DIRECTING.
   5. The C-C single bond in propene is shortened to 149 pm (partial
      double bond character), and propene has a small dipole moment.
```

### Q B9
Explain the principle of chromatography and describe its types.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE: the mixture is applied to a STATIONARY PHASE and a MOBILE
  PHASE flows over it. The components travel at DIFFERENT speeds
  because they interact differently with the two phases, so they
  become separated.

  1. ADSORPTION CHROMATOGRAPHY - based on DIFFERENTIAL ADSORPTION on
     a solid adsorbent (silica gel or alumina).

     (a) COLUMN CHROMATOGRAPHY - the adsorbent is packed in a glass
         column; the mixture is put on top and eluted with a solvent.
         The most strongly adsorbed component stays at the top and
         comes out LAST; the least adsorbed comes out FIRST.

     (b) THIN LAYER CHROMATOGRAPHY (TLC) - a thin layer of adsorbent
         is spread on a glass plate. A spot of the mixture is placed
         near the bottom and the solvent rises by capillary action,
         carrying components different distances.

                  distance moved by the substance
         Rf  =  -----------------------------------
                distance moved by the solvent front

  2. PARTITION CHROMATOGRAPHY - based on the continuous DIFFERENTIAL
```

### Q C1
Explain the various methods of purification of organic compounds.

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown):**
```
  OPENING LINE (1 mark)
  Purification depends on the DIFFERENCE IN A PHYSICAL PROPERTY
  between the compound and the impurity. The main methods are
  sublimation, crystallisation, distillation (simple, fractional,
  reduced pressure, steam), differential extraction and
  chromatography.

  1. SUBLIMATION  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: some solids pass DIRECTLY from solid to vapour and
     back on cooling, without melting.
     USE: a sublimable solid mixed with a non-sublimable impurity.
     EXAMPLES: camphor, naphthalene, benzoic acid, anthracene, NH4Cl.

          +-----------------+
          |  cold surface   |   <- pure crystals form here
          +-----------------+
             \   ....   /
              \________/
              | vapour  |
              +---------+
              | impure  |
              |  solid  |
              +---------+
                 HEAT

  2. CRYSTALLISATION  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: difference in SOLUBILITY in a suitable solvent. The
     compound must be sparingly soluble cold and highly soluble hot.
     METHOD: dissolve in minimum hot solvent -> filter hot -> cool
     slowly -> pure crystals separate; the impurities stay in the
     MOTHER LIQUOR. Coloured impurities are removed with ANIMAL
     CHARCOAL.
     EXAMPLE: benzoic acid from water.

  3. SIMPLE DISTILLATION  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: difference in BOILING POINT; the liquid is vaporised
     and the vapour condensed in a separate vessel.
     USE: a liquid with a non-volatile impurity, or two liquids whose
     boiling points differ by more than about 25-30 K.
     EXAMPLE: chloroform (334 K) from aniline (457 K).

  4. FRACTIONAL DISTILLATION  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: a FRACTIONATING COLUMN provides many surfaces on
     which the vapour condenses and re-evaporates. Each cycle enriches
     the vapour in the more volatile component.
     USE: liquids whose boiling points differ by LESS than about 25 K.
     EXAMPLES: crude petroleum fractions; acetone (329 K) and
     methanol (338 K).

  5. DISTILLATION UNDER REDUCED PRESSURE  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: a liquid boils when its vapour pressure equals the
     EXTERNAL pressure; lowering the external pressure lowers the
     boiling temperature.
     USE: liquids that DECOMPOSE at their normal boiling point.
     EXAMPLES: glycerol (b.p. 563 K, distils at 453 K under vacuum);
     concentration of sugarcane juice.

  6. STEAM DISTILLATION  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: for two immiscible liquids each exerts its own vapour
     pressure, so the mixture boils when
             p(compound) + p(water) = atmospheric pressure,
     which happens below 373 K.
     CONDITIONS: the compound must be insoluble in water, volatile in
     steam, and must not react with water.
     EXAMPLES: aniline (457 K, distils at ~371 K), o-nitrophenol,
     essential oils.

  7. DIFFERENTIAL EXTRACTION  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: an organic compound distributes itself between two
     immiscible solvents; it is shaken out of water into an organic
     solvent in a SEPARATING FUNNEL. Several small portions of
     solvent are far more efficient than one large portion. The
     solvent is finally distilled off.

  8. CHROMATOGRAPHY  (1 mark)
     PRINCIPLE: differential ADSORPTION on a solid, or differential
     PARTITION between two liquids, causes components to move at
     different speeds over a stationary phase.
     TYPES: adsorption (COLUMN, THIN LAYER) and partition (PAPER).
                     distance moved by the substance
              Rf  = ---------------------------------
                     distance moved by the solvent front
```

### Q C2
Explain the electronic displacement effects in covalent bonds:
inductive, electromeric, resonance and hyperconjugation.

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown):**
```
  INTRODUCTION (1 mark)
  The displacement of electrons in a covalent bond under the influence
  of a substituent or a reagent is called an ELECTRONIC DISPLACEMENT
  EFFECT. There are four: inductive, electromeric, resonance and
  hyperconjugation. They explain almost all reactivity and stability
  orders in organic chemistry.

  1. INDUCTIVE EFFECT  (2 marks)
     The PERMANENT, PARTIAL displacement of SIGMA bond electrons
     caused by a difference in ELECTRONEGATIVITY. It is transmitted
     along the chain but DIES OUT beyond about the third carbon.

           dd+     d+      d-
          CH3 --- CH2 --- Cl

     -I  (withdraw) : -NO2 > -CN > -COOH > -F > -Cl > -Br > -I
                           > -OH > -OR > -C6H5 > -H
     +I  (release)  : -C(CH3)3 > -CH(CH3)2 > -C2H5 > -CH3 > -H

     CONSEQUENCES:
        * acid strength: Cl3CCOOH > Cl2CHCOOH > ClCH2COOH > CH3COOH
        * carbocation stability 3 > 2 > 1 > CH3
        * carbanion stability REVERSED: CH3 > 1 > 2 > 3

  2. ELECTROMERIC EFFECT  (2 marks)
     The COMPLETE transfer of a PI electron pair of a multiple bond to
     one of the two atoms, occurring ONLY in the presence of an
     ATTACKING REAGENT. It is TEMPORARY and disappears when the
     reagent is removed.

     +E : pi electrons move TOWARDS the atom the reagent attaches to
             CH2=CH2 + H+ --> CH3-CH2+

     -E : pi electrons move AWAY from that atom
             CH3-CO-CH3 + CN- --> CH3-C(O-)(CN)-CH3

     DIFFERENCE FROM INDUCTIVE: sigma vs pi, partial vs complete,
     permanent vs temporary, no reagent vs reagent needed.

  3. RESONANCE  (2 marks)
     When one Lewis structure is insufficient, the molecule is
     described as a HYBRID of two or more CANONICAL structures. Only
     the hybrid is real; only ELECTRONS move, never the atoms.

     BENZENE: two equivalent Kekule structures -> all six C-C bonds
     are identical at 139 pm; the six pi electrons are DELOCALISED.

     RULES: same positions of atoms; same number of paired electrons;
     all atoms in one plane; more covalent bonds = more stable; a
     complete octet = more stable; separated charges = less stable;
     negative charge on the most electronegative atom.

     RESONANCE ENERGY = energy of the most stable canonical structure
     minus the actual energy of the hybrid.
        For benzene: 359 - 208.6 = about 150 kJ/mol.
     This is why benzene undergoes SUBSTITUTION, not addition.

     +R groups (donate, o/p directing): -OH, -OR, -NH2, -X
     -R groups (withdraw, m directing) : -NO2, -CN, -CHO, -COOH

  4. HYPERCONJUGATION  (1 mark)
     Delocalisation of the SIGMA electrons of an ALPHA C-H bond into
     an adjacent empty p, half-filled p or pi orbital. Called
     NO-BOND RESONANCE because the C-H bond is shown broken.

          H                     H+
          |                     |
      H - C - CH2+   <-->   H - C = CH2
          |                     |
          H                     H

     Number of contributing structures = number of ALPHA hydrogens.
     EXPLAINS:
        * (CH3)3C+ (9 alpha H) > (CH3)2CH+ (6) > CH3CH2+ (3) > CH3+ (0)
        * the same order for free radicals
        * alkene stability: (CH3)2C=C(CH3)2 > CH3CH=CHCH3 > CH2=CH2
          (basis of Saytzeff's rule)
        * -CH3 is ring-activating and o,p-directing in toluene

  RELATIVE STRENGTH (1 mark)
        RESONANCE  >  HYPERCONJUGATION  >  INDUCTIVE
```
