```
      C   Carbon      -   NON-METAL

      Sn  Tin         -   METAL
      Pb  Lead        -   METAL
```

### Q A2
Which element of group 14 is the most abundant in the earth's crust? Give
its percentage.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SILICON , 27.7 % by mass of the earth's crust.

  It is the SECOND MOST ABUNDANT ELEMENT in the crust, after oxygen.
  It never occurs free - only as SILICA (SiO2) and SILICATES.
```

### Q A3
Ionisation enthalpy decreases down group 14, yet lead has a higher first
ionisation enthalpy than tin. Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  IE1 :  Sn = 708 kJ/mol  ,  Pb = 715 kJ/mol

  In lead, the filled 4f14 and 5d10 subshells lie between the nucleus and
  the 6s2 6p2 valence shell. f and d orbitals are DIFFUSE and SHIELD THE
  NUCLEAR CHARGE VERY POORLY.

  So the valence electrons of Pb feel a larger effective nuclear charge,
  are held more tightly, and need slightly MORE energy to remove.
```

---

### Q A4
Why does the atomic radius increase sharply from C to Si but only slightly
from Si to Ge and from Sn to Pb?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Covalent radii (pm) :  C 77 -> Si 118 -> Ge 122 -> Sn 140 -> Pb 146

  From Si to Ge, ten 3d electrons are added.
  From Sn to Pb, fourteen 4f electrons are added.

  These d and f electrons shield the nuclear charge POORLY, so the outer
  electrons are pulled in and the expected increase in size is largely
  cancelled. This is the D-BLOCK CONTRACTION and the LANTHANOID
  CONTRACTION.
```

## Topic: Oxidation states and the inert pair effect

### Q A5
What is the inert pair effect?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE INERT PAIR EFFECT is the RELUCTANCE OF THE ns2 ELECTRON PAIR TO
  TAKE PART IN BOND FORMATION, which increases as we go DOWN a p-block
  group.

  CAUSE: the filled d and f subshells shield the nuclear charge poorly,
  so the ns2 pair is held very tightly and cannot be promoted.

  RESULT: in group 14 the +2 oxidation state becomes MORE stable and the
  +4 state LESS stable as we go down. Pb(II) is the stable state of lead.
```

---

### Q A6
PbO2 is a strong oxidising agent. Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In PbO2 lead is in the +4 oxidation state.

  Because of the INERT PAIR EFFECT, the +2 state is the STABLE state for
  lead and the +4 state is UNSTABLE. PbO2 therefore readily ACCEPTS two
  electrons and drops to Pb(II).

  Accepting electrons from another substance = acting as an OXIDISING
  AGENT.
        PbO2  +  4 HCl   -->   PbCl2  +  Cl2  +  2 H2O
```

---

### Q A7
SnCl2 is a good reducing agent. Explain.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In SnCl2 tin is in the +2 state. For tin the STABLE state is still +4,
  so Sn(II) readily gives away two electrons and becomes Sn(IV).

  Giving electrons to another substance = acting as a REDUCING AGENT.

        SnCl2  +  2 HgCl2   -->   SnCl4  +  Hg2Cl2 (white)
        SnCl2  +  Hg2Cl2    -->   SnCl4  +  2 Hg (grey)
```

---

### Q A8
Why does PbI4 not exist, although PbCl4 does?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Pb4+ is a STRONG OXIDISING AGENT and the iodide ion I- is a STRONG
  REDUCING AGENT. If they were brought together they would immediately
  react with each other:

        Pb4+   +   2 I-    -->    Pb2+   +   I2

  So PbI4 (and PbBr4) cannot exist. PbF4 and PbCl4 do exist because F-
  and Cl- are difficult to oxidise.
```

## Topic: Anomalous carbon and catenation

### Q A9
What is catenation? Give the order of catenation in group 14.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CATENATION is the property by which the atoms of an element link with
  one another through covalent bonds to form long chains, branched chains
  or rings.

  ORDER:      C   >>   Si   >   Ge   ~   Sn   >   Pb

  REASON: it follows the M-M bond enthalpy -
          C-C 348 , Si-Si 297 , Ge-Ge 260 , Sn-Sn 240 kJ/mol.
```

### Q A10
Give any two reasons for the anomalous behaviour of carbon.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Any TWO of these four:

     1.  Very SMALL atomic size (covalent radius only 77 pm).
     2.  HIGH ELECTRONEGATIVITY (2.5, the highest in the group).
     3.  HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPY (1086 kJ/mol).
     4.  ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS in its valence shell (n = 2 has only
         2s and 2p), so its maximum covalency is only 4.
```

### Q A11
Why does carbon show a maximum covalency of four while silicon can show six?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Carbon belongs to period 2. Its valence shell (n = 2) contains ONLY 2s
  and 2p orbitals - there is NO 2d subshell. So carbon can use at most
  four orbitals and cannot expand its octet.

  Silicon belongs to period 3 and HAS VACANT 3d ORBITALS, which it can
  use for bonding. Hence silicon can expand its covalency to six.

  EVIDENCE:  [SiF6]2- exists  but  [CF6]2- does not.
```

## Topic: Allotropes of carbon

### Q A12
Why is diamond the hardest substance known?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In diamond every carbon atom is sp3 HYBRIDISED and is joined by FOUR
  strong covalent sigma bonds to four other carbon atoms arranged
  TETRAHEDRALLY (bond angle 109.5 degrees, C-C = 154 pm).

  This repeats endlessly in THREE DIMENSIONS, giving one RIGID GIANT
  COVALENT NETWORK. To scratch or break it you must break covalent bonds
  in every direction, which needs enormous energy.
```

---

### Q A13
Why is graphite soft and used as a lubricant?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Graphite has a LAYERED structure. Inside each layer the carbon atoms
  are strongly bonded (sp2, 141.5 pm), but the LAYERS ARE 340 pm APART
  and are held to each other only by WEAK VAN DER WAALS FORCES.

  A small force is enough to make the layers SLIDE over one another.
  So graphite is soft and slippery and acts as a LUBRICANT - especially
  at high temperatures where ordinary oils would burn away.
```

---

### Q A14
Graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not. Give the reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIAMOND  : each carbon is sp3 hybridised and uses ALL FOUR of its
             valence electrons in sigma bonds. NO free electrons remain,
             so no current can flow -> INSULATOR.

  GRAPHITE : each carbon is sp2 hybridised and uses only THREE electrons
             for sigma bonds. The FOURTH electron lies in an unhybridised
             p orbital and is DELOCALISED over the layer. These mobile
             electrons carry current -> CONDUCTOR.
```

---

### Q A15
What are fullerenes? How are they prepared?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FULLERENES are cage-like, hollow, spherical allotropes of carbon with
  the general formula Cn (n even). The best known is C60, called
  BUCKMINSTERFULLERENE or the "bucky ball".

  PREPARATION: by heating GRAPHITE in an ELECTRIC ARC in an atmosphere
  of an INERT GAS (helium or argon). The graphite vaporises and condenses
  to a sooty material containing mainly C60 with some C70.
```

---

### Q A16
Why is fullerene said to be the only pure allotrope of carbon?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A C60 molecule is a CLOSED CAGE made only of fused rings, so every
  carbon atom has all its valencies satisfied inside the molecule.
  There are NO "DANGLING" (unsatisfied) BONDS anywhere on it, and
  therefore no site where impurity atoms can attach.

  Diamond and graphite both have edges and surfaces carrying dangling
  bonds, which pick up other atoms. Hence only fullerene is PURE.
```

### Q A17
How many six-membered and five-membered rings are there in C60? What is the
hybridisation of carbon in it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  TWENTY  six-membered rings (hexagons)
  TWELVE  five-membered rings (pentagons)
          = 32 faces in all , 60 carbon atoms , football shaped

  Each carbon is  sp2 HYBRIDISED , forming three sigma bonds; the
  remaining electron is delocalised over the whole sphere.

  C-C bond lengths: 143.5 pm (in a six-membered ring) and 138.3 pm
  (at the ring fusions).
```

## Topic: Reactivity, CCl4 and SiCl4

### Q A18
Why is CCl4 not hydrolysed by water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hydrolysis needs the LONE PAIR of the oxygen of water to attack the
  central atom, so the central atom must have a VACANT ORBITAL to accept
  it.

  Carbon has NO d ORBITALS in its valence shell (n = 2), all four of its
  orbitals are already used in CCl4, and it cannot expand its octet
  beyond four. The small carbon atom is also completely screened by the
  four large chlorine atoms.

  Hence  CCl4  +  H2O  -->  NO REACTION.
```

### Q A20
What is producer gas? Write the equation for its formation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRODUCER GAS is a MIXTURE OF CARBON MONOXIDE AND NITROGEN, made by
```

## Topic: Compounds of carbon

### Q A21
Why is carbon monoxide poisonous?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CO combines with the HAEMOGLOBIN of the blood to form
  CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN:

        Hb  +  CO   -->   HbCO

  CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN IS ABOUT 300 TIMES MORE STABLE than the normal
  oxygen complex, oxyhaemoglobin (HbO2). CO therefore blocks the
  haemoglobin, the blood can no longer carry oxygen to the tissues, and
  the person dies of ASPHYXIATION. CO is odourless, so there is no
  warning.
```

```
  without melting, so it leaves no liquid behind - hence "dry" ice.

  USES:
```

### Q A23
Describe the structure of the CO2 molecule.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CO2 is a LINEAR molecule:        O === C === O

     *  Carbon is  sp HYBRIDISED , bond angle = 180 degrees.
     *  The molecule is NON-POLAR because the two equal C=O bond
        dipoles point in opposite directions and cancel.
     *  Observed C-O bond length = 115 pm, SHORTER than a normal C=O
        double bond, because the molecule is a RESONANCE HYBRID:

        O=C=O  <-->  (-)O-C(+)=O  <-->  O=C(+)-O(-)
```

### Q A24
What is the greenhouse effect?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Short-wavelength solar radiation passes freely through the CO2 of the
  atmosphere and warms the earth. The warm earth re-radiates the energy
  as LONG-WAVELENGTH INFRARED radiation. CO2 ABSORBS this infrared
  radiation and sends part of it back to the earth.

  The heat is therefore TRAPPED near the surface and the average
  temperature of the earth rises. This trapping is the GREENHOUSE EFFECT
  and the resulting temperature rise is called GLOBAL WARMING.

  Other greenhouse gases: CH4, water vapour, N2O, CFCs, ozone.
```

## Topic: Compounds of silicon

### Q A25
Why is CO2 a gas while SiO2 is a solid?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CARBON is small, so its 2p orbital overlaps sideways well with the 2p
  of oxygen. Carbon therefore forms p(pi)-p(pi) DOUBLE BONDS and its
  valency is satisfied inside one small molecule, O=C=O. These discrete
  molecules are held to each other only by WEAK VAN DER WAALS FORCES,
  so CO2 is a GAS.

  SILICON is large and its 3p orbitals are diffuse, so it CANNOT form
  p(pi)-p(pi) bonds with oxygen. It instead forms FOUR SINGLE sigma
  bonds to four oxygens, each oxygen bridging to another silicon. This
  gives a GIANT 3-D COVALENT NETWORK, so SiO2 is a hard, high-melting
  SOLID.
```

### Q A26
Why is hydrofluoric acid not stored in glass bottles?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Glass contains SILICA, SiO2. Hydrofluoric acid attacks and dissolves
  silica:

        SiO2   +   4 HF    -->    SiF4   +   2 H2O

  The acid would therefore eat away the bottle. HF is stored in WAX-
  coated or PLASTIC (polythene / teflon) containers instead. The same
  reaction is used for ETCHING designs on glass.
```

### Q B1
Discuss the variation of atomic radius, ionisation enthalpy,
electronegativity and metallic character down group 14.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Answer in three columns: PROPERTY | DIRECTION | REASON

  1.  ATOMIC RADIUS - INCREASES down the group
      (C 77 -> Si 118 -> Ge 122 -> Sn 140 -> Pb 146 pm)
      REASON: a new electron shell is added at each step.
      NOTE: the increase from C to Si is large; afterwards it is small
      because of the D-BLOCK and LANTHANOID CONTRACTIONS (d and f
      electrons shield poorly).

  2.  IONISATION ENTHALPY - DECREASES down the group
      (C 1086 -> Si 786 -> Ge 761 -> Sn 708 kJ/mol)
      REASON: the atom gets bigger, so the outer electron is farther
      from the nucleus and better shielded.
      EXCEPTION: Pb (715) > Sn (708), because the 4f14 and 5d10
      electrons of lead shield very poorly.

  3.  ELECTRONEGATIVITY - DECREASES sharply from C (2.5) to Si (1.8)
      and is then almost constant (Ge 1.8, Sn 1.8, Pb 1.9).
      REASON: carbon is very small, so it attracts the shared pair much
      more strongly than the larger members.

  4.  METALLIC CHARACTER - INCREASES down the group
      C non-metal -> Si, Ge metalloids -> Sn, Pb metals.
      REASON: ionisation enthalpy falls, so the atoms lose electrons
      more easily and behave more like metals. The evidence is that the
      oxides change from ACIDIC (CO2, SiO2) to AMPHOTERIC (SnO2, PbO2).
```

### Q B2
Explain the inert pair effect and its consequences in group 14.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  The INERT PAIR EFFECT is the reluctance of the ns2 electron pair to
  take part in bond formation, an effect that increases as we go down a
  p-block group.

  CAUSE
     1.  Going down the group, filled d (3d10, 4d10, 5d10) and f (4f14)
         subshells come between the nucleus and the valence shell.
     2.  d and f orbitals are diffuse and SHIELD THE NUCLEAR CHARGE
         VERY POORLY.
     3.  So the ns2 pair of the heavier elements feels a large effective
         nuclear charge, is held tightly and is lowered in energy.
     4.  Promoting an ns electron into an np orbital (which is needed
         for the +4 state) now costs too much energy, so only the two
         p electrons are used.

  CONSEQUENCES IN GROUP 14
     *  The +2 state becomes MORE stable and the +4 state LESS stable
        as we go down:
              +2 stability  C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb
              +4 stability  C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb
     *  Pb(IV) compounds such as PbO2 and PbCl4 are STRONG OXIDISING
        AGENTS:      PbO2 + 4 HCl --> PbCl2 + Cl2 + 2 H2O
     *  Sn(II) and Ge(II) compounds such as SnCl2 are good REDUCING
        AGENTS:      SnCl2 + 2 HgCl2 --> SnCl4 + Hg2Cl2
     *  PbCl4 decomposes easily to PbCl2 + Cl2, and PbBr4 and PbI4 do
        not exist at all.
```

### Q B3
Explain the anomalous behaviour of carbon.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Carbon, the first member of group 14, differs from the rest of the
  group for FOUR reasons:

     (i)   its very SMALL atomic size (covalent radius 77 pm)
     (ii)  its HIGH ELECTRONEGATIVITY (2.5)
     (iii) its HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPY (1086 kJ/mol)
     (iv)  the ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS in its valence shell (n = 2)

  CONSEQUENCES

     1.  Maximum covalency of carbon is 4. It cannot expand its octet,
         so [CF6]2- does not exist although [SiF6]2- does.

     2.  Carbon readily forms p(pi)-p(pi) MULTIPLE BONDS with itself,
         with oxygen and with nitrogen (C=C, C=O, C=N). The heavier
         members hardly form pi bonds at all.

     3.  Because of that, CO2 is a small gaseous molecule O=C=O, while
         SiO2 is a giant three-dimensional covalent solid.

     4.  Carbon shows the MAXIMUM TENDENCY FOR CATENATION, giving
         chains and rings - the basis of organic chemistry.

     5.  CCl4 is not hydrolysed by water while SiCl4 is, because carbon
         has no vacant d orbital to accept the lone pair of water.

     6.  Carbon shows ALLOTROPY - diamond, graphite and fullerenes.
```

### Q B4
What is catenation? Why does it decrease down group 14? Give the order and
the reason with data.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  Catenation is the property by which the atoms of an element link with
  one another through covalent bonds to form straight chains, branched
  chains or closed rings.

              C - C - C - C - C          straight chain
                     C
                     |
              C - C - C - C              branched chain
              (and closed rings such as C6H6)

  ORDER IN GROUP 14
              C   >>   Si   >   Ge   ~   Sn   >   Pb
              (lead shows practically no catenation)

  REASON - IT FOLLOWS THE M-M BOND ENTHALPY

              C - C     348 kJ/mol
              Si - Si   297 kJ/mol
              Ge - Ge   260 kJ/mol
              Sn - Sn   240 kJ/mol
              Pb - Pb   negligible

  Going down the group the atoms get BIGGER, so the bonding orbitals
  must overlap across a LONGER distance and the overlap becomes POORER.
  The M-M bond is therefore longer and weaker and the chain breaks
  easily. Hence catenation falls.

  CONSEQUENCE
  Carbon alone forms millions of compounds - the whole of organic
  chemistry - while silicon forms only a handful of chains and lead none.
```

### Q B6
Describe the structure of fullerene. Why is it called the purest form of
carbon?

**ANSWER:**
```
  STRUCTURE OF C60 (BUCKMINSTERFULLERENE)

     *  It contains TWENTY six-membered rings (hexagons) and TWELVE
        five-membered rings (pentagons) - 32 faces in all - fused into
        a hollow ball shaped exactly like a FOOTBALL.
     *  Each six-membered ring is fused to hexagons and pentagons;
        each five-membered ring is fused ONLY to hexagons.
     *  ALL SIXTY CARBON ATOMS ARE EQUIVALENT. Each is sp2 HYBRIDISED
        and forms THREE sigma bonds to three neighbours.
     *  The remaining electron of each carbon is DELOCALISED over the
        whole sphere in molecular orbitals.
     *  Bond lengths: 143.5 pm inside a six-membered ring, and
        138.3 pm at the ring fusions.

  PREPARATION
     Graphite is heated in an ELECTRIC ARC in an atmosphere of HELIUM or
     ARGON. The vapour condenses to a sooty solid containing mainly C60
     with a smaller amount of C70.

  WHY IT IS THE PUREST FORM OF CARBON
     C60 is a CLOSED CAGE of fused rings, so every carbon has all its
     valencies satisfied within the molecule. There are NO DANGLING
     (unsatisfied) BONDS anywhere, and therefore no site at which
     impurity atoms could attach. Diamond and graphite both have edges
     carrying dangling bonds that pick up other atoms.
```

### Q B7
CCl4 is not hydrolysed by water but SiCl4 is readily hydrolysed. Give reasons.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE FACTS
        CCl4   +  H2O    -->   NO REACTION
        SiCl4  +  4 H2O  -->   Si(OH)4  +  4 HCl

  THE MECHANISM REQUIREMENT
  Hydrolysis begins when the LONE PAIR of the oxygen of a water molecule
  ATTACKS the central atom and forms a bond with it. The central atom
  must therefore possess a VACANT ORBITAL of low energy to accept that
  lone pair - in other words it must be able to EXPAND ITS OCTET.

  CARBON
     *  Carbon is in period 2. Its valence shell (n = 2) contains only
        2s and 2p orbitals - there is NO 2d subshell.
     *  In CCl4 all four of carbon's orbitals are already used.
     *  Carbon therefore has NO VACANT ORBITAL to accept the lone pair
        and cannot increase its covalency beyond four.
     *  The small carbon atom is also completely surrounded and screened
        by four large chlorine atoms, so water cannot even approach it.
     *  Hence CCl4 is NOT hydrolysed and is used as a fire extinguisher
        (pyrene) and a solvent.

  SILICON
     *  Silicon is in period 3 and HAS VACANT 3d ORBITALS.
     *  The oxygen lone pair of water enters a vacant 3d orbital, giving
        a five- or six-coordinate intermediate; silicon EXPANDS ITS
        OCTET.
     *  The Si-Cl bonds then break and hydrolysis is complete.
     *  Hence SiCl4 fumes in moist air and is readily hydrolysed.

  THE SAME REASONING explains why [SiF6]2- exists but [CF6]2- does not.
```

### Q B9

### Q B11
What is the greenhouse effect? How does CO2 cause global warming? What are
its consequences?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE GREENHOUSE EFFECT

     1.  SHORT-WAVELENGTH solar radiation passes freely through the
         carbon dioxide of the atmosphere and reaches the earth.

     2.  The earth's surface is warmed and re-radiates the energy as
         LONG-WAVELENGTH INFRARED radiation.

     3.  CO2 molecules ABSORB this infrared radiation strongly and
         re-emit part of it back towards the earth.

     4.  Heat is therefore TRAPPED near the surface, exactly as glass
         traps heat in a greenhouse, and the average temperature of the
         earth rises. This rise is called GLOBAL WARMING.

  WHY CO2 IS INCREASING
     *  burning of FOSSIL FUELS - coal, petrol, diesel, natural gas
     *  DEFORESTATION - fewer trees to remove CO2 by photosynthesis
     *  cement manufacture and other industry

  OTHER GREENHOUSE GASES
     methane (CH4), water vapour, nitrous oxide (N2O), CFCs, ozone

  CONSEQUENCES
     *  melting of polar ice caps and glaciers
     *  rise in sea level and flooding of coastal areas
     *  changes in rainfall patterns, droughts and crop failure
     *  more frequent extreme weather events

  NOTE: a normal greenhouse effect is NECESSARY - without it the earth
  would be about 33 degrees colder and could not support life. The
  problem is the ENHANCED greenhouse effect caused by human activity.
```

### Q B15
What are zeolites? Describe their structure and their uses.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION AND STRUCTURE
     *  Zeolites are THREE-DIMENSIONAL ALUMINOSILICATES, formed when
        SOME of the silicon atoms of a 3-D silicate framework are
        replaced by ALUMINIUM atoms.
     *  Every Si(IV) replaced by Al(III) leaves ONE UNIT OF NEGATIVE
        CHARGE on the framework (an [AlO4]5- unit).
     *  This charge is balanced by CATIONS - Na+, K+, Ca2+ - which sit
        LOOSELY inside the structure and are NOT part of the framework.
     *  The framework is honeycombed with CAVITIES and CHANNELS of a
        definite, uniform size, which small molecules can enter.
     *  General formula:  Mx/n [(AlO2)x (SiO2)y] . z H2O
     *  The name means "boiling stone", because the trapped water
        bubbles out on heating.

  USES

  1.  AS SHAPE-SELECTIVE CATALYSTS
      Only molecules small enough and of the right shape can enter the
      pores and react, so the catalyst selects by shape. ZSM-5 converts
      ALCOHOLS DIRECTLY INTO GASOLINE. Zeolites are also used for the
      CRACKING and ISOMERISATION of hydrocarbons in petroleum refining.

  2.  AS ION EXCHANGERS - SOFTENING HARD WATER
      The loosely held Na+ ions exchange with the Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions
      that cause hardness:
            Na2Z  +  Ca2+   -->   CaZ   +   2 Na+
            Na2Z  +  Mg2+   -->   MgZ   +   2 Na+
      The exhausted zeolite is REGENERATED with brine:
            CaZ   +  2 NaCl  -->  Na2Z  +  CaCl2
      (The hydrated sodium aluminosilicate used this way is PERMUTIT.)
      Zeolites are also used in detergents in place of phosphates.

  3.  AS MOLECULAR SIEVES AND DRYING AGENTS
      Because all the pores are the same size, a zeolite admits small
      molecules and excludes large ones. It is therefore used to
      separate mixtures by molecular size and to dry gases and solvents.
```

```
      One sigma bond, one pi bond and one COORDINATE bond from oxygen
      to carbon, so both atoms complete their octets.
      C-O bond length 112.8 pm; a very strong bond.

  3.  PROPERTIES
      *  Colourless, odourless, tasteless; almost INSOLUBLE in water.
      *  NEUTRAL oxide - no effect on litmus.
      *  Burns with a blue flame:   2 CO + O2 --> 2 CO2
      *  POWERFUL REDUCING AGENT:
             Fe2O3 + 3 CO --> 2 Fe + 3 CO2
             ZnO + CO --> Zn + CO2 ;  CuO + CO --> Cu + CO2
      *  Acts as a LIGAND, giving metal carbonyls:
             Ni + 4 CO --(330-350 K)--> [Ni(CO)4]   (Mond process)
      *  Estimated by:  5 CO + I2O5 --> I2 + 5 CO2

  4.  WHY CO IS POISONOUS
      Hb + O2 --> HbO2 (oxyhaemoglobin) is how blood carries oxygen.
      CO binds at the SAME site:  Hb + CO --> HbCO (CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN).
      HbCO IS ABOUT 300 TIMES MORE STABLE THAN HbO2, so CO blocks the
      haemoglobin permanently. The blood can no longer carry oxygen and
      the victim dies of ASPHYXIATION. CO is odourless, so there is no
      warning.

  5.  USES OF CO
      Reducing agent in metallurgy; fuel (water gas, producer gas);
      Mond process for nickel; manufacture of methanol
      (CO + 2 H2 --> CH3OH); organic synthesis.

  PART B - CARBON DIOXIDE                                        [4 M]

  1.  PREPARATION
      LAB:        CaCO3 + 2 HCl --> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2
      COMBUSTION: C + O2 --> CO2
      INDUSTRY:   CaCO3 --(1000-1200 K)--> CaO + CO2
      FERMENT.:   C6H12O6 --(zymase)--> 2 C2H5OH + 2 CO2

  2.  STRUCTURE
              O === C === O
      LINEAR, carbon sp HYBRIDISED, bond angle 180 degrees, NON-POLAR.
      C-O = 115 pm, shorter than a normal C=O because of RESONANCE:
              O=C=O <--> (-)O-C(+)=O <--> O=C(+)-O(-)

  3.  PROPERTIES
      *  Colourless, odourless, heavier than air; 0.03 % of the air.
      *  ACIDIC oxide:   CO2 + H2O <--> H2CO3 (weak DIBASIC acid)
      *  Turns lime water MILKY, and the milkiness clears in excess CO2:
             CO2 + Ca(OH)2 --> CaCO3 (milky) + H2O
             CaCO3 + H2O + CO2 --> Ca(HCO3)2 (clear)
      *  With alkali:  CO2 + 2 NaOH --> Na2CO3 + H2O
      *  Does not burn or support burning, BUT burning magnesium
         continues in it:   2 Mg + CO2 --> 2 MgO + C
      *  Solid CO2 is DRY ICE, which SUBLIMES at 195 K.

  4.  THE GREENHOUSE EFFECT AND GLOBAL WARMING
      Short-wave solar radiation passes through the CO2 of the
      atmosphere and warms the earth. The earth re-radiates the energy
      as LONG-WAVE INFRARED radiation, which CO2 ABSORBS and partly
      sends back. Heat is trapped near the surface and the average
      temperature of the earth rises - GLOBAL WARMING.
      CO2 is rising because of the burning of FOSSIL FUELS and
      DEFORESTATION. Other greenhouse gases are CH4, water vapour,
      N2O, CFCs and ozone.
      Consequences: melting ice caps, rising sea level, floods,
      droughts and crop failure.

  5.  USES OF CO2
      Fire extinguisher; dry ice as a refrigerant; aerated drinks;
      manufacture of urea (2 NH3 + CO2 --> NH2CONH2 + H2O); the Solvay
      process; and it is essential for photosynthesis.
```

### Q C4
Describe the important compounds of silicon — silicon dioxide, silicones,
silicates and zeolites — with their structures and uses.

**MODEL ANSWER (8 marks)**

```
  1.  SILICON DIOXIDE (SILICA), SiO2                             [2 M]

      STRUCTURE
      *  Each silicon is sp3 hybridised and bonded covalently to FOUR
         oxygen atoms arranged tetrahedrally.
      *  EACH OXYGEN IS SHARED BETWEEN TWO SILICON TETRAHEDRA, so each
         Si owns 4 x 1/2 = 2 oxygens - hence the formula SiO2.
      *  A GIANT COVALENT 3-D NETWORK; the whole crystal is one molecule.
      *  Crystalline forms: quartz, cristobalite, tridymite.

                O           O           O
                |           |           |
           O -- Si -- O -- Si -- O -- Si -- O
                |           |           |
                O           O           O

      WHY IT DIFFERS FROM CO2
      Carbon is small and forms p(pi)-p(pi) DOUBLE bonds, giving
      discrete O=C=O molecules held by weak van der Waals forces - a
      GAS. Silicon cannot form pi bonds, so it builds a 3-D network of
      single Si-O bonds - a HARD, HIGH-MELTING SOLID (~1996 K).

      REACTIONS
           SiO2 + 4 HF  -->  SiF4 + 2 H2O    (so HF is not kept in glass)
           SiO2 + 2 NaOH --(fused)--> Na2SiO3 + H2O
           SiO2 + 2 C  --(furnace)-->  Si + 2 CO

      USES: quartz is PIEZOELECTRIC (watches, oscillators); silica gel
      as a drying agent; glass, cement and ceramics; refractory bricks;
      fused quartz for optical fibres and UV apparatus.

  2.  SILICONES                                                  [2 M]

      Organosilicon POLYMERS with a backbone of alternating Si and O
      atoms carrying alkyl or aryl groups; repeating unit (R2SiO).

      PREPARATION
           RMgCl + SiCl4 --> RSiCl3 + MgCl2   (and on to R2SiCl2)
           n R2SiCl2 + n H2O --> [-O-SiR2-]n + 2n HCl

      STRUCTURE
              R     R     R
              |     |     |
        ... - Si -O-Si -O-Si - O - ...
              |     |     |
              R     R     R

      R3SiCl acts as a chain STOPPER; RSiCl3 gives a CROSS-LINKED resin.

      PROPERTIES: chemically inert, thermally very stable,
      WATER-REPELLENT, excellent electrical insulators, non-toxic.

      USES: high-temperature lubricants and greases; electrical
      insulation; waterproofing fabrics and buildings; heat-resistant
      paints; sealants; surgical and cosmetic implants.

  3.  SILICATES                                                  [2 M]

      The basic structural unit of every silicate is the [SiO4]4-
      TETRAHEDRON (sp3 silicon, four oxygens at the corners, each
      carrying one negative charge).

                          O(-)
                           |
                           Si
                         / | \
                     O(-) O(-) O(-)

      Silicates are built by SHARING CORNER OXYGEN ATOMS:

          0 shared  ->  orthosilicate  SiO4 4-      zircon ZrSiO4
          1 shared  ->  pyrosilicate   Si2O7 6-     thortveitite
          2 shared  ->  chain / ring   (SiO3)n 2n-  spodumene , beryl
          2 and 3   ->  double chain   (Si4O11)n 6n- ASBESTOS
          3 shared  ->  sheet          (Si2O5)n 2n-  TALC , MICA
          4 shared  ->  3-D network    SiO2          QUARTZ, feldspar

      The negative charge is balanced by Na+, K+, Mg2+, Ca2+, Al3+ ions.
      Sheet silicates flake and feel soapy because the sheets are only
      loosely stacked.

  4.  ZEOLITES                                                   [2 M]

      Three-dimensional ALUMINOSILICATES, made when some Si atoms of a
      3-D silicate framework are replaced by Al. Each replacement leaves
      one unit of negative charge, balanced by loosely held Na+, K+ or
      Ca2+ ions sitting in the CAVITIES and CHANNELS of the framework.
      General formula  Mx/n [(AlO2)x (SiO2)y] . z H2O.

      USES
      (a)  SHAPE-SELECTIVE CATALYSTS - only molecules of the right size
           and shape can enter the pores. ZSM-5 converts alcohols
           directly into gasoline; zeolites also crack and isomerise
           hydrocarbons in petroleum refining.
      (b)  ION EXCHANGERS for SOFTENING HARD WATER:
                Na2Z + Ca2+ --> CaZ + 2 Na+
           regenerated with brine:  CaZ + 2 NaCl --> Na2Z + CaCl2
      (c)  MOLECULAR SIEVES and drying agents, because all the pores
           are of the same size.
```

### Q C5
Explain the anomalous behaviour of carbon and the inert pair effect. How do
these two ideas explain (i) the maximum covalency of carbon, (ii) the
hydrolysis of CCl4 and SiCl4, (iii) the difference between CO2 and SiO2, and
(iv) the oxidising nature of PbO2?

**MODEL ANSWER (8 marks)**

```
  PART A - ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF CARBON                         [2 M]

      Carbon differs from Si, Ge, Sn and Pb for FOUR reasons:
         (i)   very small atomic size (77 pm)
         (ii)  high electronegativity (2.5)
         (iii) high ionisation enthalpy (1086 kJ/mol)
         (iv)  ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS in its valence shell (n = 2)

  PART B - THE INERT PAIR EFFECT                                 [2 M]

      The reluctance of the ns2 pair to take part in bonding, which
      increases down a p-block group.
      CAUSE: the intervening filled d (3d10, 4d10, 5d10) and f (4f14)
      subshells shield the nuclear charge poorly, so the ns2 pair is
      gripped tightly and cannot be promoted to the np level.
      RESULT: +2 becomes more stable and +4 less stable down the group.

  PART C - THE FOUR APPLICATIONS                                 [4 M]

  (i)   MAXIMUM COVALENCY OF CARBON
        Carbon's valence shell (n = 2) has only 2s and 2p orbitals -
        no 2d subshell exists. So carbon can use at most four orbitals
        and its maximum covalency is FOUR. Silicon and the heavier
        members have vacant d orbitals and can reach a covalency of six.
        Hence [SiF6]2- exists but [CF6]2- does not.

  (ii)  HYDROLYSIS OF CCl4 AND SiCl4
             CCl4  + H2O  -->  NO REACTION
             SiCl4 + 4 H2O --> Si(OH)4 + 4 HCl
        Hydrolysis needs the lone pair of the oxygen of water to attack
        the central atom, so that atom must have a vacant orbital.
        Carbon has NO vacant d orbital and cannot expand its octet, and
        is also screened by four large chlorine atoms - so CCl4 is not
        hydrolysed. Silicon HAS vacant 3d orbitals, accepts the lone
        pair and expands its octet - so SiCl4 is hydrolysed readily.

  (iii) CO2 VERSUS SiO2
        Carbon is small, so its 2p orbital overlaps sideways well with
        oxygen's 2p and it forms p(pi)-p(pi) DOUBLE bonds. Its valency
        is satisfied inside one small molecule, O=C=O, and those
        molecules are held only by weak van der Waals forces - so CO2
        is a GAS.
        Silicon is large with diffuse 3p orbitals, so pi overlap is
        poor. It instead forms four single sigma bonds to four oxygens,
        each oxygen bridging to another silicon, giving a GIANT 3-D
        NETWORK - so SiO2 is a hard, high-melting SOLID.

  (iv)  PbO2 AS AN OXIDISING AGENT
        Because of the inert pair effect, +2 is the stable state for
        lead and +4 is unstable. In PbO2 lead is in the +4 state, so it
        readily accepts two electrons and drops to Pb(II) - that is,
        it OXIDISES the other substance:
             PbO2 + 4 HCl --> PbCl2 + Cl2 + 2 H2O
        By the same reasoning SnCl2, in which tin is in the unstable +2
        state, is a good REDUCING agent, and PbI4 does not exist at all
        because Pb4+ would immediately oxidise I- to I2.
```
