# PATTERN 1 — Configuration, position and occurrence

### Q1
The general electronic configuration of group 14 elements is

(a) ns2 np1 &nbsp; (b) ns2 np2 &nbsp; (c) ns2 np3 &nbsp; (d) ns1 np3

**ANSWER: (b)** — `ns2 np2`. Group 13 is ns2 np1, group 15 is ns2 np3.

---

### Q2
The second most abundant element in the earth's crust is

(a) carbon &nbsp; (b) aluminium &nbsp; (c) silicon &nbsp; (d) iron

**ANSWER: (c)** — Silicon, **27.7 % by mass**. Oxygen is first (about 46 %),
aluminium is third.

---

### Q3
The correct classification of group 14 elements is

(a) all non-metals
(b) C non-metal; Si, Ge metalloids; Sn, Pb metals
(c) C, Si non-metals; Ge, Sn metalloids; Pb metal
(d) all metalloids

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
      C    Si    Ge    Sn    Pb
    non-  metal- metal- metal metal
    metal loid   loid
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Metallic character always **increases down** a group. In
> group 14 the change happens exactly in the middle: the top one is a
> non-metal, the middle two are metalloids, the bottom two are metals.

---

### Q4
The chief ore of tin is

(a) galena &nbsp; (b) cassiterite &nbsp; (c) argyrodite &nbsp; (d) magnetite

**ANSWER: (b)** — Cassiterite (tin stone), `SnO2`. Galena `PbS` is the lead
ore; argyrodite is a germanium mineral.

---

### Q5
Which isotope of carbon is used for radiocarbon dating?

(a) 12C &nbsp; (b) 13C &nbsp; (c) 14C &nbsp; (d) 11C

**ANSWER: (c)** — `14C`, radioactive, half-life about 5770 years.

# PATTERN 2 — Trends: radius, ionisation enthalpy, electronegativity

### Q6
The correct order of first ionisation enthalpy is

(a) C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb
(b) C > Si > Ge > Pb > Sn
(c) Pb > C > Si > Ge > Sn
(d) C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   C 1086 > Si 786 > Ge 761 > Pb 715 > Sn 708  kJ/mol

   The ONLY trick is that Pb is slightly HIGHER than Sn, because the
   4f14 and 5d10 electrons of lead shield the nucleus very poorly.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Whenever an option shows the plain order C > Si > Ge > Sn >
> Pb for ionisation enthalpy, look for the option where **Pb and Sn are
> swapped** — that one is usually correct.

---

### Q7
The increase in atomic radius from Si to Ge is very small because of

(a) inert pair effect &nbsp; (b) d-block contraction
(c) lanthanoid contraction &nbsp; (d) high electronegativity

**ANSWER: (b)** — Ten 3d electrons come in between Si and Ge and shield
poorly, so the expected size increase is largely cancelled. (Between Sn and
Pb it is the **lanthanoid** contraction, from the 4f14 electrons.)

---

### Q8
Which element of group 14 has the highest electronegativity?

(a) C &nbsp; (b) Si &nbsp; (c) Sn &nbsp; (d) Pb

**ANSWER: (a)** — Carbon, 2.5. After silicon (1.8) the values are almost
constant.

---

### Q9
The correct order of melting points is

(a) C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb
(b) C > Si > Ge > Pb > Sn
(c) Pb > Sn > Ge > Si > C
(d) Si > C > Ge > Sn > Pb

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   C 4373 > Si 1693 > Ge 1218 > Pb 600 > Sn 505  K

   Again the Pb / Sn pair is swapped, because their crystal structures
   are different.
```

---

### Q10
Which of the following is a semiconductor?

(a) diamond &nbsp; (b) lead &nbsp; (c) germanium &nbsp; (d) tin

**ANSWER: (c)** — Si and Ge are semiconductors. Diamond is an insulator;
Sn and Pb are metallic conductors.

# PATTERN 3 — Oxidation states and the inert pair effect

### Q11
The stability of the +2 oxidation state in group 14

(a) increases down the group
(b) decreases down the group
(c) remains constant
(d) first increases then decreases

**ANSWER: (a)** — because of the **inert pair effect**. The +4 state does the
opposite.

---

### Q12
The inert pair effect is due to

(a) small atomic size
(b) poor shielding of the nuclear charge by d and f electrons
(c) high electronegativity
(d) high ionisation enthalpy of the p electrons

**ANSWER: (b)** — the ns2 pair is gripped tightly because the intervening d
and f electrons shield very poorly, so it will not take part in bonding.

---

### Q13
Which of the following is the strongest oxidising agent?

(a) `SnCl2` &nbsp; (b) `PbO2` &nbsp; (c) `GeCl2` &nbsp; (d) `SiCl4`

**ANSWER: (b)** — In `PbO2` lead is in the unstable +4 state and readily
drops to the stable Pb(II), taking electrons from something else.
`PbO2 + 4HCl -> PbCl2 + Cl2 + 2H2O`

---

### Q14
`SnCl2` acts as a reducing agent because

(a) Sn is a metal
(b) Sn(II) is unstable and changes to the stable Sn(IV)
(c) chlorine is oxidising
(d) SnCl2 is ionic

**ANSWER: (b)** — For tin the stable state is still +4, so Sn(II) gives up
two electrons.

---

### Q15
Which compound does **not** exist?

(a) `PbF4` &nbsp; (b) `PbCl4` &nbsp; (c) `PbI4` &nbsp; (d) `PbCl2`

**ANSWER: (c)** — `PbI4` does not exist because Pb4+ is strongly oxidising
and I- is strongly reducing:  `Pb4+ + 2I- -> Pb2+ + I2`. `PbBr4` also does
not exist.

> **SHORTCUT — "the big oxidiser eats the big reducer":** any compound
> pairing a **strongly oxidising cation** (Pb4+, Tl3+, Bi5+, Cu2+) with a
> **strongly reducing anion** (I-, Br-) is unstable or non-existent. That is
> also why `CuI2` does not exist.

---

### Q16
The correct order of stability of the tetrahalides is

(a) `CX4 < SiX4 < GeX4 < SnX4 < PbX4`
(b) `CX4 > SiX4 > GeX4 > SnX4 > PbX4`
(c) `PbX4 > SnX4 > CX4`
(d) all are equally stable

**ANSWER: (b)** — stability of the +4 state falls down the group (inert pair
effect).

---

# PATTERN 4 — Catenation and anomalous carbon

### Q17
The correct order of catenation is

(a) `C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb`
(b) `Pb > Sn > Ge > Si > C`
(c) `Si > C > Ge > Sn`
(d) `C > Ge > Si > Sn`

**ANSWER: (a)** — it follows the M-M bond enthalpy:
`C-C 348 > Si-Si 297 > Ge-Ge 260 > Sn-Sn 240 kJ/mol`. Lead shows
essentially none.

---

### Q18
Carbon shows a maximum covalency of four because

(a) it is a non-metal
(b) it has no d orbitals in its valence shell
(c) it is small
(d) it has a high electronegativity

**ANSWER: (b)** — the valence shell of carbon is n = 2, which has only 2s
and 2p orbitals. There is no 2d subshell, so the octet cannot expand.

---

### Q19
Which of the following ions does **not** exist?

(a) `[SiF6]2-` &nbsp; (b) `[CF6]2-` &nbsp; (c) `[GeCl6]2-` &nbsp; (d) `[SnCl6]2-`

**ANSWER: (b)** — carbon has no d orbitals, so it cannot reach a covalency of
six. All the others can.

---

### Q20
The tendency of carbon to form p(pi)-p(pi) multiple bonds is due to

(a) its high ionisation enthalpy
(b) its small size, allowing effective sideways overlap of 2p orbitals
(c) the presence of d orbitals
(d) its low electronegativity

**ANSWER: (b)** — silicon's 3p orbitals are too large and diffuse to overlap
sideways properly, which is why Si=Si and Si=O bonds are rare.

---

# PATTERN 5 — Allotropes of carbon

### Q21
The hybridisation of carbon in diamond and graphite respectively is

(a) sp2 and sp3 &nbsp; (b) sp3 and sp2 &nbsp; (c) sp3 and sp3 &nbsp; (d) sp and sp2

**ANSWER: (b)** — **diamond sp3** (3 dimensions), **graphite sp2**
(2-dimensional sheets).

> **SHORTCUT:** "sp**3** = **3**-dimensional = diamond. sp**2** =
> **2**-dimensional layers = graphite."

---

### Q22
Graphite is a good conductor of electricity because

(a) it has a giant covalent structure
(b) each carbon has one delocalised electron in an unhybridised p orbital
(c) its layers are far apart
(d) it contains metal impurities

**ANSWER: (b)** — sp2 carbon uses only three electrons for sigma bonds; the
fourth is delocalised over the layer and is mobile.

---

### Q23
Graphite is soft and used as a lubricant because

(a) the C-C bonds are weak
(b) the layers are held by weak van der Waals forces and slide over each other
(c) it has free electrons
(d) it has a low melting point

**ANSWER: (b)** — the bonds **inside** a layer are very strong; it is the
340 pm gap **between** the layers that makes it slippery.

---

### Q24
The C-C bond lengths in diamond and in a graphite layer are respectively

(a) 141.5 pm and 154 pm &nbsp; (b) 154 pm and 141.5 pm
(c) 154 pm and 340 pm &nbsp; (d) 120 pm and 154 pm

**ANSWER: (b)** — diamond 154 pm (pure single bond); graphite 141.5 pm inside
the layer (partial double-bond character). 340 pm is the **interlayer**
distance in graphite.

---

### Q25
The number of six-membered and five-membered rings in C60 is

(a) 12 and 20 &nbsp; (b) 20 and 12 &nbsp; (c) 30 and 12 &nbsp; (d) 20 and 20

**ANSWER: (b)** — **20 hexagons and 12 pentagons**, 32 faces, 60 carbon atoms.

> **SHORTCUT:** A football always has **12 black pentagons**. So pentagons =
> 12, and the rest (20) are hexagons. Never the other way round.

---

### Q26
Fullerene is prepared by

(a) heating coal in the absence of air
(b) heating graphite in an electric arc in an atmosphere of helium or argon
(c) burning diamond in oxygen
(d) heating charcoal with steam

**ANSWER: (b)** — the graphite vaporises and condenses to soot containing
mainly C60 with some C70.

---

### Q27
Which allotrope of carbon is considered the purest?

(a) diamond &nbsp; (b) graphite &nbsp; (c) fullerene &nbsp; (d) charcoal

**ANSWER: (c)** — C60 is a closed cage with **no dangling bonds**, so no
impurity atoms can attach. Diamond and graphite have edges with unsatisfied
bonds.

---

### Q28
The thermodynamically most stable allotrope of carbon is

(a) diamond &nbsp; (b) graphite &nbsp; (c) fullerene &nbsp; (d) coke

**ANSWER: (b)** — graphite is the reference form; its standard enthalpy of
formation is taken as zero. Diamond changes to graphite on heating to
about 1773 K in the absence of air.

---

### Q29
Which of the following is soluble in benzene, giving a magenta solution?

(a) diamond &nbsp; (b) graphite &nbsp; (c) C60 &nbsp; (d) silicon carbide

**ANSWER: (c)** — C60 is a **molecular** solid, so it dissolves in organic
solvents. Diamond and graphite are giant structures and dissolve in nothing.

---

### Q30
Which statement about diamond is **incorrect**?

(a) It is sp3 hybridised
(b) It is the hardest natural substance
(c) It is a good conductor of electricity
(d) It is a good conductor of heat

**ANSWER: (c)** — diamond is an **electrical insulator** (no free electrons)
but an excellent **thermal** conductor. Do not confuse the two.

# PATTERN 6 — Reactivity, halides and hydrolysis

### Q31
`CCl4` is not hydrolysed by water while `SiCl4` is, because

(a) the C-Cl bond is stronger than the Si-Cl bond
(b) carbon has no vacant d orbitals to accept the lone pair of water
(c) CCl4 is ionic
(d) silicon is a metalloid

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Hydrolysis needs the lone pair of water's oxygen to attack the
   central atom, so a VACANT ORBITAL is required.
   Carbon (n = 2) has NO d orbitals -> cannot expand its octet -> no
   reaction. Silicon (n = 3) HAS vacant 3d orbitals -> hydrolysed:
        SiCl4 + 4 H2O --> Si(OH)4 + 4 HCl
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Option (a) is the classic trap. Si-Cl is actually the
> **stronger** bond. The answer is always **"no vacant d orbitals"**.

---

### Q32
`SiCl4` on hydrolysis gives

(a) `SiO2 + HCl` &nbsp; (b) `Si(OH)4 + HCl` &nbsp; (c) `SiH4 + Cl2` &nbsp; (d) no reaction

**ANSWER: (b)** — `SiCl4 + 4H2O -> Si(OH)4 + 4HCl`. The silicic acid then
loses water to give hydrated silica.

---

### Q33
Water gas is a mixture of

(a) `CO + N2` &nbsp; (b) `CO2 + H2` &nbsp; (c) `CO + H2` &nbsp; (d) `CH4 + H2O`

**ANSWER: (c)** — `C + H2O --(1273 K)--> CO + H2`. Producer gas is
**CO + N2**.

> **SHORTCUT:** "**WATER** gas comes from steam (H2O), so it contains
> **H**2. **PRODUCER** gas comes from air, so it contains **N**2."

---

### Q34
Which oxide is neutral?

(a) `CO2` &nbsp; (b) `CO` &nbsp; (c) `SiO2` &nbsp; (d) `SnO2`

**ANSWER: (b)** — CO is a **neutral** oxide. CO2 and SiO2 are acidic; SnO2
is amphoteric.

---

### Q35
The correct order of acidic character of the oxides is

(a) `CO2 > SiO2 > GeO2 > SnO2 > PbO2`
(b) `PbO2 > SnO2 > GeO2 > SiO2 > CO2`
(c) `SiO2 > CO2 > SnO2`
(d) all are equally acidic

**ANSWER: (a)** — acidic character falls down the group as metallic character
rises: acidic (CO2, SiO2) -> amphoteric (SnO2, PbO2).

---

### Q36
Which metal is **not** attacked by water because a protective oxide layer
forms on its surface?

(a) Sn &nbsp; (b) Pb &nbsp; (c) C &nbsp; (d) Si

**ANSWER: (b)** — a thin layer of PbO (or basic lead carbonate in air)
protects lead. Tin **does** decompose steam: `Sn + 2H2O -> SnO2 + 2H2`.

---

### Q37
The correct order of stability of the hydrides is

(a) `CH4 < SiH4 < GeH4 < SnH4` &nbsp; (b) `CH4 > SiH4 > GeH4 > SnH4`
(c) `SiH4 > CH4 > GeH4` &nbsp; (d) all equally stable

**ANSWER: (b)** — the M-H bond gets longer and weaker down the group
(C-H 414, Si-H 318, Ge-H 285, Sn-H 253 kJ/mol). **Reducing power** goes the
opposite way.

---

# PATTERN 7 — Carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide

### Q38
Carbon monoxide is poisonous because it

(a) reacts with oxygen in the lungs
(b) forms carboxyhaemoglobin, which is about 300 times more stable than
oxyhaemoglobin
(c) dissolves in blood plasma
(d) is acidic

**ANSWER: (b)** — `Hb + CO -> HbCO`. The blood can then no longer carry
oxygen, and the victim dies of asphyxiation.

---

### Q39
Carbon monoxide is prepared in the laboratory by

(a) heating limestone
(b) dehydrating formic acid with concentrated H2SO4
(c) burning carbon in excess oxygen
(d) reacting marble with dilute HCl

**ANSWER: (b)** — `HCOOH --(conc. H2SO4, 373 K)--> CO + H2O`. Options (a) and
(d) give **CO2**.

---

### Q40
The hybridisation of carbon and the shape of the CO2 molecule are

(a) sp2, bent &nbsp; (b) sp3, tetrahedral &nbsp; (c) sp, linear &nbsp; (d) sp2, trigonal planar

**ANSWER: (c)** — `O=C=O`, **sp hybridised, linear, 180 degrees**, and
non-polar because the two bond dipoles cancel.

---

### Q41
The C-O bond length in CO2 (115 pm) is shorter than a normal C=O bond because
of

(a) hydrogen bonding &nbsp; (b) resonance &nbsp; (c) the inert pair effect &nbsp; (d) sp3 hybridisation

**ANSWER: (b)** — resonance gives the bond partial triple-bond character:
`O=C=O <-> (-)O-C(+)=O <-> O=C(+)-O(-)`

---

### Q42
Dry ice is

(a) solid water &nbsp; (b) solid CO2 &nbsp; (c) solid SO2 &nbsp; (d) a mixture of ice and salt

**ANSWER: (b)** — solid CO2, which **sublimes** at 195 K and leaves no
liquid, hence "dry".

---

### Q43
A burning magnesium ribbon continues to burn in carbon dioxide because

(a) CO2 supports combustion &nbsp; (b) `2Mg + CO2 -> 2MgO + C`
(c) Mg reacts with nitrogen &nbsp; (d) CO2 decomposes into O2 spontaneously

**ANSWER: (b)** — magnesium is reactive enough to take the oxygen from CO2.
This is why a CO2 extinguisher must never be used on burning magnesium.

---

### Q44
When excess CO2 is passed through lime water, the milkiness disappears
because

(a) CaCO3 decomposes
(b) soluble `Ca(HCO3)2` is formed
(c) `Ca(OH)2` is regenerated
(d) CO2 escapes

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   CO2 + Ca(OH)2 --> CaCO3 (milky) + H2O
   CaCO3 + H2O + CO2 (excess) --> Ca(HCO3)2 (soluble, clear)
```

---

### Q45
Which of the following is **not** a greenhouse gas?

(a) CO2 &nbsp; (b) CH4 &nbsp; (c) N2 &nbsp; (d) N2O

**ANSWER: (c)** — nitrogen is not a greenhouse gas. CO2, CH4, N2O, water
vapour, CFCs and ozone are.

---

### Q46
CO2 causes global warming because it

(a) reflects sunlight back into space
(b) absorbs the long-wavelength infrared radiation re-emitted by the earth
(c) destroys the ozone layer
(d) reacts with water to form acid rain

**ANSWER: (b)** — short-wave sunlight passes through, but the long-wave
infrared radiated back by the earth is absorbed and re-emitted downward,
trapping heat.

# PATTERN 8 — Silicon compounds: silica, silicones, silicates, zeolites

### Q47
CO2 is a gas while SiO2 is a high-melting solid because

(a) Si is a metal
(b) carbon forms p(pi)-p(pi) double bonds with oxygen while silicon cannot
(c) SiO2 is ionic
(d) CO2 is polar

**ANSWER: (b)** — carbon's small size allows sideways 2p-2p overlap, giving
discrete `O=C=O` molecules; silicon must instead build a giant 3-D network of
single Si-O bonds.

---

### Q48
In SiO2, each silicon atom is bonded to

(a) 2 oxygen atoms in a linear arrangement
(b) 4 oxygen atoms tetrahedrally, each oxygen being shared between two silicons
(c) 6 oxygen atoms octahedrally
(d) 3 oxygen atoms in a plane

**ANSWER: (b)** — 4 oxygens tetrahedrally, each shared with another
tetrahedron, so each Si owns 4 x 1/2 = 2 oxygens, giving the formula SiO2.

---

### Q49
Hydrofluoric acid is not stored in glass bottles because

(a) it is very corrosive to all metals
(b) `SiO2 + 4HF -> SiF4 + 2H2O`
(c) it decomposes in the light
(d) it is volatile

**ANSWER: (b)** — HF attacks the silica in the glass. It is stored in wax or
plastic containers.

---

### Q50
Silicones are polymers containing the repeating unit

(a) `-Si-Si-Si-` &nbsp; (b) `-O-SiR2-` &nbsp; (c) `-Si-C-Si-C-` &nbsp; (d) `-SiO4-`

**ANSWER: (b)** — the backbone is `-Si-O-Si-O-` with organic groups on
silicon.

> **SHORTCUT:** Silicon does not catenate well, so a silicone backbone can
> **never** be a chain of Si-Si bonds. Oxygen atoms always sit between them.

---

### Q51
Hydrolysis of `R3SiCl` followed by condensation gives

(a) a linear polymer &nbsp; (b) a cross-linked polymer
(c) a chain terminator that stops polymer growth &nbsp; (d) a cyclic trimer only

**ANSWER: (c)** — `R3SiCl` produces only one -OH group, so it can join at one
end only and caps the chain. `R2SiCl2` gives linear chains and `RSiCl3` gives
cross-linked resins.

---

### Q52
Silicones are used for waterproofing fabrics because they are

(a) ionic &nbsp; (b) hydrophobic (water repellent) &nbsp; (c) volatile &nbsp; (d) acidic

**ANSWER: (b)** — the outward-facing alkyl (methyl) groups repel water.

---

### Q53
The basic structural unit of all silicates is

(a) `SiO2` &nbsp; (b) `SiO3 2-` &nbsp; (c) `SiO4 4-` tetrahedron &nbsp; (d) `Si2O7 6-`

**ANSWER: (c)** — one sp3 silicon surrounded tetrahedrally by four oxygens,
each carrying one negative charge.

---

### Q54
Asbestos and mica are respectively

(a) sheet and chain silicates &nbsp; (b) double-chain and sheet silicates
(c) ortho and pyro silicates &nbsp; (d) cyclic and 3-D silicates

**ANSWER: (b)** — **asbestos = double chain (amphibole)**;
**mica and talc = sheet (layer) silicates**, which is why they flake.

---

### Q55
When all four corners of every `SiO4` tetrahedron are shared, the resulting
structure is

(a) `SiO3 2-` &nbsp; (b) `Si2O5 2-` &nbsp; (c) neutral `SiO2` &nbsp; (d) `Si2O7 6-`

**ANSWER: (c)** — every oxygen then bridges two silicons, the charge
disappears and you get quartz, `SiO2`.

---

### Q56
Zeolites are

(a) pure silicates &nbsp; (b) three-dimensional aluminosilicates
(c) organosilicon polymers &nbsp; (d) carbides of silicon

**ANSWER: (b)** — some Si(IV) of a 3-D silicate framework is replaced by
Al(III), leaving a negative charge balanced by loosely held cations.

---

### Q57
ZSM-5, a zeolite, is used to

(a) soften hard water &nbsp; (b) convert alcohols directly into gasoline
(c) dry gases &nbsp; (d) manufacture glass

**ANSWER: (b)** — it is the classic example of **shape-selective catalysis**.

---

### Q58
Zeolites soften hard water because they act as

(a) oxidising agents &nbsp; (b) ion exchangers &nbsp; (c) reducing agents &nbsp; (d) dehydrating agents

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Na2Z + Ca2+ --> CaZ + 2 Na+       (Ca2+ and Mg2+ are removed)
   Regenerated with brine:  CaZ + 2 NaCl --> Na2Z + CaCl2
```

---

### Q59
Carborundum is

(a) `SiO2` &nbsp; (b) `SiC` &nbsp; (c) `CaC2` &nbsp; (d) `Al4C3`

**ANSWER: (b)** — silicon carbide, an extremely hard abrasive, made by
`SiO2 + 3C --(2000 K)--> SiC + 2CO`.

---

### Q60
Quartz is used in watches and oscillators because it is

(a) transparent &nbsp; (b) piezoelectric &nbsp; (c) a semiconductor &nbsp; (d) magnetic

**ANSWER: (b)** — a mechanical stress on a quartz crystal produces a voltage
and vice versa, giving a very steady vibration frequency.

# PATTERN 9 — Assertion-and-reason style (JEE Main favourite)

### Q61
**Assertion (A):** `PbCl4` is less stable than `PbCl2`.
**Reason (R):** Because of the inert pair effect, the +2 oxidation state is
more stable than +4 for lead.

(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
(b) Both A and R are true but R is not the correct explanation of A
(c) A is true, R is false
(d) A is false, R is true

**ANSWER: (a)** — this is exactly the inert pair effect.
`PbCl4 --(heat)--> PbCl2 + Cl2`

---

### Q62
**Assertion (A):** Graphite is a better lubricant than diamond.
**Reason (R):** The C-C bonds in graphite are weaker than those in diamond.

(a) Both A and R are true and R explains A
(b) Both A and R are true but R does not explain A
(c) A is true, R is false
(d) A is false, R is true

**ANSWER: (c)** — A is true, but R is **false**: the C-C bonds inside a
graphite layer (141.5 pm) are actually **stronger and shorter** than in
diamond. The lubricating property comes from the **weak van der Waals forces
between** the layers.

---

### Q63
**Assertion (A):** `CCl4` does not undergo hydrolysis.
**Reason (R):** Carbon does not have vacant d orbitals.

(a) Both A and R are true and R explains A
(b) Both A and R are true but R does not explain A
(c) A is true, R is false
(d) A is false, R is true

**ANSWER: (a)** — straight from the standard answer.

---

### Q64
**Assertion (A):** Silicon forms `SiO2` as a giant solid while carbon forms
`CO2` as a gas.
**Reason (R):** Silicon cannot form p(pi)-p(pi) multiple bonds with oxygen.

(a) Both A and R are true and R explains A
(b) Both A and R are true but R does not explain A
(c) A is true, R is false
(d) A is false, R is true

# PATTERN 10 — Odd-one-out and "which is incorrect"

### Q65
Which of the following statements is **incorrect**?

(a) Silicon is the second most abundant element in the earth's crust
(b) CO is a neutral oxide
(c) Lead shows the maximum tendency for catenation in group 14
(d) Fullerene dissolves in benzene

**ANSWER: (c)** — **carbon** shows the maximum catenation; lead shows
practically none.

### Q66
Which pair is **wrongly** matched?

(a) Cassiterite — `SnO2` &nbsp; (b) Galena — `PbS`
(c) Carborundum — `CaC2` &nbsp; (d) Dry ice — solid `CO2`

**ANSWER: (c)** — Carborundum is `SiC`. `CaC2` is calcium carbide, which
gives acetylene with water.

### Q67
Which of the following does **not** show allotropy?

(a) carbon &nbsp; (b) silicon &nbsp; (c) tin &nbsp; (d) sodium

**ANSWER: (d)** — carbon (diamond, graphite, fullerene), silicon and tin
(grey, white and rhombic tin) all show allotropy; sodium does not.

### Q68
Which of these is a use of graphite?

(a) as a gemstone &nbsp; (b) as a moderator in nuclear reactors
(c) as a drying agent &nbsp; (d) in solar cells

**ANSWER: (b)** — graphite slows down neutrons. Diamond is the gemstone,
silica gel is the drying agent, silicon is used in solar cells.
