# p-Block Group 14 (Carbon Family) — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

This chapter has **no numericals at all**. Every pattern is a
*trend + reason*, a *structure + reason*, or a *reaction + condition*.
Once you can name the pattern, you already know the first line of the answer.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — CONFIGURATION, MEMBERS AND CLASSIFICATION           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the general electronic configuration of group 14",
            "Classify group 14 elements as metals, non-metals and
            metalloids."
  METHOD    1. General configuration = ns2 np2 .
            2. List: C , Si , Ge , Sn , Pb .
            3. Classify:
                  C  = NON-METAL
                  Si , Ge = METALLOIDS
                  Sn , Pb = METALS
            4. If room: give the individual configurations, e.g.
                  C  [He] 2s2 2p2 , Pb [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p2 .
  TRAP      Do NOT write ns2 np1 (that is group 13) or ns2 np3
            (group 15). And Ge is a METALLOID, not a metal.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — OCCURRENCE AND ABUNDANCE                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which is the most abundant element of group 14?",
            "Name the chief ore of tin / lead."
  METHOD    1. SILICON is 27.7 % of the earth's crust - the SECOND most
               abundant element after oxygen.
            2. Carbon is only 0.02 % but occurs BOTH free (diamond,
               graphite, coal) and combined (carbonates, CO2, petroleum).
            3. Ores:  cassiterite / tin stone SnO2  (tin)
                      galena PbS                    (lead)
                      argyrodite                    (germanium)
            4. Si and Ge never occur free.
  TRAP      "Second most abundant in the EARTH'S CRUST" - not in the
            universe, not in the atmosphere. Read the wording.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — TREND + REASON DOWN THE GROUP                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Discuss the variation of ... down group 14",
            "How do atomic radii / ionisation enthalpy /
            electronegativity / melting point vary?"
  METHOD    Answer in THREE columns every single time:
                 PROPERTY  |  DIRECTION  |  REASON
            Standard reasons to reuse:
                 size UP        -> a new shell each period
                 IE DOWN        -> outer electron farther and shielded
                 EN DOWN        -> C is tiny so it pulls hardest
                 m.p. DOWN      -> giant covalent net gives way to
                                   weaker metallic bonding
                 density UP     -> mass rises faster than volume
                 metallic UP    -> IE falls, electrons lost easily
            ALWAYS add the exceptions (see Pattern 4).
  TRAP      NEVER write only the direction - half the marks are for the
            REASON. One sentence of reason per row is enough.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — THE THREE EXCEPTIONS IN THE TRENDS                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the ionisation enthalpy of Pb higher than that of
            Sn?", "Why is the melting point of Pb higher than Sn?",
            "Why is the size increase from Si to Ge so small?"
  METHOD    1. IE:   Sn 708 , Pb 715 kJ/mol.  REASON = the 4f14 and
                     5d10 electrons of lead SHIELD VERY POORLY, so the
                     valence electrons feel a bigger effective nuclear
                     charge and are held tightly.
            2. m.p.: Sn 505 K , Pb 600 K.  REASON = DIFFERENT CRYSTAL
                     STRUCTURES.
            3. Size: big jump C 77 -> Si 118 , then small jumps.
                     REASON = D-BLOCK CONTRACTION (3d10) and
                     LANTHANOID CONTRACTION (4f14) - poor shielding.
  TRAP      Never say "Pb is bigger so its IE is bigger". Size and IE
            move in OPPOSITE directions. The word you need is SHIELDING.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — OXIDATION STATES AND THE INERT PAIR EFFECT     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the inert pair effect?", "Why does the +2 state
            become more stable down the group?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: the reluctance of the ns2 electron pair to
               take part in bonding, increasing down a p-block group.
            2. CAUSE in four steps:
                  (a) filled d and f subshells come in between
                  (b) d and f orbitals SHIELD POORLY
                  (c) so the ns2 pair feels a large effective nuclear
                      charge and is held tightly
                  (d) promoting an ns electron to np costs too much
                      energy, so only the two p electrons are used
            3. RESULT: +2 stability rises , +4 stability falls:
                  +2 :  C < Si < Ge < Sn < Pb
                  +4 :  C > Si > Ge > Sn > Pb
            4. Give one example: Pb(II) is the stable state of lead.
  TRAP      The cause is POOR SHIELDING BY d AND f ELECTRONS. Do not
            write "the atom is big" and stop.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — OXIDISING / REDUCING CHARACTER OF +2 AND +4    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is PbO2 a strong oxidising agent?",
            "Why is SnCl2 a good reducing agent?"
  METHOD    1. State the inert pair effect in one line.
            2. Identify the oxidation state in the compound.
            3. Apply the rule:
                  UNSTABLE HIGH state (+4 at the bottom) -> it GAINS
                  electrons to drop to +2 -> OXIDISING AGENT.
                  UNSTABLE LOW state (+2 higher up) -> it LOSES
                  electrons to rise to +4 -> REDUCING AGENT.
            4. Write the equation:
                  PbO2 + 4 HCl --> PbCl2 + Cl2 + 2 H2O
                  SnCl2 + 2 HgCl2 --> SnCl4 + Hg2Cl2
  TRAP      Students reverse it. Remember:
            BOTTOM of the group + HIGH state = OXIDISER (PbO2).
            HIGHER up + LOW state = REDUCER (SnCl2).
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — WHY PbI4 AND PbBr4 DO NOT EXIST                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does PbI4 not exist although PbCl4 does?",
            "PbCl4 is less stable than PbCl2. Explain."
  METHOD    1. Pb4+ is a STRONG OXIDISING agent (inert pair effect).
            2. I- and Br- are STRONG REDUCING agents.
            3. They would destroy each other on contact:
                  Pb4+  +  2 I-   -->   Pb2+  +  I2
            4. So PbI4 and PbBr4 cannot exist. PbF4 and PbCl4 DO exist
               because F- and Cl- are hard to oxidise - but even PbCl4
               decomposes on warming:  PbCl4 --> PbCl2 + Cl2 .
  TRAP      Do not say "iodine is too big". The reason is a REDOX
            incompatibility, not a size problem.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF CARBON                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the anomalous behaviour of carbon", "Why does
            carbon differ from the rest of its group?"
  METHOD    Write REASONS first, then CONSEQUENCES.
            FOUR REASONS:
              (i)   very small size (77 pm)
              (ii)  high electronegativity (2.5)
              (iii) high ionisation enthalpy (1086 kJ/mol)
              (iv)  ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS (valence shell n = 2)
            CONSEQUENCES (give at least four):
               1. maximum covalency 4 ; [CF6]2- does not exist
               2. forms p(pi)-p(pi) multiple bonds (C=C , C=O)
               3. CO2 is a gas but SiO2 is a giant solid
               4. maximum catenation -> organic chemistry
               5. CCl4 is not hydrolysed but SiCl4 is
               6. shows allotropy - diamond, graphite, fullerene
  TRAP      Writing only "carbon is small" loses three marks. The
            examiner counts the FOUR reasons and the consequences
            separately.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — CATENATION                                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is catenation?", "Why does catenation decrease down
            group 14?", "Arrange in order of catenation."
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: the self-linking of like atoms by covalent
               bonds into chains, branched chains and rings.
            2. ORDER:  C >> Si > Ge ~ Sn > Pb (Pb: practically none).
            3. REASON with NUMBERS - it follows the M-M bond enthalpy:
                  C-C 348 , Si-Si 297 , Ge-Ge 260 , Sn-Sn 240 kJ/mol.
               Bigger atoms -> poorer orbital overlap over a longer
               distance -> longer, weaker M-M bond -> chain breaks.
            4. CONSEQUENCE: carbon alone gives millions of compounds.
  TRAP      The reason is BOND ENTHALPY / SIZE, not electronegativity.
            Always quote at least two of the numbers.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — MAXIMUM COVALENCY / EXPANDING THE OCTET            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is the maximum covalency of carbon 4?",
            "Why does [SiF6]2- exist but [CF6]2- does not?"
  METHOD    1. Carbon's valence shell is n = 2, which contains ONLY 2s
               and 2p orbitals - there is NO 2d subshell.
            2. So carbon can use at most four orbitals and CANNOT
               EXPAND ITS OCTET. Maximum covalency = 4.
            3. Silicon onwards HAVE VACANT d ORBITALS (3d, 4d, 5d), so
               they can reach a covalency of 6.
            4. Evidence: [SiF6]2- , [GeCl6]2- , [SnCl6]2- all exist ;
               [CF6]2- does not.
  TRAP      Do not answer "because carbon has 4 valence electrons" -
            silicon also has 4 and it reaches covalency 6. The answer
            is the ABSENCE OF d ORBITALS.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF DIAMOND           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the structure of diamond", "Why is diamond the
            hardest substance?", "Why does diamond not conduct?"
  METHOD    1. sp3 hybridised ; four sigma bonds ; TETRAHEDRAL ;
               109.5 degrees ; C-C = 154 pm.
            2. Repeats in THREE DIMENSIONS -> GIANT COVALENT NETWORK,
               one huge molecule. DRAW IT.
            3. HARD because the 3-D network of strong covalent bonds is
               RIGID in every direction.
            4. INSULATOR because ALL FOUR electrons are locked in sigma
               bonds - no free electrons.
            5. Uses: gems, abrasives, drill bits, dies, glass cutting.
  TRAP      "Strong covalent bonds" alone is not enough - graphite's
            in-layer bonds are even stronger. Say THREE-DIMENSIONAL
            RIGID NETWORK.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — STRUCTURE AND PROPERTIES OF GRAPHITE          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is graphite soft / used as a lubricant?",
            "Why does graphite conduct electricity?"
  METHOD    1. sp2 hybridised ; THREE sigma bonds at 120 degrees ;
               flat HEXAGONAL LAYERS ; C-C = 141.5 pm in the layer.
            2. Layers are 340 pm apart, held only by WEAK VAN DER
               WAALS FORCES -> they SLIDE -> SOFT , LUBRICANT.
            3. The FOURTH electron of each carbon is in an unhybridised
               p orbital -> DELOCALISED pi CLOUD -> MOBILE electrons
               -> CONDUCTS along the layers.
            4. Uses: lubricant, electrodes, pencil leads, crucibles,
               nuclear moderator.
            5. Add: graphite is the MOST STABLE allotrope of carbon.
  TRAP      Never say the C-C bonds in graphite are weak - they are
            stronger than diamond's. It is the FORCES BETWEEN THE
            LAYERS that are weak.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — FULLERENES                                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are fullerenes?", "Describe the structure of C60",
            "Why is fullerene the purest allotrope of carbon?"
  METHOD    1. Cage-like hollow molecules Cn ; C60 =
               BUCKMINSTERFULLERENE / bucky ball.
            2. STRUCTURE: 20 SIX-membered rings + 12 FIVE-membered
               rings = 32 faces , football shaped ; all 60 carbons
               EQUIVALENT and sp2 hybridised with 3 sigma bonds ;
               the extra electron is DELOCALISED ;
               bond lengths 143.5 pm and 138.3 pm.
            3. PREPARATION: heat GRAPHITE in an ELECTRIC ARC in
               HELIUM or ARGON -> soot containing C60 and some C70.
            4. PUREST because the closed cage has NO DANGLING BONDS,
               so no impurity can attach.
            5. Properties: molecular solid, soluble in benzene giving
               a magenta solution; semiconductor; K3C60 is a
               superconductor.
  TRAP      20 hexagons and 12 pentagons - NOT the reverse. And it is
            sp2, not sp3.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — DIAMOND vs GRAPHITE COMPARISON TABLE               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare diamond and graphite", "Both are carbon - why are
            their properties so different?"
  METHOD    Draw a TABLE with these rows, in this order:
               hybridisation , geometry , bond angle , C-C length ,
               structure type , free electrons , hardness , density ,
               electrical conduction , appearance , uses.
            Then finish with TWO summary sentences:
               "Diamond is hard because its bonding is 3-DIMENSIONAL."
               "Graphite is soft because its strong bonding is only
                2-DIMENSIONAL and the layers slide."
            Draw BOTH structures if there is room.
  TRAP      A table with no reasons scores poorly. Add the two summary
            sentences and the diagrams.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — REACTIVITY WITH OXYGEN AND THE NATURE OF OXIDES
                                                             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How do group 14 elements react with oxygen?",
            "Discuss the acidic / basic nature of the oxides."
  METHOD    1. Two series of oxides: MO and MO2. Write the equations:
                  C + O2 --> CO2  ;  2C + O2 (limited) --> 2CO
                  Si + O2 --> SiO2 ; 2Pb + O2 --> 2PbO
            2. Nature:  CO NEUTRAL ; CO2 and SiO2 ACIDIC ;
                        GeO2 weakly acidic ; SnO2 , PbO2 AMPHOTERIC ;
                        GeO , SnO , PbO amphoteric.
            3. Two rules:
                  (a) DOWN the group acidic -> amphoteric, because
                      metallic character rises.
                  (b) For the SAME element MO2 is MORE acidic than MO.
            4. Prove amphoterism:
                  PbO + 2 HNO3 --> Pb(NO3)2 + H2O   (as a base)
                  PbO + 2 NaOH --> Na2PbO2 + H2O    (as an acid)
  TRAP      CO is NEUTRAL, not acidic. It does not turn litmus red.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — REACTIVITY WITH WATER / WATER GAS / PRODUCER GAS
                                                             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is water gas?", "What is producer gas?",
            "How do group 14 elements react with water?"
  METHOD    1. WATER GAS = CO + H2 :
                  C + H2O --(steam over red hot coke, 1273 K)--> CO + H2
               (also called SYNTHESIS GAS / SYN GAS)
            2. PRODUCER GAS = CO + N2 :
                  2C + O2 + 4N2 --(air over red hot coke)--> 2CO + 4N2
            3. Element by element:
                  Si , Ge , Pb  - NOT attacked by water
                  (Pb: a protective PbO layer forms)
                  Sn + 2 H2O --(steam)--> SnO2 + 2 H2
                  Si + 2NaOH + H2O --> Na2SiO3 + 2 H2 (alkali does work)
  TRAP      Water gas contains HYDROGEN (from steam);
            producer gas contains NITROGEN (from air). Do not swap them.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — THE TETRAHALIDES                              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Discuss the tetrahalides of group 14",
            "Why is the stability of MX4 in the order CX4 > ... > PbX4?"
  METHOD    1. All MX4 are COVALENT, TETRAHEDRAL, sp3.
            2. Stability falls down the group (inert pair effect):
                  CX4 > SiX4 > GeX4 > SnX4 > PbX4
            3. PbCl4 decomposes on warming ; PbBr4 and PbI4 do not
               exist (Pattern 7).
            4. All are hydrolysed EXCEPT CX4 (Pattern 18).
  TRAP      Do not call them ionic. They are covalent molecular halides.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — *** CCl4 vs SiCl4 HYDROLYSIS ***                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is CCl4 not hydrolysed by water while SiCl4 is?",
            "Why does SiCl4 fume in moist air but CCl4 does not?"
            THE SINGLE MOST-ASKED QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER.
  METHOD    1. Write BOTH facts:
                  CCl4 + H2O --> NO REACTION
                  SiCl4 + 4 H2O --> Si(OH)4 + 4 HCl
            2. State the requirement: hydrolysis begins when the LONE
               PAIR of the oxygen of water ATTACKS the central atom, so
               that atom must have a VACANT ORBITAL to accept it,
               i.e. it must be able to EXPAND ITS OCTET.
            3. CARBON: period 2, valence shell has only 2s and 2p, NO
               d ORBITALS ; all four orbitals used in CCl4 ; cannot
               expand the octet ; also small and screened by four large
               Cl atoms.  -> NOT hydrolysed.
            4. SILICON: period 3, HAS VACANT 3d ORBITALS ; accepts the
               lone pair, expands its octet, the Si-Cl bonds break.
               -> readily hydrolysed.
            5. Add the extra mark: the same reason explains why
               [SiF6]2- exists but [CF6]2- does not.
  TRAP      NEVER write "the C-Cl bond is stronger than Si-Cl" - it is
            false. The answer is always "carbon has no vacant
            d orbitals and cannot expand its octet".
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — HYDRIDES OF GROUP 14                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange the hydrides in order of stability",
            "Why is CH4 more stable than SnH4?"
  METHOD    1. All are MH4 , covalent, TETRAHEDRAL, sp3.
            2. STABILITY:  CH4 > SiH4 > GeH4 > SnH4 > PbH4 .
            3. REASON: the M-H bond gets longer and weaker as the atom
               grows (C-H 414 , Si-H 318 , Ge-H 285 , Sn-H 253 kJ/mol).
            4. REDUCING POWER goes the OPPOSITE way (increases down).
            5. Carbon forms countless hydrides (organic chemistry),
               silicon only a few - this is CATENATION again.
  TRAP      Stability DOWN, reducing power UP. Do not put both in the
            same direction.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — CARBON MONOXIDE: PREPARATION AND PROPERTIES        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is CO prepared?", "Give the properties and uses of CO."
  METHOD    1. LAB:  HCOOH --(conc. H2SO4 , 373 K)--> CO + H2O
                     (conc. H2SO4 acts as a DEHYDRATING agent)
            2. COMMERCIAL: incomplete combustion ; water gas ;
               producer gas (write all three equations).
            3. STRUCTURE:  : C === O :  - one sigma, one pi and one
               COORDINATE bond ; bond length 112.8 pm.
            4. PROPERTIES: colourless, odourless, INSOLUBLE, NEUTRAL
               oxide, burns with a BLUE flame, POWERFUL REDUCING AGENT
               (Fe2O3 + 3CO --> 2Fe + 3CO2), forms METAL CARBONYLS
               (Ni + 4CO --> Ni(CO)4), POISONOUS.
            5. USES: metallurgy, fuel, Mond process, methanol synthesis.
  TRAP      CO is NEUTRAL and INSOLUBLE. Do not say it dissolves to
            give an acid - that is CO2.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — WHY CO IS POISONOUS                                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is carbon monoxide poisonous?", "Why must a charcoal
            fire not be burnt in a closed room?"
  METHOD    1. Hb + O2 --> HbO2 (OXYHAEMOGLOBIN) is how blood carries
               oxygen.
            2. Hb + CO --> HbCO (CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN) at the same site.
            3. HbCO IS ABOUT 300 TIMES MORE STABLE than HbO2, so the
               binding is effectively irreversible.
            4. Haemoglobin is blocked -> the blood cannot carry oxygen
               -> death by ASPHYXIATION. CO is odourless, so there is
               no warning.
  TRAP      The two words that earn the marks are CARBOXYHAEMOGLOBIN
            and "300 times more stable". Write both.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — CARBON DIOXIDE: STRUCTURE                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the structure of CO2", "Why is the C-O bond in
            CO2 shorter than a normal C=O bond?"
  METHOD    1. Draw  O === C === O .
            2. LINEAR ; carbon is sp HYBRIDISED ; bond angle 180 deg.
            3. NON-POLAR overall - the two equal bond dipoles cancel.
            4. C-O = 115 pm, SHORTER than a normal C=O, because of
               RESONANCE:
                  O=C=O <--> (-)O-C(+)=O <--> O=C(+)-O(-)
               giving partial triple-bond character.
  TRAP      sp hybridisation, NOT sp2. And the molecule is non-polar
            even though each individual bond is polar.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — CO2: PREPARATION, PROPERTIES AND USES              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is CO2 prepared?", "Give the properties and uses of
            carbon dioxide."
  METHOD    1. LAB: CaCO3 + 2HCl --> CaCl2 + H2O + CO2
               INDUSTRY: CaCO3 --(1000-1200 K)--> CaO + CO2
               FERMENTATION: C6H12O6 --(zymase)--> 2C2H5OH + 2CO2
            2. ACIDIC oxide: CO2 + H2O <--> H2CO3 (weak DIBASIC acid).
            3. THE LIME WATER TEST - write BOTH halves:
                  CO2 + Ca(OH)2 --> CaCO3 (MILKY) + H2O
                  CaCO3 + H2O + CO2 (excess) --> Ca(HCO3)2 (CLEAR)
            4. DRY ICE = solid CO2 , SUBLIMES at 195 K.
            5. Special: 2 Mg + CO2 --> 2 MgO + C (so it cannot put out
               burning magnesium).
            6. USES: fire extinguisher, dry ice refrigerant, aerated
               drinks, urea manufacture, Solvay process, photosynthesis.
  TRAP      The complete lime water test is milky THEN clear. Writing
            only the first half loses a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — GREENHOUSE EFFECT AND GLOBAL WARMING          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the greenhouse effect?", "How does CO2 cause
            global warming?"
  METHOD    Four steps, in this order:
            1. SHORT-WAVE solar radiation passes freely through the CO2
               and reaches the earth.
            2. The warmed earth re-radiates it as LONG-WAVELENGTH
               INFRARED radiation.
            3. CO2 ABSORBS that infrared radiation and re-emits part of
               it back downwards.
            4. Heat is TRAPPED near the surface -> the average
               temperature rises -> GLOBAL WARMING.
            Then add: other greenhouse gases (CH4, H2O vapour, N2O,
            CFCs, ozone) ; causes (fossil fuels, deforestation) ;
            effects (melting ice caps, rising sea level, droughts).
  TRAP      The key phrase is that CO2 absorbs the LONG-WAVE INFRARED
            radiated BACK BY THE EARTH - not the incoming sunlight.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — SiO2 STRUCTURE AND THE CO2 COMPARISON         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the structure of silica", "Why is CO2 a gas but
            SiO2 a high-melting solid?"
  METHOD    1. SiO2: each Si is sp3 and bonded TETRAHEDRALLY to FOUR
               oxygens ; EACH OXYGEN IS SHARED BETWEEN TWO Si, so each
               Si owns 4 x 1/2 = 2 oxygens -> the formula SiO2 ; a
               GIANT 3-D COVALENT NETWORK. DRAW IT.
            2. CO2: carbon is SMALL, so 2p-2p SIDEWAYS overlap is good
               -> p(pi)-p(pi) DOUBLE BONDS -> valency satisfied inside
               one small molecule O=C=O -> held only by WEAK VAN DER
               WAALS forces -> GAS.
            3. SiO2: silicon is LARGE with diffuse 3p orbitals -> pi
               overlap is POOR -> it forms four SINGLE sigma bonds and
               builds a network -> HARD, HIGH-MELTING SOLID (~1996 K).
            4. One-line conclusion contrasting the two.
  TRAP      The reason is p(pi)-p(pi) BONDING, not electronegativity
            and not "silicon is a metalloid".
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — REACTIONS AND USES OF SILICA                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is HF not stored in glass?", "Give the reactions and
            uses of silica."
  METHOD    1. SiO2 + 4 HF --> SiF4 + 2 H2O   (so HF eats glass ; it is
               kept in wax or plastic ; used for ETCHING glass)
            2. SiO2 + 2 NaOH --(fused)--> Na2SiO3 + H2O  (acidic oxide)
            3. SiO2 + CaO --> CaSiO3   (blast furnace slag)
            4. SiO2 + 2C --> Si + 2CO ; SiO2 + 3C --> SiC + 2CO
               (SiC = CARBORUNDUM, an abrasive)
            5. USES: quartz is PIEZOELECTRIC (watches, oscillators);
               SILICA GEL as a drying agent; glass, cement, ceramics;
               kieselguhr; fused quartz for optical fibres.
  TRAP      Silica is an ACIDIC oxide, so it reacts with ALKALI. Do not
            say it reacts with all acids - only HF attacks it.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — SILICONES                                          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are silicones?", "How are silicones prepared?",
            "Give the structure, properties and uses of silicones."
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: organosilicon POLYMERS with a backbone of
               alternating Si and O carrying alkyl/aryl groups ;
               repeating unit (R2SiO).
            2. PREPARATION - two steps:
                  RMgCl + SiCl4 --> RSiCl3 + MgCl2  (and on to R2SiCl2)
                  n R2SiCl2 + n H2O --> [-O-SiR2-]n + 2n HCl
            3. STRUCTURE - DRAW the chain:
                        R     R     R
                        |     |     |
                  ... - Si -O-Si -O-Si - O - ...
                        |     |     |
                        R     R     R
            4. CHAIN CONTROL:  R3SiCl = chain STOPPER (1 -OH) ;
               R2SiCl2 = LINEAR ; RSiCl3 = CROSS-LINKED resin.
            5. PROPERTIES: inert, thermally stable, WATER-REPELLENT,
               electrical insulators, non-toxic.
            6. USES: high-temperature lubricants, insulation,
               waterproofing, sealants, surgical implants.
  TRAP      The backbone is -Si-O-Si-O- , NEVER -Si-Si-Si-. And spell
            silicone / silicon / silica / silicate correctly.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — SILICATES AND THEIR CLASSIFICATION            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the basic structural unit of silicates?",
            "Classify silicates with examples."
  METHOD    1. BASIC UNIT = [SiO4]4- TETRAHEDRON (sp3 Si, four O at the
               corners, each carrying one negative charge). DRAW IT.
            2. Silicates are built by SHARING CORNER OXYGEN ATOMS.
            3. Give the table:
                  0 shared -> ortho   SiO4 4-      zircon ZrSiO4
                  1 shared -> pyro    Si2O7 6-     thortveitite
                  2 shared -> chain / ring (SiO3)n 2n-  spodumene, beryl
                  2 and 3  -> double chain (Si4O11)n 6n-  ASBESTOS
                  3 shared -> sheet   (Si2O5)n 2n-  TALC , MICA
                  4 shared -> 3-D     SiO2          QUARTZ , feldspar
            4. Add: the charge is balanced by Na+, K+, Mg2+, Ca2+, Al3+.
  TRAP      The unit is SiO4 4- and TETRAHEDRAL - not SiO3 and not
            planar. Asbestos = double chain ; mica and talc = sheet.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — ZEOLITES                                      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are zeolites?", "Give the structure and uses of
            zeolites", "How is hard water softened by zeolites?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: 3-D ALUMINOSILICATES, made when SOME Si of a
               3-D silicate framework is replaced by Al.
            2. Each Si(IV) -> Al(III) leaves ONE NEGATIVE CHARGE,
               balanced by LOOSELY HELD Na+, K+, Ca2+ inside the
               CAVITIES and CHANNELS.
            3. THREE USES with the reason for each:
                  (a) SHAPE-SELECTIVE CATALYSTS - uniform pore size lets
                      only the right molecules in. ZSM-5 turns ALCOHOLS
                      INTO GASOLINE ; cracking of hydrocarbons.
                  (b) ION EXCHANGERS - softening hard water:
                          Na2Z + Ca2+ --> CaZ + 2 Na+
                          regenerated:  CaZ + 2 NaCl --> Na2Z + CaCl2
                  (c) MOLECULAR SIEVES / drying agents.
  TRAP      A zeolite is an ALUMINOsilicate, not a plain silicate.
            The phrase the examiner wants is SHAPE SELECTIVE CATALYSIS.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — USES OF THE ELEMENTS AND COMPOUNDS                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give two uses of ... (graphite / diamond / silicon / tin /
            lead / silicones / CO2 / dry ice / zeolites)"
  METHOD    Give exactly TWO or THREE, sharply worded, each tied to a
            property:
              DIAMOND   gems ; abrasive and drilling (hardest)
              GRAPHITE  lubricant (layers slide) ; electrodes (conducts)
              SILICON   semiconductor chips ; solar cells
              TIN       tin plating ; solder and bronze
              LEAD      storage batteries ; radiation shielding (dense)
              SILICONES high-temp lubricants ; waterproofing ; implants
              CO2       fire extinguisher ; dry ice refrigerant ; urea
              CO        reducing agent in metallurgy ; fuel
              SILICA    quartz watches (piezoelectric) ; silica gel
              ZEOLITES  catalyst (ZSM-5) ; water softening
  TRAP      Give the PROPERTY that makes the use possible - "graphite
            is used as a lubricant BECAUSE its layers slide" - that
            phrase often carries the second mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — "GIVE REASONS" ONE-LINERS                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any short "Why ...?" question not covered above.
  METHOD    Keep this bank ready:
              *  Why does Si not form pi bonds?  -> its 3p orbitals are
                 large and diffuse, so sideways overlap is poor.
              *  Why is diamond converted to graphite on heating?
                 -> graphite is thermodynamically more stable.
              *  Why is CCl4 used in fire extinguishers?  -> it is
                 non-inflammable, heavy and unaffected by water.
              *  Why is lead not attacked by water?  -> a protective
                 layer of PbO / basic lead carbonate forms.
              *  Why do Sn and Pb conduct electricity?  -> they are
                 true metals with delocalised metallic bonding.
              *  Why is SiC called carborundum, and where is it used?
                 -> SiC, extremely hard, used as an abrasive.
              *  Why does graphite conduct in one direction only?
                 -> the delocalised electrons move only ALONG the
                 layers, not across the 340 pm gaps.
  TRAP      Answer in ONE cause + ONE consequence. Two clean sentences
            get the full two marks; a paragraph of waffle does not.
```
