## Topic: Early classification and the periodic laws

### Q A1
State the modern periodic law. Who gave it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MODERN PERIODIC LAW :

  "The physical and chemical properties of the elements are a
   periodic function of their ATOMIC NUMBERS."

  It follows from the work of HENRY MOSELEY (1913), who showed by
  X-ray studies that the atomic number, not the atomic weight, is
  the fundamental property of an element.
```

---

### Q A2
What is Dobereiner's law of triads? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW OF TRIADS - when three elements with similar properties are
  arranged in increasing order of their atomic weights, the atomic
  weight of the MIDDLE element is approximately the ARITHMETIC MEAN
  of the atomic weights of the other two.

  Example :  Li (6.9) , Na (23.0) , K (39.1)

                6.9  +  39.1
                ------------  =  23.0    =  atomic weight of Na
                     2
```

---

### Q A3
State Newlands' law of octaves. Give its main limitation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW OF OCTAVES - when elements are arranged in increasing order of
  their atomic weights, the properties of every EIGHTH element are a
  repetition of the properties of the first element,
  like the eighth note of a musical scale.

  LIMITATION - it was valid only up to calcium (Z = 20); beyond that
  the pattern failed completely, and it had no place for the noble
  gases discovered later.
```

---

### Q A4
What is Moseley's contribution to the periodic table?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Moseley studied the characteristic X-rays emitted by different
  elements and found

              sqrt ( nu )  =  a ( Z - b )

  where nu is the frequency of the X-ray and Z is the atomic number.
  The relation depended on ATOMIC NUMBER and not on atomic weight.

  This proved that ATOMIC NUMBER is the fundamental property of an
  element, and led to the MODERN PERIODIC LAW.
```

## Topic: Structure of the table, blocks and configurations

### Q A5
What is the cause of periodicity in properties of elements?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The repetition of SIMILAR OUTERMOST (VALENCE) SHELL ELECTRONIC
  CONFIGURATIONS after regular intervals of atomic number.

  Example :  Li  =  1s2 2s1        ends in  ns1
             Na  =  [Ne] 3s1       ends in  ns1
             K   =  [Ar] 4s1       ends in  ns1

  Same outer configuration -> same chemical behaviour -> same group.
```

### Q A6
Why is the sixth period the longest period, containing 32 elements?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In the sixth period the orbitals filled are  6s , 4f , 5d , 6p.

     6s  ->  2 electrons
     4f  -> 14 electrons
     5d  -> 10 electrons
     6p  ->  6 electrons
            -------------
            32 electrons     ->  32 elements
```

### Q A7
Write the general electronic configurations of the s, p, d and f blocks.

**ANSWER:**
```
     s - block   :   ns(1)  to  ns(2)
     p - block   :   ns2 np(1)  to  ns2 np(6)
     d - block   :   (n-1)d(1-10)   ns(1-2)
     f - block   :   (n-2)f(1-14)  (n-1)d(0-1)  ns2
```

### Q A8
An element has the configuration [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p3. Find its period,
group and block.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Highest n = 4                     ->  PERIOD 4
  Last electron enters 4p           ->  p - BLOCK
  Outer electrons = 4s2 + 4p3 = 5
  Group = 10 + 5 = 15               ->  GROUP 15

  The element is ARSENIC (As), Z = 33.
```

### Q A9
Why is helium placed in group 18 although its configuration is 1s2?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Helium has a COMPLETELY FILLED outermost shell (1s2). Its chemical
  behaviour - chemically inert, monatomic gas, very high ionisation
  enthalpy - matches the noble gases, not the alkaline earth metals.

  Therefore it is placed in GROUP 18 and not in group 2.
```

## Topic: Sizes

### Q A12
Define covalent radius and van der Waals radius.

**ANSWER:**
```
  COVALENT RADIUS - one half of the distance between the nuclei of
  two identical atoms joined together by a SINGLE COVALENT BOND.

  VAN DER WAALS RADIUS - one half of the distance between the nuclei
  of two adjacent atoms belonging to two NEIGHBOURING MOLECULES in
  the solid state.

  van der Waals radius  >  metallic radius  >  covalent radius
```

### Q A13
Why is a cation smaller and an anion larger than the parent atom?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CATION is SMALLER because
     - it has usually lost the whole outermost shell, so one shell less
     - the same nuclear charge now holds fewer electrons, so the
       effective nuclear charge per electron is greater and the
       electron cloud contracts.        Na 186 pm  ->  Na+ 95 pm

  ANION is LARGER because
     - the same nuclear charge must now hold more electrons
     - the extra electron increases electron-electron repulsion and
       the cloud expands.               Cl 99 pm   ->  Cl- 181 pm
```

### Q A14
What are isoelectronic species? Arrange N3-, O2-, F-, Na+, Mg2+ in
decreasing order of their ionic radii.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ISOELECTRONIC SPECIES - atoms or ions that have the SAME number of
  electrons but DIFFERENT nuclear charges.
  Here every species has 10 electrons (1s2 2s2 2p6).

  RULE : same electrons, so the larger the nuclear charge, the
         stronger the pull and the smaller the ion.

     N3-   >   O2-   >   F-   >   Na+   >   Mg2+
     Z=7       Z=8       Z=9      Z=11      Z=12
     171       140       136       95        65      pm
```

### Q A15
Arrange the following in increasing order of atomic radius:
Na, Mg, Al, Si, Cl.

**ANSWER:**
```
  All are in PERIOD 3. Across a period the atomic radius decreases,
  because the nuclear charge increases while electrons enter the same
  shell, so the effective nuclear charge increases.

     Cl  <  Si  <  Al  <  Mg  <  Na
```

## Topic: Ionisation enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, electronegativity

### Q A16
Define ionisation enthalpy. What is its unit?

**ANSWER:**
```
  IONISATION ENTHALPY - the MINIMUM amount of energy required to
  remove the MOST LOOSELY BOUND electron from an ISOLATED NEUTRAL
  GASEOUS ATOM in its GROUND STATE to form a gaseous cation.

        M (g)  +  IE(1)   ---->   M+ (g)  +  e-

  Unit : kilojoule per mole (kJ/mol), or electron volt per atom.
  It is always POSITIVE (endothermic).
```

---

### Q A17
Why is the second ionisation enthalpy of an element always greater
than the first?

**ANSWER:**
```
  After the first electron is removed the species becomes a POSITIVE
  ION which is SMALLER in size. The same nuclear charge now acts on
  fewer electrons, so the EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR CHARGE on each remaining
  electron is greater and it is held more tightly.

  Hence more energy is needed :   IE(1)  <  IE(2)  <  IE(3) ...
```

---

### Q A18
Define electron gain enthalpy. Why is the second electron gain
enthalpy of oxygen positive?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRON GAIN ENTHALPY - the enthalpy change when an electron is
  added to an isolated neutral gaseous atom in its ground state to
  form a gaseous anion.        X(g) + e-  ->  X-(g)

  The SECOND electron gain enthalpy of oxygen is POSITIVE because the
  second electron has to be forced into the O- ion, which is ALREADY
  NEGATIVELY charged and therefore REPELS the incoming electron.
  Energy must be supplied to overcome this repulsion.

        O(g)  + e-  ->  O-(g)    delta H = -141 kJ/mol
        O-(g) + e-  ->  O2-(g)   delta H = +780 kJ/mol
```

---

### Q A19
Define electronegativity. How does it differ from electron gain enthalpy?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRONEGATIVITY - the tendency of an atom IN A CHEMICAL COMPOUND
  (a bonded atom) to attract the SHARED PAIR OF ELECTRONS towards
  itself. It is a relative quantity with NO UNITS.

  DIFFERENCE :
     Electron gain enthalpy is a property of an ISOLATED FREE atom,
     is measurable and has units (kJ/mol).
     Electronegativity is a property of a BONDED atom, is not
     measurable directly and has no units.
```

### Q A20
What is the screening effect (shielding effect)? Write the expression
for the effective nuclear charge.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SCREENING (SHIELDING) EFFECT - the decrease in the force of
  attraction between the nucleus and the outermost electron, caused
  by the repulsion of the electrons present in the inner shells.

              Z ( eff )   =   Z   -   S

     Z = actual nuclear charge (atomic number)
     S = screening constant

  Screening power within a shell :   s  >  p  >  d  >  f
```

## Topic: Anomalies and chemical periodicity

### Q A22
What is a diagonal relationship? Give one pair of elements that show it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP - the similarity in properties between an
  element of the second period and the element placed DIAGONALLY
  below and to the RIGHT of it in the third period.

  Pairs :   Li ~ Mg   ,   Be ~ Al   ,   B ~ Si

  Example : both lithium and magnesium burn in nitrogen to form
  nitrides, Li3N and Mg3N2, while sodium does not.
```

---

### Q A23
Why does nitrogen not form NCl5 while phosphorus forms PCl5?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Nitrogen is a SECOND PERIOD element. Its valence shell (n = 2) has
  only 2s and 2p orbitals - there are NO VACANT d ORBITALS.
  So it cannot expand its octet, and its maximum covalency is 4.

  Phosphorus is a THIRD PERIOD element. Its valence shell (n = 3) has
  VACANT 3d ORBITALS, so it can expand its octet and show a covalency
  of 5, forming PCl5.
```

```
                            Al2O3 + 2 NaOH ->  2 NaAlO2 + H2O
```

### Q B1
State the merits of Mendeleev's periodic table.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. SYSTEMATIC STUDY OF ELEMENTS
     The 63 elements known at the time were arranged into 8 groups
     and 7 periods, so the study of elements became the study of a
     few families instead of 63 separate substances.

  2. CORRECTION OF DOUBTFUL ATOMIC WEIGHTS
     Where an element did not fit, Mendeleev trusted the properties
     and corrected the weight. The atomic weight of beryllium was
     changed from 13.5 to 9. Similar corrections were made for
     indium, gold, platinum and uranium.

  3. PREDICTION OF UNDISCOVERED ELEMENTS
     He deliberately left GAPS instead of forcing a wrong element in,
     and predicted the properties of the missing elements using the
     prefix "eka-".

        eka-boron       ->  scandium   (Sc)
        eka-aluminium   ->  gallium    (Ga)
        eka-silicon     ->  germanium  (Ge)

     For eka-silicon he predicted atomic weight 72 and density
     5.5 g/cc; germanium was later found to have 72.6 and 5.36 g/cc.

  4. ACCOMMODATION OF THE NOBLE GASES
     When He, Ne, Ar, Kr and Xe were discovered around 1894-1900,
     they were fitted in as a new ZERO GROUP without disturbing any
     other element. This was a great success for the table.
```

### Q B2
State the demerits (defects) of Mendeleev's periodic table.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. POSITION OF HYDROGEN
     Hydrogen has one electron like the alkali metals of group IA,
     but it also needs one electron like the halogens of group VIIA.
     No fixed position could be given to it.

  2. NO PLACE FOR ISOTOPES
     Isotopes have different atomic weights, so by his law they should
     get different positions. Chemically, however, they are one and
     the same element and must occupy one place.

  3. ANOMALOUS PAIRS
     In four places a HEAVIER element had to be put BEFORE a lighter
     one to keep the chemistry right, which breaks his own law:

           Ar (39.9)  before  K  (39.1)
           Co (58.9)  before  Ni (58.7)
           Te (127.6) before  I  (126.9)
           Th (232)   before  Pa (231)

  4. DISSIMILAR ELEMENTS TOGETHER, SIMILAR ELEMENTS APART
     The very reactive alkali metals (IA) were placed in the same
     group as the unreactive coinage metals Cu, Ag, Au (IB), while
     chemically similar Cu and Hg were put in different groups.

  5. NO PROPER POSITION FOR LANTHANIDES AND ACTINIDES
     These 28 elements could not be fitted into the main body of the
     table and had to be placed separately.

  6. NO EXPLANATION OF THE CAUSE OF PERIODICITY.
```

### Q B3
How are the period, group and block of an element determined from its
electronic configuration? Illustrate with two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  RULES
     PERIOD  =  the highest value of n in the configuration.
     BLOCK   =  the subshell that received the last electron.
     GROUP   :
         s-block  ->  number of ns electrons
         p-block  ->  10 + (ns + np electrons of the outer shell)
         d-block  ->  (n-1)d electrons + ns electrons
         f-block  ->  always group 3

  EXAMPLE 1 :  Z = 17,  1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p5
         highest n = 3            ->  PERIOD 3
         last electron in 3p      ->  p-BLOCK
         outer electrons = 2 + 5 = 7 ,  group = 10 + 7 = 17
         ANSWER : period 3, group 17, p-block -> CHLORINE

  EXAMPLE 2 :  Z = 26,  [Ar] 3d6 4s2
         highest n = 4            ->  PERIOD 4
         last electron in 3d      ->  d-BLOCK
         group = 6 + 2 = 8
         ANSWER : period 4, group 8, d-block -> IRON
```

### Q B4
Explain how the elements with atomic number greater than 100 are named
according to IUPAC. Give the names and symbols for Z = 104, 109 and 114.

**ANSWER:**
```
  New heavy elements are made a few atoms at a time, and two
  laboratories often claimed the same element. IUPAC therefore gave a
  TEMPORARY SYSTEMATIC NAME based only on the atomic number.

  ROOTS :
     0 nil (n)   1 un (u)   2 bi (b)   3 tri (t)   4 quad (q)
     5 pent (p)  6 hex (h)  7 sept (s) 8 oct (o)   9 enn (e)

  RULE :
     - write the digits of Z separately
     - replace each digit by its root, in order
     - add the suffix "-ium"
     - the SYMBOL is the first letter of each root, only the first
       letter capital (three letters in all)
     - drop the "n" of "enn" before "nil", and the "i" of "bi" and
       "tri" before "ium"

  EXAMPLES
     104  =  un + nil + quad + ium   =  UNNILQUADIUM   ,  Unq
             (now Rutherfordium, Rf)

     109  =  un + nil + enn  + ium   =  UNNILENNIUM    ,  Une
             (now Meitnerium, Mt)

     114  =  un + un  + quad + ium   =  UNUNQUADIUM    ,  Uuq
             (now Flerovium, Fl)
```

### Q B5
Explain the variation of atomic radius across a period and down a group,
with reasons.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ACROSS A PERIOD (left to right) - ATOMIC RADIUS DECREASES

     Reasons:
     1. The nuclear charge increases by one unit at each step.
     2. The added electron enters the SAME shell, and electrons in
        the same shell shield one another very poorly, so the
        screening effect stays almost constant.
     3. Therefore the EFFECTIVE NUCLEAR CHARGE Z(eff) increases
        steadily and the electron cloud is pulled inwards.

     Data (pm) :  Li 152 > Be 111 > B 88 > C 77 > N 74 > O 66 > F 64

  DOWN A GROUP - ATOMIC RADIUS INCREASES

     Reasons:
     1. A NEW SHELL is added at every step, so the outermost electron
        is much further from the nucleus.
     2. The extra inner shells increase the screening effect sharply.
     3. This increase in the number of shells and in the shielding
        OUTWEIGHS the increase in nuclear charge.

     Data (pm) :  Li 152 < Na 186 < K 231 < Rb 244 < Cs 262

  NOTE : the noble gases appear larger than the halogen before them
  only because their VAN DER WAALS radius is quoted, while for the
  other elements a COVALENT radius is quoted.
```

### Q B6
Explain the trends in ionisation enthalpy across a period and down a
group. Mention the factors that affect it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ACROSS A PERIOD  ->  IONISATION ENTHALPY INCREASES
     because the nuclear charge increases, the atomic size decreases
     and the shielding is almost constant, so Z(eff) rises and the
     outermost electron is held much more firmly.
     Li 520 < Be 899 < ... < F 1681 < Ne 2081 kJ/mol

  DOWN A GROUP  ->  IONISATION ENTHALPY DECREASES
     because the atomic size and the number of shielding inner shells
     both increase sharply, so the outermost electron is loosely held
     and easily removed.
     Li 520 > Na 496 > K 419 > Rb 403 > Cs 376 kJ/mol

  FACTORS AFFECTING IONISATION ENTHALPY
     1. ATOMIC SIZE      - larger size, lower IE
     2. NUCLEAR CHARGE   - larger charge, higher IE
     3. SHIELDING EFFECT - more shielding, lower IE
     4. PENETRATION      - s > p > d > f, so an s electron is
                           hardest to remove
     5. HALF-FILLED OR FULLY-FILLED SUBSHELLS - extra stability
                           raises the IE (as in N and Be)
```

### Q B7
Why is the first ionisation enthalpy of beryllium greater than that of
boron, and that of nitrogen greater than that of oxygen?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CASE 1 :  Be (899 kJ/mol)  >  B (801 kJ/mol)

     Be = 1s2 2s2          B = 1s2 2s2 2p1

     (a) In beryllium the electron is removed from a COMPLETELY
         FILLED and therefore extra-stable 2s2 subshell, which
         requires more energy.
     (b) In boron the electron is removed from a 2p orbital. A 2p
         orbital is HIGHER in energy, LESS PENETRATING than 2s, and
         is additionally SHIELDED by the 2s2 pair.
     (c) Hence the 2p electron of boron is removed more easily.

  CASE 2 :  N (1402 kJ/mol)  >  O (1314 kJ/mol)

     N = 1s2 2s2 2p3        O = 1s2 2s2 2p4

          N  : 2p    +--+--+--+          O : 2p   +--+--+--+
                     |^ |^ |^ |                   |^v|^ |^ |
                     +--+--+--+                   +--+--+--+
               exactly HALF FILLED           one orbital is PAIRED

     (a) Nitrogen has an exactly HALF-FILLED 2p3 subshell, which is
         symmetrical and extra stable, so removal is difficult.
     (b) In oxygen the fourth 2p electron must PAIR UP in an orbital
         that already contains an electron. The electron-electron
         repulsion so produced makes it easier to remove.
     (c) Also, on losing it, oxygen attains the stable 2p3 arrangement.

  THE SAME PATTERN REPEATS IN PERIOD 3 :  Mg > Al  and  P > S.
```

### Q B8
Explain why the electron gain enthalpy of fluorine is less negative
than that of chlorine, and that of oxygen is less negative than that
of sulphur.

**ANSWER:**
```
  VALUES :  F  -328 kJ/mol   ,  Cl  -349 kJ/mol
            O  -141 kJ/mol   ,  S   -200 kJ/mol

  ONE REASON EXPLAINS BOTH:

     Fluorine and oxygen are the SMALLEST atoms in their groups. The
     incoming electron has to enter the very small and COMPACT 2p
     subshell, which is already crowded with electrons.

     This produces STRONG ELECTRON-ELECTRON REPULSION, which cancels
     a large part of the energy released when the electron is added.

     Chlorine and sulphur are LARGER atoms; the electron enters the
     bigger 3p subshell where there is more room and much less
     repulsion, so more energy is released and the value is more
     negative.

  Hence the correct orders are

     GROUP 17 :   Cl  >  F  >  Br  >  I
     GROUP 16 :   S   >  Se >  O   >  Te

  Chlorine has the MOST NEGATIVE electron gain enthalpy of all
  the elements.
```

### Q B9
What is a diagonal relationship? Explain its cause, and give the
evidence for the similarity between lithium and magnesium.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP - the resemblance in properties between an
  element of the second period and the element placed diagonally
  below and to the right of it in the third period.

           Li  ~  Mg        Be  ~  Al        B  ~  Si

  CAUSE
     Moving ACROSS a period the size decreases and the
     electronegativity increases.
     Moving DOWN a group the size increases and the
     electronegativity decreases.
     A DIAGONAL move does both together, so the two effects CANCEL,
     and the diagonal pair end up with nearly the same atomic and
     ionic SIZE, the same ELECTRONEGATIVITY and the same
     CHARGE-TO-RADIUS RATIO (polarising power).

  EVIDENCE FOR Li AND Mg
     1. Both combine directly with nitrogen to form nitrides,
        Li3N and Mg3N2. Sodium does not.
     2. Both form only NORMAL oxides, Li2O and MgO, while sodium
        forms a peroxide and potassium a superoxide.
     3. Their carbonates decompose on heating :
           Li2CO3 -> Li2O + CO2   ;   MgCO3 -> MgO + CO2
        Na2CO3 does not decompose.
     4. LiCl and MgCl2 are both deliquescent and both dissolve in
        organic solvents such as alcohol, showing covalent character.
     5. LiOH and Mg(OH)2 are both weak bases, sparingly soluble in
        water, and both decompose on heating.
```

### Q B10
Explain the anomalous behaviour of the second-period elements.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The first element of each of the groups 1, 2, 13, 14, 15, 16 and 17
  (Li, Be, B, C, N, O, F) differs considerably from the rest of its
  group.

  FOUR REASONS
     1. Exceptionally SMALL ATOMIC AND IONIC SIZE.
     2. HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPY.
     3. HIGH ELECTRONEGATIVITY.
     4. ABSENCE OF VACANT d ORBITALS in the valence shell, because
        the second shell has only 2s and 2p orbitals.

  CONSEQUENCES
     - Maximum covalency of a second-period element is 4.
       N forms NCl3 but never NCl5 ; P forms both PCl3 and PCl5.
       F shows only -1 and covalency 1 ; Cl, Br, I show +1, +3, +5, +7.
       O has a covalency of 2 ; S forms SF6 with a covalency of 6.
     - LiCl is appreciably covalent and soluble in organic solvents,
       whereas NaCl is fully ionic.
     - BeCl2 is covalent and polymeric in the solid state, whereas
       MgCl2 is ionic.
     - Carbon forms strong pi bonds and shows catenation; silicon
       does not form stable pi bonds with itself.
     - Because of this anomaly, Li, Be and B show a DIAGONAL
       RELATIONSHIP with Mg, Al and Si respectively.
```

### Q B11
Describe the variation in the nature of the oxides of the third-period
elements. Write the relevant equations for the amphoteric oxide.

**ANSWER:**
```
   Na2O   MgO   Al2O3   SiO2   P4O10   SO3   Cl2O7
   ------------------------------------------------->
   strongly     amphoteric      acidic       strongly
   basic  basic         weakly         acidic  acidic
                        acidic

  REASON
     Across the period the element changes from strongly METALLIC
     (electropositive, low ionisation enthalpy) to strongly
     NON-METALLIC (electronegative). Metallic oxides are ionic and
     basic; non-metallic oxides are covalent and acidic; aluminium
     lies on the borderline and its oxide is amphoteric.

  EQUATIONS
     BASIC       Na2O  +  2 HCl    ->  2 NaCl   +  H2O
     ACIDIC      SO3   +  2 NaOH   ->  Na2SO4   +  H2O

     AMPHOTERIC  Al2O3 +  6 HCl    ->  2 AlCl3  +  3 H2O
                 Al2O3 +  2 NaOH   ->  2 NaAlO2 +  H2O
                                       (sodium meta-aluminate)

  Down a group the oxides become MORE BASIC, for example in group 15:
     N2O3 acidic , P4O6 acidic , As2O3 amphoteric , Sb2O3 amphoteric ,
     Bi2O3 basic.
```

### Q B12
Define electronegativity. Describe its variation in the periodic table
and state how it is related to the nature of a bond.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRONEGATIVITY - the tendency of an atom in a chemical compound
  (a BONDED atom) to attract the shared pair of electrons towards
  itself. It is a relative quantity and has NO UNITS.

  SCALES
     PAULING SCALE  - built from bond-energy data, with fluorine
                      fixed at 4.0 (the highest value).
     MULLIKEN SCALE -   EN = (ionisation enthalpy + electron gain
                             enthalpy) / 2

  VARIATION
     ACROSS A PERIOD -> INCREASES, because the atomic size decreases
        and the effective nuclear charge increases, so the bonded
        atom attracts the shared pair more strongly.
        Li 1.0 < Be 1.5 < B 2.0 < C 2.5 < N 3.0 < O 3.5 < F 4.0

     DOWN A GROUP -> DECREASES, because the size and the shielding
        both increase, so the shared pair is held less strongly.
        F 4.0 > Cl 3.0 > Br 2.8 > I 2.5

  RELATION TO BOND NATURE
     Large difference in electronegativity  ->  more IONIC bond
     Small difference                       ->  more COVALENT bond
     Zero difference                        ->  pure covalent (H-H)

  A high electronegativity also means high non-metallic character and
  an acidic oxide; a low value means metallic character and a basic
  oxide.
```

### Q B13
Explain the variation of metallic and non-metallic character in the
periodic table, and the variation of chemical reactivity across a period.

**ANSWER:**
```
  METALLIC (electropositive) CHARACTER - the tendency to LOSE
  electrons and form cations. It is high when the ionisation
  enthalpy is LOW.

  NON-METALLIC CHARACTER - the tendency to GAIN electrons and form
  anions. It is high when the electron gain enthalpy is strongly
  NEGATIVE and the electronegativity is high.

  ACROSS A PERIOD
     metallic character DECREASES and non-metallic character
     INCREASES, because the ionisation enthalpy increases and the
     electron gain enthalpy becomes more negative.

  DOWN A GROUP
     metallic character INCREASES and non-metallic character
     DECREASES, because the ionisation enthalpy falls.

     Most metallic element  : caesium (bottom left)
     Most non-metallic      : fluorine (top right)
     The METALLOIDS - B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po - lie along the
     zig-zag staircase separating metals from non-metals.

  CHEMICAL REACTIVITY ACROSS A PERIOD
     Reactivity is HIGH at BOTH ENDS and LOW in the MIDDLE.
        Left end  : very low ionisation enthalpy, so electrons are
                    lost easily -> highly reactive metals (Na).
        Middle    : the ionisation enthalpy is high and the electron
                    gain enthalpy is not very negative, so the
                    element neither loses nor gains easily
                    -> least reactive (Si).
        Right end : very negative electron gain enthalpy, so
                    electrons are gained eagerly -> highly reactive
                    non-metals (Cl).
        Group 18  : completely filled shells -> practically inert.
```

### Q B14
Why does the atomic radius decrease across a period but the ionic radius
of an isoelectronic series also decrease with increasing atomic number?
Illustrate.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BOTH have the SAME underlying cause: the number of electrons stays
  in the same shell while the nuclear charge keeps rising, so the
  effective nuclear charge per electron increases and the electron
  cloud is drawn inwards.

  ACROSS A PERIOD
     Na  Mg  Al  Si  P  S  Cl
     Z rises 11 -> 17 but every atom keeps its outermost shell at
     n = 3, so the shielding barely changes and the size falls.

  ISOELECTRONIC SERIES (all with 10 electrons, 1s2 2s2 2p6)
     N3-   O2-   F-   Ne   Na+   Mg2+   Al3+
     Z=7   Z=8   Z=9  Z=10 Z=11  Z=12   Z=13
     171   140   136   -    95    65     50     pm

     The electron count is fixed at 10, so the shielding is fixed;
     only Z rises, so Z(eff) per electron rises and the ion shrinks.

  RULE TO REMEMBER
     For an isoelectronic series, arrange in INCREASING atomic number
     and the size automatically comes out in DECREASING order.
```

### Q B15
Write a short note on the classification of elements into s, p, d and f
blocks, giving their general characteristics.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A BLOCK is named after the subshell that receives the last electron.

  s - BLOCK  (groups 1 and 2)   general configuration  ns(1-2)
     Soft, highly reactive metals with low ionisation enthalpies.
     They form ionic compounds and strongly basic oxides.
     Group 1 = alkali metals, group 2 = alkaline earth metals.

  p - BLOCK  (groups 13 to 18)  general configuration  ns2 np(1-6)
     Contains metals, metalloids and non-metals. Group 18 are the

  f - BLOCK  general configuration  (n-2)f(1-14) (n-1)d(0-1) ns2
     Called INNER TRANSITION elements. They are the LANTHANOIDS
     (Ce 58 to Lu 71) and the ACTINOIDS (Th 90 to Lr 103), all
     placed in group 3 and shown separately below the main table.
     All actinoids are radioactive.
```

### Q B16
An element X has the electronic configuration [Ar] 3d5 4s1.
(a) Identify it. (b) Give its period, group and block.
(c) Why is its configuration not [Ar] 3d4 4s2?

**ANSWER:**
```

  (b) highest n = 4                    ->  PERIOD 4

      because
        - a HALF-FILLED d subshell (d5) is symmetrical and therefore

      electron shifts into 3d to achieve this extra stability.
```

### Q C1  *** THE MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED LONG ANSWER IN THIS CHAPTER ***
Define atomic radius, ionic radius, ionisation enthalpy, electron gain
enthalpy and electronegativity, and explain how each of them varies
along a period and down a group, giving reasons.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  THE ONE IDEA BEHIND ALL FIVE TRENDS

      Z(eff)  =  Z  -  S

      ACROSS a period the nuclear charge Z increases while the added
      electrons enter the SAME shell, so the screening S is nearly
      constant and Z(eff) INCREASES.

      DOWN a group a whole NEW SHELL is added, so both the distance
      and the screening increase, and Z(eff) remains almost unchanged
      while the atom becomes much bigger.
```

```
  1. ATOMIC RADIUS

     Definition (covalent radius) - one half of the distance between
     the nuclei of two identical atoms joined by a single covalent
     bond.

     ACROSS A PERIOD : DECREASES, because Z(eff) increases and the
        electron cloud is pulled in.
        Li 152 > Be 111 > B 88 > C 77 > N 74 > O 66 > F 64 pm

     DOWN A GROUP : INCREASES, because a new shell is added and the
        increase in shell number and shielding outweighs the
        increase in nuclear charge.
        Li 152 < Na 186 < K 231 < Rb 244 < Cs 262 pm
```

```
  2. IONIC RADIUS

     A CATION is always SMALLER than its parent atom, because it has
     one shell fewer and the same nuclear charge now holds fewer
     electrons.                     Na 186 pm  ->  Na+ 95 pm

     An ANION is always LARGER than its parent atom, because the same
     nuclear charge holds more electrons and the extra
     electron-electron repulsion expands the cloud.
                                    Cl 99 pm   ->  Cl- 181 pm

     ISOELECTRONIC SERIES : with the same number of electrons, the
     radius decreases as the nuclear charge increases.
         N3- > O2- > F- > Ne > Na+ > Mg2+ > Al3+

     Across a period ionic radii decrease; down a group they increase.
```

```
  3. IONISATION ENTHALPY

     Definition - the minimum energy required to remove the most
     loosely bound electron from an isolated neutral gaseous atom in
     its ground state.       M(g) + IE1 -> M+(g) + e-

     ACROSS A PERIOD : INCREASES, because Z(eff) rises and the size
        falls, so the electron is held more firmly.
        Li 520 -> Ne 2081 kJ/mol

     DOWN A GROUP : DECREASES, because the size and shielding both
        increase and the outer electron is loosely held.
        Li 520 -> Cs 376 kJ/mol

     EXCEPTIONS :  Be > B  and  N > O   (also Mg > Al and P > S)
        Be has a stable fully-filled 2s2, while B loses a 2p electron
        that is higher in energy and shielded by 2s2.
        N has a stable half-filled 2p3, while in O the fourth 2p
        electron must pair up and is pushed out by repulsion.

     SUCCESSIVE VALUES :  IE1 < IE2 < IE3, because each removal
        leaves a smaller, more positive ion.
```

```
  4. ELECTRON GAIN ENTHALPY

     Definition - the enthalpy change when an electron is added to an
     isolated neutral gaseous atom in its ground state to form an
     anion.       X(g) + e- -> X-(g)

     ACROSS A PERIOD : becomes MORE NEGATIVE, because the size falls
        and Z(eff) rises, so the incoming electron is attracted more
        strongly. Halogens have the most negative values.

     DOWN A GROUP : becomes LESS NEGATIVE, because the incoming
        electron enters a shell far from the nucleus.

     EXCEPTIONS :  Cl (-349) is more negative than F (-328), and
        S (-200) is more negative than O (-141), because F and O are
        very small and their compact 2p subshell causes strong
        electron-electron repulsion with the incoming electron.

     The SECOND electron gain enthalpy is always POSITIVE.
```

```
  5. ELECTRONEGATIVITY

     Definition - the tendency of a bonded atom in a compound to
     attract the shared pair of electrons towards itself. It is
     relative and has no units. On the Pauling scale F = 4.0 is the
     highest and Cs = 0.7 the lowest.

     ACROSS A PERIOD : INCREASES, because the size decreases and
        Z(eff) increases.       Li 1.0 -> F 4.0

     DOWN A GROUP : DECREASES, because the size and shielding
        increase.               F 4.0 -> I 2.5
```

```
  | |   Li   Be   B    C    N    O    F                            | |
```

### Q C2
Describe the long form of the periodic table. Explain the division of
elements into s, p, d and f blocks and the characteristics of each block.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  BASIS
     The long form (Bohr's) table is based on the MODERN PERIODIC LAW:
     "the physical and chemical properties of the elements are a
      periodic function of their atomic numbers."
     Elements are arranged in increasing order of atomic number, and
     the arrangement follows the filling of the orbitals.

  PERIODS - 7 horizontal rows
     The period number equals the principal quantum number n of the
     outermost shell. A new period begins whenever a new ns orbital
     begins to fill. The number of elements in a period equals the
     number of electrons that fill the orbitals of that period.

     +--------+------------------+----------+---------------+
     | PERIOD |  ORBITALS FILLED | ELEMENTS |  NAME         |
     +--------+------------------+----------+---------------+
     |   1    |  1s              |    2     | very short    |
     |   2    |  2s 2p           |    8     | short         |
     |   3    |  3s 3p           |    8     | short         |
     |   4    |  4s 3d 4p        |   18     | long          |
     |   5    |  5s 4d 5p        |   18     | long          |
     |   6    |  6s 4f 5d 6p     |   32     | longest       |
     |   7    |  7s 5f 6d 7p     |   32     | complete      |
     +--------+------------------+----------+---------------+

  GROUPS - 18 vertical columns numbered 1 to 18 by IUPAC.
     All elements of one group have the same number of valence
     electrons and therefore similar chemical properties.
     Group 1 alkali metals, group 2 alkaline earth metals,
     groups 3-12 transition elements, group 17 halogens,
     group 18 noble gases.
```

```
  THE FOUR BLOCKS

     +------+                                    +----------------+
     |      |                                    |                |
     |  s   |   +--------------------------+     |       p        |
     | ns1  |   |            d             |     |   ns2 np1-6    |
     | ns2  |   |   (n-1)d1-10  ns1-2      |     |                |
     +------+   +--------------------------+     +----------------+

                +-------------------------------------------+
                |  f :  (n-2)f1-14 (n-1)d0-1 ns2            |
                |  Lanthanoids 58-71 , Actinoids 90-103     |
                +-------------------------------------------+

  1. s-BLOCK  (groups 1 and 2), configuration ns(1-2)
     Soft metals with low ionisation enthalpies and low
     electronegativity; highly reactive; form ionic compounds and
     strongly basic oxides; good reducing agents; they show fixed
     oxidation states +1 and +2.

  2. p-BLOCK  (groups 13 to 18), configuration ns2 np(1-6)
     Contains metals, metalloids and non-metals. Group 18 noble gases
     have completely filled shells and are almost inert. Oxides are
     mostly acidic. s-block and p-block together are called the
     REPRESENTATIVE or NORMAL elements.

  3. d-BLOCK  (groups 3 to 12), configuration (n-1)d(1-10) ns(1-2)
     The TRANSITION elements. They are hard metals with high melting
     points, show VARIABLE oxidation states because the (n-1)d and ns
     electrons are close in energy, form COLOURED ions and COMPLEX
     compounds, and are used as CATALYSTS. Four series: 3d (Sc-Zn),
     4d (Y-Cd), 5d (La, Hf-Hg) and 6d.

  4. f-BLOCK, configuration (n-2)f(1-14) (n-1)d(0-1) ns2
     The INNER TRANSITION elements: LANTHANOIDS (Ce 58 to Lu 71) and
     ACTINOIDS (Th 90 to Lr 103). They are all placed in group 3 and
     shown in two rows below the main table to keep the table
     compact. They show a large number of oxidation states; all the
     actinoids are radioactive.

  NOTE : helium (1s2) is placed in group 18 with the noble gases
  because its outermost shell is completely filled.
```

### Q C3
Give an account of the early attempts at the classification of elements
and describe Mendeleev's periodic table with its merits and demerits.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  1. DOBEREINER'S TRIADS (1817-1829)
     Elements with similar properties were grouped in threes. When
     arranged in increasing order of atomic weight, the weight of the
     middle element was nearly the arithmetic mean of the other two.
         Li 6.9 , Na 23.0 , K 39.1    ->  (6.9 + 39.1)/2 = 23.0
         Ca 40.1, Sr 87.6 , Ba 137.3  ->  (40.1 + 137.3)/2 = 88.7
         Cl 35.5, Br 79.9 , I 126.9   ->  (35.5 + 126.9)/2 = 81.2
     LIMITATION : only three or four such triads could be found.

  2. NEWLANDS' LAW OF OCTAVES (1865)
     Arranged in increasing atomic weight, the properties of every
     EIGHTH element repeated those of the first, like musical notes.
         Li  Be  B  C  N  O  F  Na    ->  Na repeats Li
     LIMITATIONS : valid only up to calcium; two elements had to be
     put in one slot; unlike elements fell in the same column; and
     there was no place for the noble gases discovered later.

  3. LOTHAR MEYER'S CURVE (1869)
     A graph of ATOMIC VOLUME against ATOMIC WEIGHT gave a repeating
     wave. The alkali metals Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs all occupied the
     PEAKS, and elements with similar properties occupied similar
     positions on the curve. This showed that physical properties are
     a periodic function of atomic weight.
```

```
  4. MENDELEEV'S PERIODIC TABLE (1869)

     LAW : "The physical and chemical properties of the elements are
            a periodic function of their ATOMIC WEIGHTS."

     STRUCTURE
        - 63 known elements arranged in increasing atomic weight.
        - EIGHT vertical groups, I to VIII. Groups I to VII were each
          divided into subgroups A and B; group VIII contained three
          elements in each row (Fe, Co, Ni ; Ru, Rh, Pd ; Os, Ir, Pt).
        - SEVEN horizontal rows (periods or series).
        - The formulae of the highest oxide and the hydride followed
          the group number:
             Group      I    II   III   IV    V     VI    VII
             Oxide     R2O   RO   R2O3  RO2   R2O5  RO3   R2O7
             Hydride   RH    RH2  RH3   RH4   RH3   RH2   RH
```

```
     MERITS
     (a) It made the study of 63 elements systematic.
     (b) It corrected doubtful atomic weights - beryllium was changed
         from 13.5 to 9; corrections were also made for In, Au, Pt, U.
     (c) It PREDICTED undiscovered elements. Gaps were left, and the
         properties of eka-boron, eka-aluminium and eka-silicon were
         predicted; these were later found to be scandium, gallium
         and germanium, with properties very close to the predictions
         (eka-silicon: predicted weight 72 and density 5.5 g/cc;
         germanium: 72.6 and 5.36 g/cc).
     (d) The NOBLE GASES, discovered much later, could be added as a
         new zero group without disturbing the rest of the table.

     DEMERITS
     (a) No fixed position for HYDROGEN - it resembles both group IA
         and group VIIA.
     (b) No place for ISOTOPES, which have different atomic weights
         but are the same element.
     (c) ANOMALOUS PAIRS - a heavier element had to be placed before
         a lighter one: Ar before K, Co before Ni, Te before I,
         Th before Pa. This violates his own law.
     (d) Chemically similar elements were separated (Cu in IB, Hg in
         IIB) while dissimilar ones were placed together (alkali
         metals IA with coinage metals IB).
     (e) No proper position for the lanthanides and actinides.
     (f) The cause of periodicity was not explained.
```

```
  5. THE CORRECTION - MOSELEY (1913)
     From X-ray studies Moseley obtained  sqrt(nu) = a (Z - b),
     showing that the ATOMIC NUMBER, not the atomic weight, is the
     fundamental property. Replacing atomic weight by atomic number
     gave the MODERN PERIODIC LAW and removed the demerits (b) and
     (c) at once, and explained (f), because the atomic number fixes
     the electronic configuration, which fixes the chemistry.
```

### Q C4
Explain the periodic trends in the chemical properties of elements:
valence, oxidation state, metallic and non-metallic character, the
anomalous behaviour of second-period elements, the diagonal relationship
and the nature of the oxides.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  1. VALENCE
     Valence is the number of electrons an atom loses, gains or shares
     to reach a noble gas configuration. For representative elements
     it is the number of valence electrons, or 8 minus that number,
     whichever is smaller.

        Period 2 :  Li  Be  B   C   N   O   F   Ne
        Valence  :   1   2   3   4   3   2   1    0

     Across a period the valence rises 1, 2, 3, 4 and then falls
     3, 2, 1, 0. Down a group it stays CONSTANT, which is why the
     members of a group behave as one family.

  2. OXIDATION STATE
     Group 1 shows +1, group 2 shows +2, group 13 shows +3, group 15
     shows -3, +3 and +5, group 17 shows -1 and also +1, +3, +5, +7
     except FLUORINE, which shows only -1 because it is the most
     electronegative element and has no d orbitals. The d-block
     elements show VARIABLE oxidation states, because their (n-1)d
     and ns electrons are close in energy and both sets can be used
     in bonding.

  3. METALLIC AND NON-METALLIC CHARACTER
     Metallic character is the tendency to LOSE electrons; it is high
     when the ionisation enthalpy is low. Non-metallic character is
     the tendency to GAIN electrons; it is high when the electron
     gain enthalpy is strongly negative.

     Across a period : metallic character DECREASES and non-metallic
        character INCREASES (ionisation enthalpy rises).
     Down a group    : metallic character INCREASES and non-metallic
        character DECREASES (ionisation enthalpy falls).

     Caesium is the most metallic and fluorine the most non-metallic
     element. The METALLOIDS B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te and Po lie along
     the zig-zag staircase separating the two.

  4. CHEMICAL REACTIVITY
     Across a period, reactivity is HIGH at BOTH ENDS and LOW in the
     MIDDLE. On the left, metals lose electrons very easily; on the
     right, non-metals gain electrons very readily; in the middle the
     elements do neither easily. Group 18 elements are inert because
     their shells are completely filled.
     Down a group, metals become MORE reactive and non-metals become
     LESS reactive.

  5. ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF SECOND-PERIOD ELEMENTS
     Li, Be, B, C, N, O and F differ from the rest of their groups
     because of
        (a) their very small atomic and ionic size,
        (b) their high ionisation enthalpy,
        (c) their high electronegativity, and
        (d) the ABSENCE of vacant d orbitals in the valence shell.
     Because of (d) their MAXIMUM COVALENCY IS 4 : nitrogen forms
     NCl3 but never NCl5, while phosphorus forms PCl5 using its 3d
     orbitals; fluorine shows only -1 while chlorine shows up to +7;
     oxygen has a covalency of 2 while sulphur forms SF6.

  6. DIAGONAL RELATIONSHIP
     Li resembles Mg, Be resembles Al and B resembles Si.
     Cause : moving across a period the size falls and the
     electronegativity rises, while moving down a group the opposite
     happens; a diagonal move cancels the two, so the pair have
     nearly the same size, electronegativity and charge-to-radius
     ratio.
     Evidence : Li and Mg both form nitrides (Li3N, Mg3N2) and normal
     oxides, and their carbonates decompose on heating. BeO and Al2O3
     are both amphoteric, and BeCl2 and AlCl3 are both covalent
     Lewis acids.

  7. NATURE OF THE OXIDES
        Na2O   MgO   Al2O3    SiO2   P4O10   SO3   Cl2O7
        --------------------------------------------------->
        strongly basic  amphoteric  weakly acidic   strongly
        basic                       acidic          acidic

     Metal oxides are ionic and BASIC, non-metal oxides are covalent
     and ACIDIC, and the borderline oxide Al2O3 is AMPHOTERIC:
         Al2O3 + 6 HCl   ->  2 AlCl3  + 3 H2O
         Al2O3 + 2 NaOH  ->  2 NaAlO2 + H2O
     Down a group the oxides become more basic; in group 15, N2O3 and
     P4O6 are acidic, As2O3 and Sb2O3 are amphoteric and Bi2O3 is
     basic.
```
