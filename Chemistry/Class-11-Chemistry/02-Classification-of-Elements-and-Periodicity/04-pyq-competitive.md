# PATTERN 1 — Early classification, laws and history

### Q1
According to Dobereiner's law of triads, the atomic weight of the middle
element is approximately

(a) the sum of the other two &nbsp; (b) the arithmetic mean of the other two
&nbsp; (c) the product of the other two &nbsp; (d) half of the third

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   wt(middle) = [ wt(first) + wt(third) ] / 2
   Li 6.9 , Na 23.0 , K 39.1  ->  (6.9 + 39.1)/2 = 23.0
```

---

### Q2
Newlands' law of octaves was found to be valid only up to

(a) oxygen &nbsp; (b) calcium &nbsp; (c) iron &nbsp; (d) zinc

**ANSWER: (b)** — the pattern broke down beyond calcium (Z = 20).

---

### Q3
In the Lothar Meyer curve of atomic volume against atomic weight, the
peaks are occupied by

(a) halogens &nbsp; (b) noble gases &nbsp; (c) alkali metals &nbsp;
(d) transition metals

**ANSWER: (c)** — Li, Na, K, Rb and Cs sit at the peaks because they have
the largest atomic volumes in their periods.

---

### Q4
Mendeleev's "eka-silicon" was later discovered as

(a) scandium &nbsp; (b) gallium &nbsp; (c) germanium &nbsp; (d) technetium

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   eka-boron      ->  Scandium   (Sc)
   eka-aluminium  ->  Gallium    (Ga)
   eka-silicon    ->  Germanium  (Ge)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Remember the alphabetical link — **B**oron→**S**candium,
> **A**luminium→**G**allium, **S**ilicon→**G**ermanium. The prediction pair
> always goes one step DOWN and one step RIGHT.

---

### Q5
Which of the following is NOT a defect of Mendeleev's periodic table?

(a) no fixed position for hydrogen
(b) no place for isotopes
(c) elements arranged in order of increasing atomic weight
(d) it left gaps for undiscovered elements

**ANSWER: (d)** — leaving gaps was his greatest **merit**, not a defect;
it allowed him to predict Sc, Ga and Ge.

---

### Q6
Moseley's work showed that the fundamental property of an element is its

(a) atomic weight &nbsp; (b) atomic volume &nbsp; (c) atomic number &nbsp;
(d) density

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
      sqrt ( nu )  =  a ( Z - b )

   The X-ray frequency depends on Z only, not on atomic weight.
```

---

### Q7
Which of the following pairs was placed in "anomalous" order (heavier
before lighter) in Mendeleev's table?

(a) Na and Mg &nbsp; (b) Te and I &nbsp; (c) Li and Be &nbsp; (d) C and N

**ANSWER: (b)** — Te (127.6) had to be placed before I (126.9).
The other anomalous pairs are Ar–K, Co–Ni and Th–Pa.

# PATTERN 2 — Structure of the long form table

### Q8
The number of elements in the sixth period of the periodic table is

(a) 8 &nbsp; (b) 18 &nbsp; (c) 32 &nbsp; (d) 50

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Period 6 fills   6s(2) + 4f(14) + 5d(10) + 6p(6)  =  32
```

> **SHORTCUT:** memorise the sequence **2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32**.
> Every "how many elements in period n" question dies instantly.

---

### Q9
The number of elements in which the last electron enters the 4f subshell is

(a) 10 &nbsp; (b) 14 &nbsp; (c) 18 &nbsp; (d) 32

**ANSWER: (b)** — the f subshell holds 14 electrons; these are the
14 lanthanoids, Ce (58) to Lu (71).

---

### Q10
Which of the following is NOT regarded as a typical transition element?

(a) Sc &nbsp; (b) Fe &nbsp; (c) Cu &nbsp; (d) Zn

**ANSWER: (d)** — zinc is 3d10 4s2 and its common ion Zn2+ is 3d10.
It has a completely filled d subshell in both the atom and the ion, so it
does not show the typical transition properties.

The general outer electronic configuration of the p-block elements is

(a) ns1-2 &nbsp; (b) ns2 np1-6 &nbsp; (c) (n-1)d1-10 ns1-2 &nbsp;
(d) (n-2)f1-14 ns2

**ANSWER: (b)**

# PATTERN 3 — Period, group and block from a configuration

### Q12
An element has the configuration 1s2 2s2 2p6 3s2 3p6 3d5 4s2.
Its period and group are

(a) period 3, group 7 &nbsp; (b) period 4, group 7 &nbsp;
(c) period 4, group 5 &nbsp; (d) period 3, group 5

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   highest n = 4                   ->  period 4
   last electron in 3d             ->  d-block
   group = 3d(5) + 4s(2) = 7       ->  group 7      (Mn, Z = 25)
```

---

### Q13
The element with the configuration [Kr] 4d10 5s2 5p3 belongs to

(a) group 13, period 5 &nbsp; (b) group 15, period 5 &nbsp;
(c) group 15, period 4 &nbsp; (d) group 5, period 5

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   p-block:  group = 10 + (5s2 + 5p3) = 10 + 5 = 15
   highest n = 5  ->  period 5           (Sb, Z = 51)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** for a p-block element, **group = 12 + (number of p electrons)**.
> np1→13, np2→14, np3→15, np4→16, np5→17, np6→18. One second, no arithmetic.

---

### Q14
An element X has the configuration [Ar] 3d10 4s1. It belongs to

(a) group 1, s-block &nbsp; (b) group 11, d-block &nbsp;
(c) group 12, d-block &nbsp; (d) group 10, d-block

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   group = 3d(10) + 4s(1) = 11      ->  copper, Z = 29
   The LAST electron entered 3d, so it is a d-block element even
   though the outermost electron is in 4s.
```

---

### Q15
The element with atomic number 114 belongs to which group and period?

(a) group 14, period 7 &nbsp; (b) group 4, period 7 &nbsp;
(c) group 14, period 6 &nbsp; (d) group 18, period 7

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Z = 114  ->  [Rn] 5f14 6d10 7s2 7p2
   highest n = 7 -> period 7 ; p-block, 2 p electrons -> group 14
   (It sits just below lead. Now named Flerovium, Fl.)
```

---

### Q16
Which of the following elements belongs to the f-block?

(a) Z = 26 &nbsp; (b) Z = 35 &nbsp; (c) Z = 58 &nbsp; (d) Z = 82

**ANSWER: (c)** — Z = 58 is cerium, [Xe] 4f1 5d1 6s2, the first
lanthanoid. All f-block elements are placed in group 3.

# PATTERN 4 — IUPAC nomenclature for Z above 100

### Q17
The IUPAC symbol for the element with atomic number 105 is

(a) Unp &nbsp; (b) Unq &nbsp; (c) Uup &nbsp; (d) Unh

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   105  ->  un (u) + nil (n) + pent (p)   ->   Unp
            name : Unnilpentium      (now Dubnium, Db)
```

---

### Q18
The IUPAC name of the element with atomic number 120 is

(a) Ununnilium &nbsp; (b) Unbinilium &nbsp; (c) Unnilbium &nbsp;
(d) Ununbium

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   120  ->  1     2    0
            un    bi   nil    +  ium   =  UNBINILIUM  ,  symbol Ubn
```

> **SHORTCUT:** learn the ten roots as a single chant —
> **nil un bi tri quad pent hex sept oct enn**. Then just spell out the digits.
> The symbol is the first letter of each root, three letters,
> **only the first one capital**.

---

### Q19
The element "Unnilennium" has the atomic number

(a) 109 &nbsp; (b) 190 &nbsp; (c) 108 &nbsp; (d) 119

**ANSWER: (a)** — un(1) + nil(0) + enn(9) = 109 (now Meitnerium, Mt).

# PATTERN 5 — Atomic and ionic size ordering

### Q20
The correct order of atomic radii is

(a) Na < Mg < Al < Si &nbsp; (b) Si < Al < Mg < Na &nbsp;
(c) Mg < Na < Si < Al &nbsp; (d) Al < Si < Na < Mg

**ANSWER: (b)** — all are in period 3, and the radius **decreases** across a
period because Z(eff) increases while the electrons enter the same shell.

---

### Q21
Which of the following has the largest size?

(a) Na &nbsp; (b) Na+ &nbsp; (c) Mg &nbsp; (d) Mg2+

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Na > Mg  (radius falls across the period)
   and every atom is bigger than its own cation.
   Order :  Na > Mg > Na+ > Mg2+
```

---

### Q22
The correct order of ionic radii is

(a) N3- < O2- < F- < Na+ &nbsp; (b) Na+ < F- < O2- < N3- &nbsp;
(c) F- < O2- < Na+ < N3- &nbsp; (d) O2- < F- < N3- < Na+

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   All four are isoelectronic (10 electrons).
   RULE : larger nuclear charge -> smaller ion.
   Z :  N 7 , O 8 , F 9 , Na 11
   Size :  N3- > O2- > F- > Na+
   In INCREASING order that is  Na+ < F- < O2- < N3-
```

> **SHORTCUT:** for an isoelectronic set, write them in order of
> **increasing atomic number** — the size order is automatically the reverse.

---

### Q23
Which one of the following is the smallest in size?

(a) N3- &nbsp; (b) O2- &nbsp; (c) F- &nbsp; (d) Al3+

**ANSWER: (d)** — all have 10 electrons; Al has the highest nuclear charge
(Z = 13), so Al3+ is the smallest (about 50 pm).

---

### Q24
Van der Waals radius, covalent radius and metallic radius of the same
element are in the order

(a) covalent > metallic > van der Waals
(b) van der Waals > metallic > covalent
(c) metallic > van der Waals > covalent
(d) all are equal

**ANSWER: (b)** — a covalent bond pulls the atoms right into each other, a
metallic bond is weaker, and van der Waals contact barely pulls at all.

---

# PATTERN 6 — Ionisation enthalpy

### Q25
The correct order of first ionisation enthalpy is

(a) Li < Be < B < C &nbsp; (b) Li < B < Be < C &nbsp;
(c) B < Li < Be < C &nbsp; (d) C < B < Be < Li

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Values (kJ/mol) :  Li 520 , Be 899 , B 801 , C 1086
   Be is HIGHER than B because Be has a stable fully-filled 2s2,
   while boron loses a 2p electron that is higher in energy and
   shielded by the 2s2 pair.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** in every period the trend rises, but **dips at group 13 and
> at group 16**. Memory line: **"Be Beats B, N Beats O"** (and Mg > Al, P > S).

---

### Q26
Which of the following has the highest first ionisation enthalpy?

(a) N &nbsp; (b) O &nbsp; (c) F &nbsp; (d) Ne

**ANSWER: (d)** — Ne has a completely filled 2s2 2p6 shell and the highest
Z(eff) in period 2. Its IE1 is 2081 kJ/mol.

---

### Q27
The successive ionisation enthalpies of an element (kJ/mol) are
577, 1820, 2750 and 11600. The element belongs to group

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 2 &nbsp; (c) 13 &nbsp; (d) 14

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   577  ->  1820  ->  2750  ->  11600
                          |______|
                          HUGE JUMP after the THIRD electron

   So the element has 3 valence electrons  ->  group 13
   (It is aluminium.)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** count how many ionisations happen BEFORE the huge jump.
> That number = number of valence electrons = the group (1, 2 or 13–17).

---

### Q28
The second ionisation enthalpy is always greater than the first because

(a) the atom becomes larger
(b) the cation formed is smaller and holds its electrons more tightly
(c) the nuclear charge decreases
(d) the shielding increases

**ANSWER: (b)** — after losing one electron the same nuclear charge acts on
fewer electrons, so Z(eff) per electron rises and the ion contracts.

---

### Q29
Which of the following pairs shows a break in the normal increasing trend
of ionisation enthalpy across period 3?

(a) Na and Mg &nbsp; (b) Mg and Al &nbsp; (c) Si and P &nbsp; (d) Cl and Ar

**ANSWER: (b)** — Mg (737) > Al (577), the period-3 version of the Be > B
anomaly. The other break in period 3 is P (1012) > S (1000).

---

# PATTERN 7 — Electron gain enthalpy

### Q30
The element with the most negative electron gain enthalpy is

(a) F &nbsp; (b) Cl &nbsp; (c) Br &nbsp; (d) O

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Cl  -349   >   F  -328   >   Br  -325   >   I  -295   kJ/mol

   Chlorine has the most negative electron gain enthalpy of ALL
   the elements. Fluorine is smaller, so the incoming electron
   suffers strong electron-electron repulsion in its compact 2p
   subshell, which cancels part of the energy released.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** the "top element is not the best acceptor" rule.
> In group 17 it is **Cl > F**, and in group 16 it is **S > O**.
> Same reason both times: 2p is too small and too crowded.

---

### Q31
The second electron gain enthalpy of oxygen is positive because

(a) oxygen is very electronegative
(b) the O- ion repels the incoming electron
(c) oxygen has a half-filled 2p subshell
(d) the O2- ion is very small

**ANSWER: (b)** — the incoming electron must be forced into an already
negatively charged ion, so energy has to be supplied.
```
   O(g)  + e-  ->  O-(g)     delta H  =  -141 kJ/mol
   O-(g) + e-  ->  O2-(g)    delta H  =  +780 kJ/mol
```

---

### Q32
Which of the following has a POSITIVE electron gain enthalpy?

(a) Cl &nbsp; (b) S &nbsp; (c) Ne &nbsp; (d) Br

**ANSWER: (c)** — the noble gases have completely filled ns2 np6 shells, so
an incoming electron would have to enter the next shell. The same is true of
group 2 elements (Be, Mg) which have filled ns2.

---

# PATTERN 8 — Electronegativity

### Q33
The correct order of electronegativity on the Pauling scale is

(a) F > Cl > Br > I &nbsp; (b) I > Br > Cl > F &nbsp;
(c) Cl > F > Br > I &nbsp; (d) F > Br > Cl > I

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   F 4.0  >  Cl 3.0  >  Br 2.8  >  I 2.5
   Electronegativity DECREASES down a group (size and shielding rise).
```

> **TRAP:** do NOT confuse this with electron gain enthalpy, where the order
> is **Cl > F**. Electronegativity is a smooth trend; electron gain enthalpy
> is not.

---

### Q34
The most electropositive element in the periodic table is

(a) Li &nbsp; (b) Na &nbsp; (c) Cs &nbsp; (d) F

**ANSWER: (c)** — caesium has the lowest ionisation enthalpy and the lowest
electronegativity (0.7), so it loses electrons most readily.

---

### Q35
Electronegativity differs from electron gain enthalpy in that
electronegativity

(a) is measured in kJ/mol
(b) refers to an isolated gaseous atom
(c) refers to a bonded atom and has no units
(d) is always negative

**ANSWER: (c)**

# PATTERN 9 — Anomalies, diagonal relationship, oxides

### Q36
Which pair of elements shows a diagonal relationship?

(a) Li and Na &nbsp; (b) Be and Al &nbsp; (c) B and Al &nbsp;
(d) C and N

**ANSWER: (b)** — the diagonal pairs are Li~Mg, Be~Al and B~Si.
Both BeO and Al2O3 are amphoteric, and both chlorides are covalent
Lewis acids.

---

### Q37
Nitrogen does not form NCl5 because

(a) nitrogen is a gas
(b) nitrogen has no vacant d orbitals in its valence shell
(c) chlorine is too large
(d) nitrogen is electropositive

**ANSWER: (b)** — the second shell has only 2s and 2p orbitals, so the
maximum covalency of a second-period element is 4. Phosphorus has vacant
3d orbitals and does form PCl5.

---

### Q38
Which of the following oxides is amphoteric?

(a) Na2O &nbsp; (b) MgO &nbsp; (c) Al2O3 &nbsp; (d) SO3

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Al2O3 + 6 HCl   ->  2 AlCl3  + 3 H2O     (behaves as a base)
   Al2O3 + 2 NaOH  ->  2 NaAlO2 + H2O       (behaves as an acid)

   Other amphoteric oxides : BeO , ZnO , PbO , SnO , As2O3 , Sb2O3
```

---

### Q39
The most acidic oxide among the following is

(a) Na2O &nbsp; (b) MgO &nbsp; (c) SiO2 &nbsp; (d) Cl2O7

**ANSWER: (d)** — acidic character of oxides increases across a period as
the element becomes more non-metallic. Cl2O7 is the oxide of the most
electronegative element in period 3.

---

### Q40
In group 15, the oxide that is basic is

(a) N2O3 &nbsp; (b) P4O6 &nbsp; (c) As2O3 &nbsp; (d) Bi2O3

**ANSWER: (d)** — metallic character increases down a group, so the oxides
become more basic: N2O3 acidic, P4O6 acidic, As2O3 and Sb2O3 amphoteric,
Bi2O3 basic.

---

### Q41
Chemical reactivity across a period is

(a) highest in the middle
(b) highest at both ends and lowest in the middle
(c) constant
(d) lowest at both ends

**ANSWER: (b)** — the left end loses electrons very easily (low IE) and the
right end gains them very readily (very negative electron gain enthalpy);
the middle elements do neither easily.

---

### Q42
The screening (shielding) power of the subshells follows the order

(a) f > d > p > s &nbsp; (b) s > p > d > f &nbsp; (c) p > s > d > f &nbsp;
(d) d > s > p > f

**ANSWER: (b)** — an s electron penetrates closest to the nucleus, so it
screens the outer electrons best.

```
  THE 10-SECOND MENTAL PICTURE

     Going RIGHT  ->  atom gets SMALLER and holds electrons HARDER
                      (IE up, EN up, non-metallic up, oxides acidic)

     Going DOWN   ->  atom gets BIGGER and gives electrons AWAY
                      (IE down, EN down, metallic up, oxides basic)

  Every question in this chapter is one of those two lines,
  plus about six exceptions. Learn the exceptions and you are done.
```
