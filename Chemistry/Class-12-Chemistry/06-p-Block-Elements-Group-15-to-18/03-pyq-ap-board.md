**A1. What is the inert pair effect?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   The reluctance of the ns2 electron pair to take part in bond
>   formation, because the nucleus is poorly shielded by the
>   intervening d and f electrons, is called the INERT PAIR EFFECT.
>
>   As a result the stability of the LOWER oxidation state (group
>   valency minus 2) INCREASES down the group.
>
>   Example: in group 15, N and P prefer +5, but Bi strongly prefers
>            +3. BiCl3 is stable while BiCl5 hardly exists.
> ```

---

**A2. Why does the +3 oxidation state become more stable than +5 down group 15?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because of the INERT PAIR EFFECT. Going down the group the ns2
>   electron pair is held more and more tightly (poor shielding by the
>   d and f electrons), so it stops participating in bonding. Only the
>   three np electrons are used, giving the +3 state.
>
>        N, P  ->  +5 preferred        Sb, Bi  ->  +3 preferred
> ```

---

**A3. Why is Bi(V) a stronger oxidising agent than Sb(V)?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because the inert pair effect is much stronger in bismuth, the +5
>   state of Bi is very unstable and is desperate to return to the
>   stable +3 state. To do that it must ACCEPT electrons - which is
>   exactly what an oxidising agent does. Sb(V) is comparatively
>   stable, so it is a weaker oxidiser.
> ```

---

**A4. Why does NH3 have a higher boiling point than PH3?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   NH3 boils at 239.7 K ; PH3 boils at 185.5 K.
>
>   Nitrogen is SMALL and HIGHLY ELECTRONEGATIVE (3.0), so the N-H
>   bond is strongly polar and NH3 molecules are held together by
>   INTERMOLECULAR HYDROGEN BONDING. Extra energy is needed to break
>   these hydrogen bonds, so the boiling point is high.
>
>   Phosphorus is large and has almost the same electronegativity as
>   hydrogen (2.1), so the P-H bond is practically NON-POLAR. PH3 has
>   NO hydrogen bonding - only weak van der Waals forces - so it boils
>   much lower.
> ```

---

**A5. Give the bond angle order in the hydrides of group 15 and give the reason.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   NH3 (107.8) > PH3 (93.6) > AsH3 (91.8) > SbH3 (91.3) > BiH3 (~90)
>
>   REASON: the ELECTRONEGATIVITY of the central atom decreases down
>   the group. In NH3 the electronegative N pulls the bonding pairs
>   close to itself; they crowd together, repel strongly and open the
>   angle out. Down the group the central atom pulls weakly, the bond
>   pairs sit far away near the H atoms, repulsion is small and the
>   angle falls towards 90 degrees - the angle between PURE p ORBITALS,
>   which the heavier elements use in place of sp3 hybrid orbitals.
> ```

---

**A6. Why is NF3 less basic than NH3?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Basicity depends on how freely the LONE PAIR on nitrogen can be
>   donated.
>
>   In NH3 the three H atoms are LESS electronegative than N, so they
>   PUSH electron density towards N. The lone pair is electron rich
>   and easily donated -> strongly basic.
>
>   In NF3 the three F atoms are MORE electronegative than N, so they
>   PULL electron density AWAY from N (-I effect). The lone pair is
>   contracted and held tightly, so it is donated with great
>   difficulty -> very weakly basic.
> ```

---

**A7. Why is NCl3 hydrolysed by water while NF3 is not?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   In NCl3 the Cl atom has VACANT 3d ORBITALS which can accept the
>   lone pair of a water molecule, so hydrolysis proceeds:
>
>        NCl3 + 3 H2O  -->  NH3 + 3 HOCl
>
>   In NF3 neither the N atom nor the F atom has any vacant d orbital
>   (both are period-2 elements), so water has nothing to attack.
>   Also the N-F bond is very strong. Hence NF3 is not hydrolysed.
> ```

---

**A8. Why is N2 less reactive at room temperature?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   In the N2 molecule the two atoms are joined by a TRIPLE BOND whose
>   bond dissociation enthalpy is 941.4 kJ/mol - one of the strongest
>   bonds known. An enormous amount of energy is needed to break it,
>   so N2 does not react at ordinary temperature.
> ```

---

**A9. State the conditions used in the Haber process.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>        N2(g) + 3 H2(g)  <==>  2 NH3(g)     dH = -46.1 kJ/mol
>
>        PRESSURE     :  200 atm
>        TEMPERATURE  :  700 K   (an OPTIMUM / compromise temperature)
>        CATALYST     :  IRON OXIDE (finely divided iron)
>        PROMOTERS    :  K2O and Al2O3
> ```

---

**A10. Why is a high pressure used in the Haber process?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>        N2 + 3 H2 <==> 2 NH3
>        LEFT = 4 moles of gas       RIGHT = 2 moles of gas
>
>   The forward reaction DECREASES the number of gaseous moles. By
>   LE CHATELIER'S PRINCIPLE, increasing the pressure shifts the
>   equilibrium towards the side with FEWER molecules - the right -
>   so the yield of ammonia increases.
> ```

---

**A11. The Haber process is exothermic, so why is it not carried out at a low temperature?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   A low temperature would indeed give a BETTER YIELD (Le Chatelier),
>   but the RATE would be far too slow, because the very strong
>   N(triple)N bond would not break.
>
>   So 700 K is used as a COMPROMISE (OPTIMUM) temperature: high
>   enough for a workable rate, low enough for an acceptable yield.
> ```

> ```

**A13. Why is concentrated nitric acid stored in dark bottles / why does it turn yellow?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Nitric acid decomposes in light and on warming:
>
>        4 HNO3  --(heat / light)-->  4 NO2 + 2 H2O + O2
>
>   The brown NO2 produced dissolves in the acid and colours it
>   YELLOW or brown. A dark bottle keeps the light out and prevents
>   this decomposition.
> ```

---

**A16. Why is hydrogen gas not produced when a metal reacts with nitric acid?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because HNO3 is a powerful OXIDISING AGENT. The nitrate ion NO3-
>   accepts electrons more readily than H+ does, so it is the nitrate
>   - not the hydrogen ion - that is reduced. The products are
>   therefore NO, NO2 or N2O instead of H2.
>
>   (Exception: very dilute HNO3 with Mg or Mn does give H2.)
> ```

---

**A17. Why is white phosphorus more reactive than red phosphorus?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   White phosphorus exists as discrete P4 TETRAHEDRA in which the
>   P-P-P bond angle is forced down to only 60 degrees. This creates
>   a large ANGULAR STRAIN, so the P-P bonds are weak and break
>   easily -> HIGH REACTIVITY.
>
>   In red phosphorus the tetrahedra are opened out and joined into a
>   POLYMERIC CHAIN. The strain is relieved and the structure is a
>   stable giant covalent one -> LOW REACTIVITY.
> ```

---

**A18. Why is white phosphorus stored under water?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Its ignition temperature is only 303 K (30 degrees C), which is
>   below normal room temperature in summer, so in air it would catch
>   fire on its own. White phosphorus is insoluble in water, so
>   keeping it under water safely cuts off the air.
> ```

**A20. Why does PCl5 have two different P-Cl bond lengths?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   PCl5 is TRIGONAL BIPYRAMIDAL (sp3d).
>        3 EQUATORIAL P-Cl bonds = 202 pm
>        2 AXIAL      P-Cl bonds = 240 pm
>
>   Each AXIAL bond pair is repelled by THREE equatorial bond pairs at
>   90 degrees, whereas each EQUATORIAL bond pair is repelled by only
>   TWO bond pairs at 90 degrees. The greater repulsion pushes the
>   axial bonds further out, making them LONGER and WEAKER.
> ```

**A23. Give the basicity of H3PO2, H3PO3 and H3PO4 and justify it.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Basicity = the number of O-H groups. A hydrogen bonded directly
>   to PHOSPHORUS is NOT ionisable, because the P-H bond is almost
>   non-polar.
>
>        H3PO2   2 P-H , 1 O-H   ->  MONOBASIC
>        H3PO3   1 P-H , 2 O-H   ->  DIBASIC
>        H3PO4   0 P-H , 3 O-H   ->  TRIBASIC
> ```

---

**A24. Why are H3PO2 and H3PO3 reducing agents while H3PO4 is not?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Reducing power comes from the P-H BOND. H3PO2 has two P-H bonds
>   and H3PO3 has one, so both can give up hydrogen and act as
>   reducing agents. H3PO4 has NO P-H bond at all, and phosphorus in
>   it is already in its maximum oxidation state (+5), so it cannot
>   be oxidised further and is NOT a reducing agent.
>
>        4 AgNO3 + 2 H2O + H3PO2 --> 4 Ag + 4 HNO3 + H3PO4
> ```

**A25. How is ozone prepared? Why is a silent electric discharge used?**

**A26. Draw the structure of ozone. Why are both O-O bonds equal in length?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>                    O
>                  // \\        ANGULAR (bent), sp2 hybridised
>                 //   \\       bond angle = 117 degrees
>                O       O      both O-O bonds = 128 pm
>
>   Both bonds are EQUAL because ozone is a RESONANCE HYBRID of the
>   two structures below - the double bond is DELOCALISED over both
>   positions rather than fixed on one side. The observed length,
>   128 pm, lies between a single O-O bond (148 pm) and a double
>   O=O bond (121 pm).
>
>        O                  O
>      // \      <-->      /  \\
>     O     O             O      O
> ```

**A28. Why is SO3 not dissolved directly in water in the Contact process?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because SO3 + H2O --> H2SO4 is VIOLENTLY exothermic and produces
>   a dense choking MIST (fog) of sulphuric acid droplets which does
>   not condense, so most of the product would be lost.
>
>   Instead SO3 is absorbed in concentrated H2SO4 to give OLEUM,
>        SO3 + H2SO4 --> H2S2O7
>   which is then safely diluted:
>        H2S2O7 + H2O --> 2 H2SO4
> ```

>   of dissolving sulphur trioxide in concentrated sulphuric acid.
>
>        Formula :  H2S2O7
>        SO3 + H2SO4  -->  H2S2O7
>

> ```

**A30. Why does concentrated H2SO4 char sugar?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because concentrated H2SO4 is a powerful DEHYDRATING AGENT. It
>   has an enormous affinity for water and pulls the hydrogen and
>   oxygen out of the sugar molecule as water, leaving behind carbon:
>
>        C12H22O11  --(conc. H2SO4)-->  12 C  +  11 H2O
>
>   A swelling BLACK spongy mass of carbon is left and the vessel
>   becomes hot.
> ```

**A32. Why is the electron gain enthalpy of fluorine less negative than that of chlorine?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because the fluorine atom is EXTREMELY SMALL and its 2p subshell
>   is very compact and already crowded with electrons. The incoming
>   electron therefore suffers strong INTER-ELECTRONIC REPULSION, so
>   less energy is released.
>
>   Chlorine's 3p subshell is larger and more diffuse, so the extra
>   electron is accommodated comfortably and more energy is released.
>
>        ORDER:  Cl > F > Br > I   (most negative first)
> ```

---

**A33. Why is F2 a stronger oxidising agent than Cl2 even though its electron gain enthalpy is less negative?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Oxidising power in solution depends on the WHOLE energy cycle,
>   not on electron gain enthalpy alone. For fluorine BOTH of the
>   other two terms are strongly favourable:
>
>        (i)  the F-F bond dissociation enthalpy is very LOW, so F2
>             splits into atoms easily;
>        (ii) the F- ion is very small, so its HYDRATION ENTHALPY is
>             very high (very negative).
>
>   These two outweigh the smaller electron gain enthalpy, making F2
>   the strongest oxidising agent among the halogens.
> ```

---

**A34. Why is the bond dissociation enthalpy of F2 lower than that of Cl2?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because the two fluorine atoms are very small, the F-F bond is
>   very short. The three LONE PAIRS on each atom are therefore
>   forced very close together and repel one another strongly, which
>   weakens the bond.
>
>        ORDER:  Cl2 > Br2 > F2 > I2
> ```

**A35. Explain the bleaching action of chlorine.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Chlorine bleaches only IN THE PRESENCE OF MOISTURE.
>
>        Cl2 + H2O  -->  HCl + HOCl
>        HOCl       -->  HCl + [O]        (nascent oxygen)
>        coloured substance + [O]  -->  colourless substance
>
>   Overall:  Cl2 + H2O --> 2 HCl + [O]
>
>   It bleaches by OXIDATION, which destroys the dye molecule, so the
>   bleaching is PERMANENT.
> ```

---

**A36. Why is the bleaching action of SO2 temporary but that of Cl2 permanent?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   SO2 bleaches by REDUCTION:
>        SO2 + 2 H2O  -->  H2SO4 + 2 [H]
>        coloured substance + 2[H] --> colourless
>   The dye is only reduced, not destroyed. Atmospheric oxygen
>   re-oxidises it later and the ORIGINAL COLOUR RETURNS -> TEMPORARY.
>
>   Cl2 bleaches by OXIDATION with nascent oxygen, which chemically
>   DESTROYS the coloured molecule for good -> PERMANENT.
> ```

**A38. Give the acid strength order of the oxoacids of chlorine and the reason.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>        HOCl  <  HClO2  <  HClO3  <  HClO4        (increasing)
>         +1       +3        +5        +7
>
>   REASON: each additional highly electronegative OXYGEN pulls
>   electron density away from the O-H bond, weakening it so that H+
>   is released more easily. The anion left behind also spreads its
>   negative charge over more oxygen atoms (better resonance
>   stabilisation), so it is more stable.
>
>   HClO4 is one of the strongest acids known.
> ```

---

**A39. Why does fluorine form only one oxoacid, HOF?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Because fluorine is the MOST ELECTRONEGATIVE element and has NO
>   d ORBITALS in its valence shell. It can therefore never take a
>   POSITIVE oxidation state, which is required in HXO2, HXO3 and
>   HXO4. Only HOF, where F is -1, is possible - and even that is
>   unstable.
> ```

---

**A40. Why are interhalogen compounds more reactive than the halogens?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   The X-X' bond joins two DIFFERENT atoms of different size and
>   electronegativity, so it is POLAR and WEAKER than the pure
>   non-polar X-X bond of an ordinary halogen molecule. A weaker
>   bond breaks more easily, so interhalogens are more reactive.
>
>   (F2 is the exception - it is the most reactive substance of all.)
> ```

**A41. Give the shape and hybridisation of ClF3 and IF7.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   ClF3 :  Cl uses 3 electrons for bonding and keeps 2 LONE PAIRS.
>           Total pairs = 5  ->  sp3d hybridisation.
>           Shape = BENT T-SHAPED (the lone pairs sit equatorially).
>
>   IF7  :  I uses all 7 electrons for bonding, 0 lone pairs.

>           Shape = PENTAGONAL BIPYRAMIDAL.
> ```

**A42. Why are the noble gases chemically inert?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   1. Their outermost shell is COMPLETELY FILLED, ns2 np6 (He = 1s2)
>      - the most stable possible arrangement.
>   2. They have VERY HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPIES, so they will not
>      lose an electron.
>   3. Their ELECTRON GAIN ENTHALPY IS POSITIVE, so they will not
>      gain an electron either.
>
>   With no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons they form no
>   bonds and exist as MONOATOMIC gases.
> ```

---

**A43. Why does only xenon among the noble gases form compounds?**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   Xenon is large, so its outermost electrons are far from the
>   nucleus and poorly shielded. Its IONISATION ENTHALPY is therefore
>   LOW ENOUGH for the strongest oxidising elements - FLUORINE and
>   OXYGEN - to pull electrons away from it.
>
>   Radon would react even more readily but it is intensely
>   radioactive and short lived, so its chemistry is barely studied.
> ```

---

**A44. Give the structure and hybridisation of XeF2 and XeF4.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   XeF2 :  2 bond pairs + 3 LONE PAIRS = 5 pairs -> sp3d.
>           The 3 lone pairs occupy the equatorial positions, so the
>           shape is LINEAR (F-Xe-F = 180 degrees).
>
>   XeF4 :  4 bond pairs + 2 LONE PAIRS = 6 pairs -> sp3d2.
>           The 2 lone pairs sit opposite each other (above and
>           below), so the shape is SQUARE PLANAR (all angles 90).
> ```

---

**A45. Give the structure and hybridisation of XeF6 and XeO3.**

> **ANSWER:**

**B1. Explain the anomalous behaviour of nitrogen.**

> **ANSWER:**
>

>     1. very SMALL ATOMIC SIZE
>     2. very HIGH ELECTRONEGATIVITY and high ionisation enthalpy

>       no d orbitals to expand its octet. Hence NCl5 does not exist

**B2. Discuss the trends in the hydrides of group 15 (stability, basicity and reducing character).**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   The hydrides are  NH3, PH3, AsH3, SbH3, BiH3.
>   All are PYRAMIDAL (3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair, sp3).
>
>   1. THERMAL STABILITY   NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 > BiH3
>      As the central atom gets bigger, the E-H bond gets LONGER and
>      its bond enthalpy FALLS, so the hydride decomposes more easily.
>      BiH3 is so unstable it is difficult even to prepare.

>      its electron density falls and it is donated less readily.

>      character at all.

>      Exactly the reverse, and for the same reason - the weakest
>      E-H bond gives up hydrogen most readily.

>                              > SbH3 (91.3) > BiH3 (~90)

>      repulsion falls; the heavier atoms use nearly PURE p orbitals,
>      which are at 90 degrees.
>
>   5. BOILING POINT       NH3 > SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3
>      NH3 is abnormally high because of HYDROGEN BONDING; after that
>      the boiling points rise with molecular size.
> ```

**B20. What is aqua regia? Explain how it dissolves gold, and give the reactions of concentrated HNO3 with copper and with carbon.**

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   AQUA REGIA = 3 parts concentrated HCl : 1 part concentrated HNO3
>   (by volume). The Latin name means "royal water".
>

>     (i)  the HNO3 acts as a strong OXIDISING AGENT and oxidises

>

> ```

## C1. How is ammonia manufactured by Haber's process? Explain the conditions using Le Chatelier's principle, and give the properties of ammonia. (8 marks)

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   MARK SPLIT:  equation + conditions 2 | Le Chatelier reasoning 3
>                flow diagram 1 | properties 2
> ```
>
> **1. THE REACTION (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>        N2(g)  +  3 H2(g)   <==>   2 NH3(g)
>
>                 dH  =  - 46.1 kJ / mol      (EXOTHERMIC)
>                 dS  =  negative  (4 moles of gas -> 2 moles)
>
>   RAW MATERIALS: nitrogen from liquid air; hydrogen from natural
>   gas or the water-gas shift reaction.
> ```
>
> **2. THE CONDITIONS (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>        PRESSURE     :  200 atm    ( 200 x 10^5 Pa )
>        TEMPERATURE  :  700 K      (an OPTIMUM temperature)
>        CATALYST     :  IRON OXIDE (finely divided iron)
>        PROMOTERS    :  K2O  and  Al2O3
> ```
>
> **3. JUSTIFICATION BY LE CHATELIER'S PRINCIPLE (3 marks)**
>
> ```
>   LE CHATELIER'S PRINCIPLE: if a system at equilibrium is
>   disturbed, the equilibrium shifts in the direction that opposes
>   the disturbance.
>
>   (a) EFFECT OF PRESSURE
>       Left side has 4 moles of gas, right side has 2. The forward
>       reaction DECREASES the number of gas molecules, so raising
>       the pressure drives the equilibrium to the RIGHT.
>       ->  HIGH pressure (200 atm) is used.
>
>   (b) EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE
>       The reaction is EXOTHERMIC, so a LOW temperature would give
>       the BEST YIELD. But at a low temperature the rate is
>       hopelessly slow, because the N(triple)N bond (941 kJ/mol)
>       will not break.
>       ->  700 K is a COMPROMISE, the "OPTIMUM TEMPERATURE":
>           low enough for a good yield, high enough for a good rate.
>
>   (c) EFFECT OF THE CATALYST
>       Iron lowers the ACTIVATION ENERGY, so equilibrium is reached
>       much FASTER. A catalyst speeds up the forward and backward
>       reactions equally, so it does NOT alter the yield or the
>       value of Kp - it only saves time. The promoters K2O and
>       Al2O3 improve the catalyst's efficiency and its life.
>
>   (d) REMOVAL OF THE PRODUCT
>       The outgoing gas is cooled; ammonia liquefies (b.p. 239.7 K)
>       while N2 and H2 remain gases. Removing a PRODUCT shifts the
>       equilibrium to the RIGHT to replace it, so more NH3 is made.
>       The unreacted N2 and H2 are RECYCLED.
> ```
>
> **4. FLOW DIAGRAM (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>    N2 (liquid air)  ---+
>                        +--> COMPRESSOR (200 atm)
>    H2 (natural gas) ---+              |
>                                       v
>                      +-------------------------------+
>                      |   CATALYST CHAMBER            |
>                      |   Fe + K2O + Al2O3            |
>                      |   700 K , 200 atm             |
>                      +-------------------------------+
>                                       |
>                      NH3 + unreacted N2 and H2
>                                       v
>                      +-------------------------------+
>                      |   CONDENSER (cooling)         |
>                      |   NH3 liquefies, drawn off    |
>                      +-------------------------------+
>                        |                        |
>                        v                        |
>                  LIQUID AMMONIA        unreacted N2 + H2
>                    (product)                    |
>                        +-- RECYCLED to compressor <--+
> ```
>
> **5. PROPERTIES OF AMMONIA (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   PHYSICAL: colourless gas with a pungent smell; highly soluble in
>   water because of hydrogen bonding; pyramidal, sp3, bond angle
>   107.8 degrees; m.p. 198.4 K, b.p. 239.7 K.
>
>   CHEMICAL:
>     (i)   BASIC:      NH3 + H2O <=> NH4+ + OH-
>                       NH3 + HCl --> NH4Cl  (dense WHITE fumes)
>     (ii)  PRECIPITATES metal hydroxides:
>                       FeCl3 + 3 NH4OH --> Fe(OH)3 (brown) + 3 NH4Cl
>     (iii) FORMS COMPLEXES:
>                       Cu2+ + 4 NH3 --> [Cu(NH3)4]2+  DEEP BLUE
>     (iv)  BURNS:      4 NH3 + 3 O2 --> 2 N2 + 6 H2O
>                       4 NH3 + 5 O2 --(Pt/Rh)--> 4 NO + 6 H2O
>
>   USES: fertilisers (urea, ammonium sulphate, ammonium nitrate);
>   manufacture of nitric acid and sodium carbonate; as a
>   refrigerant, because liquid ammonia has a large enthalpy of
>   vaporisation.
> ```

## C2. Describe the manufacture of nitric acid by Ostwald's process. Give the reactions of nitric acid with copper (dilute and concentrated) and its uses. (8 marks)

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   MARK SPLIT:  three steps with conditions 3 | concentration 1
>                flow line 1 | Cu reactions 2 | uses 1
> ```
>
> **1. PRINCIPLE (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   Nitric acid is made by the CATALYTIC OXIDATION OF AMMONIA (which
>   itself comes from the Haber process), in three stages.
> ```
>
> **2. THE THREE STEPS (3 marks)**
>
> ```
>   STEP 1 - CATALYTIC OXIDATION OF AMMONIA
>
>     4 NH3(g) + 5 O2(g) <=(Pt/Rh gauze, 500 K, 9 bar)=> 4 NO + 6 H2O
>
>        Catalyst    :  PLATINUM - RHODIUM GAUZE
>        Temperature :  500 K
>        Pressure    :  9 bar
>        The contact time is only about ONE MILLISECOND - if the gas
>        stays in contact longer, NO decomposes back to N2 and the
>        yield falls. The reaction is exothermic and, once lit, is
>        self-sustaining; the gauze glows red hot.
>
>   STEP 2 - OXIDATION OF NITRIC OXIDE (the gases are cooled first)
>
>     2 NO(g) + O2(g)  <==>  2 NO2(g)
>
>        Favoured by LOW temperature and HIGH pressure.
>        The colourless NO turns BROWN as NO2 is formed.
>
>   STEP 3 - ABSORPTION OF NO2 IN WATER
>
>     3 NO2(g) + H2O(l)  -->  2 HNO3(aq)  +  NO(g)
>
>        The NO produced here is RECYCLED into step 2, so nothing is
>        wasted. Modern plants use excess air:
>            4 NO2 + O2 + 2 H2O --> 4 HNO3
> ```
>
> **3. CONCENTRATION OF THE ACID (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   The acid obtained is about 68 % by mass. Ordinary distillation
>   cannot concentrate it further because 68 % HNO3 forms an
>   AZEOTROPE (constant boiling mixture, b.p. 393 K).
>
>   To obtain 98 % "fuming nitric acid", the 68 % acid is DEHYDRATED
>   using CONCENTRATED SULPHURIC ACID.
> ```
>
> **4. FLOW LINE (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>    NH3 + air --> [CONVERTER  Pt/Rh gauze, 500 K, 9 bar] --> NO
>       --> [COOLER, then OXIDATION with air] --> NO2
>       --> [ABSORPTION TOWER with water] --> 68 % HNO3
>       --> [DEHYDRATION with conc. H2SO4] --> 98 % HNO3
> ```
>
> **5. REACTIONS WITH COPPER (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   WITH DILUTE NITRIC ACID
>        3 Cu + 8 HNO3(dilute) --> 3 Cu(NO3)2 + 2 NO + 4 H2O
>        Colourless NITRIC OXIDE is evolved; it turns brown at the
>        mouth of the tube on meeting air (2 NO + O2 --> 2 NO2).
>        The solution turns BLUE because of Cu2+ ions.
>
>   WITH CONCENTRATED NITRIC ACID
>        Cu + 4 HNO3(conc.) --> Cu(NO3)2 + 2 NO2 + 2 H2O
>        Dense REDDISH BROWN fumes of nitrogen dioxide appear at once
>        and the reaction is vigorous.
>
>   WHY NO HYDROGEN IS EVOLVED
>        HNO3 is a powerful OXIDISING AGENT. The nitrate ion accepts
>        electrons more readily than H+ does, so NO3- is reduced
>        instead of H+, giving NO, NO2 or N2O rather than H2.
>
>   FOR COMPARISON, ZINC
>        4 Zn + 10 HNO3(dilute) --> 4 Zn(NO3)2 + N2O + 5 H2O
>          Zn + 4 HNO3(conc.)   -->   Zn(NO3)2 + 2 NO2 + 2 H2O
>
>   PASSIVITY: Cr, Al and Fe become PASSIVE in concentrated HNO3
>   because a thin protective oxide layer forms on the surface.
>   Gold and platinum are not attacked at all - only AQUA REGIA
>   (3 conc. HCl : 1 conc. HNO3) dissolves them.
> ```
>
> **6. USES (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   Manufacture of ammonium nitrate FERTILISER; EXPLOSIVES (TNT,
>   nitroglycerine, picric acid); nitration in the dye and drug
>   industries; pickling of stainless steel; etching of metals;
>   as an oxidiser in rocket fuel; and as a laboratory reagent.
> ```

## C3. Describe the manufacture of sulphuric acid by the Contact process and explain its properties as an acid, a dehydrating agent and an oxidising agent. (8 marks)

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   MARK SPLIT:  the five steps 3 | Le Chatelier / why oleum 2
>                flow line 1 | three properties 2
> ```
>
> **1. THE FIVE STEPS (3 marks)**
>
> ```
>   STEP 1 - PRODUCE SULPHUR DIOXIDE
>        S + O2  -->  SO2
>        4 FeS2 + 11 O2 --> 2 Fe2O3 + 8 SO2   (roasting iron pyrites)
>
>   STEP 2 - PURIFY THE SO2
>        The gas is passed through a dust precipitator, cooling
>        pipes, a scrubber, a drying tower (conc. H2SO4) and an
>        arsenic purifier. DUST and especially ARSENIC compounds
>        must be removed because they POISON the catalyst, and the
>        gas must be thoroughly DRY.
>
>   STEP 3 - CATALYTIC OXIDATION TO SULPHUR TRIOXIDE
>
>        2 SO2(g) + O2(g)  <=(V2O5, 720 K, 2 bar)=>  2 SO3(g)
>                          dH = - 196.6 kJ / mol
>
>          Catalyst    :  VANADIUM PENTOXIDE, V2O5
>          Temperature :  720 K
>          Pressure    :  2 bar
>          Conversion  :  about 97 %
>
>   STEP 4 - ABSORB SO3 IN CONCENTRATED SULPHURIC ACID
>        SO3 + H2SO4  -->  H2S2O7   (OLEUM, pyrosulphuric acid)
>
>   STEP 5 - DILUTE THE OLEUM
>        H2S2O7 + H2O  -->  2 H2SO4   (96 - 98 % pure)
> ```
>
> **2. WHY OLEUM, AND JUSTIFICATION OF THE CONDITIONS (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   WHY IS SO3 NOT PUT STRAIGHT INTO WATER?
>     SO3 + H2O --> H2SO4 is violently exothermic and gives a dense
>     choking MIST of sulphuric acid droplets that will not condense,
>     so most of the product is lost. Dissolving SO3 in existing
>     H2SO4 to give OLEUM is smooth and controllable; the oleum is
>     then diluted safely.
>
>   LE CHATELIER APPLIED TO STEP 3
>     - The reaction is EXOTHERMIC, so a LOW temperature favours the
>       yield; but the rate would then be too slow. 720 K is the
>       COMPROMISE (optimum) temperature.
>     - 3 moles of gas become 2 moles, so HIGH pressure favours the
>       yield; but the conversion at only 2 bar is already about
>       97 %, so higher pressure is not worth the cost.
>     - EXCESS AIR (excess O2) pushes the equilibrium to the right.
>     - V2O5 does not change the yield, only the rate.
> ```
>
> **3. FLOW LINE (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>    S or FeS2 --> [BURN in air] --> SO2 --> [PURIFY and DRY] -->
>       --> [CONVERTER  V2O5, 720 K, 2 bar] --> SO3 -->
>       --> [ABSORPTION TOWER, conc. H2SO4] --> OLEUM H2S2O7 -->
>       --> [DILUTE with water] --> H2SO4 (98 %)
> ```
>
> **4. PROPERTIES (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   STRUCTURE: tetrahedral about sulphur, sp3 hybridised, with two
>   S-OH bonds and two S=O bonds.
>
>   (a) AS A STRONG DIBASIC ACID
>        H2SO4 + H2O --> H3O+ + HSO4-      (first ionisation, large)
>        HSO4- + H2O --> H3O+ + SO4 2-     (second, Ka2 = 1.2x10^-2)
>       It forms two series of salts - NaHSO4 and Na2SO4 - and
>       displaces more volatile acids from their salts:
>        2 NaCl + H2SO4 --> Na2SO4 + 2 HCl
>        2 KNO3 + H2SO4 --> K2SO4  + 2 HNO3
>
>   (b) AS A DEHYDRATING AGENT
>       It has a huge affinity for water and removes the ELEMENTS of
>       water from compounds that contain no water at all:
>        C12H22O11 --(conc. H2SO4)--> 12 C + 11 H2O   (sugar chars)
>        (COOH)2   --(conc. H2SO4)--> CO + CO2 + H2O
>        HCOOH     --(conc. H2SO4)--> CO + H2O
>        CuSO4.5H2O --(conc. H2SO4)--> CuSO4 + 5 H2O
>                                      (blue turns white)
>
>   (c) AS AN OXIDISING AGENT (hot concentrated acid, reduced to SO2)
>        Cu + 2 H2SO4 --> CuSO4 + SO2 + 2 H2O
>        C  + 2 H2SO4 --> CO2 + 2 SO2 + 2 H2O
>        S  + 2 H2SO4 --> 3 SO2 + 2 H2O
>       This is why HBr and HI cannot be made by heating their salts
>       with conc. H2SO4 - the acid oxidises them to Br2 and I2.
>
>   USES: fertilisers (ammonium sulphate, superphosphate of lime),
>   petroleum refining, lead storage batteries, detergents, dyes,
>   drugs, paints, metallurgical pickling, and as a laboratory
>   reagent and drying agent.
>
>   SAFETY: always add ACID TO WATER slowly with stirring, never the
>   other way round; dilution is strongly exothermic.
> ```

## C4. Discuss the general characteristics of the elements of group 15 — configuration, oxidation states, trends, and the anomalous behaviour of nitrogen. (8 marks)

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   MARK SPLIT:  configuration and members 1 | oxidation states 1
>                trends with reasons 3 | inert pair effect 1
>                anomalous nitrogen 2
> ```
>
> **1. MEMBERS AND CONFIGURATION (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   The elements are  N , P , As , Sb , Bi
>   General configuration:   ns2 np3     (half-filled p subshell)
>
>        N   [He] 2s2 2p3          Sb  [Kr] 4d10 5s2 5p3
>        P   [Ne] 3s2 3p3          Bi  [Xe] 4f14 5d10 6s2 6p3
>        As  [Ar] 3d10 4s2 4p3
>
>   N and P are NON-METALS, As and Sb are METALLOIDS, Bi is a METAL.
>   Metallic character INCREASES down the group as the ionisation
>   enthalpy falls.
> ```
>
> **2. OXIDATION STATES (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   Common states:  -3 , +3 , +5   (also +1, +2, +4 in the oxides).
>
>   - The -3 state becomes LESS stable down the group, because the
>     electronegativity falls and the large atom cannot hold three
>     extra electrons. Only NH3 is truly basic; BiH3 is barely so.
>   - The +5 state becomes LESS stable and the +3 state MORE stable
>     down the group - the INERT PAIR EFFECT.
>   - Nitrogen shows every state from -3 to +5, but its maximum
>     COVALENCY is only 4 because it has no d orbitals.
> ```
>
> **3. TRENDS AND THEIR REASONS (3 marks)**
>
> ```
>   +----------------------+---------------+------------------------+
>   | PROPERTY             |  N -> Bi      |  REASON                |
>   +----------------------+---------------+------------------------+
>   | Atomic / ionic radius| INCREASES     | a new shell is added   |
>   |                      |               | at every step          |
>   | Ionisation enthalpy  | DECREASES     | bigger atom, more      |
>   |                      |               | shielding, weaker pull |
>   | Electronegativity    | DECREASES     | same reason            |
>   | Metallic character   | INCREASES     | IE falls, electrons    |
>   |                      |               | given up more easily   |
>   | Melting point        | UP to As then | large Bi atoms form    |
>   |                      | DOWN at Bi    | weaker bonds           |
>   | Catenation           | DECREASES     | E-E bond gets longer   |
>   |                      |               | and weaker             |
>   | Stability of +5      | DECREASES     | INERT PAIR EFFECT      |
>   | Stability of -3      | DECREASES     | EN falls               |
>   +----------------------+---------------+------------------------+
>
>   HYDRIDES:   NH3 , PH3 , AsH3 , SbH3 , BiH3 - all PYRAMIDAL, sp3.
>     Thermal stability   NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 > BiH3
>     Basicity            NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 > BiH3
>     Reducing character  BiH3 > SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3 > NH3
>     Bond angle          107.8 > 93.6 > 91.8 > 91.3 > ~90
>
>   OXIDES: the trioxide of the top element is ACIDIC and that of
>   the bottom element is BASIC, because metallic character rises:
>        N2O3 (acidic) > P2O3 > As2O3 (amphoteric) > Sb2O3
>             > Bi2O3 (basic)
>
>   HALIDES: trihalides EX3 (all except NX5 also form pentahalides
>   EX5). Nitrogen forms NO pentahalide, having no d orbitals.
>
>   IONISATION ENTHALPY NOTE: group 15 elements have HIGHER
>   ionisation enthalpies than their group 14 and group 16
>   neighbours, because ns2 np3 is EXACTLY HALF FILLED and therefore
>   extra stable. This is why IE of N is greater than IE of O.
> ```
>
> **4. THE INERT PAIR EFFECT (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   The reluctance of the ns2 electron pair to take part in bonding,
>   owing to the poor shielding of the nucleus by the intervening d
>   and f electrons, is called the INERT PAIR EFFECT. As a result
>   the stability of the LOWER oxidation state increases down the
>   group.
>
>   Evidence: BiCl3 is stable while BiCl5 hardly exists, and Bi(V)
>   compounds are powerful OXIDISING AGENTS because Bi(V) is
>   desperate to fall back to the stable Bi(III) state.
> ```
>
> **5. ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF NITROGEN (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   THE THREE CAUSES: very small size, high electronegativity, and
>   NO d ORBITALS in the valence shell.
>
>   (a) N2 is a GAS because nitrogen forms p-pi - p-pi MULTIPLE
>       BONDS, giving the small diatomic molecule N(triple bond)N.
>       Phosphorus atoms are too large for sideways p overlap and so
>       form single bonds, building the solid P4 tetrahedron.
>   (b) N2 is nearly INERT at room temperature - triple bond
>       enthalpy 941.4 kJ/mol.
>   (c) Maximum COVALENCY 4, not 5, because the octet cannot be
>       expanded; NCl5 does not exist, PCl5 does.
>   (d) NH3 shows HYDROGEN BONDING; PH3 does not.
>   (e) NF3 is NOT hydrolysed by water, whereas PCl3 and PCl5 are,
>       because P has vacant 3d orbitals to accept water's lone pair
>       and N has none.
> ```

## C5. Discuss the compounds of xenon — preparation, structures and hybridisations — and explain why the noble gases are inert. (8 marks)

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   MARK SPLIT:  why inert 2 | why only Xe 1 | preparation 2
>                structures with hybridisation 2 | hydrolysis/uses 1
> ```
>
> **1. WHY ARE THE NOBLE GASES INERT? (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   1. Their outermost shell is COMPLETELY FILLED, ns2 np6 (He = 1s2)
>      - the most stable electronic arrangement possible.
>   2. They have VERY HIGH IONISATION ENTHALPIES, the highest in
>      each period, so they will not LOSE an electron. Helium has
>      the highest ionisation enthalpy of any element.
>   3. Their ELECTRON GAIN ENTHALPY IS POSITIVE - energy must be
>      supplied to force an electron in - so they will not GAIN one.
>
>   Having no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons, they form
>   no chemical bonds and exist as MONOATOMIC gases.
> ```
>
> **2. WHY DOES ONLY XENON FORM COMPOUNDS? (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   Xenon is the largest non-radioactive member of the group. Its
>   outer electrons are far from the nucleus and poorly shielded, so
>   its IONISATION ENTHALPY IS LOW ENOUGH for the two most
>   electronegative and oxidising elements - FLUORINE and OXYGEN -
>   to remove electrons from it.
>
>   Radon would react even more readily, but it is intensely
>   radioactive and short lived.
>
>   HISTORY: Neil Bartlett (1962) noticed that Xe and O2 have almost
>   the same ionisation enthalpy. Since PtF6 had oxidised O2 to
>   O2+[PtF6]-, he tried xenon and obtained Xe+[PtF6]- - the first
>   noble gas compound ever made.
> ```
>
> **3. PREPARATION OF THE FLUORIDES (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>        Xe : F2 = 2 : 1   --(673 K , 1 bar)-->        XeF2
>        Xe : F2 = 1 : 5   --(873 K , 7 bar)-->        XeF4
>        Xe : F2 = 1 : 20  --(573 K , 60-70 bar)-->    XeF6
>
>        XeF4 + O2F2  --(143 K)-->  XeF6 + O2
>
>   MEMORY: the more fluorines you want, the more fluorine and the
>   more pressure you use. The ratios run 2:1, 1:5, 1:20.
>
>   The oxides come from HYDROLYSIS of the fluorides (see point 5).
> ```
>
> **4. STRUCTURES AND HYBRIDISATIONS (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   METHOD: count the bond pairs and lone pairs on Xe; their total
>   gives the hybridisation; then remove the lone pairs to read off
>   the shape.
>
>   +---------+------+------+-------+-----------+---------------------+
>   | MOLECULE| b.p. | l.p. | TOTAL | HYBRID.   | SHAPE               |
>   +---------+------+------+-------+-----------+---------------------+
>   | XeF2    |  2   |  3   |   5   |  sp3d     | LINEAR              |
>   | XeF4    |  4   |  2   |   6   |  sp3d2    | SQUARE PLANAR       |
>   | XeF6    |  6   |  1   |   7   |  sp3d3    | DISTORTED OCTAHEDRAL|
>   | XeO3    |  3   |  1   |   4   |  sp3      | PYRAMIDAL           |
>   | XeOF4   |  5   |  1   |   6   |  sp3d2    | SQUARE PYRAMIDAL    |
>   | XeO2F2  |  4   |  1   |   5   |  sp3d     | SEE-SAW             |
>   +---------+------+------+-------+-----------+---------------------+
>
>     XeF2  LINEAR (sp3d)              XeF4  SQUARE PLANAR (sp3d2)
>
>           F                              F         F
>           |                                \      /
>          Xe   3 lone pairs in                 Xe     + 1 lone pair
>           |   the equatorial plane          /    \     above and
>           F                                F      F    1 below
>      180 degrees                       all angles 90 degrees
>
>
>     XeF6  DISTORTED OCTAHEDRAL       XeO3  PYRAMIDAL (sp3)
>           (sp3d3)
>           F    F                              ..   <- lone pair
>            \   |   /                           Xe
>             \  |  F                          /  |  \
>              (Xe)                           O   O   O
>             /  |  \       the single
>          F    F    F      lone pair          3 bond pairs +
>                          distorts the        1 lone pair,
>       NOT a regular      regular octahedron  like NH3
>       octahedron
>
>
>     XeOF4  SQUARE PYRAMIDAL (sp3d2)
>                O
>                ||
>                Xe      four F in a square plane, one O above,
>              / /|\ \   + 1 lone pair below
>             F  F F  F
> ```
>
> **5. HYDROLYSIS AND USES (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   HYDROLYSIS
>        2 XeF2 + 2 H2O  -->  2 Xe + 4 HF + O2
>        6 XeF4 + 12 H2O -->  4 Xe + 2 XeO3 + 24 HF + 3 O2
>                             (a disproportionation)
>        XeF6 + 3 H2O    -->  XeO3 + 6 HF        (complete)
>        XeF6 + H2O      -->  XeOF4 + 2 HF       (partial)
>        XeF6 + NaF      -->  Na+[XeF7]-
>
>   All the xenon fluorides are powerful FLUORINATING and OXIDISING
>   agents. XeO3 is a colourless EXPLOSIVE solid.
>
>   USES OF THE NOBLE GASES: He in weather balloons and in the
>   He-O2 mixture for divers; Ne in neon signs; Ar as an inert
>   atmosphere in welding and in light bulbs; Kr and Xe in high
>   efficiency lamps and flash bulbs; Rn in radiotherapy.
> ```

## C6. Discuss the general characteristics of group 17 (the halogens) — trends, the anomalous behaviour of fluorine, the oxoacids of chlorine, and interhalogen compounds. (8 marks)

> **ANSWER:**
>
> ```
>   MARK SPLIT:  configuration 1 | trends with reasons 2
>                fluorine anomalies 2 | oxoacids 1.5
>                interhalogens 1.5
> ```
>
> **1. MEMBERS AND CONFIGURATION (1 mark)**
>
> ```
>   The elements are  F , Cl , Br , I , At  (At is radioactive).
>   General configuration:  ns2 np5  - one electron short of a full
>   octet, which is why they are the most reactive non-metals and
>   are called the HALOGENS ("salt makers").
>
>   Physical state: F2 and Cl2 are GASES, Br2 is a LIQUID (the only
>   non-metal that is liquid at room temperature) and I2 is a SOLID.
>   Colour deepens down the group: pale yellow, greenish yellow,
>   reddish brown, violet black.
> ```
>
> **2. TRENDS AND REASONS (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   +--------------------------+-------------------+-----------------+
>   | PROPERTY                 |  F -> I           | REASON          |
>   +--------------------------+-------------------+-----------------+
>   | Atomic radius            | INCREASES         | new shell each  |
>   |                          |                   | step            |
>   | Ionisation enthalpy      | DECREASES         | size and        |
>   |                          |                   | shielding grow  |
>   | Electronegativity        | DECREASES         | F is the MOST   |
>   |                          |                   | electronegative |
>   |                          |                   | element of all  |
>   | Electron gain enthalpy   | Cl > F > Br > I   | see point 3     |
>   | Bond enthalpy of X2      | Cl2 > Br2 > F2>I2 | see point 3     |
>   | Melting / boiling point  | INCREASE          | van der Waals   |
>   |                          |                   | forces grow     |
>   | Oxidising power          | F2 > Cl2>Br2>I2   | low bond        |
>   |                          |                   | enthalpy + high |
>   |                          |                   | hydration       |
>   |                          |                   | enthalpy of F-  |
>   | Acid strength of HX      | HF < HCl<HBr<HI   | H-X bond        |
>   |                          |                   | weakens         |
>   | Thermal stability of HX  | HF > HCl>HBr>HI   | same reason     |
>   +--------------------------+-------------------+-----------------+
>
>   OXIDATION STATES: all show -1. Cl, Br and I also show +1, +3,
>   +5 and +7 because they have vacant d orbitals. FLUORINE shows
>   ONLY -1.
> ```
>
> **3. ANOMALOUS BEHAVIOUR OF FLUORINE (2 marks)**
>
> ```
>   CAUSES: very small size, highest electronegativity, no d orbitals.
>
>   (a) The ELECTRON GAIN ENTHALPY of F is LESS negative than that
>       of Cl, because F's tiny 2p subshell is crowded and repels
>       the incoming electron.       Cl > F > Br > I
>
>   (b) The F-F BOND ENTHALPY is abnormally LOW, because the lone
>       pairs on two very close F atoms repel one another.
>                                     Cl2 > Br2 > F2 > I2
>
>   (c) In spite of (a), F2 is the STRONGEST OXIDISING AGENT,
>       because the low bond enthalpy and the very high hydration
>       enthalpy of the tiny F- ion outweigh it.
>
>   (d) F shows ONLY the -1 state and forms only one, unstable,
>       oxoacid HOF - no d orbitals, no positive oxidation state.
>
>   (e) HF is a LIQUID (hydrogen bonded) and a WEAK acid (very
>       strong H-F bond), unlike the other hydrogen halides.
>
>   (f) Metal fluorides are largely IONIC; AgF is soluble in water
>       while AgCl, AgBr and AgI are not.
> ```
>
> **4. OXOACIDS OF CHLORINE (1.5 marks)**
>
> ```
>   +----------+-----------------------+-----------+
>   | FORMULA  | NAME                  | OX. STATE |
>   +----------+-----------------------+-----------+
>   | HOCl     | hypochlorous acid     |    +1     |
>   | HClO2    | chlorous acid         |    +3     |
>   | HClO3    | chloric acid          |    +5     |
>   | HClO4    | perchloric acid       |    +7     |
>   +----------+-----------------------+-----------+
>
>   ACID STRENGTH:  HOCl < HClO2 < HClO3 < HClO4  (INCREASES)
>     because each extra electronegative oxygen pulls electron
>     density from the O-H bond (making H+ easier to lose) and the
>     resulting anion spreads its charge over more oxygen atoms,
>     giving better resonance stabilisation. HClO4 is one of the
>     strongest acids known.
>
>   ACROSS THE HALOGENS:  HOCl > HOBr > HOI  (decreases), because
>     Cl is the most electronegative of the three.
>
>   OXIDISING POWER runs the OPPOSITE way:
>     HOCl > HClO2 > HClO3 > HClO4
> ```
>
> **5. INTERHALOGEN COMPOUNDS (1.5 marks)**
>
> ```
>   Compounds between TWO DIFFERENT halogens, formula XX'n with
>   n = 1, 3, 5, 7 (always odd); X is the larger halogen.
>
>   +--------+------------------+---------------------+----------+
>   | TYPE   | EXAMPLES         | SHAPE               | HYBRID.  |
>   +--------+------------------+---------------------+----------+
>   | XX'    | ClF, BrCl, ICl   | Linear              | sp3      |
>   | XX'3   | ClF3, BrF3       | Bent T-shaped       | sp3d     |
>   | XX'5   | BrF5, IF5        | Square pyramidal    | sp3d2    |
>   | XX'7   | IF7              | Pentagonal          | sp3d3    |
>   |        |                  | bipyramidal         |          |
>   +--------+------------------+---------------------+----------+
>
>   WHY MORE REACTIVE THAN THE HALOGENS: the X-X' bond is between
>   two different atoms, so it is POLAR and WEAKER than the
>   non-polar X-X bond, and a weaker bond breaks more easily.
>
>   HYDROLYSIS:  ClF3 + 2 H2O --> 3 HF + HClO2
>   USES: fluorinating agents; ClF3 and BrF3 make UF6 for uranium
>         enrichment:  U + 3 ClF3 --> UF6 + 3 ClF
> ```
