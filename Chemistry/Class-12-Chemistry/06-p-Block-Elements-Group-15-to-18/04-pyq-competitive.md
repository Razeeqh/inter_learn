# PATTERN 1 — ORDERS AMONG THE HYDRIDES

This is the single most-asked pattern in the whole chapter. Every order comes
from ONE of two ideas: **hydrogen bonding** (only for NH3, H2O, HF) or
**the E-H bond getting longer and weaker down the group**.

```
  THE MASTER KEY - learn this box and you answer every hydride question

     GOING DOWN A GROUP the central atom gets BIGGER, so the E-H bond
     gets LONGER and WEAKER. Therefore, going down:

         THERMAL STABILITY   goes DOWN   (weak bond breaks on heating)
         BASICITY            goes DOWN   (lone pair spread thin)
         BOND ANGLE          goes DOWN   (EN falls, pure p orbitals)
         ACIDIC CHARACTER    goes UP     (H+ leaves the weak bond easily)
         REDUCING POWER      goes UP     (H is released easily)

     FIRST THREE DOWN, LAST TWO UP.   That is the entire pattern.
```

### Q1
The correct order of the **bond angle** is

(a) NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 &nbsp; (b) SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3 > NH3
(c) PH3 > NH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 &nbsp; (d) NH3 > AsH3 > PH3 > SbH3

**ANSWER: (a) NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3**

```
  NH3   107.8 deg      N is very electronegative, pulls the bond pairs
  PH3    93.6 deg      close in, so they repel hard and the angle opens.
  AsH3   91.8 deg      Below N the central atom uses almost PURE p
  SbH3   91.3 deg      orbitals, and pure p orbitals are 90 deg apart.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** after nitrogen the numbers all collapse to "about 90".
> Only NH3 is above 100. So the first member is always the biggest angle.

### Q2
The correct order of the **boiling points** of group 15 hydrides is

(a) NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 &nbsp; (b) NH3 > SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3
(c) SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3 > NH3 &nbsp; (d) SbH3 > NH3 > AsH3 > PH3

**ANSWER: (b) NH3 > SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3**

```
  TWO forces are fighting here:

    (i)  HYDROGEN BONDING - only NH3 has it, and it throws NH3 straight
         to the top.
    (ii) VAN DER WAALS forces - these grow with molecular size, so among
         the REST the order is PH3 < AsH3 < SbH3.

  Put the two together:   NH3  >  SbH3  >  AsH3  >  PH3
                          ^ H-bond      ^ then plain size
```

> **SHORTCUT:** boiling-point questions are V-SHAPED. The first member is
> high (H-bonding), it drops to the second member, then it climbs again.
> Same for H2O > H2Te > H2Se > H2S and HF > HI > HBr > HCl.

### Q3
The strongest **reducing agent** among the following is

(a) NH3 &nbsp; (b) PH3 &nbsp; (c) AsH3 &nbsp; (d) BiH3

**ANSWER: (d) BiH3**

```
  Reducing power = how easily the hydride GIVES AWAY its hydrogen.
  Bi is the biggest atom, so the Bi-H bond is the longest and weakest,
  so it lets its hydrogen go most easily.

     BiH3 > SbH3 > AsH3 > PH3 > NH3
```

### Q4
The correct order of **acidic strength** is

(a) HF > HCl > HBr > HI &nbsp; (b) HI > HBr > HCl > HF
(c) HCl > HF > HBr > HI &nbsp; (d) HBr > HI > HCl > HF

**ANSWER: (b) HI > HBr > HCl > HF**

```
  H-X bond enthalpy (kJ/mol):  HF 567 > HCl 431 > HBr 366 > HI 299
  The WEAKEST bond releases H+ most easily  ->  HI is the strongest acid.

  HF is a WEAK acid even though F is the most electronegative element,
  because the H-F bond is so short and strong.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** for hydrides, acid strength ALWAYS follows the weakest
> bond, never the electronegativity. Down any group, acidity increases.

---

### Q5
Which of the following has the **highest thermal stability**?

(a) H2O &nbsp; (b) H2S &nbsp; (c) H2Se &nbsp; (d) H2Te

**ANSWER: (a) H2O**

```
  H2O > H2S > H2Se > H2Te > H2Po
  Oxygen is the smallest, so the O-H bond is the shortest and strongest
  and needs the most heat to break.

  NOTE the mirror image: the ACIDITY order is exactly the reverse,
  H2O < H2S < H2Se < H2Te.
```

### Q6
The correct order of **basicity** is

(a) NH3 < PH3 < AsH3 < SbH3 &nbsp; (b) NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 > BiH3
(c) PH3 > NH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 &nbsp; (d) BiH3 > SbH3 > NH3 > PH3

**ANSWER: (b) NH3 > PH3 > AsH3 > SbH3 > BiH3**

```
  Basicity = the willingness of the LONE PAIR to be donated.
  Down the group the atom gets bigger, so the lone pair is spread over
  a much larger volume - it is "thinner" and less able to attack a
  proton. So basicity falls sharply. NH3 is the only one that is a
  genuinely useful base.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** BASICITY and THERMAL STABILITY always run the SAME way
> (both decrease down). ACIDITY and REDUCING POWER run the OTHER way.

# PATTERN 2 — ACID STRENGTH OF OXIDES AND OXOACIDS

```
  THE THREE RULES THAT SETTLE EVERY QUESTION IN THIS PATTERN

  RULE 1   MORE OXYGEN  ->  MORE ACIDIC
           HOCl < HClO2 < HClO3 < HClO4        (+1 < +3 < +5 < +7)
           SO2 < SO3   ,   N2O3 < N2O5   ,   H2SO3 < H2SO4
           WHY: each extra O pulls electron density off the O-H bond,
           AND the anion left behind spreads its charge over more
           oxygens (better resonance stabilisation).

  RULE 2   ACROSS A PERIOD acidity INCREASES (metal -> non-metal)
           Na2O  MgO  Al2O3  SiO2  P4O10  SO3  Cl2O7
           basic     amphoteric   ------ acidic ------>

  RULE 3   DOWN A GROUP acidity DECREASES (non-metal -> metal)
           N2O3 acidic  ->  As2O3 amphoteric  ->  Bi2O3 basic
```

### Q7
The correct order of acid strength is

(a) HClO4 > HClO3 > HClO2 > HOCl &nbsp; (b) HOCl > HClO2 > HClO3 > HClO4
(c) HClO3 > HClO4 > HOCl > HClO2 &nbsp; (d) HClO2 > HOCl > HClO4 > HClO3

**ANSWER: (a) HClO4 > HClO3 > HClO2 > HOCl**

```
  Count the oxygens:  4 > 3 > 2 > 1.  More oxygen, stronger acid.
  HClO4 (perchloric acid) is one of the strongest acids known.
```

### Q8
The correct order of **oxidising power** is

(a) HClO4 > HClO3 > HClO2 > HOCl &nbsp; (b) HOCl > HClO2 > HClO3 > HClO4
(c) HClO3 > HOCl > HClO4 > HClO2 &nbsp; (d) all four are equal

**ANSWER: (b) HOCl > HClO2 > HClO3 > HClO4**

```
  > TRAP - THE MOST COMMON MISTAKE IN THIS CHAPTER.
  ACID STRENGTH and OXIDISING POWER of the chlorine oxoacids run in
  OPPOSITE directions.

      ACID  STRENGTH :  HOCl  <  HClO2  <  HClO3  <  HClO4
      OXIDISING POWER:  HOCl  >  HClO2  >  HClO3  >  HClO4

  Reason for the second one: the acid with the FEWEST oxygens holds
  them least tightly and releases nascent oxygen most easily.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "The strongest acid is the weakest oxidiser." HClO4 wins
> the acid race and loses the oxidiser race.

### Q9
Which of the following oxides is **amphoteric**?

(a) N2O3 &nbsp; (b) P2O3 &nbsp; (c) As2O3 &nbsp; (d) Bi2O3

**ANSWER: (c) As2O3**

```
  Going down group 15 the trioxides march from acidic to basic:

     N2O3    P2O3    As2O3        Sb2O3        Bi2O3
     acidic  acidic  AMPHOTERIC   amphoteric   BASIC
                     ^ the crossover point

  Reason: metallic character increases down the group, and metallic
  oxides are basic.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** the amphoteric ones you must know by heart:
> **Al2O3, ZnO, PbO, SnO2, BeO, As2O3, Sb2O3.**

### Q10
The correct order of acidic strength of the oxides of nitrogen is

(a) N2O5 > N2O3 > NO > N2O &nbsp; (b) N2O > NO > N2O3 > N2O5
(c) N2O3 > N2O5 > NO2 > NO &nbsp; (d) NO > NO2 > N2O3 > N2O5

**ANSWER: (a) N2O5 > N2O3 > NO > N2O**

```
  Oxidation state of N:  N2O5 (+5) > N2O3 (+3) > NO (+2) > N2O (+1)
  Higher oxidation state = more acidic oxide.

  N2O and NO are in fact NEUTRAL oxides - they react with neither acid
  nor base. So they sit at the bottom of any acidity list.
```

### Q11
Among HOCl, HOBr and HOI, the strongest acid is

(a) HOI &nbsp; (b) HOBr &nbsp; (c) HOCl &nbsp; (d) all equal

**ANSWER: (c) HOCl**

```
  Here the number of oxygens is the SAME (one each), so the deciding
  factor is the ELECTRONEGATIVITY of the halogen. Cl is the most
  electronegative of the three, so it pulls hardest on the O-H bond.

      HOCl  >  HOBr  >  HOI
```

> **SHORTCUT:** same number of O atoms -> compare electronegativity.
> Different number of O atoms -> just count the oxygens.

# PATTERN 3 — SHAPE AND HYBRIDISATION

```
  THE 4-STEP VSEPR MACHINE - works on any molecule in this chapter

  STEP 1  valence electrons of the CENTRAL atom
  STEP 2  subtract 1 for each atom bonded to it (subtract 0 for a
          doubly-bonded O in an oxoacid - it uses two, gives back two)
  STEP 3  what is left, divided by 2  =  number of LONE PAIRS
  STEP 4  bond pairs + lone pairs = STERIC NUMBER

          2 -> sp        3 -> sp2       4 -> sp3
          5 -> sp3d      6 -> sp3d2     7 -> sp3d3

  Then DELETE the lone pairs to read off the SHAPE.
```

### Q12
The hybridisation and shape of **XeF4** are

(a) sp3d, see-saw &nbsp; (b) sp3d2, square planar
(c) sp3d3, distorted octahedral &nbsp; (d) sp3, tetrahedral

**ANSWER: (b) sp3d2, square planar**

```
  Xe has 8 valence electrons. Four are used in four Xe-F bonds,
  leaving 4 electrons = 2 LONE PAIRS.
  4 bp + 2 lp = 6 pairs -> sp3d2, octahedral electron geometry.
  The two lone pairs go OPPOSITE each other (one up, one down) to be
  as far apart as possible, leaving the four F atoms in one plane.

           F        F
             \    /
               Xe          + one lone pair ABOVE
             /    \        + one lone pair BELOW
           F        F           all angles 90 deg
```

### Q13
**XeF2** is linear because

(a) it is sp hybridised &nbsp; (b) it has no lone pairs
(c) it is sp3d hybridised with 3 lone pairs in the equatorial plane
(d) it is sp3 hybridised with 2 lone pairs

**ANSWER: (c) sp3d with 3 lone pairs in the equatorial plane**

```
  8 valence electrons - 2 used for bonds = 6 left = 3 LONE PAIRS.
  2 bp + 3 lp = 5 -> sp3d, trigonal bipyramidal electron geometry.
  Lone pairs ALWAYS take the roomier EQUATORIAL positions, so all
  three sit in the middle plane and the two F atoms are pushed to the
  axial positions - 180 degrees apart. Hence LINEAR.

  > TRAP: linear does NOT mean sp. XeF2 is linear AND sp3d.
```

### Q14
The shape of **XeO3** is

(a) trigonal planar &nbsp; (b) pyramidal &nbsp; (c) T-shaped &nbsp; (d) square planar

**ANSWER: (b) pyramidal**

```
  3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair = 4 -> sp3, tetrahedral electron geometry.
  Delete the lone pair -> PYRAMIDAL, exactly like NH3.

  Compare with XeO4: 4 bond pairs, ZERO lone pairs -> sp3, TETRAHEDRAL.
```

### Q15
Which pair has the **same** hybridisation?

(a) SO2 and SO3 &nbsp; (b) NH3 and BF3 &nbsp; (c) PCl5 and PCl3 &nbsp; (d) XeF4 and XeF2

**ANSWER: (a) SO2 and SO3 — both sp2**

```
  SO2  1 lone pair + 2 sigma bonds -> steric number 3 -> sp2, BENT (119.5)
  SO3  0 lone pairs + 3 sigma bonds -> steric number 3 -> sp2, TRIGONAL
                                                          PLANAR (120)

  Same hybridisation, DIFFERENT shape - because SO2 has a lone pair.

  Why the others are wrong:
     NH3 sp3, BF3 sp2        PCl5 sp3d, PCl3 sp3
     XeF4 sp3d2, XeF2 sp3d
```

> **SHORTCUT:** hybridisation is decided by bp + lp. SHAPE is decided by
> bp only. Two molecules can share a hybridisation and look nothing alike.

### Q16
Solid PCl5 exists as

(a) PCl5 molecules &nbsp; (b) [PCl4]+ [PCl6]-
(c) [PCl4]+ Cl- &nbsp; (d) [PCl2]+ [PCl6]-

**ANSWER: (b) [PCl4]+ [PCl6]-**

```
     [PCl4]+   TETRAHEDRAL   sp3
     [PCl6]-   OCTAHEDRAL    sp3d2

  Gaseous PCl5 is TRIGONAL BIPYRAMIDAL, sp3d, with the two AXIAL bonds
  (240 pm) LONGER and WEAKER than the three EQUATORIAL bonds (202 pm).

  > TRAP: solid PBr5 is [PBr4]+ Br- , NOT [PBr4]+[PBr6]- , because six
    big bromine atoms will not fit around one phosphorus atom.
```

# PATTERN 4 — THE INERT PAIR EFFECT

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |   THE INERT PAIR EFFECT IN ONE SENTENCE                              |
  |                                                                      |
  |   The ns2 electron pair refuses to take part in bonding, because     |
  |   the intervening d and f electrons shield the nucleus BADLY, so     |
  |   the ns2 pair feels a high effective nuclear charge and is held     |
  |   too tightly.                                                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |   CONSEQUENCE:  going DOWN a group,                                  |
  |        the LOWER  oxidation state (group valency - 2) gets STABLER   |
  |        the HIGHER oxidation state gets LESS stable                   |
  |                                                                      |
  |        group 13   +3 -> +1     Tl+ beats Tl3+                        |
  |        group 14   +4 -> +2     Pb2+ beats Pb4+                       |
  |        group 15   +5 -> +3     Bi3+ beats Bi5+                       |
  |        group 16   +6 -> +4     Po4+ beats Po6+                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |   AND: an UNSTABLE high oxidation state = a STRONG OXIDISING AGENT,  |
  |        because it is desperate to fall back down.                    |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Q17
Bi(V) is a stronger oxidising agent than Sb(V) because

(a) Bi is more electronegative &nbsp; (b) of the inert pair effect
(c) Bi has a smaller size &nbsp; (d) Bi has no d orbitals

**ANSWER: (b) because of the inert pair effect**

```
  Bi is at the BOTTOM of group 15, so the inert pair effect is at its
  strongest there. Bi(V) is therefore very unstable and desperate to
  drop to the stable Bi(III). To drop from +5 to +3 it must ACCEPT
  two electrons - which is exactly what an oxidising agent does.
```

### Q18
Which of the following is the **most stable**?

(a) BiCl5 &nbsp; (b) SbCl5 &nbsp; (c) AsCl5 &nbsp; (d) BiCl3

**ANSWER: (d) BiCl3**

```
  BiCl3 is the +3 compound of the lowest element in the group - the
  most inert-pair-stabilised combination possible.
  BiCl5 barely exists at all.
  (AsCl5 is also notoriously unstable, but for a different reason -
   the poor shielding of the 3d electrons in arsenic.)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** if the question offers a HIGH oxidation state of a
> HEAVY element (BiCl5, PbCl4, Tl(III), PbO2), that is the UNSTABLE
> one and the strong OXIDISER. Pick it whenever the question asks for
> "least stable" or "best oxidising agent".

### Q19
The oxidation state exhibited by nitrogen ranges from

(a) -3 to +5 &nbsp; (b) 0 to +5 &nbsp; (c) -3 to +3 &nbsp; (d) -5 to +5

**ANSWER: (a) -3 to +5**

```
  Nitrogen shows EVERY state from -3 to +5:
     -3 NH3   -2 N2H4   -1 NH2OH   0 N2   +1 N2O   +2 NO
     +3 N2O3 / HNO2     +4 NO2     +5 N2O5 / HNO3

  It can never go above +5 because it has only 5 valence electrons
  and NO d orbitals to expand into. That is why NCl5 does not exist
  while PCl5 does.
```

### Q21
Which one of the following does **NOT** contain a peroxide (-O-O-) linkage?

(a) H2S2O8 &nbsp; (b) H2SO5 &nbsp; (c) H2S2O7 &nbsp; (d) Na2O2

**ANSWER: (c) H2S2O7 (oleum / pyrosulphuric acid)**

```
  H2S2O8  Marshall's acid       HO-SO2-O-O-SO2-OH    1 peroxide link
  H2SO5   Caro's acid           HO-SO2-O-OH          1 peroxide link
  H2S2O7  Pyrosulphuric acid    HO-SO2-O-SO2-OH      NO peroxide -
                                                     a plain S-O-S bridge
  Na2O2   sodium peroxide       Na-O-O-Na            1 peroxide link
```

> **SHORTCUT:** count the oxygens in the S acid. H2SO4 needs 4 per S.
> H2S2O7 has 7 for two S atoms - one short of 8, so one O is SHARED
> (a bridge). H2S2O8 has 8 for two S atoms - none shared, so the extra
> O must be sitting in a PEROXIDE link. **Odd number of O = bridge.
> Even number with a spare = peroxide.**

### Q24
In the Haber process, the promoters used along with the iron catalyst are

(a) Mo and Al2O3 &nbsp; (b) K2O and Al2O3 &nbsp; (c) V2O5 and MnO2 &nbsp; (d) Pt and Rh

**ANSWER: (b) K2O and Al2O3**

```
  CATALYST  = iron (finely divided / iron oxide)
  PROMOTERS = K2O and Al2O3 - they do not catalyse the reaction
              themselves, they make the iron work better and last longer.

```

### Q25
The optimum temperature of 700 K is used in the Haber process because

(a) it gives the maximum yield of NH3
(b) it is the lowest temperature at which the reaction starts
(c) it is a compromise between a good yield and a workable rate
(d) the catalyst melts above this temperature

**ANSWER: (c) it is a compromise between yield and rate**

```
  The reaction is EXOTHERMIC (dH = -46.1 kJ/mol), so by Le Chatelier a
  LOW temperature would give the BEST YIELD. But at low temperature the
  N(triple bond)N bond (941 kJ/mol) simply will not break and the rate
  is hopeless.

  So 700 K is the "OPTIMUM" or "COMPROMISE" temperature:
      low enough for a decent yield, high enough for a decent rate.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** in ANY exothermic industrial process question, the
> answer to "why not lower temperature?" is always the single word
> **RATE**, and the answer to "why not higher?" is always **YIELD**.

### Q26
In the Contact process, SO3 is **not** absorbed directly in water because

(a) SO3 is insoluble in water &nbsp; (b) it forms a dense mist of H2SO4
(c) the reaction is endothermic &nbsp; (d) it would poison the catalyst

**ANSWER: (b) it forms a dense mist (fog) of sulphuric acid**

```
  SO3 + H2O -> H2SO4 is VIOLENTLY exothermic. The heat throws up a
  choking fog of fine acid droplets which will neither condense nor be
  collected, so most of the product is lost.

  Instead:   SO3 + H2SO4  -->  H2S2O7   (OLEUM)
             H2S2O7 + H2O -->  2 H2SO4  (safe, controlled dilution)
```

### Q27

# PATTERN 8 — PARAMAGNETISM AND BOND ORDER OF THE NITROGEN OXIDES

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |   THE ONE TEST THAT ANSWERS EVERY QUESTION IN THIS PATTERN:          |
  |                                                                      |
  |        COUNT THE TOTAL ELECTRONS.                                    |
  |                                                                      |
  |        ODD  number of electrons  ->  one unpaired  ->  PARAMAGNETIC  |
  |        EVEN number of electrons  ->  usually all paired ->           |
  |                                       DIAMAGNETIC                    |
  |                                       (O2 is the famous exception)   |
  |                                                                      |
  |   THE ODD-ELECTRON SPECIES OF THIS CHAPTER:                          |
  |        NO   (15 electrons total, 11 valence)   PARAMAGNETIC          |
  |        NO2  (23 electrons total)               PARAMAGNETIC          |
  |        ClO2                                    PARAMAGNETIC          |
  |                                                                      |
  |   BOND ORDER OF THE NO FAMILY - remove an electron from an           |
  |   ANTIBONDING orbital and the bond gets STRONGER:                    |
  |                                                                      |
  |        NO-   bond order 2.0    2 unpaired   paramagnetic             |
  |        NO    bond order 2.5    1 unpaired   PARAMAGNETIC             |
  |        NO+   bond order 3.0    0 unpaired   DIAMAGNETIC              |
  |                                                                      |
  |        NO+ is ISOELECTRONIC with N2 and CO (14 electrons, bond       |
  |        order 3). Its bond is SHORTER and STRONGER than NO's.         |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Q36
The bond order in NO+ is

(a) 2.0 &nbsp; (b) 2.5 &nbsp; (c) 3.0 &nbsp; (d) 1.5

**ANSWER: (c) 3.0**

```
  NO has 15 electrons and a bond order of 2.5. The electron removed to
  make NO+ comes out of an ANTIBONDING pi* orbital. Removing an
  ANTIBONDING electron INCREASES the bond order:

        2.5  +  0.5  =  3.0

  NO+ therefore has a shorter, stronger bond than NO.
  BOND LENGTH ORDER:   NO-  >  NO  >  NO+
```

> **SHORTCUT:** remove an antibonding electron -> bond order goes UP.
> Remove a bonding electron -> bond order goes DOWN. For NO, O2 and
> anything after N2, the electron leaves an ANTIBONDING orbital.

### Q37
Which of the following is **diamagnetic**?

(a) NO &nbsp; (b) NO2 &nbsp; (c) N2O4 &nbsp; (d) ClO2

**ANSWER: (c) N2O4**

```
  NO2 has 23 electrons - ODD, so ONE UNPAIRED electron - PARAMAGNETIC.
  Two NO2 molecules pair up that odd electron by forming an N-N bond:

        2 NO2  <=(cool)=>  N2O4

  N2O4 has 46 electrons - all paired - DIAMAGNETIC and COLOURLESS.
  That is why brown NO2 fades to a colourless solid on cooling.
```

# PATTERN 9 — COUNTING BRIDGES, P-H BONDS AND LINKAGES

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |   HOW TO COUNT WITHOUT MEMORISING - draw the skeleton                |
  |                                                                      |
  |   A "BRIDGE" is an oxygen shared between TWO central atoms:          |
  |         P - O - P     is one P-O-P bridge                            |
  |         S - O - S     is one S-O-S bridge                            |
  |                                                                      |
  |   A "PEROXIDE LINK" is TWO oxygens joined to each other:             |
  |         S - O - O - S     or     S - O - O - H                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |   THE FIVE YOU MUST KNOW COLD:                                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |     P4O10    6 P-O-P bridges  +  4 terminal P=O                      |
  |     P4O6     6 P-O-P bridges  +  0 terminal P=O                      |
  |     H4P2O7   1 P-O-P bridge   ,  0 P-H  ,  4 O-H   (tetrabasic)      |
  |     H4P2O5   1 P-O-P bridge   ,  2 P-H  ,  2 O-H   (dibasic)         |
  |     H4P2O6   0 P-O-P bridges (it has a direct P-P bond!),            |
  |                                  0 P-H  ,  4 O-H   (tetrabasic)      |
  |                                                                      |
  |     H2S2O7   1 S-O-S bridge   ,  0 peroxide  ,  2 O-H  (dibasic)     |
  |     H2S2O8   0 S-O-S bridge   ,  1 peroxide  ,  2 O-H  (dibasic)     |
  |     H2SO5    0 S-O-S bridge   ,  1 peroxide  ,  2 O-H                |
  |     H2S2O3   0 bridge - it has a direct S=S link                     |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Q40
The number of **P-O-P bridges** in P4O10 is

(a) 4 &nbsp; (b) 5 &nbsp; (c) 6 &nbsp; (d) 10

**ANSWER: (c) 6**

```
  P4O10 keeps the P4 TETRAHEDRON. A tetrahedron has 6 EDGES, and an
  oxygen atom is inserted into every edge -> 6 BRIDGING oxygens.
  The remaining 4 oxygens sit one on each corner as terminal P=O.

         6 bridging  +  4 terminal  =  10 oxygens.  It checks.

  P4O6 is the same tetrahedron with the 6 bridges but NO terminal
  oxygens -> 6 P-O-P bridges, 0 P=O.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** a tetrahedron has **6 edges**, so any P4Ox cage has
> **6 bridges**. The rest of the oxygens are terminal.

# PATTERN 10 — BASICITY OF OXOACIDS

```

  |                                                                      |
  |        BASICITY  =  the number of  O-H  groups                       |
  |                                                                      |
  |   A hydrogen bonded straight to PHOSPHORUS cannot leave as H+,       |
  |   because P (2.1) and H (2.1) have almost the same                   |

  |   Only an H sitting on an OXYGEN comes off as a proton.              |
  |                                                                      |
  |        H3PO2   1 O-H  ->  MONOBASIC                                  |
  |        H3PO3   2 O-H  ->  DIBASIC                                    |
  |        H3PO4   3 O-H  ->  TRIBASIC                                   |

  |        H4P2O6  4 O-H  ->  TETRABASIC                                 |
  |        H4P2O5  2 O-H  ->  DIBASIC   (it has 2 P-H bonds)             |
  |        (HPO3)n 1 O-H per unit -> monobasic per unit                  |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  H3PO2 has THREE hydrogens but TWO of them are bonded to phosphorus.
  Only the ONE in the O-H group is ionisable.

  It is also the STRONGEST reducing agent of the three, because it has
  the most P-H bonds:
     4 AgNO3 + 2 H2O + H3PO2  -->  4 Ag(silver mirror) + 4 HNO3 + H3PO4
```

> **SHORTCUT:** never count hydrogens from the formula. **Draw the
> structure and count the O-H groups.** That is the whole question.

```
            F
```

# PATTERN 12 — ASSERTION AND REASON

```
  THE FOUR OPTIONS ARE ALWAYS THESE FOUR. Learn them once.

    (a)  Both A and R are TRUE, and R IS the correct explanation of A
    (b)  Both A and R are TRUE, but R is NOT the correct explanation
    (c)  A is TRUE, R is FALSE
    (d)  A is FALSE, R is TRUE

  HOW TO ATTACK ONE, IN ORDER:
    STEP 1  Decide if A is true or false. Ignore R completely for now.
    STEP 2  Decide if R is true or false, on its own merits.
    STEP 3  ONLY IF BOTH ARE TRUE, ask: does R actually cause A?
            A true statement that has nothing to do with A gives (b).
```

### Q52
**Assertion (A):** NF3 is a much weaker base than NH3.
**Reason (R):** Fluorine is more electronegative than hydrogen.

**ANSWER: (a) — both true, and R is the correct explanation**

```
  In NH3 the H atoms PUSH electron density towards N, so the lone pair
  is rich and easily donated -> strongly basic.
  In NF3 the three highly electronegative F atoms PULL electron density
  AWAY from N (-I effect), so the lone pair is starved and reluctant to
  be donated -> very weakly basic.

  Supporting evidence: dipole moment of NH3 = 1.46 D, NF3 = only 0.24 D.
```

---

### Q53
**Assertion (A):** HF is a weaker acid than HCl.
**Reason (R):** Fluorine is the most electronegative element.

**ANSWER: (b) — both statements are TRUE, but R does NOT explain A**

```
  A is true: HF is a weak acid (Ka ~ 6.6 x 10^-4) while HCl is strong.
  R is true: F really is the most electronegative element.

  BUT the reason HF is weak is NOT electronegativity - if anything,
  high electronegativity should make it a STRONGER acid. The real
  reason is the very HIGH H-F BOND ENTHALPY (567 kJ/mol): the bond is
  short and strong, so the proton is hard to release. Hydrogen bonding
  in HF also holds the molecules together.

  Two true statements that do not connect  ->  option (b).
```

> **SHORTCUT:** in an assertion-reason question, a reason that is
> merely a TRUE FACT about the element (not about the bond, the shape
> or the energy) is usually a decoy -> answer (b).

### Q54
**Assertion (A):** F2 has a lower bond dissociation enthalpy than Cl2.
**Reason (R):** The lone pairs on the two small fluorine atoms repel each other strongly across the short F-F bond.

**ANSWER: (a) — both true, R correctly explains A**

```
  Bond dissociation enthalpy:  Cl2 > Br2 > F2 > I2
  F2 is ABNORMALLY LOW. The F atoms are so small that the bond is very
  short, which pushes the three lone pairs on each atom right up
  against each other. That repulsion weakens the bond.

  > TRAP: F2 still has the LOW bond enthalpy AND is the STRONGEST
    oxidising agent. Those are not contradictory - the weak bond is
    part of WHY it oxidises so well (it splits easily), and the tiny
    F- ion also has a huge hydration enthalpy.
```

### Q55
**Assertion (A):** Chlorine bleaches a coloured cloth permanently.
**Reason (R):** Chlorine bleaches by reduction, producing nascent hydrogen.

**ANSWER: (c) — A is TRUE but R is FALSE**

```
  A is TRUE: chlorine bleaching IS permanent.
  R is FALSE: chlorine bleaches by OXIDATION, producing nascent OXYGEN.

     Cl2 + H2O  -->  HCl + HOCl
     HOCl       -->  HCl + [O]         <- nascent OXYGEN
     dye + [O]  -->  colourless        <- the dye is DESTROYED

  It is SO2 that bleaches by REDUCTION with nascent HYDROGEN, and that
  is why SO2 bleaching is TEMPORARY - air re-oxidises the dye and the
  colour comes back.

  Also remember: DRY chlorine does not bleach at all. Moisture is
  essential, because water is needed to make the HOCl.
```

### Q56
**Assertion (A):** N2 is chemically inert at room temperature.
**Reason (R):** The N(triple bond)N bond enthalpy is 941.4 kJ/mol, one of the highest known.

**ANSWER: (a) — both true, R correctly explains A**

```
  Nitrogen forms a p-pi - p-pi TRIPLE bond because the atom is small
  enough for sideways p-orbital overlap. Breaking that triple bond
  needs an enormous amount of energy, so at ordinary temperature
  nothing happens. Only at about 2000 K (or at 700 K over an iron
  catalyst, as in the Haber process) does N2 react.

  Phosphorus, being bigger, cannot make p-pi - p-pi bonds. It forms
  SINGLE bonds instead and builds the strained P4 tetrahedron - which
  is why white phosphorus is one of the most reactive solids known,
  igniting in air at just 303 K.
```
