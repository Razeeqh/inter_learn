## THE 60-SECOND VERSION

The **d block** is the big block in the MIDDLE of the periodic table (groups 3 to 12).
In these elements, the electron that gets added last goes into a **d orbital of the
INNER shell**, that is the (n-1)d shell. Because the last electron enters an inner
shell, they sit "in transition" between the s block metals on the left and the
p block non-metals on the right — hence the name **transition elements**.

The **f block** is the two rows pulled OUT and printed at the bottom of the table.
Here the last electron enters an **(n-2)f orbital**, two shells inside. These are
called **inner transition elements**.

```
   THE ONE SENTENCE THAT EXPLAINS 80% OF THIS CHAPTER
   ---------------------------------------------------

   Transition metals have  PARTLY FILLED d ORBITALS.

   Partly filled d orbitals give you:

        variable oxidation states   (d and s electrons have similar energy,
                                     so different numbers can be removed)
        colour                      (d-d transition is possible)
        paramagnetism               (unpaired electrons exist)
        catalytic activity          (variable oxidation states + big surface)
        complex formation           (small size, high charge, empty d orbitals)

   Zn, Cd, Hg have a COMPLETELY FILLED d10 shell in the atom AND in their
   common ion (M2+). So they get NONE of the above, and that is exactly why
   they are NOT regarded as typical transition elements.
```

**If you understand only that box, you can answer half the theory questions.**
