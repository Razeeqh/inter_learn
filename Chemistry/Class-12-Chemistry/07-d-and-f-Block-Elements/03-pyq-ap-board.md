## Topic: position, definition, configuration

### Q A1
What are transition elements? Why are they called so?

**ANSWER:**
```
  TRANSITION ELEMENTS are the d block elements whose ATOMS have a
  partly filled d subshell, or which give rise to CATIONS with a
  partly filled d subshell.

  They are called "transition" elements because they lie IN BETWEEN
  the highly metallic s block elements and the non-metallic p block
  elements, and their properties change gradually from one to the other.
```

### Q A2
Write the general electronic configuration of the d block and f block elements.

**ANSWER:**
```
  d BLOCK   (transition)          ( n - 1 ) d 1-10    n s 1-2

  f BLOCK   (inner transition)    ( n - 2 ) f 1-14
                                  ( n - 1 ) d 0-1
                                        n   s 2
```

### Q A4
Why are Zn, Cd and Hg not regarded as typical transition elements?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Their electronic configurations are:
       Zn = [Ar] 3d10 4s2     Cd = [Kr] 4d10 5s2    Hg = [Xe] 5d10 6s2

  The d subshell is COMPLETELY FILLED (d10) in the atom, and it remains
  COMPLETELY FILLED (d10) in their common M2+ ions.

  Since they have NO partly filled d subshell either in the atom or in
  the ion, they do not satisfy the definition of a transition element.
```

### Q A5
Write the electronic configurations of chromium and copper. Why are they exceptional?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Cr (24)  =  [Ar] 3d5  4s1        (expected 3d4 4s2)
  Cu (29)  =  [Ar] 3d10 4s1        (expected 3d9 4s2)

  REASON:  exactly HALF-FILLED (d5) and COMPLETELY FILLED (d10)
  subshells have extra stability because of their greater SYMMETRY
  and greater EXCHANGE ENERGY.
```

## Topic: general properties

### Q A7
Why do transition elements show variable oxidation states?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In transition elements the (n-1)d and ns orbitals have ALMOST THE
  SAME ENERGY.

  Therefore both the ns electrons and a VARIABLE number of (n-1)d
  electrons can take part in bond formation and can be removed using
  only a small amount of extra energy.

  Different numbers of electrons lost  ->  different oxidation states,
  differing by one unit each time.
```

---

### Q A8
Which element of the 3d series shows the maximum number of oxidation states? Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MANGANESE (Mn) - it shows +2, +3, +4, +5, +6 and +7.

  REASON: Mn is [Ar] 3d5 4s2 , so it has the greatest number of
  available (unpaired + s) electrons: 5 + 2 = 7. All seven can be
  lost, giving oxidation states from +2 up to +7.
```

---

### Q A9
Why is the atomic radius almost constant in the middle of the 3d series?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Two effects oppose each other and almost cancel:

    (a) the increasing NUCLEAR CHARGE pulls the electron cloud IN,
    (b) the increasing d-d ELECTRON REPULSION and the SCREENING by the
        added d electron push the outer electrons OUT.

  Since the two are nearly equal in the middle of the series, the net
  change in radius is almost zero (Fe, Co and Ni are all about 125-126 pm).
```

### Q A10
Why is E° (Cu2+/Cu) positive? What is its consequence?

**ANSWER:**
```
  E° (Cu2+/Cu) = + 0.34 V

  REASON: copper has a HIGH enthalpy of sublimation and a HIGH
  ionisation enthalpy, and its enthalpy of hydration is NOT large
  enough to compensate for them.

  CONSEQUENCE: copper lies below hydrogen in the activity series, so
  it does NOT liberate hydrogen gas from dilute acids.
```

### Q A11
Why is E° (Mn2+/Mn) more negative than expected?

**ANSWER:**
```
  E° (Mn2+/Mn) = - 1.18 V , much more negative than the trend suggests.

  REASON: the product ion Mn2+ has a 3d5 configuration, which is
  HALF-FILLED and therefore extra stable. Because the product is so
  stable, manganese gives up its two electrons very readily.
```

### Q A12
Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of Fe2+.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Fe (26) = [Ar] 3d6 4s2      ->    Fe2+ = [Ar] 3d6

  3d6  =  [updn][ up ][ up ][ up ][ up ]       n = 4 unpaired electrons

  mu  =  sqrt( n (n+2) )  =  sqrt( 4 x 6 )  =  sqrt 24  =  4.90 BM
```

## Topic: colour, catalysis, complexes

### Q A15
Why are Zn2+ and Sc3+ salts colourless while Cu2+ salts are blue?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Colour arises from a d-d TRANSITION, which needs a PARTLY FILLED
  d subshell.

  Sc3+ = 3d0   ->  no d electron available to jump   ->  COLOURLESS
  Zn2+ = 3d10  ->  d subshell is full, no vacant place to jump into
                                                     ->  COLOURLESS
  Cu2+ = 3d9   ->  partly filled, a d-d transition is possible
                                                     ->  BLUE
```

### Q A16
What are interstitial compounds? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Compounds formed when small atoms such as H, C, N or B occupy the
  EMPTY SPACES (interstices) between the metal atoms in the crystal
  lattice of a transition metal.

  Examples:  TiC , Mn4N , Fe3H , VH0.56 , TiH1.7 , steel (Fe + C)

  They are hard, have high melting points, are non-stoichiometric,
```

## Topic: f block

### Q A23
What is lanthanoid contraction?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The STEADY DECREASE in the atomic and ionic radii of the lanthanoid
  elements with INCREASING atomic number, from La (57) to Lu (71),
  is called the LANTHANOID CONTRACTION.

  CAUSE: the added electron enters the inner 4f subshell, and 4f
  electrons SHIELD the outer electrons from the nucleus very POORLY,
  so the effective nuclear charge keeps increasing.
```

### Q A25
Why is the +3 oxidation state the most common for lanthanoids?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Lanthanoids lose their two 6s electrons and one 4f (or 5d) electron
  fairly easily, giving the M3+ ion.

  Removing a FOURTH electron requires a very large amount of energy,
  which is not compensated by lattice or hydration energy.

  Therefore +3 is the characteristic and most stable oxidation state
  for practically all the lanthanoids.
```

---

### Q A26
Why are all actinoids radioactive?

**ANSWER:**
```
  All the actinoids have atomic numbers of 90 and above. Their nuclei
  contain too many protons and neutrons to be stable, so the nuclei
  break down spontaneously, emitting radiation.

  This is the biggest single difference between the actinoids and the
  lanthanoids (of which only promethium is radioactive).
```

### Q B1
Explain the general characteristics of transition elements with reference to
(a) metallic character and (b) atomic radii.

**ANSWER:**
```
  (a) METALLIC CHARACTER

      All transition elements are metals. They are hard, malleable,
      ductile, and good conductors of heat and electricity, with very
      high melting and boiling points.

      REASON:  small atomic size, high nuclear charge, and - most
      importantly - UNPAIRED d ELECTRONS that take part in metallic
      bonding along with the s electrons. More unpaired electrons means
      more "ropes" holding the lattice, so a stronger metallic bond.

      Cr has the highest melting point of the 3d series (most unpaired
      electrons available). Zn has the lowest, because its d10 shell
      is full and takes no part in bonding. Hg is even a liquid.

  (b) ATOMIC RADII

      Sc  Ti  V   Cr  Mn  Fe  Co  Ni  Cu  Zn   (pm)
      164 147 135 129 137 126 125 125 128 137

      - falls sharply at the START (nuclear charge increases, the new
        electron goes into the same inner d shell)
      - stays almost CONSTANT in the middle (increasing nuclear charge
        is cancelled by increasing d-d repulsion and screening)
      - rises slightly at the END (repulsion in the nearly full d shell
        beats the nuclear pull)
```

### Q B2
Explain why transition metals show variable oxidation states. Illustrate with
the 3d series.

**ANSWER:**
```
  REASON
     The (n-1)d and ns orbitals have almost the SAME energy, so both
     the s electrons and a variable number of d electrons can be lost
     with only a small energy difference. Hence oxidation states that
     differ by ONE unit are possible (unlike the p block, where they
     differ by two).

  ILLUSTRATION - the 3d series

     Sc  +3
     Ti  +2 +3 +4
     V   +2 +3 +4 +5
     Cr  +2 +3 +4 +5 +6
     Mn  +2 +3 +4 +5 +6 +7      <-- MAXIMUM
     Fe  +2 +3 (+4 +5 +6)
     Co  +2 +3
     Ni  +2
     Cu  +1 +2
     Zn  +2 only

  TWO POINTS TO NOTE
     1. The number of oxidation states INCREASES up to Mn and then
        DECREASES, because after Mn the d electrons begin to PAIR UP
        and paired electrons are harder to remove.
     2. The HIGHEST oxidation states appear only in oxides and
        fluorides (MnO4- is +7, CrO4 2- is +6), because O and F are
        the most electronegative and can stabilise a high positive
        charge.
```

### Q B3
Explain the magnetic properties of transition elements. Calculate the magnetic
moments of Ti3+, Cr3+ and Mn2+.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Most transition metal ions have UNPAIRED d electrons, so most of
  their compounds are PARAMAGNETIC - they are attracted into a
  magnetic field. Ions with all electrons paired are DIAMAGNETIC.

  The magnetic moment is calculated from the SPIN-ONLY formula:

         mu  =  sqrt( n ( n + 2 ) )     Bohr Magneton (BM)

         n = number of unpaired electrons

  CALCULATIONS

     Ti (22) = [Ar]3d2 4s2   ->  Ti3+ = 3d1   ->  n = 1
        mu = sqrt(1 x 3) = 1.73 BM

     Cr (24) = [Ar]3d5 4s1   ->  Cr3+ = 3d3   ->  n = 3
        mu = sqrt(3 x 5) = sqrt 15 = 3.87 BM

     Mn (25) = [Ar]3d5 4s2   ->  Mn2+ = 3d5   ->  n = 5
        mu = sqrt(5 x 7) = sqrt 35 = 5.92 BM   (the maximum possible)

  The paramagnetism INCREASES with the number of unpaired electrons,
  so it rises up to Mn2+/Fe3+ in the middle of the series and then
  falls again to zero at Zn2+ (3d10).
```

### Q B4
Why do transition metal ions form coloured compounds? Explain with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Transition metal ions have a PARTLY FILLED d subshell.

  When ligands (water molecules, ions and so on) surround the metal
  ion, their electric field SPLITS the five d orbitals into two sets
  separated by a small energy gap.

                                   ---  ---     upper set
                                    ^
     --- --- --- --- ---            |  gap = delta  (small, matches
     free ion, all equal            v              visible light)
                                   --- --- ---   lower set

  An electron in the lower set ABSORBS a photon of visible light and
  jumps to the upper set. This is called a d-d TRANSITION.

  That colour is removed from the white light passing through, and we
  see the COMPLEMENTARY colour. Hence the ion appears coloured.

  EXAMPLES
     Ti3+ (3d1) purple ,  V3+ (3d2) green ,  Cr3+ (3d3) violet
     Mn2+ (3d5) light pink ,  Fe2+ (3d6) green ,  Ni2+ (3d8) green
     Cu2+ (3d9) blue

  IONS THAT ARE COLOURLESS
     Sc3+ (3d0) and Ti4+ (3d0) - no d electron is available to jump
     Zn2+ (3d10) and Cu+ (3d10) - the d subshell is full, so there is
     no vacant place to jump into.
```

### Q B5
Why do transition metals and their compounds act as good catalysts? Give examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THREE REASONS

  1. VARIABLE OXIDATION STATES
     The metal can accept an electron from one reactant and hand it to
     another, moving between two oxidation states and then returning to
     the original. This offers a new path of LOWER ACTIVATION ENERGY.

     Example, the Fe3+ catalysed reaction of I- with S2O8 2-:
        2 Fe3+ + 2 I-      ->  2 Fe2+ + I2
        2 Fe2+ + S2O8 2-   ->  2 Fe3+ + 2 SO4 2-
        the Fe3+ is regenerated - a true catalyst.

  2. LARGE SURFACE AREA WITH FREE VALENCIES
     Surface atoms have unused valencies because of partly filled d
     orbitals. Reactant molecules are ADSORBED there. This increases
     their concentration at the surface AND weakens the bonds inside
     the reactant molecules.

  3. FORMATION OF UNSTABLE INTERMEDIATE COMPOUNDS
     which break down readily to give the product and the free catalyst.

  EXAMPLES
     Fe    - Haber process (NH3)
     V2O5  - Contact process (H2SO4)
     Ni    - hydrogenation of oils
     Pt/Rh - catalytic converters, Ostwald process
     MnO2  - decomposition of KClO3
```

### Q B6
Why do transition elements form complex compounds? Give examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A complex compound has a central metal ion surrounded by ligands
  attached by coordinate bonds.

  Transition metals form complexes readily because of THREE features:

     1. SMALL SIZE of the metal ion
     2. HIGH IONIC CHARGE
        (1 and 2 together give a high charge density, so the ion
         strongly attracts the lone pairs of the ligands)
     3. VACANT d ORBITALS of suitable energy that can ACCEPT the lone
        pairs donated by the ligands

  EXAMPLES
        [Fe(CN)6] 4-      [Fe(CN)6] 3-
        [Cu(NH3)4] 2+     (deep blue, formed with excess ammonia)
        [Ni(CN)4] 2-      [Co(NH3)6] 3+
```

### Q B7
What are interstitial compounds? Give their characteristic properties.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Transition metal crystals have small empty spaces (INTERSTICES)
  between the metal atoms. When small atoms such as H, C, N or B
  occupy these spaces, the product is an INTERSTITIAL COMPOUND.

        M     M     M          M = metal atom
           x     x             x = small H / C / N atom in the hole
        M     M     M

  EXAMPLES:  TiC , Mn4N , Fe3H , VH0.56 , TiH1.7 , steel

  PROPERTIES
     1. NON-STOICHIOMETRIC - the composition is not a whole-number
        ratio, so no definite formula and no definite oxidation state.
     2. VERY HARD - often harder than the pure metal.
     3. VERY HIGH melting points, higher than the pure metal.
     4. They RETAIN metallic conductivity.
     5. They are chemically INERT.

  REASON for the hardness and high melting point: the trapped atoms
  form extra bonds with the metal atoms and stop the metal layers
  from sliding over one another. This is exactly why steel is harder
  than pure iron.
```

### Q B8
Explain the chromate - dichromate equilibrium. What is the effect of pH?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In solution, the chromate ion and the dichromate ion exist in
  equilibrium, and the position of the equilibrium depends on the pH.

     2 CrO4 2-  +  2 H+    <-->    Cr2O7 2-  +  H2O
       YELLOW                        ORANGE

     Cr2O7 2-   +  2 OH-   <-->    2 CrO4 2- +  H2O
       ORANGE                        YELLOW

  EFFECT OF pH

     ADDING ACID (pH decreases)
        The extra H+ pushes the first equilibrium to the RIGHT
        (Le Chatelier's principle), so the YELLOW solution turns ORANGE.
        In acidic solution the DICHROMATE ion predominates.

     ADDING ALKALI (pH increases)
        OH- removes H+, so the equilibrium shifts to the LEFT and the
        ORANGE solution turns YELLOW.
        In alkaline solution the CHROMATE ion predominates.

  IMPORTANT: chromium remains in the +6 oxidation state in BOTH ions.
  This is only an interconversion of ions, NOT a redox reaction.
```

### Q B9
Describe the oxidising action of potassium permanganate in acidic, alkaline
and neutral media, with half equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  KMnO4 is a powerful oxidising agent. What it is reduced to depends
  entirely on the medium.

  1. ACIDIC MEDIUM  (dilute H2SO4)   -  STRONGEST

        Mn changes from +7 to +6   -  gain of 1 electron

        It also supplies nascent oxygen:
              2 MnO4- + 2 OH-  ->  2 MnO4 2- + H2O + [O]

  MEMORY:   acid 5 electrons, neutral 3 electrons, alkali 1 electron.
```

### Q B11
What is lanthanoid contraction? Give its causes and consequences.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
     The steady decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of the
     lanthanoid elements with increasing atomic number, from
     La (57) to Lu (71).
          La3+ = 103 pm   ...   Lu3+ = 86 pm

  CAUSE
     Along the series the nuclear charge increases by +1 each time and
     the additional electron enters the SAME inner 4f subshell. The 4f
     orbitals have a diffuse, badly shaped charge cloud, so a 4f
     electron SHIELDS the outer electrons very POORLY. Hence the
     effective nuclear charge felt by the outer electrons keeps
     increasing and pulls them steadily inwards.

  CONSEQUENCES
     1. The 4d and 5d series have almost the same atomic radii, so
        Zr (160 pm) and Hf (159 pm), Nb and Ta, Mo and W behave like
        "twin pairs" with nearly identical chemistry and occur together.
     2. The lanthanoids themselves are extremely difficult to separate,
        because their sizes and properties are so similar.
     3. The basic strength of the hydroxides decreases steadily:
        La(OH)3 is the most basic, Lu(OH)3 the least.
     4. The tendency to form complexes increases slightly from La3+ to
        Lu3+, because the charge density increases as the ion shrinks.
```

### Q B12
Compare the properties of lanthanoids and actinoids.

**ANSWER:**
```
  +----+---------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |    |     LANTHANOIDS           |        ACTINOIDS                |
  +----+---------------------------+---------------------------------+
  | 1  | 4f orbitals are filled    | 5f orbitals are filled          |
  | 2  | Ce(58) to Lu(71)          | Th(90) to Lr(103)               |
  | 3  | [Xe] 4f1-14 5d0-1 6s2     | [Rn] 5f1-14 6d0-1 7s2           |
  | 4  | Only Pm is radioactive    | ALL are radioactive             |
  | 5  | Mainly +3; +2 and +4 rare | +3, +4, +5, +6 and even +7      |
  | 6  | No oxo-cations            | Oxo-cations UO2 2+, PuO2 2+     |
  | 7  | Less complex formation    | Greater complex formation       |
  | 8  | Less basic compounds      | More basic compounds            |
```

### Q B13
Explain the anomalous values of the standard electrode potentials of Mn, Ni
and Zn.

**ANSWER:**
```
  E° (M2+/M) values in volts:

    Ti     V     Cr     Mn     Fe     Co     Ni     Cu     Zn
  -1.63 -1.18  -0.90  -1.18  -0.44  -0.28  -0.25  +0.34  -0.76

  The general trend is that the values become LESS NEGATIVE across the
  series. Mn, Ni and Zn break that trend by being MORE NEGATIVE than
  expected.

  E° depends on three terms:
     enthalpy of SUBLIMATION + IONISATION enthalpy  (energy required)
     enthalpy of HYDRATION                          (energy released)

  Mn   -  Mn2+ is 3d5, a HALF-FILLED and extra stable configuration,
          so manganese loses its electrons very readily.

  Zn   -  Zn2+ is 3d10, a COMPLETELY FILLED and extra stable
          configuration, so zinc also ionises readily.

  Ni   -  Ni2+ has an exceptionally HIGH (very negative) HYDRATION
          ENTHALPY, which releases a large amount of energy and drives
          the process forward.

  Cu is the opposite case: its E° is POSITIVE (+0.34 V) because its
  high sublimation and ionisation enthalpies are not compensated by
  its hydration enthalpy. That is why copper does not liberate H2 from
  dilute acids.
```

### Q B14
Write the preparation of potassium dichromate from chromite ore with equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ORE:  CHROMITE  FeCr2O4   ( = FeO . Cr2O3 )

  STEP 1  Fuse the finely powdered ore with sodium carbonate in
          EXCESS AIR:

     4 FeCr2O4 + 8 Na2CO3 + 7 O2  --(fused, air)-->
                           8 Na2CrO4 + 2 Fe2O3 + 8 CO2
                          (yellow sodium chromate)

  STEP 2  Extract with water and acidify the yellow solution with
          concentrated sulphuric acid:

     2 Na2CrO4 + H2SO4   ->   Na2Cr2O7 + Na2SO4 + H2O
                              (orange sodium dichromate)

  STEP 3  Treat the hot concentrated solution with potassium chloride.
          Na2Cr2O7 is very soluble but K2Cr2O7 is much less soluble,
          so orange-red crystals of K2Cr2O7 separate out on cooling:

     Na2Cr2O7 + 2 KCl   ->   K2Cr2O7 + 2 NaCl
```

### Q B15
Write the preparation of potassium permanganate from pyrolusite with equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ORE:  PYROLUSITE  MnO2

  STEP 1  Fuse the powdered ore with potassium hydroxide in the

                         green        purple
            cathode  :  H2O + e-  ->  OH- + (1/2) H2

     (b) DISPROPORTIONATION in acidic or neutral solution:

            3 MnO4 2- + 4 H+  ->  2 MnO4- + MnO2 + 2 H2O

  The deep purple solution is concentrated and KMnO4 crystallises out
  as dark purple crystals.
```

### Q B16
Give the uses of K2Cr2O7 and KMnO4. Why is K2Cr2O7 a primary standard but
KMnO4 is not?

**ANSWER:**
```
  USES OF K2Cr2O7
     - as a PRIMARY STANDARD in volumetric analysis
     - in leather tanning and in the dyeing industry
     - in the chromyl chloride test for chlorides
     - as a strong oxidising agent in the laboratory

  USES OF KMnO4
     - as an oxidising agent in volumetric analysis (permanganometry)
     - as a disinfectant and germicide (dilute solution = Condy's fluid)
     - for bleaching wool, cotton and silk
     - in the preparation of benzoic acid from toluene
     - as Baeyer's reagent (cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO4) to test for
       unsaturation in alkenes

  WHY K2Cr2O7 IS A PRIMARY STANDARD
     It can be obtained in a very pure state, it is not hygroscopic
     (it does not absorb moisture from the air), and its solution is
     stable for a long time. So a solution of exactly known
     concentration can be prepared just by weighing it.

  WHY KMnO4 IS NOT
     It is difficult to obtain perfectly pure (it usually contains
     traces of MnO2), and its solution slowly decomposes on standing,
     especially in light. So its solution must be standardised against
     a primary standard such as oxalic acid before use.
```

### Q C3
Explain the general properties of transition elements, giving the reason for
each: metallic character, atomic radii, ionisation enthalpy, oxidation states,
magnetic properties, colour, catalysis and complex formation.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  1. METALLIC CHARACTER
     All are metals - hard, malleable, ductile, good conductors, with
     very high melting and boiling points.
     REASON: small size, high nuclear charge, and UNPAIRED d ELECTRONS
     that join the metallic bonding along with the s electrons.
     Cr has the highest m.p. of the 3d series; Zn the lowest (d10 full,
     no d participation); Hg is a liquid.

  2. ATOMIC RADII
     Falls sharply at the start of a series, is almost CONSTANT in the
     middle, and rises slightly at the end.
     REASON: increasing nuclear charge (pulls in) is opposed by
     increasing d-d repulsion and screening (pushes out). In the middle
     they cancel almost exactly.
     Down a group, the 4d and 5d elements have nearly the SAME size
     because of the LANTHANOID CONTRACTION (Zr 160 pm, Hf 159 pm).

  3. IONISATION ENTHALPY
     Higher than the s block, lower than the p block, and increases
     only slowly and irregularly across a series.
     REASON: the added d electron SCREENS the outer s electron from
     the increased nuclear charge, so the two effects partly cancel.
     Zn has the highest first value because of its stable 3d10 4s2.

  4. VARIABLE OXIDATION STATES
     REASON: (n-1)d and ns orbitals have almost the same energy, so
     both s and a variable number of d electrons can be lost. Hence
     states differing by ONE unit. Mn shows the maximum (+2 to +7)
     because it has 7 available electrons. The highest states occur
     only in oxides and fluorides.

  5. MAGNETIC PROPERTIES
     Most compounds are PARAMAGNETIC.
     REASON: presence of UNPAIRED d electrons.
          mu = sqrt( n(n+2) ) BM
     Mn2+ and Fe3+ (both 3d5) give the maximum, 5.92 BM.
     Ions with d0 or d10 (Sc3+, Zn2+, Cu+) are DIAMAGNETIC.

  6. COLOUR
     Most ions are coloured.
     REASON: a PARTLY FILLED d subshell allows a d-d TRANSITION - an
     electron absorbs visible light and jumps from the lower to the
     higher set of split d orbitals. The complementary colour is seen.
     d0 and d10 ions are COLOURLESS.

  7. CATALYTIC PROPERTIES
     REASON: (a) variable oxidation states let the metal give and take
     electrons, providing a low activation energy path; (b) the large
     surface has free valencies that ADSORB and weaken the reactants;
     (c) unstable intermediate compounds are formed and broken down.
     Examples: Fe (Haber), V2O5 (Contact), Ni (hydrogenation).

  8. COMPLEX FORMATION
     REASON: small ionic size, high ionic charge, and VACANT d
     ORBITALS of suitable energy that accept lone pairs from ligands.
     Examples: [Fe(CN)6]4- , [Cu(NH3)4]2+ , [Co(NH3)6]3+.

  9. INTERSTITIAL COMPOUNDS AND ALLOYS
     Small atoms (H, C, N, B) fit into the holes of the metal lattice,
     giving hard, high-melting, non-stoichiometric interstitial
     compounds such as TiC and steel. Because their atomic radii are
     similar, transition metals also substitute freely for one another
     to form alloys such as brass, bronze and stainless steel.
```

### Q C4
Write an essay on the f block elements: their position, electronic
configurations, oxidation states, lanthanoid contraction with its causes and
consequences, and a comparison of lanthanoids with actinoids.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  1. POSITION
     The f block consists of the two rows placed separately at the
     bottom of the periodic table. In these elements the last electron
     enters an (n-2)f orbital, that is, an orbital two shells inside
     the outermost shell. They are therefore called INNER TRANSITION
     ELEMENTS.

        LANTHANOIDS  -  Ce (58) to Lu (71) , the 4f series,
                        following lanthanum La (57)
        ACTINOIDS    -  Th (90) to Lr (103) , the 5f series,
                        following actinium Ac (89)

  2. ELECTRONIC CONFIGURATIONS

        GENERAL   :   (n-2) f 1-14   (n-1) d 0-1   n s 2
        LANTHANOIDS:  [Xe] 4f 1-14   5d 0-1   6s 2
        ACTINOIDS  :  [Rn] 5f 1-14   6d 0-1   7s 2

     Examples:  Ce = [Xe] 4f1 5d1 6s2
                Gd = [Xe] 4f7 5d1 6s2   (half-filled 4f7)
                Lu = [Xe] 4f14 5d1 6s2  (full 4f14)

  3. OXIDATION STATES
     +3 is the characteristic and most stable state of ALL lanthanoids,
     because the two 6s and one 4f/5d electron are lost easily but a
     fourth is not.
     A few show +2 or +4 when the resulting ion reaches an empty,
     half-filled or full 4f shell:
          Ce4+ (4f0)  -  a good oxidising agent
          Eu2+ (4f7)  -  a good reducing agent
          Tb4+ (4f7)  ,  Yb2+ (4f14)

     Actinoids show a much wider range: +3, +4, +5, +6 and even +7,
     because their 5f, 6d and 7s orbitals have very similar energies
     and all can take part in bonding.

  4. LANTHANOID CONTRACTION

     DEFINITION: the steady decrease in the atomic and ionic radii of
     the lanthanoids with increasing atomic number, from La to Lu
     (La3+ = 103 pm down to Lu3+ = 86 pm).

     CAUSE: the extra electron enters the same inner 4f subshell, and
     4f orbitals have a diffuse charge cloud that SHIELDS very POORLY.
     The effective nuclear charge on the outer electrons therefore
     keeps increasing and pulls them steadily inwards.

     CONSEQUENCES:
       (i)   the 4d and 5d series have almost identical radii, so
             Zr/Hf, Nb/Ta and Mo/W are "twin pairs" with practically
             the same chemistry and are found together;
       (ii)  the lanthanoids themselves are extremely difficult to
             separate from one another;
       (iii) the basic strength of the hydroxides falls steadily from
             La(OH)3 (most basic) to Lu(OH)3 (least basic);
       (iv)  the tendency to form complexes increases slightly from
             La3+ to Lu3+ as the charge density increases.

  5. USES
     MISCH METAL - about 95 % lanthanoid metal + about 5 % iron, with
     traces of S, C, Ca and Al. Alloyed with magnesium it is used in
     bullets, shells and lighter FLINTS, because it is pyrophoric.
     Mixed lanthanoid oxides are used as catalysts in petroleum
     cracking, in TV screens, and in glass and ceramics. Small amounts
     added to steel improve its strength and workability.

  6. COMPARISON OF LANTHANOIDS AND ACTINOIDS

  +----+---------------------------+---------------------------------+
  | 1  | 4f orbitals filled        | 5f orbitals filled              |
  | 2  | Ce(58) to Lu(71)          | Th(90) to Lr(103)               |
  | 3  | [Xe]4f1-14 5d0-1 6s2      | [Rn]5f1-14 6d0-1 7s2            |
  | 4  | Only Pm is radioactive    | ALL are radioactive             |
  | 5  | Mainly +3                 | +3 to +7                        |
  | 6  | No oxo-cations            | UO2 2+ , PuO2 2+                |
  | 7  | Weaker complex formation  | Stronger complex formation      |
  | 8  | Less basic compounds      | More basic compounds            |
  | 9  | Mostly lightly coloured   | Mostly strongly coloured        |
  | 10 | Lanthanoid contraction    | Actinoid contraction is GREATER |
  | 11 | Magnetism easy to explain | Magnetism hard to explain       |
  | 12 | All occur naturally       | Beyond U they are man-made      |
  +----+---------------------------+---------------------------------+
```
