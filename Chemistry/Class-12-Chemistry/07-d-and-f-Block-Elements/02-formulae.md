# 2. EVERY PROPERTY TREND WITH ITS REASON

## 2.1 Atomic / ionic radii across a series

| Region | What happens | REASON |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| Start (Sc to V) | radius **decreases sharply** | nuclear charge rises, new electron enters the SAME inner d shell, so the nucleus pulls the cloud in |
| Middle (Cr to Ni) | radius **almost constant** | increasing nuclear charge (pulls in) is cancelled by increasing d-d electron repulsion and screening (pushes out) |
| End (Cu, Zn) | radius **increases slightly** | d shell now nearly full; electron-electron repulsion and screening beat the nuclear pull |

```
  METALLIC RADII of the 3d series / pm

   Sc   Ti   V    Cr   Mn   Fe   Co   Ni   Cu   Zn
   164  147  135  129  137  126  125  125  128  137
```

## 2.2 Down a group — the lanthanoid contraction effect

| Group | 3d | 4d | 5d | Comment |
|-------|----|----|----|---------|
| 4 | Ti 147 | Zr 160 | Hf 159 | Zr and Hf are almost IDENTICAL |
| 5 | V 135 | Nb 146 | Ta 146 | Nb and Ta are identical |
| 6 | Cr 129 | Mo 139 | W 139 | Mo and W are identical |

```
  REASON:  the 14 LANTHANOIDS sit between the 4d and 5d series.
           Across them the size shrinks steadily (LANTHANOID CONTRACTION),
           and that shrinkage exactly cancels the expected increase from
           adding a new shell.

  RESULT:  Zr/Hf , Nb/Ta , Mo/W are "twin pairs" - same size, same
           chemistry, found together, very hard to separate.
```

## 2.3 Ionisation enthalpy

| Observation | REASON |
|-------------|--------|
| Higher than the s block, lower than the p block | small size + high nuclear charge, but the outer electron is an s electron |
| Increases across a series only SLOWLY and IRREGULARLY | added d electron SCREENS the 4s electron, so increased nuclear charge is partly cancelled |
| Zn has the highest first IE (906 kJ/mol) | fully filled, very stable 3d10 4s2 |
| Mn and Zn have abnormally high THIRD ionisation enthalpies | Mn2+ is 3d5 (half filled) and Zn2+ is 3d10 (full) — both very stable and hard to break |

```
  FIRST IONISATION ENTHALPY / kJ mol-1

   Sc   Ti   V    Cr   Mn   Fe   Co   Ni   Cu   Zn
   631  656  650  653  717  762  758  736  745  906
```

## 2.4 Metallic character, melting point, enthalpy of atomisation

| Property | Trend | REASON |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Hardness, m.p., b.p., enthalpy of atomisation | HIGH; rise to a maximum in the middle of the series then fall | UNPAIRED d electrons join the metallic bonding; the more unpaired d electrons, the stronger the bond |
| Cr | highest m.p. of the 3d series | maximum number of unpaired electrons available for bonding |
| Mn | anomalously LOW dip | its stable, symmetrical 3d5 4s2 arrangement resists taking part in bonding |
| Zn, Cd, Hg | LOWEST m.p.; Hg is a LIQUID | d10 shell is full and takes no part; only s electrons bond |
| Density | increases across the series | mass increases while volume decreases |

## 2.5 Oxidation states

| Element | Oxidation states | Most stable |
|---------|------------------|-------------|
| Sc | +3 | +3 |
| Ti | +2, +3, +4 | +4 |
| V | +2, +3, +4, +5 | +5 |
| Cr | +2, +3, +4, +5, +6 | +3 |
| **Mn** | **+2, +3, +4, +5, +6, +7** | +2 |
| Fe | +2, +3, (+4, +5, +6) | +3 |
| Co | +2, +3, (+4) | +2 |
| Ni | +2, (+3, +4) | +2 |
| Cu | +1, +2 | +2 |
| Zn | +2 only | +2 |

```
  WHY VARIABLE OXIDATION STATES?
     The (n-1)d and ns orbitals have ALMOST THE SAME ENERGY, so both
     the s electrons and a variable number of d electrons can be lost.

  WHY DO THEY DIFFER BY ONE UNIT (unlike the p block, which jumps by 2)?
     Same reason - the energies are so close that electrons come off
     one at a time.

  WHY DOES Mn SHOW THE MAXIMUM (+2 to +7)?
     It has the greatest number of available electrons: 5 d + 2 s = 7.

  WHY DOES THE NUMBER FALL AFTER Mn?
     The d electrons start PAIRING UP; paired electrons are held more
     tightly and are harder to remove.

  WHERE DO THE HIGHEST STATES APPEAR?
     Only in OXIDES and FLUORIDES - O and F are the most electronegative
     and can stabilise a very high positive charge.
        MnO4- (+7) , CrO4 2- (+6) , VF5 (+5) , OsF6 (+6) , Mn2O7 (+7)

  WHERE DO ZERO / LOW STATES APPEAR?
     In carbonyl complexes.   Ni(CO)4 - nickel is ZERO.
                              Fe(CO)5 - iron is ZERO.
```

## 2.6 Standard electrode potentials E° (M2+ / M)

```
  +------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
  |  Ti  |   V   |  Cr   |  Mn   |  Fe   |  Co   |  Ni   |  Cu   |  Zn   |
  +------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
  |-1.63 | -1.18 | -0.90 | -1.18 | -0.44 | -0.28 | -0.25 | +0.34 | -0.76 |
  +------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+-------+
```

| Case | Value | Why it breaks the trend |
|------|-------|-------------------------|
| **Mn** | -1.18 V, much more negative than expected | product Mn2+ is 3d5, HALF-FILLED and extra stable, so Mn ionises readily |
| **Ni** | -0.25 V, more negative than expected | Ni2+ has a very high (very negative) HYDRATION ENTHALPY |
| **Zn** | -0.76 V, more negative than expected | product Zn2+ is 3d10, COMPLETELY FILLED and extra stable |
| **Cu** | **+0.34 V — the only POSITIVE value** | high sublimation enthalpy + high ionisation enthalpy are NOT compensated by its hydration enthalpy; therefore Cu does not liberate H2 from dilute acids |

```
  E° is decided by three energy terms:

     M(s) --sublimation--> M(g) --ionisation--> M2+(g) --hydration--> M2+(aq)
           (costs energy)        (costs energy)        (RELEASES energy)
```

## 2.7 The four "characteristic" properties at a glance

| Property | Condition needed | Fails for |
|----------|------------------|-----------|
| Colour | partly filled d subshell (d1 to d9) so a d-d transition is possible | d0 (Sc3+, Ti4+) and d10 (Zn2+, Cu+, Cd2+, Hg2+) |
| Paramagnetism | at least one UNPAIRED electron | d0 and d10 ions — these are diamagnetic |
| Variable oxidation state | (n-1)d and ns energies close together | Zn, Cd, Hg — only +2 |
| Catalysis | variable oxidation states + free surface valencies | Zn, Cd, Hg — poor catalysts |

# 4. COLOUR — d-d TRANSITION

```
  CONDITION FOR COLOUR:   the ion must have a PARTLY FILLED d subshell.

  MECHANISM:
     ligands split the 5 d orbitals into two levels ->
     an electron absorbs visible light and jumps from the lower to the
     upper level (a d-d TRANSITION) ->
     that colour is removed from white light ->
     we SEE the COMPLEMENTARY colour.
```

| Ion | d config | Colour | Ion | d config | Colour |
|-----|----------|--------|-----|----------|--------|
| Sc3+ | 3d0 | colourless | Mn2+ | 3d5 | light pink |
| Ti4+ | 3d0 | colourless | Fe3+ | 3d5 | yellow |
| Ti3+ | 3d1 | purple / violet | Fe2+ | 3d6 | green |
| V3+ | 3d2 | green | Co2+ | 3d7 | pink |
| V2+ | 3d3 | violet | Ni2+ | 3d8 | green |
| Cr3+ | 3d3 | violet / green | Cu2+ | 3d9 | blue |
| Mn3+ | 3d4 | violet | Cu+ | 3d10 | colourless |
| Cr2+ | 3d4 | blue | Zn2+ | 3d10 | colourless |

```
  THE EXCEPTION YOU MUST QUOTE:

     KMnO4  (Mn is +7 -> 3d0)  is intensely PURPLE
     K2Cr2O7 (Cr is +6 -> 3d0) is intensely ORANGE

     They have NO d electrons, so there can be NO d-d transition.
     Their colour is due to CHARGE TRANSFER - an electron momentarily
     moves from an oxygen atom to the metal.
```

```
  |                                                                  |
```

# 7. THE f BLOCK — LANTHANOIDS AND ACTINOIDS

## 7.1 Lanthanoid contraction

```
  DEFINITION
     The steady DECREASE in atomic and ionic radii with increasing
     atomic number from La (57) to Lu (71).

     La3+ = 103 pm   ...   Lu3+ = 86 pm

  CAUSE
     The extra electron enters the SAME inner 4f subshell. 4f orbitals
     have a diffuse, badly shaped charge cloud and SHIELD very POORLY.
     So the effective nuclear charge on the outer electrons keeps rising
     and pulls them inwards.

  CONSEQUENCES (learn all four)
     1. 4d and 5d series have nearly the same size - Zr/Hf, Nb/Ta, Mo/W
        are "twin pairs" with almost identical chemistry.
     2. Separation of the lanthanoids from each other is very difficult
        (their properties are almost identical).
     3. Basic strength of the hydroxides DECREASES:
        La(OH)3 = most basic  ->  Lu(OH)3 = least basic.
     4. Complex-forming tendency increases slightly from La3+ to Lu3+
        because charge density increases.
```

## 7.2 Some specific lanthanoid configurations

| Element | Z | Configuration | Note |
|---------|---|---------------|------|
| La | 57 | [Xe] 5d1 6s2 | no 4f electron |
| Ce | 58 | [Xe] 4f1 5d1 6s2 | Ce4+ is 4f0, a good oxidiser |
| Eu | 63 | [Xe] 4f7 6s2 | Eu2+ is 4f7, a good reducer |
| Gd | 64 | [Xe] 4f7 5d1 6s2 | half-filled 4f7 |
| Yb | 70 | [Xe] 4f14 6s2 | Yb2+ is 4f14 |
| Lu | 71 | [Xe] 4f14 5d1 6s2 | fully filled 4f14 |

```
  MISCH METAL
     about 95 % lanthanoid metal  +  about 5 % iron
     + traces of S , C , Ca , Al

     USES:  alloyed with magnesium for bullets, shells and
            LIGHTER FLINTS (it is pyrophoric - it sparks when scratched)
```

## 7.3 LANTHANOIDS vs ACTINOIDS — the comparison table

| # | LANTHANOIDS | ACTINOIDS |
|---|-------------|-----------|
| 1 | 4f orbitals are filled | 5f orbitals are filled |
| 2 | Ce (58) to Lu (71) | Th (90) to Lr (103) |
| 3 | [Xe] 4f1-14 5d0-1 6s2 | [Rn] 5f1-14 6d0-1 7s2 |
| 4 | Only promethium (Pm) is radioactive | **ALL are radioactive** |
| 5 | Main oxidation state is +3; +2 and +4 are rare | +3 is common, but +4, +5, +6 and +7 also occur commonly |
| 6 | Do not readily form oxo-cations | Form oxo-cations such as UO2 2+, PuO2 2+ |
| 7 | Less tendency to form complexes | Greater tendency to form complexes |
| 8 | Compounds are less basic | Compounds are more basic |
| 9 | Ions are mostly colourless or lightly coloured | Ions are mostly coloured |
| 10 | Lanthanoid contraction (4f shields poorly) | Actinoid contraction is GREATER (5f shields even more poorly) |
| 11 | Magnetic properties are easy to explain | Magnetic properties are difficult to explain |
| 12 | All occur naturally | Elements after uranium are man-made (transuranic) |
