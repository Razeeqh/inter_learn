# Chemistry in Everyday Life — DRUGS, CLASSES AND EXAMPLES FACT SHEET

**There is not one formula in this chapter. There is not one numerical.
There is ONE BIG TABLE.**

Say it plainly: **this is one of the cheapest sources of marks in the whole
Chemistry paper for a weak student, because every single question is direct
recall.** Nobody will ask you to derive anything, calculate anything or think
about anything. They will ask "what is an antacid, give two examples" — and
either you know the two names or you do not.

| # | CLASS OF DRUG | WHAT IT DOES (the definition in one line) | HOW IT WORKS | EXAMPLES YOU MUST NAME |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ANTACID** | Removes **excess acid** from the stomach and raises the pH to a safe level | Old: **neutralises** the acid. New: **blocks the H2 receptor** or the proton pump so acid is never released | `NaHCO3`, `Mg(OH)2`, `Al(OH)3`, **cimetidine**, **ranitidine**, **omeprazole**, lansoprazole |
| 2 | **ANTIHISTAMINE** (antiallergic) | Interferes with the natural action of **histamine**, so allergy symptoms stop | Competes with histamine for the **H1 receptor** binding site — an **antagonist** | **brompheniramine** (Dimetapp), **terfenadine** (Seldane) |
| 3 | **TRANQUILISER** | Treats **stress** and mild or severe **mental disease**; component of **sleeping pills** | Acts on the higher centres of the **central nervous system** | **chlordiazepoxide**, **meprobamate**, **equanil**, **valium** (diazepam), **serotonin**, **barbiturates** (veronal, luminal, seconal) |
| 3a | **ANTIDEPRESSANT** (a tranquiliser sub-class) | Lifts a low mood caused by too little **noradrenaline** | **Inhibits the enzyme** that degrades noradrenaline, so its level rises | **iproniazid**, **phenelzine** |
| 4 | **ANALGESIC — NON-NARCOTIC** | Reduces pain **without** loss of consciousness; **non-addictive** | Blocks the chemicals that signal pain and inflammation | **aspirin** (acetylsalicylic acid), **paracetamol**, ibuprofen, analgin |
| 5 | **ANALGESIC — NARCOTIC** (opiate) | Relieves severe pain and produces sleep; **habit forming / addictive** | Binds to opioid receptors in the brain | **morphine**, **codeine**, **heroin** (all from the **opium poppy**) |
| 6 | **ANTIBIOTIC** | Produced wholly or partly by chemical synthesis; in **low concentration** kills or inhibits microorganisms | Interferes with the **metabolic processes** of the microbe | **penicillin**, **ampicillin**, **amoxicillin**, **chloramphenicol**, **streptomycin**, **tetracycline**, **ofloxacin**, erythromycin, vancomycin |
| 7 | **ANTISEPTIC** | Kills or prevents the growth of microorganisms; applied to **LIVING tissue** (wounds, cuts, skin) | Kills microbes without damaging living tissue | **dettol** (chloroxylenol + terpineol), **bithionol** (in soaps), **tincture of iodine** (2-3 % I2 in alcohol-water), **iodoform**, dilute boric acid, **0.2 % phenol**, soframycin, furacin |
| 8 | **DISINFECTANT** | Kills microorganisms; applied to **NON-LIVING objects** (floors, drains, instruments) | Same chemicals, but stronger — too harsh for skin | **1 % phenol**, **chlorine 0.2-0.4 ppm** in drinking water, **SO2** in very low concentration |
| 9 | **ANTIFERTILITY DRUG** | Controls the **birth rate**; the active chemical of birth control pills | Mixture of synthetic **estrogen** and **progesterone** derivatives; suppresses ovulation | **norethindrone** (progesterone derivative), **ethynylestradiol / novestrol** (estrogen derivative) |
| 10 | **SULPHA DRUG** (sulphonamide) | Antibacterial drug; the classic example of classification by **chemical structure** | All contain the group `-SO2-NH-` | **sulphanilamide**, **sulphapyridine**, **sulphadiazine**, prontosil |
| 11 | **ARTIFICIAL SWEETENER** | Tastes sweet but gives **no calories**; for **diabetics** and calorie control | Not metabolised by the body — excreted unchanged | **saccharin**, **aspartame**, **alitame**, **sucralose** |
| 12 | **FOOD PRESERVATIVE** | Prevents the **spoilage of food** due to microbial growth | Toxic to microbes / draws water out of them | **sodium benzoate** `C6H5COONa`, **salts of sorbic acid**, **salts of propanoic acid**; traditional: **sugar, salt, vegetable oil** |
| 13 | **ANTIOXIDANT** | **Retards the oxidation** of food (stops fats going rancid) | More reactive towards oxygen than the food is, so it is oxidised first | **BHA** (butylated hydroxyanisole), **BHT** (butylated hydroxytoluene), SO2, sulphite salts, vitamin E |
| 14 | **SOAP** | Cleansing agent — sodium or potassium salt of a **long chain fatty acid** | Forms a **micelle**: oil-loving tails inwards, water-loving heads outwards | sodium **stearate** `C17H35COONa`, sodium **palmitate** `C15H31COONa`, sodium **oleate** `C17H33COONa` |
| 15 | **SYNTHETIC DETERGENT** | Has all the properties of soap but contains **no soap**; works in **hard AND soft water** | Same micelle action, but its Ca / Mg salts are **soluble** | **sodium lauryl sulphate**, **sodium alkylbenzene sulphonate**, **cetyltrimethylammonium bromide**, **polyethyleneglycol stearate** |

# 4. THE FOUR BASES OF CLASSIFICATION OF DRUGS
# ==========================================================

| # | BASIS | GROUPED BY | EXAMPLE | WHEN TO USE IT IN AN ANSWER |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pharmacological effect** | the **effect** on the body | all **analgesics**; all **antiseptics** | when the question says "useful for a doctor treating a symptom" |
| 2 | **Drug action** | the **biochemical process** acted on | **antihistamines** (all inhibit histamine) | when the question names a *process* being blocked |
| 3 | **Chemical structure** | a **common structural feature** | **sulphonamides**, all containing `-SO2-NH-` | when the question shows structures or names a functional group |
| 4 | **Molecular targets** | the **biomolecule** attacked | drugs acting on **enzymes**; drugs acting on **receptors** | when the question says "most useful to a medicinal chemist" |

```
  MEMORY HOOK:   "P A S T"
        P - Pharmacological effect
        A - Action
        S - Structure
        T - Targets
```

## 5.1 The three words

|---|---|---|

```
  *** THE PHENOL POINT — memorise the two numbers ***

        0.2 %  phenol  ->  ANTISEPTIC
        1.0 %  phenol  ->  DISINFECTANT

        Same chemical. Only the CONCENTRATION decides the name.
```

## 5.2 Bactericidal versus bacteriostatic

| | MEANING | EXAMPLES |
|---|---|---|
| **BACTERICIDAL** | **KILLS** the bacteria | **penicillin**, **ofloxacin**, aminoglycosides |
| **BACTERIOSTATIC** | only **INHIBITS THE GROWTH** | **tetracycline**, **chloramphenicol**, **erythromycin** |

```
  MEMORY:  "-CIDAL"  = kill   (like pesti-CIDE, sui-CIDE)
           "-STATIC" = stand still (stationary, it just stops growing)
```

## 5.3 Spectrum

| SPECTRUM | MEANING | EXAMPLES |
|---|---|---|
| **BROAD** | effective against **several different types** of bacteria (Gram +ve and Gram −ve) | **chloramphenicol**, **ampicillin**, **amoxicillin**, **tetracycline**, **ofloxacin**, vancomycin |
| **NARROW** | effective against **one group only** (Gram +ve *or* Gram −ve) | **penicillin G**, **streptomycin** |
| **LIMITED** | effective against a **single organism or disease** | **dysidazirine** |

# ==========================================================

| GROUP | DEFINITION | EXAMPLES | NOTE |
|---|---|---|---|

## 8.1 SAPONIFICATION (making soap)

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |      CH2 - OOC - R                            CH2 - OH            |
  |       |                                        |                  |
  |      CH  - OOC - R   +  3 NaOH  --(heat)-->   CH  - OH  + 3 RCOONa|
  |       |                                        |                  |
  |      CH2 - OOC - R                            CH2 - OH            |
  |                                                                   |
  |      fat or oil          sodium               GLYCEROL     SOAP   |
  |     (glyceryl ester)     hydroxide          (by-product)          |
  |                                                                   |
  |   IN WORDS:   FAT / OIL  +  NaOH  --(heat)-->  SOAP + GLYCEROL    |
  |                                                                   |
  |   The soap is precipitated by adding NaCl = "SALTING OUT".        |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  DO NOT FORGET:  the "3" in front of NaOH, and the word GLYCEROL.
```

## 8.2 SOAP IN HARD WATER (why it fails)

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  2 C17H35COO-Na+  +  Ca2+  ---->  (C17H35COO)2Ca  +  2 Na+        |
  |     sodium stearate                calcium stearate               |
  |     SOLUBLE  (soap)                INSOLUBLE  (SCUM)              |
  |                                                                   |
  |  2 C17H35COO-Na+  +  Mg2+  ---->  (C17H35COO)2Mg  +  2 Na+        |
  |                                    magnesium stearate, INSOLUBLE  |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  THE THREE CONSEQUENCES (three separate marks):
     1. soap is WASTED
     2. NO LATHER until all Ca2+ / Mg2+ is used up
     3. the insoluble SCUM STICKS TO THE CLOTH

  IN ACIDIC WATER soap also fails:
       C17H35COO-Na+  +  H+  ---->  C17H35COOH  +  Na+
                                     insoluble fatty acid
```

## 8.3 THE MICELLE — draw this, it is a whole 4-mark answer

```
      ONE SOAP MOLECULE                CH3-(CH2)16-COO- Na+
                                       <------------>  <----->
                                          TAIL          HEAD
                                       hydrophobic    hydrophilic
                                       (oil loving)   (water loving)

      drawn as:      ~~~~~~~~~~~~~(O)


      THE MICELLE

                          O   O   O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                     O  \ |   |   | /  O
                        \ \   |   / /
                   O ----\ \  |  / /---- O
                          \ \ | / /
                    O -----\\\|///----- O
                       ,----[ O I L ]----,     <-- greasy dirt trapped
                    O -----///|\\\----- O
                          / / | \ \
                   O ----/ /  |  \ \---- O
                        / /   |   \ \
                     O  / |   |   | \  O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                          O   O   O

          O  = the IONIC HEAD, pointing OUT into the water
          |  = the HYDROCARBON TAIL, pointing IN into the oil

      The outside of the micelle is NEGATIVELY CHARGED, so micelles
      REPEL each other and stay dispersed. Rinsing carries them away.
```

> **The single commonest drawing error:** heads pointing inwards.
> **HEADS OUT (they love water), TAILS IN (they love oil).**

## 8.4 THE THREE DETERGENT TYPES

| TYPE | THE ACTIVE PART | STRUCTURE / EXAMPLE | USE |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ANIONIC** | the **anion** (negative) | **sodium lauryl sulphate** `CH3(CH2)10CH2-O-SO3- Na+`; **sodium dodecylbenzene sulphonate** `CH3(CH2)11-C6H4-SO3- Na+` | household washing of clothes and dishes; **TOOTHPASTE** |
| **CATIONIC** | the **cation** (positive) — a **quaternary ammonium salt** | **cetyltrimethylammonium bromide** `CH3(CH2)15-N+(CH3)3 Br-` | **hair conditioners**; **germicidal**; **EXPENSIVE, so limited use** |
| **NON-IONIC** | **no ions at all** | **polyethyleneglycol stearate** `CH3(CH2)16CH2-O-(CH2CH2O)n-CH2CH2OH`; **glyceryl stearate** | **liquid dishwashing detergents** |

```
  MAKING SODIUM LAURYL SULPHATE - the two steps

     CH3-(CH2)10-CH2-OH  --(conc. H2SO4)-->  CH3-(CH2)10-CH2-O-SO3H
     lauryl alcohol                          lauryl hydrogensulphate

     CH3-(CH2)10-CH2-O-SO3H  +  NaOH  -->  CH3-(CH2)10-CH2-O-SO3- Na+
                                            SODIUM LAURYL SULPHATE
```

## 8.5 BIODEGRADABILITY

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |   STRAIGHT (unbranched) hydrocarbon chain                         |
  |        ->  bacteria degrade it easily                             |
  |        ->  BIODEGRADABLE  ->  no pollution                        |
  |                                                                   |
  |   HIGHLY BRANCHED hydrocarbon chain                               |
  |        ->  bacteria CANNOT degrade it                             |
  |        ->  NON-BIODEGRADABLE  ->  foams and pollutes rivers       |
  |                                                                   |
  |   REMEDY:  manufacture detergents with STRAIGHT chains.           |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  BIODEGRADABLE example
      CH3-(CH2)11-C6H4-SO3Na
      sodium 4-(1-dodecyl)benzenesulphonate      -- straight chain

  NON-BIODEGRADABLE example
      sodium 4-(1,3,5,7-tetramethyloctyl)benzenesulphonate
                                                 -- four methyl branches
```

## 8.6 SOAP versus DETERGENT

| | SOAP | SYNTHETIC DETERGENT |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Na/K salt of a **long chain fatty acid** | Na salt of a long chain **sulphonic acid / sulphate**, or a quaternary ammonium salt |
| Made from | animal fats and vegetable oils (a **food** source) | hydrocarbons from **petroleum** |
| Hard water | **fails** — forms scum | **works** — Ca/Mg salts are soluble |
| Acidic water | **fails** | **works** |
| Biodegradability | always biodegradable | only if the chain is **straight** |
| Cleansing power | weaker | stronger |

## 8.7 TYPES OF SOAP

| TYPE | THE ONE FACT |
|---|---|
| **Toilet soap** | better grades of fats and oils, **excess alkali removed**, colour and perfume added |
| **Floating soap** | **air bubbles beaten in** before hardening |
| **Transparent soap** | soap **dissolved in ethanol**, then the solvent evaporated |
| **Medicated soap** | contains substances of **medicinal value**, e.g. the antiseptic **bithionol** |
| **Shaving soap** | contains **glycerol to prevent rapid drying**; **rosin** added, forming sodium rosinate, which **lathers well** |
| **Laundry soap** | contains **fillers** — sodium rosinate, sodium silicate, borax, sodium carbonate |
| **Soap chips** | a thin sheet of melted soap run onto a cool cylinder and the small broken pieces **scraped off** |
| **Soap granules** | **dried miniature soap bubbles** |
| **Soap powders / scouring soaps** | soap + a **scouring (abrasive) agent** (powdered pumice, fine sand) + **builders** (sodium carbonate, trisodium phosphate) |
