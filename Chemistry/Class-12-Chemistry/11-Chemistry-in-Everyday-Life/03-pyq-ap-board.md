## Topic: Drugs, classification, drug-target interaction

### Q A1
What are drugs? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DRUGS are chemicals of LOW MOLECULAR MASS (about 100 - 500 u) which
  interact with macromolecular targets in the body and produce a
  biological response.

  When that response is therapeutic and non-toxic, the drug is used as
  a MEDICINE.

  Example:  aspirin  (an analgesic).
```

---

### Q A2
What is chemotherapy?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CHEMOTHERAPY is the use of CHEMICALS for the treatment of disease.

  Example: the use of antibiotics such as penicillin to treat a
           bacterial infection.
```

---

### Q A3
Define the therapeutic index of a drug. What does a high value mean?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The THERAPEUTIC INDEX is the ratio of the TOXIC dose of a drug to
  its THERAPEUTIC (effective) dose.

                              toxic dose
       therapeutic index  =  --------------
                            therapeutic dose

  A HIGH value means a LARGE SAFETY MARGIN, i.e. the drug is SAFE.
  A LOW value means the toxic dose is close to the useful dose, so the
  drug is DANGEROUS.
```

---

### Q A4
On what four bases are drugs classified?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. On the basis of PHARMACOLOGICAL EFFECT
  2. On the basis of DRUG ACTION
  3. On the basis of CHEMICAL STRUCTURE
  4. On the basis of MOLECULAR TARGETS

  Memory hook:  P A S T
```

---

### Q A5
Which classification of drugs is most useful to a doctor, and which to a
medicinal chemist? Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MOST USEFUL TO A DOCTOR  :  classification on PHARMACOLOGICAL EFFECT
     because it puts every drug that produces the same effect (e.g. all
     pain killers) into one group, which is what a doctor needs when
     treating a symptom.

  MOST USEFUL TO A MEDICINAL CHEMIST : classification on MOLECULAR
     TARGETS, because it groups drugs by the biomolecule they act on,
     which is what is needed when designing a new drug.
```

---

### Q A6
What is an enzyme inhibitor? Name its two types.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An ENZYME INHIBITOR is a drug that BLOCKS the action of an enzyme, so
  the enzyme can no longer catalyse its reaction.

  TWO TYPES:
     1. COMPETITIVE INHIBITOR - binds at the ACTIVE SITE and competes
        with the substrate.
     2. ALLOSTERIC (NON-COMPETITIVE) INHIBITOR - binds at a different
        site called the ALLOSTERIC SITE and changes the SHAPE of the
        active site.
```

---

### Q A7
Define an agonist and an antagonist.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AGONIST      - a drug that MIMICS the natural chemical messenger,
                 binds to the receptor and produces the SAME response
                 as the messenger. Used when the natural messenger is
                 deficient.

  ANTAGONIST   - a drug that binds to the receptor and BLOCKS it, so
                 the natural messenger cannot bind and the message is
                 stopped.
                 Example: cimetidine, ranitidine (H2 antagonists).
```

## Topic: Antacids and antihistamines

### Q A8
What are antacids? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANTACIDS are chemicals which remove the EXCESS ACID from the stomach
  and raise the pH to an appropriate level.

  Examples:  sodium hydrogencarbonate (NaHCO3), magnesium hydroxide,
             aluminium hydroxide,
             and the modern ones - cimetidine and ranitidine.
```

---

### Q A9
Why is the use of sodium hydrogencarbonate as an antacid not recommended?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because if it is taken in EXCESS it makes the stomach ALKALINE.
  The stomach responds by producing EVEN MORE ACID than before, so the
  acidity returns worse than at the start.

  Metal hydroxides such as Mg(OH)2 and Al(OH)3 are better, because they
  are only weakly basic and cannot make the stomach alkaline.
```

---

### Q A10
Name two drugs which act as histamine H2 receptor blockers. What do they do?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CIMETIDINE  (Tagamet)  and  RANITIDINE  (Zantac).

  They BLOCK the histamine H2 receptor in the stomach wall, so
  histamine cannot trigger the release of hydrochloric acid.
  Unlike ordinary antacids they PREVENT the acid from being formed
  instead of merely neutralising it.
```

---

### Q A11
Which bacterium is responsible for hyperacidity and ulcers, and how is it
treated?

**ANSWER:**
```
  HELICOBACTER PYLORI.

  It is treated with ANTIBIOTICS (usually along with an antacid),
  because unless the bacterium itself is killed the ulcer keeps
  returning.
```

---

### Q A12
What are antihistamines? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANTIHISTAMINES (antiallergic drugs) are drugs which INTERFERE WITH
  THE NATURAL ACTION OF HISTAMINE by competing with histamine for the
  binding sites of the H1 RECEPTOR.

  Examples:  BROMPHENIRAMINE (Dimetapp),  TERFENADINE (Seldane).
```

---

### Q A13
Why do antihistamines like brompheniramine not affect the release of acid in
the stomach? *(asked almost every year)*

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because histamine acts through MORE THAN ONE KIND OF RECEPTOR:

       H1 receptor  ->  allergy symptoms
       H2 receptor  ->  secretion of stomach acid

  The two receptors have DIFFERENT SHAPES at their binding sites.
  Brompheniramine and terfenadine are designed to fit only the
  H1 receptor, so they CANNOT bind to the H2 receptor and therefore
  cannot stop the release of acid.
```

## Topic: Neurologically active drugs

### Q A14
What are tranquilisers? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**

  STRESS and of mild or severe MENTAL DISEASES. They act on the higher

### Q A15
Name two barbiturates. What are they used for?

**ANSWER:**
```
  VERONAL  and  LUMINAL   (also SECONAL).

  They are derivatives of BARBITURIC ACID and act as HYPNOTICS, that
  is, SLEEP PRODUCING agents. They are used as tranquilisers and in
  sleeping pills.
```

---

### Q A16
What are analgesics? Name their two types.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANALGESICS are drugs which REDUCE OR ABOLISH PAIN without causing
  impairment of consciousness, mental confusion, incoordination or
  paralysis.

  TWO TYPES:
     1. NON-NARCOTIC (non-addictive)  - aspirin, paracetamol
     2. NARCOTIC (addictive, opiates) - morphine, codeine, heroin
```

### Q A17
Give two uses of aspirin other than as a pain killer.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. ANTIPYRETIC - it brings down fever.
  2. ANTIBLOOD-CLOTTING agent - it prevents the blood from clotting,
     so it is given in low doses to PREVENT HEART ATTACKS.

  (It is also ANTI-INFLAMMATORY, used in arthritis.)
```

---

### Q A18
Why are narcotic analgesics given only under strict medical supervision?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because they are HABIT FORMING and cause ADDICTION.

  In doses higher than the medicinal dose they produce STUPOR, COMA
  and CONVULSIONS, and can cause DEATH.

  In medicinal doses they are used for post-operative pain, cardiac
  pain, the pains of terminal cancer and child birth.
```

## Topic: Antimicrobials

### Q A19
What are antibiotics? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANTIBIOTICS are substances produced wholly or partly by CHEMICAL
  SYNTHESIS which, in LOW CONCENTRATIONS, inhibit the growth of or
  destroy microorganisms by intervening in their metabolic processes.

  Examples:  penicillin, chloramphenicol
             (also streptomycin, tetracycline, ampicillin, ofloxacin)
```

---

### Q A20
Distinguish between bactericidal and bacteriostatic antibiotics, with one
example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BACTERICIDAL    - KILLS the bacteria.
                    Example: PENICILLIN (also ofloxacin,
                             aminoglycosides)

  BACTERIOSTATIC  - only INHIBITS THE GROWTH of the bacteria; it does
                    not kill them.
                    Example: TETRACYCLINE (also chloramphenicol,
                             erythromycin)
```

---

### Q A21
What are broad spectrum antibiotics? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BROAD SPECTRUM antibiotics are those which are effective against
  SEVERAL DIFFERENT TYPES of harmful bacteria - both Gram positive and
  Gram negative.

  Examples:  chloramphenicol, ampicillin
             (also amoxicillin, tetracycline, ofloxacin, vancomycin)
```

---

### Q A22
Give an example of a narrow spectrum and a limited spectrum antibiotic.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NARROW SPECTRUM   -  penicillin G  (also streptomycin)
                       effective against one group only, Gram positive
                       OR Gram negative.

  LIMITED SPECTRUM  -  dysidazirine
                       effective against a single organism or disease.
```

### Q A23
Name the antibiotic used for (a) typhoid and (b) tuberculosis.

**ANSWER:**
```
  (a) TYPHOID       ->  CHLORAMPHENICOL
                        (broad spectrum, orally effective; also used
                         for dysentery, acute fever, urinary
                         infections, meningitis and pneumonia)

  (b) TUBERCULOSIS  ->  STREPTOMYCIN   (narrow spectrum)
```

### Q A24
Distinguish between an antiseptic and a disinfectant, with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANTISEPTIC    - kills or prevents the growth of microorganisms and is
                  applied to LIVING TISSUE - wounds, cuts, ulcers,
                  diseased skin.
                  Example: DETTOL (chloroxylenol + terpineol),
                           tincture of iodine.

  DISINFECTANT  - kills microorganisms but is applied to INANIMATE
                  (non-living) OBJECTS - floors, drains, instruments.
                  Example: 1 % phenol, chlorine at 0.2-0.4 ppm.
```

---

### Q A25
How can the same substance act both as an antiseptic and as a disinfectant?

**ANSWER:**
```
  By changing its CONCENTRATION.

       0.2 % solution of PHENOL   ->  ANTISEPTIC
       1.0 % solution of PHENOL   ->  DISINFECTANT

  The chemical is the same; only the concentration decides whether it
  is mild enough for living tissue or strong enough only for objects.
```

  TINCTURE OF IODINE is a 2 - 3 % solution of IODINE in a mixture of

## Topic: Cleansing agents

### Q A33
What is saponification? Write the equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SAPONIFICATION is the hydrolysis of a FAT or OIL with aqueous SODIUM
  HYDROXIDE to give SOAP and GLYCEROL.

      CH2-OOC-R                         CH2-OH
       |                                 |
      CH -OOC-R  +  3 NaOH  --(heat)-->  CH -OH  +  3 RCOONa
       |                                 |
      CH2-OOC-R                         CH2-OH

      fat / oil                        GLYCEROL      SOAP
```

---

### Q A34
Why does soap not work well in hard water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Hard water contains Ca2+ and Mg2+ ions. These react with soap to
  form INSOLUBLE calcium and magnesium salts (SCUM):

     2 C17H35COONa  +  Ca2+  --->  (C17H35COO)2Ca  +  2 Na+
                                    INSOLUBLE  (scum)

  Result: the soap is WASTED, NO LATHER forms until all the Ca2+ and
  Mg2+ is used up, and the sticky scum CLINGS TO THE CLOTH.
```

---

### Q A35
What is a micelle?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A MICELLE is the spherical aggregate formed by soap molecules around
  a droplet of oil or grease.

  The HYDROCARBON TAILS (water-hating) point INWARDS into the oil, and
  the IONIC HEADS (water-loving) point OUTWARDS into the water.

  Because the surface of the micelle is negatively charged, micelles
  repel one another and remain dispersed, so the dirt can be rinsed
  away.
```

---

### Q A36
What are synthetic detergents? Name their three types.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SYNTHETIC DETERGENTS are cleansing agents which have ALL THE
  PROPERTIES OF SOAPS but CONTAIN NO SOAP. They can be used in both
  soft and hard water.

  THREE TYPES:
     1. ANIONIC detergents    - e.g. sodium lauryl sulphate
     2. CATIONIC detergents   - e.g. cetyltrimethylammonium bromide
     3. NON-IONIC detergents  - e.g. polyethyleneglycol stearate
```

---

### Q A37
Why do detergents lather in hard water while soap does not?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because the CALCIUM and MAGNESIUM salts of detergents are SOLUBLE in
  water, whereas the calcium and magnesium salts of soap are INSOLUBLE
  and form scum.

  So a detergent remains in solution in hard water and continues to
  lather and clean.
```

---

### Q A38
Which type of detergent is used in toothpaste, and which in hair conditioners?

**ANSWER:**
```
  TOOTHPASTE          ->  ANIONIC detergent,
                          SODIUM LAURYL SULPHATE

  HAIR CONDITIONERS   ->  CATIONIC detergent,
                          CETYLTRIMETHYLAMMONIUM BROMIDE
                          (it also has germicidal properties, but it
                           is expensive so its use is limited)
```

---

### Q A39
What are biodegradable detergents? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BIODEGRADABLE detergents are those which can be BROKEN DOWN BY
  BACTERIA, so they do not accumulate and pollute water. Detergents
  with STRAIGHT (unbranched) hydrocarbon chains are biodegradable.

  Example: sodium 4-(1-dodecyl)benzenesulphonate,
           CH3-(CH2)11-C6H4-SO3Na

  Detergents with HIGHLY BRANCHED chains are NON-biodegradable and
  cause water pollution.
```

Why is glycerol added to shaving soap?

  GLYCEROL is added to shaving soap to PREVENT RAPID DRYING of the

### Q B1
How are drugs classified? Explain each basis with one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Drugs are classified on FOUR bases.

  1. ON PHARMACOLOGICAL EFFECT
     Drugs producing the SAME KIND OF EFFECT are grouped together,
     whatever their structure.
     Example: ANALGESICS - all drugs that relieve pain.
     Most useful to a DOCTOR.

  2. ON DRUG ACTION
     Drugs acting on the SAME BIOCHEMICAL PROCESS are grouped
     together.
     Example: ANTIHISTAMINES - all inhibit the action of histamine.

  3. ON CHEMICAL STRUCTURE
     Drugs sharing a COMMON STRUCTURAL FEATURE are grouped together.
     They often have similar pharmacological activity.
     Example: SULPHONAMIDES (sulpha drugs), all containing -SO2-NH-.

  4. ON MOLECULAR TARGETS
     Drugs are grouped by the BIOMOLECULE they act on - enzymes,
     receptors, nucleic acids, lipids, carbohydrates.
     Example: drugs that act on ENZYMES form one group.
     Most useful to a MEDICINAL CHEMIST.

  Memory hook: P A S T
```

---

### Q B2
Explain how enzymes act as drug targets. How do drugs inhibit enzymes?

**ANSWER:**
```
  HOW AN ENZYME WORKS  (2 marks)

  1. It HOLDS the substrate in a pocket on its surface called the
     ACTIVE SITE. The substrate fits like a hand into a glove and is
     held there by ionic bonding, hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole
     interaction and van der Waals forces.

  2. It PROVIDES THE FUNCTIONAL GROUPS that attack the substrate and
     the right acidic / basic environment, so the reaction goes much
     faster.

  HOW A DRUG INHIBITS AN ENZYME  (2 marks)

  A drug that blocks an enzyme is called an ENZYME INHIBITOR.
  It works in one of two ways:

  (a) COMPETITIVE INHIBITOR
      The drug RESEMBLES THE SUBSTRATE and occupies the ACTIVE SITE
      itself, so the substrate is locked out. Drug and substrate
      COMPETE for the same place.

           +----------------+          +----------------+
           |     ( S )      |   ->     |     [ D ]      |
           |   active site  |          |   active site  |
           +----------------+          +----------------+
              normal                     inhibited

  (b) ALLOSTERIC (NON-COMPETITIVE) INHIBITOR
      The drug binds at a DIFFERENT site called the ALLOSTERIC SITE.
      This CHANGES THE SHAPE of the active site, so the substrate can
      no longer recognise it.

  If the drug binds by WEAK bonds the inhibition is REVERSIBLE.
  If it binds by a STRONG COVALENT bond the inhibition is
  IRREVERSIBLE.
```

### Q B3
Explain how receptors act as drug targets. What are agonists and antagonists?

**ANSWER:**
```
  WHAT A RECEPTOR IS  (1 mark)
  Receptors are PROTEINS EMBEDDED IN THE CELL MEMBRANE. Most of the
  receptor lies inside the membrane, but the part that RECEIVES THE
  MESSAGE projects OUT of the cell.

  HOW THE MESSAGE IS PASSED  (1 mark)
  Nerve cells do not touch. A CHEMICAL MESSENGER (a neurotransmitter
  such as histamine, noradrenaline or serotonin) is released into the
  gap and binds to the receptor on the next cell. Binding CHANGES THE
  SHAPE of the receptor, and this change carries the message into the
  cell.

  AGONISTS  (1 mark)
  Drugs that MIMIC the natural messenger. They bind to the receptor and
  PRODUCE THE SAME RESPONSE as the messenger.
  Used when the body's own messenger is DEFICIENT.

  ANTAGONISTS  (1 mark)
  Drugs that BIND TO THE RECEPTOR AND BLOCK IT, so the natural
  messenger cannot bind and the message is stopped.
  Used when the body's message is too strong.
  Examples: cimetidine and ranitidine (H2 antagonists);
            brompheniramine and terfenadine (H1 antagonists).

  WHY DRUGS ARE SELECTIVE
  Different receptors have different SHAPES at their binding sites, so
  a drug fits only one type of receptor. That is why an H1 blocker
  cures an allergy but does not stop stomach acid.
```

### Q B4
Explain the treatment of acidity. How did the treatment develop from antacids
to receptor blockers?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE PROBLEM
  The stomach produces HCl to digest food. Excess acid causes
  hyperacidity, heartburn and ULCERS.

  STAGE 1 - NEUTRALISATION  (1 mark)
  SODIUM HYDROGENCARBONATE was used:
        NaHCO3 + HCl  --->  NaCl + H2O + CO2
  DRAWBACK: in excess it makes the stomach ALKALINE, which triggers
  the production of EVEN MORE acid.

  A mixture of ALUMINIUM HYDROXIDE and MAGNESIUM HYDROXIDE was used
  instead, because these are only weakly basic and cannot make the
  stomach alkaline.
  BUT all of these only remove acid that has ALREADY been released.
  Relief is temporary.

  STAGE 2 - H2 RECEPTOR BLOCKERS  (2 marks)
  It was found that HISTAMINE stimulates the secretion of acid in the
  stomach. So a drug was designed to BLOCK the HISTAMINE H2 RECEPTOR,
  preventing acid from ever being released.
        CIMETIDINE (Tagamet)  and  RANITIDINE (Zantac)
  This was a revolution in the treatment of acidity and ulcers.

  STAGE 3 - PROTON PUMP INHIBITORS  (0.5 mark)
        OMEPRAZOLE and LANSOPRAZOLE prevent the formation of acid
        altogether.

  THE BACTERIAL CAUSE  (0.5 mark)
  Hyperacidity and ulcers are often caused by the bacterium
  HELICOBACTER PYLORI, which must be killed with ANTIBIOTICS or the
  ulcer returns.
```

### Q B5
What are antihistamines? Explain their action, and say why they do not affect
the secretion of stomach acid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION  (1 mark)
  ANTIHISTAMINES (antiallergic drugs) interfere with the natural
  action of HISTAMINE by competing with histamine for the binding
  sites of the H1 RECEPTOR.

  WHAT HISTAMINE DOES  (1 mark)
  Histamine is released during an allergic reaction. It contracts the
  smooth muscles of the bronchi and gut, causes nasal congestion,
  running nose, watery eyes and skin rashes, and relaxes the muscles
  of the blood vessels.

  EXAMPLES  (1 mark)
  BROMPHENIRAMINE (Dimetapp) and TERFENADINE (Seldane).
  They are ANTAGONISTS at the H1 receptor.

  WHY THEY DO NOT AFFECT STOMACH ACID  (1 mark)
  Histamine also stimulates acid secretion, but it does so through a
  DIFFERENT receptor - the H2 receptor - which has a DIFFERENT SHAPE
  at its binding site.
  Antihistamines are designed to fit ONLY the H1 receptor, so they
  cannot bind to the H2 receptor and cannot stop the release of acid.
  (Equally, cimetidine and ranitidine fit only H2 and cannot cure an
   allergy.)
```

### Q B6
Write a note on tranquilisers.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  TRANQUILISERS are chemical compounds used for the treatment of
  STRESS and of mild or severe MENTAL DISEASES. They act on the higher
  centres of the CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM and are an essential component
  of SLEEPING PILLS.

  2. MILD TRANQUILISERS

  3. BENZODIAZEPINES
     VALIUM (diazepam) and SEROTONIN - relieve anxiety.

  4. BARBITURATES  (derivatives of barbituric acid)
     These are HYPNOTICS, i.e. SLEEP PRODUCING agents.
     Examples: VERONAL, LUMINAL, SECONAL
```

### Q B7
Classify analgesics. Discuss the uses and dangers of narcotic analgesics.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION  (1 mark)

### Q B10
Write a note on penicillin.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DISCOVERY
  Penicillin was discovered by ALEXANDER FLEMING in 1929. He noticed
  that a mould (Penicillium notatum) growing accidentally on a culture
  plate had killed the bacteria around it.
  It was later isolated and purified by CHAIN and FLOREY, and came into
  wide clinical use during the SECOND WORLD WAR, saving an enormous
  number of lives from infected wounds.
  Fleming, Chain and Florey shared the NOBEL PRIZE.

  PROPERTIES
  - It is NARROW SPECTRUM (penicillin G) - mainly effective against
    Gram positive bacteria.
  - It is BACTERICIDAL - it kills bacteria rather than merely
    inhibiting them.
  - It is highly effective, but SOME PATIENTS ARE ALLERGIC to it, so
    it must be given with care.

  SEMI-SYNTHETIC PENICILLINS
  By modifying the SIDE CHAIN of the penicillin molecule, chemists
  produced AMPICILLIN and AMOXICILLIN. Both are BROAD SPECTRUM
  antibiotics.
```

### Q B13
Explain the cleansing action of soap with a diagram.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1 - THE TWO ENDS OF A SOAP MOLECULE  (1 mark)

     SODIUM STEARATE       CH3-(CH2)16-COO- Na+
                           <------------->  <----->
                              TAIL            HEAD

     TAIL : long hydrocarbon chain; NON-POLAR; HYDROPHOBIC
            (water-hating) but oil-loving
     HEAD : the -COO- Na+ group; IONIC; HYDROPHILIC (water-loving)

  STEP 2 - WHY WATER ALONE FAILS  (0.5 mark)
     Dirt is held to cloth by OIL and GREASE, which are non-polar.
     Water is polar, so it cannot wet or dissolve grease.

  STEP 3 - THE MICELLE  (1.5 marks)
     When soap is added, the TAILS bury themselves in the oil and the
     HEADS stay in the water. The oil drop is completely enclosed in a
     ball called a MICELLE.

                          O   O   O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                     O  \ |   |   | /  O
                   O ----\ \  |  / /---- O
                    O -----\\\|///----- O
                       ,----[ O I L ]----,
                    O -----///|\\\----- O
                   O ----/ /  |  \ \---- O
                     O  / |   |   | \  O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                          O   O   O

              O = ionic HEAD, pointing OUT into the water
              |  = hydrocarbon TAIL, pointing IN into the oil

  STEP 4 - HOW THE DIRT LEAVES  (1 mark)
     The outside of the micelle is NEGATIVELY CHARGED, so micelles
     REPEL one another and cannot rejoin. They stay dispersed as an
     EMULSION. Agitation lifts them off the cloth and rinsing with
     water carries them away.

  DEFINITION  (0.5 mark)
  Synthetic detergents are cleansing agents which have ALL THE
  PROPERTIES OF SOAPS but CONTAIN NO SOAP. They can be used in both

### Q C1 *(revision-grade)*
Write an essay on drugs: their definition, classification on four bases, and
drug-target interaction with enzymes and receptors.

**ANSWER — mark split shown:**
```
  PART 1 - DEFINITIONS                                     (2 marks)

     DRUG - a chemical of low molecular mass (100-500 u) which
     interacts with a macromolecular target in the body and produces a
     biological response.
     MEDICINE - a drug whose response is therapeutic and non-toxic.
     CHEMOTHERAPY - the use of chemicals for the treatment of disease.
     THERAPEUTIC INDEX - the ratio of the toxic dose to the therapeutic
     dose; a HIGH value means a SAFE drug.

  PART 2 - CLASSIFICATION ON FOUR BASES                    (2 marks)

     (a) PHARMACOLOGICAL EFFECT - by the effect produced.
         Example: analgesics. Most useful to a DOCTOR.
     (b) DRUG ACTION - by the biochemical process acted on.
         Example: antihistamines all inhibit histamine.
     (c) CHEMICAL STRUCTURE - by a common structural feature.
         Example: sulphonamides, all containing -SO2-NH-.
     (d) MOLECULAR TARGETS - by the biomolecule attacked.
         Example: drugs acting on enzymes. Most useful to a MEDICINAL
         CHEMIST.

  PART 3 - ENZYMES AS DRUG TARGETS                         (2 marks)

     An enzyme (i) HOLDS the substrate in its ACTIVE SITE by ionic
     bonding, hydrogen bonding, dipole-dipole interaction and van der
     Waals forces, and (ii) PROVIDES THE FUNCTIONAL GROUPS that attack
     the substrate.

     A drug that blocks an enzyme is an ENZYME INHIBITOR:
        COMPETITIVE - resembles the substrate and occupies the ACTIVE
                      SITE, so the substrate is locked out.
        ALLOSTERIC (NON-COMPETITIVE) - binds at the ALLOSTERIC SITE and
                      CHANGES THE SHAPE of the active site.
     Weak bonds -> reversible inhibition.
     Covalent bond -> irreversible inhibition.

  PART 4 - RECEPTORS AS DRUG TARGETS                       (2 marks)

     Receptors are PROTEINS EMBEDDED IN THE CELL MEMBRANE whose binding
     site projects out of the cell. A CHEMICAL MESSENGER binds to it,
     changes its shape, and the message passes into the cell.

        AGONIST     - mimics the natural messenger and produces the
                      SAME response.
        ANTAGONIST  - binds and BLOCKS the receptor, stopping the
                      message. Examples: cimetidine, ranitidine (H2);
                      brompheniramine, terfenadine (H1).

     Different receptors have different binding-site shapes, which is
     why drugs are SELECTIVE.
```

### Q C3 *(revision-grade)*
Write an essay on cleansing agents: soaps, their preparation, cleansing action
and limitations; and synthetic detergents, their types and the problem of
biodegradability.

**ANSWER — mark split shown:**
```
  1. WHAT SOAP IS, AND SAPONIFICATION                       (2 marks)

     SOAPS are the sodium or potassium salts of long chain FATTY ACIDS
     such as stearic, oleic and palmitic acid.
     They are made by heating a fat or oil with aqueous NaOH -
     SAPONIFICATION:

        CH2-OOC-R                         CH2-OH
         |                                 |
        CH -OOC-R  +  3 NaOH  --(heat)-->  CH -OH  +  3 RCOONa
         |                                 |
        CH2-OOC-R                         CH2-OH
        fat / oil                        GLYCEROL      SOAP

     The soap is precipitated with NaCl ("salting out"); the glycerol
     is recovered as a by-product.

  2. CLEANSING ACTION - THE MICELLE                         (2 marks)

     A soap molecule has a NON-POLAR HYDROPHOBIC TAIL (oil-loving) and
     an IONIC HYDROPHILIC HEAD (water-loving).
     The tails bury themselves in the greasy dirt and the heads stay in
     the water, so the dirt is enclosed in a ball called a MICELLE.
     The micelle surface is negatively charged, so micelles repel one
     another, remain dispersed as an emulsion, and are rinsed away.

                          O   O   O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                   O ----\ \  |  / /---- O
                       ,----[ O I L ]----,
                   O ----/ /  |  \ \---- O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                          O   O   O
              O = ionic head (outside),  | = tail (inside)

  3. THE LIMITATION OF SOAP                                 (1 mark)

     In HARD WATER, Ca2+ and Mg2+ form INSOLUBLE salts:
        2 C17H35COONa + Ca2+ ---> (C17H35COO)2Ca + 2 Na+   (SCUM)
     Soap is wasted, no lather forms, and the scum sticks to the cloth.
     Soap also fails in ACIDIC water, being converted to the insoluble
     free fatty acid.

  4. SYNTHETIC DETERGENTS AND THEIR THREE TYPES             (2 marks)

     Detergents have all the properties of soap but contain no soap,
     and work in hard water because their Ca and Mg salts are SOLUBLE.
        ANIONIC   - sodium lauryl sulphate, sodium alkylbenzene
                    sulphonate; household washing and TOOTHPASTE
        CATIONIC  - cetyltrimethylammonium bromide, a quaternary
                    ammonium salt; hair conditioners; germicidal;
                    expensive
        NON-IONIC - polyethyleneglycol stearate, glyceryl stearate;
                    liquid dishwashing detergents

  5. BIODEGRADABILITY                                       (1 mark)

     STRAIGHT chain detergents are degraded by bacteria and are
     BIODEGRADABLE. HIGHLY BRANCHED chains cannot be degraded, so they
     survive sewage treatment, reach rivers and lakes, foam and cause
     POLLUTION. The remedy is to make detergents with straight,
     unbranched chains.
```
