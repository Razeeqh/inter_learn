# Chemistry in Everyday Life — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The drug named in the question changes.
The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

```
  There are only about 24 shapes in this whole chapter, and every one of
  them is "state the definition and give the examples". Once you can
  recognise the shape, the answer writes itself.
```

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — DEFINE DRUG / MEDICINE / CHEMOTHERAPY                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are drugs?"  "What is chemotherapy?"
            "Define a medicine."
  METHOD    1. Give the definition in one full sentence.
            2. ALWAYS add an example. The example carries half the mark.

            DRUG  - a chemical of LOW MOLECULAR MASS (100-500 u) which
                    interacts with a MACROMOLECULAR TARGET in the body
                    and produces a BIOLOGICAL RESPONSE.
                    Example: aspirin.
            MEDICINE - a drug whose biological response is THERAPEUTIC
                    and NON-TOXIC.
            CHEMOTHERAPY - the use of CHEMICALS for the TREATMENT OF
                    DISEASE. Example: penicillin for an infection.
  TRAP      Leaving out the molecular mass range, and leaving out the
            example. In a 2-mark question that is 1 mark thrown away.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — THERAPEUTIC INDEX                                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the therapeutic index?"  "Which drug is safer?"
            or two doses are given.
  METHOD    Definition: the RATIO of the TOXIC dose of a drug to its
                        THERAPEUTIC (effective) dose.

                                    toxic dose
            therapeutic index  =  ---------------
                                 therapeutic dose

            HIGH value -> LARGE safety margin -> SAFE drug
            LOW  value -> small safety margin -> DANGEROUS drug

            If numbers are given, just divide.
  TRAP      Writing that it measures "how well the drug works".
            It measures SAFETY, not strength. Also, put TOXIC on top,
            not underneath.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — CLASSIFY DRUGS ON FOUR BASES                        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How are drugs classified?"  "On what bases are drugs
            classified?"
  METHOD    FOUR classes, each with ONE example. One mark each.

            1. PHARMACOLOGICAL EFFECT - by the EFFECT on the body.
               Example: analgesics. Useful to a DOCTOR.
            2. DRUG ACTION - by the BIOCHEMICAL PROCESS acted on.
               Example: antihistamines (all inhibit histamine).
            3. CHEMICAL STRUCTURE - by a COMMON STRUCTURAL FEATURE.
               Example: sulphonamides, all containing -SO2-NH-.
            4. MOLECULAR TARGETS - by the BIOMOLECULE attacked.
               Example: drugs acting on enzymes / receptors.
               Useful to a MEDICINAL CHEMIST.

            Memory hook: P A S T
  TRAP      Naming the four but giving no examples. Also mixing up
            (2) and (4): "which PROCESS" = drug action;
            "which MOLECULE" = molecular target.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — ENZYMES AS DRUG TARGETS                             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How do enzymes act as drug targets?"
            "How do drugs inhibit enzymes?"
  METHOD    PART 1 - how an enzyme catalyses (2 marks)
              (a) it HOLDS the substrate in a pocket called the ACTIVE
                  SITE, by ionic bonding, hydrogen bonding,
                  dipole-dipole interaction and van der Waals forces
              (b) it PROVIDES THE FUNCTIONAL GROUPS that attack the
                  substrate and the right acidic/basic environment

            PART 2 - how a drug blocks it (2 marks)
              A drug that blocks an enzyme is an ENZYME INHIBITOR.
              COMPETITIVE  - resembles the substrate, occupies the
                             ACTIVE SITE, so the substrate is locked out
              ALLOSTERIC (NON-COMPETITIVE) - binds at the ALLOSTERIC
                             SITE and CHANGES THE SHAPE of the active
                             site
              Weak bonds -> REVERSIBLE.  Covalent bond -> IRREVERSIBLE.

            Draw the active-site box diagram. It earns marks.
  TRAP      Saying an allosteric inhibitor "blocks the active site".
            It does NOT go near the active site - it DISTORTS it from
            somewhere else.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — RECEPTORS AS DRUG TARGETS / AGONIST vs ANTAGONIST  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How do receptors act as drug targets?"
            "What are agonists and antagonists?"
  METHOD    1. RECEPTORS are PROTEINS EMBEDDED IN THE CELL MEMBRANE
               whose binding site projects OUT of the cell.
            2. Nerve cells do not touch; a CHEMICAL MESSENGER crosses
               the gap, binds to the receptor, CHANGES ITS SHAPE, and
               the message passes into the cell.
            3. AGONIST    - MIMICS the messenger, gives the SAME
                            response. Used when the messenger is
                            deficient.
               ANTAGONIST - BINDS AND BLOCKS the receptor so the
                            messenger cannot act.
                            Examples: cimetidine, ranitidine (H2);
                                      brompheniramine, terfenadine (H1)
            4. Drugs are SELECTIVE because different receptors have
               different BINDING SITE SHAPES.
  TRAP      Using the word "inhibitor" for a receptor.
            ENZYME -> inhibitor.  RECEPTOR -> antagonist.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — DEFINE ANTACID + EXAMPLES                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are antacids?"  "Give two examples of antacids."
  METHOD    ANTACIDS are chemicals which REMOVE THE EXCESS ACID from
            the stomach and RAISE THE pH to an appropriate level.
            Examples: NaHCO3, Mg(OH)2, Al(OH)3,
                      cimetidine, ranitidine, omeprazole.
  TRAP      Writing only NaHCO3. Give at least one MODERN example
            (cimetidine or ranitidine) - it shows you know the
            receptor-blocker idea.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — THE DRAWBACK OF NaHCO3 / THE ANTACID STORY         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is sodium hydrogencarbonate not recommended?"
            "Explain the treatment of acidity."
  METHOD    STAGE 1  NaHCO3 neutralises acid:
                       NaHCO3 + HCl -> NaCl + H2O + CO2
                     DRAWBACK: in EXCESS it makes the stomach ALKALINE,
                     which triggers the release of EVEN MORE ACID.
                     Metal hydroxides Mg(OH)2 / Al(OH)3 are better
                     because they are only WEAKLY basic.
                     But all of these only neutralise acid ALREADY
                     released - temporary relief.
            STAGE 2  HISTAMINE stimulates acid secretion, so block the
                     H2 RECEPTOR: CIMETIDINE and RANITIDINE.
                     They PREVENT the acid being released.
            STAGE 3  OMEPRAZOLE and LANSOPRAZOLE prevent the FORMATION
                     of acid.
            PLUS     HELICOBACTER PYLORI causes ulcers -> treat with
                     ANTIBIOTICS.
  TRAP      Forgetting the word "EXCESS". NaHCO3 is fine in a small
            dose; the problem is only when too much is taken.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — ANTIHISTAMINES + THE H1 / H2 QUESTION              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are antihistamines?"
            "Why do antihistamines not affect stomach acid?"
  METHOD    DEFINITION: drugs which INTERFERE WITH THE NATURAL ACTION
            OF HISTAMINE by competing with histamine for the binding
            sites of the H1 RECEPTOR.
            EXAMPLES: BROMPHENIRAMINE (Dimetapp),
                      TERFENADINE (Seldane).

            THE "WHY NOT ACID" ANSWER, three steps:
              1. Histamine acts through MORE THAN ONE receptor:
                    H1 -> allergy ;  H2 -> stomach acid
              2. The two receptors have DIFFERENT SHAPES at their
                 binding sites.
              3. Antihistamines are designed to fit ONLY H1, so they
                 cannot bind H2 and cannot stop acid release.
  TRAP      Answering "because they are different drugs". That is not
            an explanation. The mark is for the DIFFERENT RECEPTOR
            SHAPES.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — TRANQUILISERS                                      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are tranquilisers?"  "Name two tranquilisers."
            "Write a note on tranquilisers."
  METHOD    DEFINITION: chemical compounds used for the treatment of
            STRESS and mild or severe MENTAL DISEASES; they act on the
            higher centres of the CENTRAL NERVOUS SYSTEM and are an
            essential component of SLEEPING PILLS.

            THE GROUPS (for a 4-mark answer):
              ANTIDEPRESSANTS - IPRONIAZID, PHENELZINE. They INHIBIT
                 THE ENZYMES that degrade NORADRENALINE, whose low
                 level causes depression.
              MILD - CHLORDIAZEPOXIDE, MEPROBAMATE (relieve tension)
              EQUANIL - depression AND HYPERTENSION
              VALIUM, SEROTONIN - anxiety
              BARBITURATES - VERONAL, LUMINAL, SECONAL; HYPNOTIC
                 (sleep producing), derivatives of barbituric acid
  TRAP      Calling barbiturates analgesics. They are HYPNOTICS.
            And do not forget that noradrenaline is a NEUROTRANSMITTER,
            not a drug.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — ANALGESICS: DEFINE AND CLASSIFY                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are analgesics?"  "Classify analgesics."
            "Narcotic vs non-narcotic."
  METHOD    DEFINITION: drugs which REDUCE OR ABOLISH PAIN WITHOUT
            causing impairment of consciousness, mental confusion,
            incoordination or paralysis.

            NON-NARCOTIC (non-addictive): ASPIRIN, PARACETAMOL
               effective for skeletal pain such as ARTHRITIS
            NARCOTIC (addictive, opiates): MORPHINE, CODEINE, HEROIN
               all from the OPIUM POPPY
               USES: post-operative pain, cardiac pain, terminal
                     cancer, child birth
               DANGER: HABIT FORMING; in high doses stupor, coma,
                     convulsions, death
  TRAP      Writing "aspirin is a narcotic". It is NOT. And the
            definition must include the phrase "WITHOUT impairment of
            consciousness" - that is what separates an analgesic from
            an anaesthetic.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — THE EXTRA USES OF ASPIRIN                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give two uses of aspirin other than as an analgesic."
            "Why is aspirin given to heart patients?"
  METHOD    1. ANALGESIC - relieves pain
            2. ANTIPYRETIC - reduces fever
            3. ANTI-INFLAMMATORY - used in arthritis
            4. *** ANTIBLOOD-CLOTTING agent *** - it prevents the
               blood from clotting, so low doses are given to PREVENT
               HEART ATTACKS.
            Chemically it is ACETYLSALICYLIC ACID.
  TRAP      Only writing "pain and fever". The mark the examiner is
            hunting for is the ANTIBLOOD-CLOTTING / heart attack use.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — DEFINE ANTIBIOTIC + EXAMPLES                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are antibiotics?"  "Give two examples."
  METHOD    ANTIBIOTICS are substances produced WHOLLY OR PARTLY BY
            CHEMICAL SYNTHESIS which, in LOW CONCENTRATIONS, INHIBIT
            THE GROWTH OF or DESTROY microorganisms by INTERVENING IN
            THEIR METABOLIC PROCESSES.
            Examples: penicillin, chloramphenicol, streptomycin,
                      tetracycline, ampicillin, ofloxacin.
  TRAP      Leaving out "in LOW CONCENTRATIONS" and "by intervening in
            their metabolic processes". Those phrases are the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — BACTERICIDAL vs BACTERIOSTATIC                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between bactericidal and bacteriostatic."
            "Which of these kills bacteria?"
  METHOD    BACTERICIDAL   - KILLS the bacteria.
                             penicillin, ofloxacin, aminoglycosides
            BACTERIOSTATIC - only INHIBITS THE GROWTH; does not kill.
                             tetracycline, chloramphenicol,
                             erythromycin
            Memory: "-CIDAL" = kill (pesti-cide);
                    "-STATIC" = stationary, just stops growing.
  TRAP      Every year students put chloramphenicol in the wrong list.
            Learn the two blocks of three as blocks, not one by one.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — SPECTRUM OF ANTIBIOTICS                           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are broad spectrum antibiotics?"
            "Classify antibiotics on the basis of spectrum."
  METHOD    BROAD SPECTRUM  - effective against SEVERAL DIFFERENT types
                              of bacteria, both Gram positive and Gram
                              negative.
                              chloramphenicol, ampicillin, amoxicillin,
                              tetracycline, ofloxacin, vancomycin
            NARROW SPECTRUM - effective against ONE GROUP only
                              (Gram +ve OR Gram -ve).
                              penicillin G, streptomycin
            LIMITED SPECTRUM- effective against a SINGLE ORGANISM or
                              disease.
                              dysidazirine
  TRAP      Putting streptomycin in the broad list. It is NARROW.
            Only one limited-spectrum example exists in the syllabus,
            so "limited" always means DYSIDAZIRINE.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — NOTE ON PENICILLIN                                  4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write a note on penicillin."  "Who discovered penicillin?"
  METHOD    DISCOVERY - ALEXANDER FLEMING, 1929, from the mould
              Penicillium notatum growing accidentally on a culture
              plate. Purified by CHAIN and FLOREY; used widely in the
              SECOND WORLD WAR; all three shared the NOBEL PRIZE.
            PROPERTIES - NARROW spectrum (penicillin G);
              BACTERICIDAL; highly effective against Gram positive
              bacteria; SOME PATIENTS ARE ALLERGIC to it.
            SEMI-SYNTHETIC FORMS - by changing the SIDE CHAIN:
              AMPICILLIN and AMOXICILLIN, both BROAD spectrum.
  TRAP      Forgetting to say penicillin itself is NARROW spectrum
            while its semi-synthetic derivatives are BROAD.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — ANTISEPTIC vs DISINFECTANT                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between an antiseptic and a disinfectant."
            "Give an example of each."     *** MOST ASKED PATTERN ***
  METHOD    ANTISEPTIC   - kills or prevents the growth of
                           microorganisms; applied to LIVING TISSUE -
                           wounds, cuts, ulcers, diseased skin.
                           dettol (chloroxylenol + terpineol),
                           bithionol, tincture of iodine (2-3 % I2 in
                           alcohol-water), iodoform, dilute boric acid,
                           0.2 % phenol
            DISINFECTANT - kills microorganisms; applied to INANIMATE
                           (NON-LIVING) OBJECTS - floors, drains,
                           instruments.
                           1 % phenol, chlorine 0.2-0.4 ppm in drinking
                           water, SO2 in very low concentration
            THE EXTRA MARK: the SAME substance can be both -
                           0.2 % phenol = antiseptic,
                           1 % phenol = disinfectant.
  TRAP      Writing "antiseptic is weak and disinfectant is strong".
            That is not the definition. The definition is WHERE IT IS
            APPLIED - living vs non-living.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — ANTIFERTILITY DRUGS                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are antifertility drugs?"  "Name two."
  METHOD    Drugs used to CONTROL THE BIRTH RATE - the chemicals of
            BIRTH CONTROL PILLS. They are mixtures of synthetic
            ESTROGEN and PROGESTERONE derivatives which suppress
            ovulation.
            NORETHINDRONE     - progesterone derivative
            ETHYNYLESTRADIOL  - estrogen derivative (novestrol)
  TRAP      Spelling. If the examiner cannot recognise the name, you
            get nothing. Practise writing both words five times.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — ARTIFICIAL SWEETENERS                          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are artificial sweetening agents?"
            "Why is aspartame limited to cold foods?"
            "Arrange in order of sweetness."
  METHOD    DEFINITION: chemicals which TASTE SWEET but are NOT
            METABOLISED, so they give NO CALORIES; used by DIABETICS
            and for weight control.

            THE FOUR, WITH THE NUMBERS:
              ASPARTAME  100 x - methyl ester of a dipeptide;
                         UNSTABLE AT COOKING TEMPERATURE -> COLD FOODS
                         AND SOFT DRINKS ONLY
              SACCHARIN  550 x - the FIRST one; sold as its soluble
                         SODIUM SALT; inert, harmless, EXCRETED
                         UNCHANGED
              SUCRALOSE  600 x - TRICHLORO derivative of SUCROSE;
                         tastes and looks like sugar; STABLE in cooking
              ALITAME   2000 x - high potency; sweetness DIFFICULT TO
                         CONTROL

            ORDER: aspartame < saccharin < sucralose < alitame
  TRAP      Writing "aspartame is unstable" without saying AT COOKING
            TEMPERATURE and without the consequence (cold foods and
            soft drinks only).
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — FOOD PRESERVATIVES                                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are food preservatives?"  "Give two examples."
  METHOD    Chemicals which PREVENT THE SPOILAGE OF FOOD due to
            MICROBIAL GROWTH.
            Chemical:    SODIUM BENZOATE C6H5COONa (most common; used
                         in small concentrations; METABOLISED in the
                         body so it is safe),
                         SALTS OF SORBIC ACID,
                         SALTS OF PROPANOIC ACID
            Traditional: table SALT, SUGAR, VEGETABLE OILS
  TRAP      Giving BHA or BHT as the answer. Those are ANTIOXIDANTS.
            Preservative = stops MICROBES. Antioxidant = stops
            OXIDATION.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — ANTIOXIDANTS                                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are antioxidants?"  "Give two examples."
  METHOD    Compounds which RETARD THE OXIDATION of food. They are MORE
            REACTIVE TOWARDS OXYGEN than the food is, so they are
            oxidised first and protect the food.
            BHA - butylated hydroxyanisole
            BHT - butylated hydroxytoluene
            (also SO2, sulphite salts, vitamin E)
            EXTRA: BHA + BHT together are more effective than either
            alone; added to butter they raise storage life from months
            to YEARS.
  TRAP      Not giving the MECHANISM ("more reactive towards oxygen
            than the food"). That phrase is worth half the question.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — SAPONIFICATION                                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is saponification?"  "How is soap manufactured?"
  METHOD    SAPONIFICATION is the hydrolysis of a FAT or OIL with
            aqueous SODIUM HYDROXIDE to give SOAP and GLYCEROL.

               CH2-OOC-R                         CH2-OH
                |                                 |
               CH -OOC-R  +  3 NaOH  --(heat)-->  CH -OH  + 3 RCOONa
                |                                 |
               CH2-OOC-R                         CH2-OH
               fat / oil                        GLYCEROL     SOAP

            The soap is precipitated by adding NaCl - "SALTING OUT".
            SOAPS are the Na or K salts of LONG CHAIN FATTY ACIDS
            (stearic, oleic, palmitic).
  TRAP      Forgetting the "3" before NaOH (a fat has THREE ester
            links), and forgetting to name GLYCEROL as the by-product.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — CLEANSING ACTION OF SOAP (THE MICELLE)             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the cleansing action of soap."
            "What is a micelle?"  "Draw a micelle."
  METHOD    STEP 1  A soap molecule has TWO ENDS:
                      TAIL - long hydrocarbon chain, NON-POLAR,
                             HYDROPHOBIC (oil-loving)
                      HEAD - -COO- Na+, IONIC, HYDROPHILIC
                             (water-loving)
            STEP 2  Grease is non-polar and water is polar, so water
                    alone cannot remove it.
            STEP 3  The TAILS bury themselves in the oil; the HEADS
                    stay in the water. The oil drop is enclosed in a
                    ball = a MICELLE.
            STEP 4  The micelle surface is NEGATIVELY CHARGED, so
                    micelles REPEL one another and stay dispersed as an
                    EMULSION. Agitation lifts them off the cloth and
                    RINSING carries them away.

            ALWAYS DRAW THE MICELLE.
                          O   O   O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                   O ----\ \  |  / /---- O
                       ,----[ O I L ]----,
                   O ----/ /  |  \ \---- O
                       O  |   |   |  O
                          O   O   O
                O = ionic head OUTSIDE,  | = tail INSIDE
  TRAP        *** THE BIGGEST SINGLE ERROR IN THIS CHAPTER ***
              Drawing the heads INSIDE and the tails OUTSIDE.
              Ask "where is the water?" It is outside - so the
              WATER-LOVING heads must be outside.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — WHY SOAP FAILS IN HARD WATER                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does soap not lather in hard water?"
            "What is the disadvantage of soap?"
  METHOD    Hard water contains Ca2+ and Mg2+, which form INSOLUBLE
            salts with soap:

              2 C17H35COONa + Ca2+ ---> (C17H35COO)2Ca + 2 Na+
                                         INSOLUBLE = SCUM
              2 C17H35COONa + Mg2+ ---> (C17H35COO)2Mg + 2 Na+

            THREE CONSEQUENCES (three separate marks):
              1. the SOAP IS WASTED
              2. NO LATHER forms until all the Ca2+/Mg2+ is used up
              3. the sticky SCUM STICKS TO THE CLOTH

            Soap also fails in ACIDIC water:
              C17H35COONa + H+ -> C17H35COOH (insoluble) + Na+
  TRAP      Writing the answer in words only. WRITE THE EQUATION -
            that is where the mark is.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — THE THREE TYPES OF SYNTHETIC DETERGENT              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are synthetic detergents?"  "Give the three types."
            "Which detergent is used in toothpaste?"
  METHOD    DEFINITION: cleansing agents having ALL THE PROPERTIES OF
            SOAPS but CONTAINING NO SOAP; usable in BOTH soft and hard
            water.

            1. ANIONIC - the ANION does the cleaning.
               Sodium salts of sulphonated long chain alcohols or
               hydrocarbons.
                  SODIUM LAURYL SULPHATE
                     CH3(CH2)10CH2-O-SO3- Na+
                  SODIUM ALKYLBENZENE SULPHONATE (ABS)
                     CH3(CH2)11-C6H4-SO3- Na+
               USE: household washing; TOOTHPASTE
            2. CATIONIC - the CATION does the cleaning.
               QUATERNARY AMMONIUM SALTS.
                  CETYLTRIMETHYLAMMONIUM BROMIDE
                     CH3(CH2)15-N+(CH3)3 Br-
               USE: HAIR CONDITIONERS; GERMICIDAL; EXPENSIVE, so
                    limited use
            3. NON-IONIC - NO ions at all. An ether or an ester.
                  POLYETHYLENEGLYCOL STEARATE
                     CH3(CH2)16CH2-O-(CH2CH2O)n-CH2CH2OH
                  also GLYCERYL STEARATE
               USE: LIQUID DISHWASHING DETERGENTS
  TRAP      Giving only names and no uses. Every type needs
            NAME + EXAMPLE + USE. And do not forget that cationic
            detergents are EXPENSIVE - that is a stated mark point.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — BIODEGRADABILITY OF DETERGENTS                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are biodegradable detergents?"
            "Which of these two detergents pollutes water?"
            "Why do some detergents cause pollution?"
  METHOD    STRAIGHT (unbranched) hydrocarbon chain
                 -> bacteria degrade it easily
                 -> BIODEGRADABLE -> no pollution
                 e.g. CH3-(CH2)11-C6H4-SO3Na,
                      sodium 4-(1-dodecyl)benzenesulphonate

            HIGHLY BRANCHED hydrocarbon chain
                 -> bacteria CANNOT degrade it
                 -> NON-BIODEGRADABLE
                 -> survives sewage treatment, reaches rivers and
                    lakes, FOAMS and POLLUTES
                 e.g. sodium 4-(1,3,5,7-tetramethyloctyl)-
                      benzenesulphonate

            REMEDY: manufacture detergents with STRAIGHT chains.
  TRAP      Just writing "detergents pollute water". The mark is for
            the word BRANCHED. Look only at the SHAPE of the chain,
            never at the head group.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — SOAP versus DETERGENT (comparison)                  4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare soaps and synthetic detergents."
            "Why are detergents preferred to soaps?"
  METHOD    Write SIX rows in a table:
              1. Soap = Na/K salt of a long chain FATTY ACID;
                 detergent = Na salt of a long chain SULPHONIC ACID /
                 SULPHATE, or a quaternary ammonium salt
              2. Soap from ANIMAL FATS and VEGETABLE OILS (a food
                 source); detergent from PETROLEUM hydrocarbons
              3. Soap FAILS in hard water (scum);
                 detergent WORKS (soluble Ca/Mg salts)
              4. Soap FAILS in acidic water; detergent WORKS
              5. Soap is always BIODEGRADABLE; a detergent only if the
                 chain is STRAIGHT
              6. Soap has WEAKER cleansing action; detergent STRONGER
  TRAP      Only writing the hard water point. Six rows = four marks;
            one row = one mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — TYPES OF SOAP                                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Name four types of soap."  "Why is glycerol added to
            shaving soap?"  "How is transparent soap made?"
  METHOD    TOILET SOAP    - better grades of fats and oils, EXCESS
                             ALKALI REMOVED, colour and perfume added
            FLOATING SOAP  - AIR BUBBLES beaten in
            TRANSPARENT    - soap DISSOLVED IN ETHANOL, solvent
                             evaporated
            MEDICATED      - substances of MEDICINAL VALUE added,
                             e.g. the antiseptic BITHIONOL
            SHAVING SOAP   - GLYCEROL to PREVENT RAPID DRYING;
                             ROSIN added, forming sodium rosinate,
                             which LATHERS WELL
            LAUNDRY SOAP   - FILLERS: sodium rosinate, sodium
                             silicate, borax, sodium carbonate
            SOAP CHIPS     - melted soap on a cool cylinder, small
                             broken pieces SCRAPED OFF
            SOAP GRANULES  - DRIED MINIATURE SOAP BUBBLES
            SOAP POWDERS   - soap + SCOURING (abrasive) agent +
                             BUILDERS (sodium carbonate, trisodium
                             phosphate)
  TRAP      Swapping glycerol and rosin. GLYCEROL stops DRYING;
            ROSIN gives LATHER.
```
