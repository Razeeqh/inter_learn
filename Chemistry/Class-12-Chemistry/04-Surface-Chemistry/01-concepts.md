# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 1 — WHAT IS A "SURFACE" AND WHY IT MATTERS
# ===============================================================

Take a cube of iron, 1 cm on each side. Its surface area is 6 cm².
Now cut it into tiny cubes 1 nanometre across. You now have the *same amount* of
iron but a surface area of **about 6000 square metres** — bigger than a football field.

```
     ONE BIG CUBE                        MANY TINY CUBES
     +-----------+                       +-+-+-+-+-+-+
     |           |                       +-+-+-+-+-+-+
     |           |     cut it up          +-+-+-+-+-+-+
     |           |   ----------->        +-+-+-+-+-+-+
     |           |                       +-+-+-+-+-+-+
     +-----------+                       +-+-+-+-+-+-+

     surface = 6 cm2                    surface = thousands of m2
     SAME MASS OF METAL. HUGELY DIFFERENT SURFACE.
```

**Why does surface area matter so much?**

An atom sitting deep inside a solid is surrounded on all sides by neighbours. All its
bonding forces are satisfied. It is content.

An atom sitting **on the surface** has neighbours below and beside it, but **nothing
above it**. Its bonding forces on the upward side are *unsatisfied*. It is "hungry".

```
     INSIDE THE SOLID                   ON THE SURFACE
                                             ^  ^  ^
          o                             <-- UNSATISFIED FORCES -->
       o  X  o                                   |
          o                                      o
                                              o  X  o
     all forces balanced                         o
     -> nothing happens
                                       forces UNBALANCED upward
                                       -> it GRABS passing molecules
```

That hungry, unbalanced surface force is the reason for **everything** in this
chapter: adsorption, catalysis, and the strange behaviour of colloids.

> **TRAP:** Students write "surface area increases so adsorption increases" without
> saying *why*. The examiner wants the words **"unbalanced / residual / unsatisfied
> forces at the surface"**. That phrase is worth a mark on its own.

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 2 — ADSORPTION, ABSORPTION AND SORPTION
# ===============================================================

## 2.1 The three words

```
  ADSORPTION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  The accumulation of a substance at the SURFACE of another substance,
  so that its concentration on the surface is HIGHER than in the bulk.

  It is a SURFACE phenomenon.


  ABSORPTION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  The substance is taken up UNIFORMLY THROUGHOUT the whole bulk
  of the other substance.

  It is a BULK phenomenon.


  SORPTION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  When adsorption and absorption take place AT THE SAME TIME.
  Example: dyes on cotton, hydrogen gas on palladium.
```

## 2.2 See it in a picture

```
   ADSORPTION  (only the skin)          ABSORPTION  (all the way in)

    o o o o o o o o o o                   . . . . . . . . . .
   +-------------------+                 +-------------------+
   |                   |                 |  o     o      o   |
   |     S O L I D     |                 |     o     o       |
   |                   |                 |  o     o     o    |
   |   (still empty)   |                 |     o     o    o  |
   +-------------------+                 +-------------------+

   e.g. water vapour on              e.g. water in a sponge,
        silica gel                        chalk in water
```

## 2.3 The comparison table (learn this — it is a standing 2-mark question)

| Feature | ADSORPTION | ABSORPTION |
|---|---|---|
| Where it happens | Only at the **surface** | Throughout the **whole bulk** |
| Concentration | Different at surface and inside | **Uniform** everywhere |
| Type of phenomenon | Surface phenomenon | Bulk phenomenon |
| Speed | **Fast** at first, then slows to equilibrium | Happens at a **uniform, steady rate** |
| Heat change | **Exothermic** (heat is released) | Usually **endothermic** |
| Reversible? | Generally reversible (physisorption) | Not easily reversed |
| Everyday example | Water vapour on **silica gel**; a gas on charcoal | Water in a **sponge**; water in anhydrous CaCl2 |

## 2.4 Everyday examples you can quote

```
  ADSORPTION                            ABSORPTION
  -------------------------------       ------------------------------
  Silica gel packet in a shoe box       Sponge soaking up water
  Charcoal removing colour from         Chalk stick placed in ink
    raw sugar solution                    (ink goes right through it)
  Gas mask charcoal holding poison      Water vapour taken up by
    gases                                 anhydrous calcium chloride
  Ammonia gas on charcoal               Cotton absorbing sweat
```

> **THE CLASSIC EXAM TRICK — the chalk stick.**
> Dip a chalk stick in ink. The **surface goes dark blue** (the *dye* is ADSORBED),
> but when you break the chalk, the **inside is white and wet** (the *water* was
> ABSORBED). One object, both phenomena — this is the standard illustration.

## 2.5 Adsorbate and adsorbent

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |   ADSORBATE   =  the substance that GETS adsorbed                 |
  |                  (the molecule that sticks)                       |
  |                                                                   |
  |   ADSORBENT   =  the surface ON WHICH adsorption happens          |
  |                  (the thing doing the holding)                    |
  |                                                                   |
  |   Example:   H2 gas on charcoal                                   |
  |              ADSORBATE = H2         ADSORBENT = charcoal          |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  MEMORY:  adsorBATE = the BAIT that gets caught.
           adsorBENT = the surface that BENDS to hold it.
```

**Common adsorbents:** activated charcoal, silica gel, alumina gel (Al2O3),
clay, colloidal Fe(OH)3, finely divided metals (Ni, Pt, Pd), metal oxides.

## 2.6 Desorption

**Desorption** = the reverse of adsorption; removing the adsorbed substance from
the surface (usually by heating or lowering the pressure).

## 2.7 Why is adsorption always exothermic?

This is a favourite 2-mark question. Use thermodynamics:

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |         delta G   =   delta H   -   T  delta S                    |
  |                                                                   |
  |   A gas molecule flying freely gets PINNED DOWN on a surface.     |
  |   Freedom lost  ->  RANDOMNESS FALLS  ->  delta S is NEGATIVE.    |
  |                                                                   |
  |   Adsorption happens on its own, so it is SPONTANEOUS,            |
  |   which means delta G must be NEGATIVE.                           |
  |                                                                   |
  |   delta G = delta H - T delta S                                   |
  |   For delta G to be negative when delta S is negative,            |
  |   delta H must be NEGATIVE and large enough.                      |
  |                                                                   |
  |   Therefore:   ADSORPTION IS ALWAYS EXOTHERMIC.                   |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

The heat released when 1 mole of adsorbate is adsorbed is the
**enthalpy of adsorption** (or heat of adsorption).

### WORKED EXAMPLE 1

**Q.** When ammonia gas is passed over freshly heated charcoal in a closed flask,
the pressure of the flask falls and the flask becomes warm. Explain, naming the
adsorbate and adsorbent.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Charcoal is a highly porous solid with a very large surface area.
     Its surface atoms have unbalanced residual forces.

  2. These forces hold the NH3 molecules on the surface.
     -> this is ADSORPTION (a surface phenomenon).

  3. ADSORBATE  =  NH3 gas       ADSORBENT  =  charcoal

  4. NH3 molecules leave the gas phase and stick to the surface,
     so the number of free gas molecules falls
     ->  the PRESSURE FALLS.

  5. The molecules lose freedom of motion (delta S negative).
     Since the process is spontaneous, delta H must be negative,
     so heat is given out  ->  the FLASK BECOMES WARM.
```

> **TRAP:** Never write "the gas is absorbed by charcoal". Charcoal **ADSORBS**.
> Writing the wrong word loses the mark even if the rest is perfect.

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 3 — PHYSISORPTION AND CHEMISORPTION
# ===============================================================

This is **the single most-asked 4-mark question of the chapter.** Learn the table
until you can reproduce all eight rows blind.

## 3.1 The two kinds of "stickiness"

```
  PHYSICAL ADSORPTION  (PHYSISORPTION / van der Waals adsorption)
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  The adsorbate is held by WEAK van der Waals forces.
  No chemical bond is made. Nothing changes chemically.

  Think of it as:  a magnet holding a paperclip through paper - loose,
  and you can pull it off easily.


  CHEMICAL ADSORPTION  (CHEMISORPTION / activated adsorption)
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  The adsorbate is held by real CHEMICAL BONDS to the surface atoms.
  A surface compound is effectively formed.

  Think of it as:  glue. To get it off you must break something.
```

```
     PHYSISORPTION                        CHEMISORPTION

     o  o  o  o  o    <- 3rd layer        o  o  o  o  o
     o  o  o  o  o    <- 2nd layer        |  |  |  |  |    <- chemical BONDS
     o  o  o  o  o    <- 1st layer        |  |  |  |  |
    ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~                      =================
    weak van der Waals                    ONE layer only
    MULTILAYER possible                   MONOLAYER only
```

## 3.2 THE BIG COMPARISON TABLE

| # | Property | PHYSISORPTION | CHEMISORPTION |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nature of forces** | Weak **van der Waals** forces | Strong **chemical bond** (covalent / ionic) |
| 2 | **Enthalpy of adsorption** | Low: **20 – 40 kJ/mol** | High: **80 – 240 kJ/mol** |
| 3 | **Specificity** | **Not specific** — any gas sticks to any solid | **Highly specific** — only if a bond can form |
| 4 | **Reversibility** | **Reversible** (easily desorbed) | **Irreversible** |
| 5 | **Effect of temperature** | **Decreases** as temperature rises; favoured by **low temperature** | **Increases** with temperature at first, then decreases; favoured by **high temperature** |
| 6 | **Effect of surface area** | Increases with surface area | Increases with surface area |
| 7 | **Number of layers** | **Multimolecular** layers form | **Unimolecular (monolayer)** only |
| 8 | **Activation energy** | **No** appreciable activation energy needed | **High** activation energy needed |
| 9 | **Effect of pressure** | Increases with pressure (reversible with pressure) | Also increases with pressure |
| 10 | **Nature of adsorbate** | More easily liquefiable gases adsorb more (high critical temperature) | Depends on the chemical reactivity of the gas |
| 11 | **Example** | H2, N2, O2 on charcoal at low temperature | H2 chemisorbed on nickel; N2 on iron in the Haber process |

## 3.3 The temperature curves — draw these

```
   PHYSISORPTION                        CHEMISORPTION
   (falls steadily with T)              (rises, peaks, then falls)

   x/m |                                x/m |
       |\                                   |        __
       | \                                  |      /    \
       |  \                                 |    /        \
       |   \                                |  /            \
       |    \___                            |/                \___
       +----------- T                       +-------------------- T
                                                 ^
   Adsorption is exothermic,            it needs ACTIVATION ENERGY, so
   so raising T pushes the              heating helps at first; later the
   equilibrium BACKWARD                 exothermic equilibrium takes over
   (Le Chatelier).                      and it falls again.
```

> **TRAP:** The most common error in the whole chapter is writing
> "chemisorption decreases with temperature". It **increases first, then decreases**.
> Say both halves and say *why* (activation energy first, exothermic equilibrium later).

## 3.4 Can one turn into the other?

Yes. At low temperature a gas may be **physisorbed** on a metal. Raise the
temperature and the same gas becomes **chemisorbed** on the same metal, because it
now has enough energy to cross the activation barrier.

```
  Example:   H2 on nickel

     At 190 K   ->   PHYSISORBED  (weakly held, van der Waals)
     At 300 K   ->   CHEMISORBED  (Ni-H bonds formed)
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 2

**Q.** Why is chemisorption called "activated adsorption"?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. In chemisorption a real chemical bond has to be formed between the
     adsorbate molecule and the surface atoms.

  2. Forming that bond requires the old bonds in the adsorbate to be
     stretched or broken first, so an ENERGY BARRIER must be crossed.

  3. That barrier is the ACTIVATION ENERGY. Molecules must be supplied
     with it (usually by heating) before adsorption can occur.

  4. Because it needs activation energy, chemisorption is called
     ACTIVATED ADSORPTION.
     Physisorption needs no activation energy, so it is NOT activated.
```

# TOPIC 4 — FACTORS AFFECTING ADSORPTION OF A GAS ON A SOLID
# ===============================================================

There are **four headings**. Learn the headings first; the sentences will follow.

```
                FACTORS AFFECTING ADSORPTION
                            |
     +---------+------------+-----------+-----------+
     |         |            |           |
  NATURE OF  NATURE OF   SURFACE     TEMPERATURE   PRESSURE
  THE GAS    THE SOLID     AREA
 (adsorbate) (adsorbent)
```

## 4.1 Nature of the adsorbate (the gas)

```
  EASILY LIQUEFIABLE GASES ARE ADSORBED MORE.

  Why?  Easily liquefiable gases have STRONGER van der Waals forces,
        and van der Waals forces are exactly what physisorption uses.

  Measure of "easily liquefiable" = CRITICAL TEMPERATURE.
  HIGHER critical temperature  ->  MORE adsorption.
```

```
  +-----------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
  |   GAS     |  CRITICAL TEMP (K)    |  VOLUME ADSORBED ON     |
  |           |                       |  1 g CHARCOAL (mL)      |
  +-----------+-----------------------+-------------------------+
  |   SO2     |         630           |          380            |
  |   NH3     |         406           |          180            |
  |   HCl     |         324           |          165            |
  |   CH4     |         190           |           16            |
  |   CO      |         134           |           10            |
  |   N2      |         126           |            8            |
  |   H2      |          33           |            4.7          |
  +-----------+-----------------------+-------------------------+

  Read down the table: as critical temperature FALLS, the amount
  adsorbed FALLS. The order is exactly parallel.
```

## 4.2 Nature of the adsorbent

- Not all solids adsorb equally. **Activated charcoal, silica gel, alumina gel,
  clay and finely divided metals** are good adsorbents.
- **Activated charcoal** (charcoal heated in vacuum or superheated steam at
  650–1300 K to drive out already-adsorbed gases) is the champion, because
  activation opens up its pores.
- **Metals** are chosen for chemisorption (Ni, Pt, Pd, Fe).

## 4.3 Surface area of the adsorbent

```
  MORE SURFACE AREA  ->  MORE ADSORPTION.

  The extent of adsorption is measured per gram of adsorbent, so what
  really matters is SPECIFIC SURFACE AREA = surface area per gram.

  This is why adsorbents are used FINELY POWDERED, POROUS or ACTIVATED:
      - activated charcoal is riddled with pores
      - a catalyst is used "finely divided"
      - metals are deposited on porous supports
```

## 4.4 Effect of temperature

```
  Adsorption is EXOTHERMIC:      Gas  +  Solid  <-->  Gas/Solid  +  HEAT

  By Le Chatelier's principle, raising the temperature pushes an
  exothermic equilibrium BACKWARD.

  ->  PHYSISORPTION DECREASES as temperature rises.
  ->  CHEMISORPTION first INCREASES (activation energy is supplied),
      passes through a maximum, and then DECREASES.
```

## 4.5 Effect of pressure

```
  MORE PRESSURE  ->  MORE ADSORPTION  (at constant temperature).

  But it saturates. The graph of x/m against P looks like this:

       x/m |
           |                _____________  SATURATION
           |              /
           |            /       <-- region C: x/m independent of P
           |          /                    (surface is FULL)
           |        /
           |      /             <-- region B: x/m = k P^(1/n)
           |    /
           |  /                 <-- region A: x/m proportional to P
           | /                      (low pressure, straight line)
           |/
           +------------------------------ P

     Region A  (low P)         :   x/m  is proportional to P^1
     Region B  (medium P)      :   x/m  is proportional to P^(1/n) , n > 1
     Region C  (high P)        :   x/m  is proportional to P^0  = constant
```

This graph — the **adsorption isotherm** — leads straight into the next topic.

> **DEFINITION you must be able to state:** An **adsorption isotherm** is a graph
> showing how the extent of adsorption (x/m) varies with **pressure at constant
> temperature**.

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 5 — THE FREUNDLICH ADSORPTION ISOTHERM
# ===============================================================

## 5.1 The equation

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |                      x                                            |
  |                     ---   =   k  P^(1/n)          (n > 1)         |
  |                      m                                            |
  |                                                                   |
  |   x   =  mass of gas adsorbed                                     |
  |   m   =  mass of the adsorbent                                    |
  |   x/m =  extent of adsorption (mass adsorbed per gram of solid)   |
  |   P   =  equilibrium pressure of the gas                          |
  |   k, n=  constants for a given adsorbate-adsorbent pair at a      |
  |          given temperature                                        |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

The value of `1/n` lies between **0 and 1**.

```
   If  1/n = 1   ->   x/m = kP        adsorption proportional to pressure
   If  1/n = 0   ->   x/m = k         adsorption independent of pressure
   In practice   ->   0 < 1/n < 1     the curved middle region
```

## 5.2 The logarithmic form (this is what gets asked)

Take log to base 10 on both sides:

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |         x                             1                           |
  |    log ---    =    log k     +       ---   log P                  |
  |         m                             n                           |
  |                                                                   |
  |         |                |                |         |             |
  |         y        =       c       +      slope   x   x             |
  |                                                                   |
  |    Plot  log(x/m)  on the y-axis  against  log P  on the x-axis   |
  |    ->  a STRAIGHT LINE                                            |
  |                                                                   |
  |             SLOPE      =  1 / n                                   |
  |             INTERCEPT  =  log k                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 5.3 The two graphs — draw BOTH in the exam

```
  GRAPH 1 : the isotherm itself          GRAPH 2 : the log plot
  (x/m against P)                        (log x/m against log P)


   x/m |                                 log |
       |            ______  saturation   x/m |                    o
       |          /                          |                 o
       |        /                            |              o
       |      /                              |           o        slope = 1/n
       |    /                                |        o
       |  /                                  |     o
       | /                                   |  o
       |/                             log k  |o
       +--------------------- P              +--------------------- log P
                                             |
       curved, flattens out                  perfect straight line
                                             intercept on y-axis = log k
```

> **TRAP:** The intercept is **log k**, NOT k. If a question gives you the
> intercept as 0.3, then log k = 0.3 so k = 10^0.3 = 2. Students constantly
> forget to un-log it.

## 5.4 Why it FAILS at high pressure — the limitation

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  LIMITATION OF THE FREUNDLICH ISOTHERM                            |
  |                                                                   |
  |  It is an EMPIRICAL (experimental) relation, not derived from     |
  |  theory. It works only over a LIMITED range of pressure.          |
  |                                                                   |
  |  At HIGH PRESSURE the surface becomes SATURATED - every site is   |
  |  occupied - so x/m becomes INDEPENDENT of pressure                |
  |  (that is, 1/n -> 0).                                             |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The Freundlich equation still predicts that x/m keeps rising     |
  |  with P, so the experimental graph DEVIATES FROM THE STRAIGHT     |
  |  LINE at high pressure. The equation therefore FAILS at high      |
  |  pressure.                                                        |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
   log |
   x/m |                       . . . .   <-- experiment FLATTENS
       |                 . .
       |            .  /
       |         . /                     <-- Freundlich straight line
       |      ./                             keeps going up
       |   ./
       | ./
       |/
       +--------------------------------- log P
                          ^
                    they separate here (high pressure)
```

## 5.5 For adsorption from solution

Replace pressure with concentration:

```
              x
             ---   =   k  C^(1/n)
              m

              x                        1
        log  ---   =   log k    +     ---  log C
              m                        n

   C = equilibrium concentration of the solute in the solution
```

## 5.6 The Langmuir isotherm (outline only)

You only need to be able to *state* it.

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  LANGMUIR ADSORPTION ISOTHERM  -  assumptions                     |
  |                                                                   |
  |   1. Adsorption forms only a MONOLAYER (one molecule thick).      |
  |   2. The surface has a fixed number of identical adsorption       |
  |      sites; each site holds one molecule.                         |
  |   3. Adsorbed molecules do not interact with one another.         |
  |   4. Adsorption and desorption reach a DYNAMIC EQUILIBRIUM.       |
  |                                                                   |
  |                x            a P                                   |
  |               ---   =   -----------                               |
  |                m         1  +  b P                                |
  |                                                                   |
  |   At LOW pressure  (bP << 1):    x/m  =  aP        (linear)       |
  |   At HIGH pressure (bP >> 1):    x/m  =  a/b       (constant)     |
  |                                                                   |
  |   So Langmuir EXPLAINS the saturation that Freundlich cannot.     |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 3

**Q.** In an experiment the plot of log(x/m) against log P gave a straight line
with slope 0.5 and y-intercept 0.6021. Find k and n, and calculate x/m when
P = 4 atm.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Step 1  Compare with the log form:

             log (x/m)  =  log k  +  (1/n) log P

          slope     =  1/n       =  0.5      ->   n = 1 / 0.5  =  2
          intercept =  log k     =  0.6021

  Step 2  Un-log the intercept:

             k  =  10^0.6021  =  4          (since log 4 = 0.6021)

  Step 3  Write the isotherm:

             x/m  =  4  x  P^(1/2)   =  4 sqrt(P)

  Step 4  Put P = 4 atm:

             x/m  =  4  x  sqrt(4)  =  4 x 2  =  8

  ANSWER:   n = 2 ,  k = 4 ,  x/m = 8
```

**Check:** log(x/m) = log 8 = 0.9031. And log k + 0.5 log 4 = 0.6021 + 0.5(0.6021)
= 0.6021 + 0.3010 = 0.9031. ✓ It matches.

### WORKED EXAMPLE 4

**Q.** 1.0 g of charcoal adsorbs 0.4 g of a gas at a certain pressure. If k = 2 and
n = 2 for this system, find the pressure (in the same units used for k).

**ANSWER:**
```
  x/m  =  0.4 / 1.0  =  0.4

  x/m  =  k P^(1/n)
  0.4  =  2  x  P^(1/2)

  P^(1/2)  =  0.4 / 2  =  0.2

  P  =  (0.2)^2  =  0.04

  ANSWER:  P = 0.04 (pressure units)
```

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 6 — APPLICATIONS OF ADSORPTION
# ===============================================================

Six one-liners. Each is worth half a mark; together they are a 4-mark answer.

| # | Application | How adsorption does it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gas masks** | Activated charcoal in the mask **adsorbs poisonous gases**, so only clean air reaches the miner/soldier |
| 2 | **Removal of moisture (drying)** | **Silica gel** and **alumina gel** adsorb water vapour and keep the air in an instrument or a package dry |
| 3 | **Heterogeneous catalysis** | The reactants are **adsorbed on the catalyst surface**, weakened, and react faster (Haber process, hydrogenation of oils) |
| 4 | **Chromatography** | Different components of a mixture are **adsorbed to different extents** on the column (alumina/silica) and so separate out |
| 5 | **Froth flotation** | **Pine oil** is preferentially adsorbed on the **sulphide ore** particles, making them water-repellent so they rise with the froth while gangue sinks |
| 6 | **Control of humidity** | **Silica gel** and **alumina gel** are used as **dehumidifiers** to control humidity in rooms and storerooms |

**Extra ones worth mentioning:**

```
  7.  HIGH VACUUM production - after a rotary pump, activated charcoal
      cooled in liquid air adsorbs the last traces of gas.

  8.  DECOLOURISATION - animal charcoal removes the coloured impurities
      from raw sugar solution by adsorbing them.

  9.  MEDICINE - many drugs act by getting adsorbed on germs and killing
      them; kaolin and charcoal tablets adsorb toxins in the stomach.

 10.  ADSORPTION INDICATORS - eosin, fluorescein: the indicator is
      adsorbed on the precipitate at the end point and the colour changes.

 11.  ION EXCHANGE / softening of hard water - permutit (zeolite) works by
      exchanging ions held on its surface.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 5

**Q.** Explain how froth flotation depends on adsorption.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Froth flotation is used to concentrate SULPHIDE ORES.

  2. The finely powdered ore is put in water with a small amount of a
     collector such as PINE OIL, and air is blown through it.

  3. Pine oil is PREFERENTIALLY ADSORBED on the surface of the sulphide
     ore particles (not on the gangue).

  4. Once coated with oil, the ore particles become WATER-REPELLENT
     (hydrophobic) and stick to the air bubbles.

  5. The bubbles carry them to the top as a FROTH, which is skimmed off.
     The heavier, water-wetted gangue sinks and is left behind.

  So the whole separation depends on SELECTIVE ADSORPTION of the
  collector on the ore particles.
```

# TOPIC 7 — CATALYST AND CATALYSIS
# ===============================================================

## 7.1 The definitions

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  CATALYST                                                         |
  |  A substance which ALTERS THE RATE of a chemical reaction and     |
  |  itself remains CHEMICALLY UNCHANGED IN MASS AND COMPOSITION      |
  |  at the end of the reaction.                                      |
  |                                                                   |
  |  CATALYSIS                                                        |
  |  The phenomenon of changing the rate of a reaction by using a     |
  |  catalyst.                                                        |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **TRAP:** Do **not** write "a catalyst increases the rate". A catalyst
> **ALTERS** the rate — it may increase it (positive) or decrease it (negative).
> Also do **not** write "remains unchanged" alone; the phrase is "**chemically
> unchanged in mass and composition**" (its *physical* state may change — a lump
> of MnO2 comes out as a powder).

## 7.2 What a catalyst does and does NOT do

```
  A CATALYST DOES:                     A CATALYST DOES NOT:
  --------------------------           ------------------------------
  Lower the ACTIVATION ENERGY          Change delta H of the reaction
  Provide an alternative path          Change the EQUILIBRIUM CONSTANT K
  Speed up forward AND backward        Change the POSITION of equilibrium
     reactions equally                 Start a reaction that is
  Help equilibrium arrive SOONER          thermodynamically impossible
                                       Get consumed
```

> **TRAP (a guaranteed MCQ):** "A catalyst increases the yield of the product."
> **FALSE.** It only makes you reach the *same* equilibrium *faster*.

## 7.3 Positive and negative catalysts

| Type | What it does | Effect on Ea | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positive catalyst** (promotor of rate) | **Increases** the rate | **Lowers** activation energy | MnO2 in the decomposition of KClO3; Fe in the Haber process; V2O5 in the Contact process |
| **Negative catalyst** (**inhibitor**) | **Decreases** the rate | **Raises** activation energy | **Glycerol** or **acetanilide** slows the decomposition of H2O2; **alcohol** slows the oxidation of chloroform; **TEL** (tetraethyl lead) slows the knocking of petrol |

```
  Reactions to quote:

  POSITIVE:   2 KClO3  --(MnO2, heat)-->  2 KCl  +  3 O2
              N2 + 3 H2  --(Fe)-->  2 NH3

  NEGATIVE:   2 H2O2  --(glycerol slows it)-->  2 H2O  +  O2
```

## 7.4 Promoters and poisons

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  PROMOTER  (activator)                                            |
  |  A substance which is NOT itself a catalyst, but which            |
  |  INCREASES THE ACTIVITY of a catalyst when added to it.           |
  |                                                                   |
  |     Example:  Mo (molybdenum) is a promoter for Fe in the         |
  |               HABER process.                                      |
  |               Al2O3 and K2O also promote Fe in the Haber process. |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  CATALYTIC POISON  (inhibitor of the catalyst)                    |
  |  A substance which DESTROYS OR REDUCES THE ACTIVITY of a          |
  |  catalyst, usually by getting strongly chemisorbed on the active  |
  |  sites and blocking them.                                         |
  |                                                                   |
  |     Example:  ARSENIC (As2O3) poisons the platinum catalyst in    |
  |               the CONTACT process.                                |
  |               CO poisons the iron catalyst in the HABER process.  |
  |               Sulphur compounds poison Ni in oil hydrogenation.   |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  MEMORY:  PROMOTER makes the catalyst BETTER.
           POISON   makes the catalyst DEAD.
           Neither of them is itself a catalyst.
```

> **TRAP:** Students confuse a **negative catalyst** with a **catalytic poison**.
> A **negative catalyst** slows down the *reaction*.
> A **poison** destroys the *catalyst*. Different things.

## 7.5 Autocatalysis (worth one line)

When a **product of the reaction itself acts as the catalyst**, it is autocatalysis.
Example: in the titration of oxalic acid with KMnO4, the **Mn2+** produced catalyses
the further reaction — which is why the first drop decolourises slowly and later
drops instantly.

# TOPIC 8 — HOMOGENEOUS AND HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS
# ===============================================================

```
  HOMOGENEOUS CATALYSIS
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  The catalyst is in the SAME PHASE as the reactants.
  (all gases, or all in the same solution)

  HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  The catalyst is in a DIFFERENT PHASE from the reactants.
  (usually a SOLID catalyst with GASEOUS or LIQUID reactants)
  Also called CONTACT CATALYSIS or SURFACE CATALYSIS.
```

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 9 — THE ADSORPTION THEORY OF HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS
# ===============================================================

This is the "modern adsorption theory". It explains **how** a solid catalyst works
by combining the old adsorption theory with the intermediate-compound theory.

## 9.1 The five steps — memorise them in order

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  STEP 1   DIFFUSION of the reactant molecules TO the surface      |
  |           of the catalyst.                                        |
  |                                                                   |
  |  STEP 2   ADSORPTION of the reactant molecules on the surface     |
  |           (the free valencies of the surface atoms hold them).    |
  |                                                                   |
  |  STEP 3   REACTION on the surface - an activated intermediate     |
  |           complex is formed and breaks down into products.        |
  |                                                                   |
  |  STEP 4   DESORPTION of the product molecules from the surface,   |
  |           freeing the site for fresh reactant molecules.          |
  |                                                                   |
  |  STEP 5   DIFFUSION of the product molecules AWAY from the        |
  |           catalyst surface.                                       |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  MEMORY:  D - A - R - D - D
           Diffuse in, Adsorb, React, Desorb, Diffuse out.
```

## 9.2 Picture the five steps

```
   STEP 1                 STEP 2                 STEP 3
   diffusion in           adsorption             reaction on surface

   A-A   B-B              A A   B B              A-B   A-B
     \   /                | |   | |               \ \   / /
      \ /                 | |   | |                \ \ / /
  ==============     ==============          ==============
    CATALYST            CATALYST                 CATALYST
                    (bonds are WEAKENED)     (new bonds form)


   STEP 4                 STEP 5
   desorption             diffusion out

     A-B  A-B                 A-B    A-B
       ^    ^                  ^      ^
  ==============          ==============
    CATALYST                 CATALYST
                          surface is FREE again
```

**Why does it speed up the reaction?** Two reasons:
1. The reactants are **concentrated** on the surface, so collisions are far more frequent.
2. Adsorption **weakens the bonds** in the reactant molecules, which **lowers the
   activation energy**.

## 9.3 THE ENERGY DIAGRAM — draw this, it carries marks

```
   E |
   N |            /\
   E |           /  \                    <-- UNCATALYSED path
   R |          /    \                       (high hump, Ea is LARGE)
   G |         /      \
   Y |        /        \
     |       /   /\     \
     |      /   /  \     \                <-- CATALYSED path
     |     /   /    \     \                   (small hump, Ea is SMALL)
     |    /   /      \     \
     |   /  /          \    \
     |  / /              \   \
  R  | //                  \  \
  ---|/                      \ \
     |  REACTANTS              \ \_______________
     |                                PRODUCTS
     |
     |<---------- delta H is the SAME either way ------------>
     +-------------------------------------------------------
                   REACTION PROGRESS

   Ea (uncatalysed)  =  the TALL hump
   Ea (catalysed)    =  the SHORT hump

   THE CATALYST LOWERS Ea. IT DOES NOT CHANGE delta H.
```

> **TRAP:** In this diagram the **level of the reactants and the level of the
> products must be identical on both paths**. Only the hump height changes.
> If you draw the products at a different level you have said the catalyst
> changes delta H — which is wrong and costs the mark.

### WORKED EXAMPLE 6

**Q.** Explain why a solid catalyst is always used in a finely divided state.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Heterogeneous catalysis works on the SURFACE of the catalyst
     (adsorption theory).

  2. The rate therefore depends on the number of ACTIVE SITES available,
     which depends on the SURFACE AREA.

  3. Grinding the catalyst into a fine powder greatly increases the
     surface area per gram (specific surface area) without using any
     more metal.

  4. More surface  ->  more adsorption of reactants  ->  more active
     sites  ->  faster reaction.

  Example: iron in the Haber process is used FINELY DIVIDED; nickel for
  hydrogenation of oils is used as a fine powder.
```

# TOPIC 10 — SHAPE SELECTIVE CATALYSIS AND ZEOLITES
# ===============================================================

## 10.1 The definition

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  SHAPE SELECTIVE CATALYSIS                                        |
  |                                                                   |
  |  Catalysis in which the reaction depends upon the PORE SIZE of    |
  |  the catalyst and the SIZE AND SHAPE of the reactant and product  |
  |  molecules.                                                       |
  |                                                                   |
  |  ZEOLITES are the classic shape-selective catalysts.              |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 10.2 What a zeolite is

```
  ZEOLITES are MICROPOROUS ALUMINOSILICATES.

  General formula:   Mx/n [(AlO2)x (SiO2)y] . m H2O

  Structure: a three-dimensional network of SiO4 and AlO4 tetrahedra
  joined together through OXYGEN atoms, leaving a honeycomb of
  CHANNELS AND CAVITIES of a definite size.

     +---O---+---O---+---O---+
     |       |       |       |
     O  cav  O  cav  O  cav  O      <- the "cages"
     |  ity  |  ity  |  ity  |
     +---O---+---O---+---O---+
     |       |       |       |
     O  cav  O  cav  O  cav  O
     |  ity  |  ity  |  ity  |
     +---O---+---O---+---O---+

  Only molecules SMALL ENOUGH to enter the cavity can react.
  Bigger molecules are simply turned away at the door - which is why
  zeolites are called MOLECULAR SIEVES.
```

## 10.3 Preparation and use

- Zeolites are prepared by heating an aluminosilicate with sodium/potassium
  hydroxide solution under pressure (hydrothermal method).
- Before use they are **heated in vacuum** to drive out the water of hydration,
  which opens up the pores.

```
  ZSM-5   ("Zeolite Sieve of Molecular porosity - 5")
  -------------------------------------------------------------------
  The most important industrial zeolite.

  USE:  It converts ALCOHOLS DIRECTLY INTO GASOLINE (PETROL)
        by DEHYDRATING them to a mixture of hydrocarbons.

        alcohols  --(ZSM-5)-->  hydrocarbons (gasoline) + H2O

  Zeolites are also used in the PETROCHEMICAL INDUSTRY for
  CRACKING of hydrocarbons and ISOMERISATION.
  Hydrated zeolites (permutit) are used to SOFTEN HARD WATER
  by ion exchange.
```

> **2-mark answer for "What is ZSM-5?":** ZSM-5 is a zeolite catalyst used in
> shape-selective catalysis; it converts alcohols directly into gasoline (petrol)
> by dehydrating them into a mixture of hydrocarbons.

# TOPIC 11 — ENZYME CATALYSIS
# ===============================================================

## 11.1 What enzymes are

```
  ENZYMES are complex NITROGENOUS ORGANIC COMPOUNDS produced by living
  plants and animals. Chemically they are PROTEINS of high molecular mass
  that form COLLOIDAL SOLUTIONS in water.

  They are the catalysts of living cells, so they are called
  BIOCHEMICAL CATALYSTS, and the phenomenon is BIOCHEMICAL CATALYSIS.

  Enzyme catalysis is a case of HOMOGENEOUS catalysis in a colloidal
  solution (sometimes described as "microheterogeneous" because the
  enzyme is colloidal).
```

## 11.2 THE CHARACTERISTICS — the 4-mark answer

| # | Characteristic | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Most efficient** | Very small amounts of enzyme cause huge reactions. One molecule of an enzyme can transform about **10^6 molecules of reactant per minute** |
| 2 | **Highly specific** | Each enzyme catalyses **one particular reaction** for **one particular substrate**. Urease hydrolyses only urea, nothing else |
| 3 | **Mild conditions (temperature)** | They work best at **optimum temperature 298–310 K** (body temperature). Too hot and the protein is **denatured**; activity is destroyed |
| 4 | **Mild conditions (pH)** | They work best at an **optimum pH, usually 5–7**. Activity falls sharply on either side |
| 5 | **Activators and coenzymes** | Activity is **increased by activators/co-enzymes** — e.g. a metal ion (Na+, Co2+, Cu2+) loosely bound to the enzyme. Amylase is activated by NaCl |
| 6 | **Inhibitors and poisons** | Just like ordinary catalysts, enzymes are **inhibited or poisoned** by certain substances. Many **drugs work by inhibiting an enzyme** of the germ |
| 7 | **Increased by light** | Activity is increased in the presence of **ultraviolet light** (and lost with too much) |

```
  THE TWO CURVES TO REMEMBER

  ACTIVITY vs TEMPERATURE            ACTIVITY vs pH

  act |        __                    act |        __
      |      /    \                      |      /    \
      |    /        \                    |    /        \
      |  /            \                  |  /            \
      |/                \_               |/                \_
      +------------------- T             +------------------- pH
          ^                                  ^
       298-310 K                          pH 5 - 7
       (optimum)                          (optimum)

  Both are BELL-SHAPED with a sharp maximum. Outside it, the enzyme
  is DENATURED and the activity collapses.
```

## 11.3 THE LOCK-AND-KEY MECHANISM

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  On the surface of the huge enzyme molecule there are cavities of |
  |  a particular SHAPE with ACTIVE GROUPS (-NH2, -COOH, -SH, -OH)    |
  |  around them. These cavities are the ACTIVE SITES.                |
  |                                                                   |
  |  A substrate molecule of exactly the RIGHT SHAPE fits into the    |
  |  active site like a KEY fits a LOCK, and is held there by the     |
  |  active groups.                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  This forms an ACTIVATED ENZYME-SUBSTRATE COMPLEX, which then     |
  |  breaks up to give the products and REGENERATES the free enzyme.  |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
   STEP 1  binding                       STEP 2  reaction & release

     S                                        P1   P2
    ___                                       __   __
   /   \                                     /  \ /  \
   |   |   substrate                          ^   ^
     |                                        |   |
     v                                     +--+---+--+
  +--+---+--+                              |         |
  |  \___/  |   <- active site (cavity)    |  \___/  |  <- site is EMPTY,
  |         |                              |         |     enzyme is FREE
  |  ENZYME |                              |  ENZYME |     to work again
  +---------+                              +---------+

  E  +  S   <-->   [ E - S ]   -->   E  +  P
  enzyme  substrate  complex        enzyme  product
```

**Why specificity follows immediately:** a substrate of the wrong shape simply
cannot fit into the cavity, so no complex forms and no reaction happens. The lock
only accepts its own key.

## 11.4 THE ENZYME TABLE — learn at least six rows

| Enzyme | Source | Reaction it catalyses |
|---|---|---|
| **Invertase (sucrase)** | Yeast | `Sucrose + H2O --> Glucose + Fructose` |
| **Zymase** | Yeast | `Glucose --> 2 C2H5OH + 2 CO2` (fermentation to ethanol) |
| **Diastase** | Malt | `Starch (2 C6H10O5)n + n H2O --> n Maltose (C12H22O11)` |
| **Maltase** | Yeast | `Maltose + H2O --> 2 Glucose` |
| **Urease** | Soybean / bacteria | `Urea (NH2CONH2) + H2O --> 2 NH3 + CO2` |
| **Pepsin** | Stomach | `Proteins --> Peptides / amino acids` |
| **Trypsin** | Pancreas (intestinal juice) | `Proteins --> Amino acids` |
| **Amylase (ptyalin)** | Saliva | `Starch --> Maltose` |
| **Lipase** | Pancreas | `Fats + H2O --> Fatty acids + Glycerol` |

```
  THE FERMENTATION CHAIN - a favourite question:

     Starch  --(DIASTASE)-->  Maltose  --(MALTASE)-->  Glucose
                                                          |
                                                    (ZYMASE)
                                                          |
                                                          v
                                              2 C2H5OH  +  2 CO2

     Sucrose --(INVERTASE)--> Glucose + Fructose --(ZYMASE)--> ethanol
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 7

**Q.** Why does the activity of an enzyme fall sharply above 310 K?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Enzymes are PROTEINS. Their catalytic power comes from the exact
     three-dimensional SHAPE of the active site.

  2. That shape is held together by weak forces (hydrogen bonds,
     disulphide links, etc.).

  3. Above the optimum temperature (about 310 K) these weak forces
     break and the protein is DENATURED - the active site loses
     its shape.

  4. The substrate can no longer fit the "lock", the enzyme-substrate
     complex cannot form, and the catalytic activity is lost.

  This is why enzymes have a narrow BELL-SHAPED activity-temperature
  curve with a maximum at 298-310 K.
```

# TOPIC 12 — COLLOIDS: WHAT THEY ARE
# ===============================================================

## 12.1 Classify by particle size

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |   TRUE SOLUTION      particle size   LESS than  1 nm              |
  |                                                                   |
  |   COLLOID            particle size   1 nm  to  1000 nm            |
  |                                                                   |
  |   SUSPENSION         particle size   MORE than 1000 nm  (1 um)    |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

    <----------------- increasing particle size ----------------->

    |----------|---------------------------|-------------------|
   0        1 nm                       1000 nm             bigger
       TRUE            COLLOID                SUSPENSION
     SOLUTION
```

**Definition of a colloid:** A colloid is a **heterogeneous system** in which one
substance (the **dispersed phase**) is dispersed as **particles of size 1–1000 nm**
in another substance (the **dispersion medium**).

## 12.2 The three-way comparison table

| Property | TRUE SOLUTION | COLLOID | SUSPENSION |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Particle size** | < 1 nm | 1 – 1000 nm | > 1000 nm |
| **Nature** | Homogeneous | **Heterogeneous** (looks homogeneous) | Heterogeneous |
| **Visible to the eye?** | No | No | **Yes** |
| **Seen under a microscope?** | No | Only in an **ultramicroscope** | Yes, ordinary microscope |
| **Tyndall effect** | **Absent** | **PRESENT** | Present (but it settles) |
| **Passes through filter paper?** | Yes | **Yes** | **No** |
| **Passes through parchment / animal membrane?** | **Yes** | **No** | No |
| **Settles on standing?** | Never | **No** (Brownian motion keeps it up) | **Yes, settles down** |
| **Diffusion** | Fast | **Slow** | Does not diffuse |
| **Example** | Sugar in water, NaCl in water | Milk, blood, smoke, starch sol | Sand in water, chalk in water |

> **THE KEY DISTINGUISHING TEST:** A colloid **passes through filter paper** but
> **does NOT pass through a parchment membrane**. That single line separates it from
> both a true solution and a suspension.

## 12.3 Dispersed phase and dispersion medium

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  DISPERSED PHASE    =  the substance present as the tiny          |
  |                        colloidal PARTICLES  (the "solute")        |
  |                                                                   |
  |  DISPERSION MEDIUM  =  the substance in which they are spread     |
  |                        out  (the "solvent")                       |
  |                                                                   |
  |  Example - MILK:                                                  |
  |     dispersed phase   =  liquid FAT droplets                      |
  |     dispersion medium =  WATER                                    |
  |                                                                   |
  |  Example - SMOKE:                                                 |
  |     dispersed phase   =  SOLID carbon particles                   |
  |     dispersion medium =  AIR (gas)                                |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 12.4 THE EIGHT TYPES OF COLLOIDAL SYSTEM — the exam table

| # | Dispersed phase | Dispersion medium | Name | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solid | Solid | **Solid sol** | Coloured gemstones (ruby glass), some alloys |
| 2 | Solid | Liquid | **SOL** | Paints, cell fluids, muddy water, starch sol, gold sol |
| 3 | Solid | Gas | **AEROSOL** | **Smoke**, dust |
| 4 | Liquid | Solid | **GEL** | Cheese, butter, jellies, curd |
| 5 | Liquid | Liquid | **EMULSION** | **Milk**, hair cream |
| 6 | Liquid | Gas | **AEROSOL** | **Fog, mist, cloud**, insecticide sprays |
| 7 | Gas | Solid | **Solid foam** | Pumice stone, foam rubber, bread |
| 8 | Gas | Liquid | **FOAM / froth** | Whipped cream, soap lather, soda water froth |

```
  WHY IS THERE NO 9th TYPE?

  GAS in GAS is NOT a colloid. Two gases always mix completely at the
  MOLECULAR level, giving a HOMOGENEOUS mixture - a true solution.
  There is no boundary and no particle of colloidal size.

  MEMORY BY MEDIUM:
       medium = GAS     ->  AEROSOL  (smoke = solid, fog = liquid)
       medium = LIQUID  ->  SOL (solid) / EMULSION (liquid) / FOAM (gas)
       medium = SOLID   ->  SOLID SOL (solid) / GEL (liquid) / SOLID FOAM (gas)
```

**Special names for sols by their medium:**

```
   Water as medium    ->  HYDROSOL  (or aquasol)
   Alcohol as medium  ->  ALCOSOL
   Benzene as medium  ->  BENZOSOL
   Gas as medium      ->  AEROSOL
```

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 13 — LYOPHILIC AND LYOPHOBIC COLLOIDS
# ===============================================================

This table is the **second most-asked 4-mark question** of the chapter.

```
  LYOPHILIC   "lyo" = solvent  ,  "philic" = loving
              SOLVENT-LOVING colloids.
              The dispersed phase has a strong affinity for the medium.
              If water is the medium they are called HYDROPHILIC.

  LYOPHOBIC   "lyo" = solvent  ,  "phobic" = hating
              SOLVENT-HATING colloids.
              The dispersed phase has NO affinity for the medium.
              If water is the medium they are called HYDROPHOBIC.
```

## 13.1 The comparison table

| # | Property | LYOPHILIC SOL | LYOPHOBIC SOL |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Affinity for medium** | **Strong** affinity (solvent-loving) | **No** affinity (solvent-hating) |
| 2 | **Preparation** | Formed **directly** by simply mixing the substance with the medium | Cannot be made directly; needs **special methods** (Bredig's arc, peptization, chemical) |
| 3 | **Reversibility** | **REVERSIBLE** — evaporate the medium and add it back, the sol reforms | **IRREVERSIBLE** — once coagulated it cannot be reformed by adding the medium |
| 4 | **Stability** | **Very stable**; self-stabilised by solvation | **Less stable**; needs a **stabilising agent** |
| 5 | **Effect of electrolyte** | **Not easily coagulated**; a large amount of electrolyte is needed | **Easily coagulated** by even a small amount of electrolyte |
| 6 | **Hydration / solvation** | Particles are **heavily solvated** (a layer of solvent surrounds them) | Particles are **not solvated** |
| 7 | **Viscosity** | **Much higher** than the medium | **Nearly the same** as the medium |
| 8 | **Surface tension** | **Lower** than that of the medium | Nearly the **same** as the medium |
| 9 | **Tyndall effect** | **Weak** (less light scattered) | **Strong** (more light scattered) |
| 10 | **Migration in an electric field** | May move to either electrode, or not at all | **Move only to one electrode** (definite charge) |
| 11 | **Also called** | **Intrinsic** / **emulsoid** colloids | **Extrinsic** / **suspensoid** colloids |
| 12 | **Examples** | **Gum, gelatin, starch, rubber, proteins, agar-agar** | **Metal sols (Au, Ag, Cu), metal hydroxides Fe(OH)3, Al(OH)3, metal sulphides As2S3** |

> **TRAP:** "Lyophilic sols are reversible, lyophobic sols are irreversible" and
> "lyophobic sols need a stabiliser" are the two rows examiners look for first.
> If you only remember four rows, remember rows 1, 3, 4 and 12.

## 13.2 Classification by the nature of the particles

There are **three types**: multimolecular, macromolecular and associated.

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  1. MULTIMOLECULAR COLLOIDS                                       |
  |                                                                   |
  |     A LARGE NUMBER of atoms or small molecules (each smaller      |
  |     than 1 nm) come together on dissolution to form an            |
  |     AGGREGATE of colloidal size.                                  |
  |     Held together by weak VAN DER WAALS forces.                   |
  |     Usually LYOPHOBIC in character.                               |
  |                                                                   |
  |     Examples:  GOLD SOL     - thousands of Au atoms per particle  |
  |                SULPHUR SOL  - about a thousand S8 molecules       |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  2. MACROMOLECULAR COLLOIDS                                       |
  |                                                                   |
  |     The molecules are THEMSELVES so BIG (macromolecules /         |
  |     polymers) that ONE molecule is already of colloidal size.     |
  |     Very stable, and LYOPHILIC in character.                      |
  |                                                                   |
  |     Natural:     starch, cellulose, proteins, enzymes,            |
  |                  gelatin, natural rubber                          |
  |     Synthetic:   polythene, nylon, polystyrene, synthetic rubber  |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  3. ASSOCIATED COLLOIDS  (MICELLES)                               |
  |                                                                   |
  |     Substances which behave as NORMAL STRONG ELECTROLYTES at      |
  |     LOW concentration, but at HIGHER concentration their ions     |
  |     ASSOCIATE into aggregates of colloidal size called MICELLES.  |
  |                                                                   |
  |     Examples:  SOAPS  (sodium stearate C17H35COONa)               |
  |                DETERGENTS (sodium dodecyl sulphate)               |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 13.3 Micelles, CMC and the Kraft temperature

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  CRITICAL MICELLE CONCENTRATION  (CMC)                            |
  |  The MINIMUM CONCENTRATION above which micelles begin to form.    |
  |  For soaps in water, CMC is about 10^-4 to 10^-3 mol/L.           |
  |                                                                   |
  |  KRAFT TEMPERATURE  (Tk)                                          |
  |  The MINIMUM TEMPERATURE above which micelles are formed.         |
  |                                                                   |
  |  MICELLES FORM ONLY WHEN BOTH CONDITIONS ARE MET:                 |
  |      concentration  >  CMC        AND      temperature  >  Tk     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  A micelle may contain 100 or more molecules.                     |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Why do micelles form?** A soap molecule has **two ends of opposite character**:

```
   A SOAP MOLECULE   (sodium stearate,  C17H35COO- Na+)

   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~O
   <--- long hydrocarbon TAIL --------> ^
        C17H35                          |
        HYDROPHOBIC / non-polar         COO- head
        (water-hating, oil-loving)      HYDROPHILIC / polar
                                        (water-loving)

   Such a molecule is called AMPHIPATHIC / AMPHIPHILIC.


   THE MICELLE (in water)  -  tails hide inside, heads face the water

                    -   -   -
                 -  \  |  /   -
              -   \   \|/   /   -
              -----\---o---/-----      o  = the oily/greasy centre
              -    /   /|\   \   -     -  = the COO- heads facing water
                 -  /  |  \   -        \|/ = the hydrocarbon tails
                    -   -   -               pointing INWARD

   In water:  IONIC HEADS OUTSIDE, hydrocarbon tails inside.
   In a non-polar solvent (REVERSE micelle): the other way round.
```

## 14.1 Chemical methods (condensation)

Build the colloidal particle up from molecules by a chemical reaction.

| Method | Reaction | Sol formed |
|---|---|---|
| **Double decomposition** | `As2O3 + 3 H2S --> As2S3 (sol) + 3 H2O` | **As2S3 sol** (negative) |
| **Oxidation** | `SO2 + 2 H2S --> 3 S (sol) + 2 H2O` | **Sulphur sol** |
| **Reduction** | `2 AuCl3 + 3 SnCl2 --> 2 Au (sol) + 3 SnCl4` | **Gold sol** |
| **Hydrolysis** | `FeCl3 + 3 H2O --(boiling)--> Fe(OH)3 (sol) + 3 HCl` | **Fe(OH)3 sol** (positive) |

> **How to remember:** the four chemical methods are the four reaction types you
> already know — **double decomposition, oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis**.
> Learn one equation for each.

## 14.2 Bredig's arc method (electrical disintegration)

Used for making sols of **metals: gold, silver, platinum, copper**.

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  BREDIG'S ARC METHOD                                              |
  |                                                                   |
  |  An ELECTRIC ARC is struck between two rods of the metal held     |
  |  under water containing a TRACE OF ALKALI (NaOH) as stabiliser.   |
  |  The whole vessel stands in an ICE BATH.                          |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The intense heat of the arc VAPORISES the metal (dispersion),    |
  |  and the vapour is immediately CONDENSED by the cold water into   |
  |  colloidal particles (condensation).                              |
  |                                                                   |
  |  So it is BOTH a dispersion AND a condensation method.            |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
                    ~~ ARC ~~
        metal rod  =====))(( =====  metal rod
                        ^^
      +-----------------||------------------+
      |  water + trace of NaOH              |
      |     .   .    .     .   .            |   <- metal sol forms here
      |  .     .   .    .     .    .        |
      +-------------------------------------+
      |     ICE BATH (keeps it cold)        |
      +-------------------------------------+
```

## 14.3 Peptization

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  PEPTIZATION                                                      |
  |  The process of converting a FRESHLY PREPARED PRECIPITATE into a  |
  |  COLLOIDAL SOL by shaking it with the dispersion medium in the    |
  |  presence of a SMALL AMOUNT OF ELECTROLYTE.                       |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The electrolyte used is called the PEPTIZING AGENT.              |
  |                                                                   |
  |  HOW IT WORKS:  the precipitate particles ADSORB one kind of ion  |
  |  from the electrolyte, become CHARGED, and then REPEL each other  |
  |  and break up into colloidal-sized particles.                     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  EXAMPLE:  a fresh precipitate of Fe(OH)3 is shaken with a little |
  |  FeCl3 solution. The Fe3+ ions are adsorbed on the precipitate,   |
  |  which becomes POSITIVELY CHARGED and breaks up into a            |
  |  Fe(OH)3 SOL.                                                     |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
     PRECIPITATE                       SOL

     ####  ####                     +  +  +  +
     ####  ####    + a little      +  +  +  +   all particles now carry
     ####  ####      FeCl3          +  +  +  +   the SAME charge, so they
     ####  ####    ----------->    +  +  +  +    REPEL and stay apart
     (stuck together)              (dispersed)
```

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 15 — PURIFICATION OF COLLOIDS
# ===============================================================

**Why purify?** A freshly made sol contains **excess electrolyte**. A small amount
is needed for stability, but too much will **coagulate** the sol. So the excess
must be removed.

## 15.1 Dialysis

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  DIALYSIS                                                         |
  |  The removal of dissolved substances (ions / small molecules)     |
  |  from a colloidal solution by DIFFUSION THROUGH A SUITABLE        |
  |  MEMBRANE (parchment paper, cellophane, animal membrane).         |
  |                                                                   |
  |  PRINCIPLE:  ions and small molecules PASS THROUGH the membrane;  |
  |              colloidal particles are TOO BIG and CANNOT.          |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
     running water IN                       running water OUT
          |                                        ^
          v                                        |
      +---------------------------------------------+
      |     +-----------------------------+         |
      |     |  SOL  +  electrolyte        |         |
      |     |   o o o   (o = colloid)     |         |
      |     |  o  o  o                    |         |
      |     +--|---------------|----------+         |
      |        v  ions escape  v                    |
      |    - +      - +      - +                    |
      |         WATER carries them away             |
      +---------------------------------------------+
        parchment / cellophane BAG hangs in the tank

     The colloid STAYS IN the bag. The electrolyte WASHES AWAY.
```

**Drawback:** it is very **slow** (may take days).

## 15.2 Electrodialysis

```
  Dialysis SPEEDED UP by applying an ELECTRIC FIELD across the membrane.
  The electric field pulls the ions through the membrane much faster.

           (-) cathode                      anode (+)
              |                                 |
      +-------|---------------------------------|-------+
      |       |    +------------------+         |       |
      |  <-- Na+   |  SOL in the bag  |     Cl- -->     |
      |       |    +------------------+         |       |
      +-------|---------------------------------|-------+

  NOTE: electrodialysis works only for IONIC (electrolyte) impurities.
  Non-electrolyte impurities are not removed this way.
```

## 15.3 Ultrafiltration

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  ULTRAFILTRATION                                                  |
  |  Separating the colloidal particles from the dissolved substances |
  |  by filtering through a SPECIAL GRADED FILTER - an ULTRAFILTER.   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  Ordinary filter paper has pores that are TOO BIG - the colloid   |
  |  passes straight through.                                         |
  |                                                                   |
  |  MAKING AN ULTRAFILTER PAPER:                                     |
  |     soak ordinary filter paper in a COLLODION solution            |
  |     (4% nitrocellulose in alcohol + ether),                       |
  |     then HARDEN it with FORMALDEHYDE and dry it.                  |
  |     This narrows the pores so colloid cannot pass.                |
  |                                                                   |
  |  Ultrafiltration is SLOW, so it is done under PRESSURE or SUCTION.|
  |  The colloid left on the filter can be stirred back into fresh    |
  |  medium to give a PURE SOL.                                       |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 16.1 Physical and colligative properties

- Sols look **homogeneous** to the eye but are really **heterogeneous**.
- **Colligative properties are very small**, because the number of *particles* is
  tiny (each particle contains thousands of molecules). So the osmotic pressure,
  depression in freezing point etc. are much lower than for a true solution of the
  same mass concentration. **Osmotic pressure** is the one that can still be
  measured usefully — it is used to find the molecular mass of macromolecules.

## 16.2 THE TYNDALL EFFECT (optical property)

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  TYNDALL EFFECT                                                   |
  |  The SCATTERING OF LIGHT by colloidal particles, which makes the  |
  |  path of a beam of light through the sol VISIBLE as a bright      |
  |  luminous CONE (the TYNDALL CONE) when viewed from the side.      |
  |                                                                   |
  |  A TRUE SOLUTION shows NO Tyndall effect - its particles are too  |
  |  small to scatter light. This is the standard TEST to tell a      |
  |  colloid from a true solution.                                    |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
   THE EXPERIMENT - draw this diagram

                          |  eye  (viewing from the SIDE)
                          v
                        \ | /
                          |
         light      +------------------+
         source     |    .  . . .  .   |
        ((( ))) ------>  . TYNDALL  .  |  -----> beam continues
          lamp     |  .   CONE   .  .  |
        + lens     |    .  . . .   .   |
                   +------------------+
                     the SOL in a glass cell

     The colloidal particles SCATTER the light sideways, so an
     observer at 90 degrees SEES the path of the beam.
```

**THE TWO CONDITIONS for the Tyndall effect (this is asked directly):**

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  1. The DIAMETER of the dispersed particles must NOT be MUCH      |
  |     SMALLER than the WAVELENGTH of the light used.                |
  |                                                                   |
  |  2. The REFRACTIVE INDICES of the dispersed phase and the         |
  |     dispersion medium must DIFFER GREATLY in magnitude.           |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Everyday examples of the Tyndall effect:**

```
  - A beam of sunlight entering a dusty room through a small hole
  - The beam of a cinema projector in a smoky hall
  - Headlights of a car in fog
  - The blue colour of the sky (scattering by dust and air molecules)
  - Sunlight through the leaves of a dense forest ("cathedral" beams)
```

**Application:** the **ULTRAMICROSCOPE** uses the Tyndall effect. It does not show
the particles themselves — it shows the **bright specks of light they scatter**
against a dark background, so you can count and follow them.

## 16.3 BROWNIAN MOVEMENT (kinetic property)

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  BROWNIAN MOVEMENT                                                |
  |  The CONTINUOUS, RAPID, ZIG-ZAG (random) motion of colloidal      |
  |  particles, seen under an ultramicroscope.                        |
  |                                                                   |
  |  CAUSE:  the UNBALANCED / UNEQUAL BOMBARDMENT of the colloidal    |
  |  particle by the molecules of the DISPERSION MEDIUM from all      |
  |  sides. At any instant the number of hits on one side is not      |
  |  the same as on the other, so the particle is kicked about.       |
  |                                                                   |
  |  IMPORTANCE:  Brownian motion STOPS THE PARTICLES FROM SETTLING   |
  |  under gravity, and so is one of the reasons a sol is STABLE.     |
  |  It is also direct evidence for the KINETIC MOLECULAR THEORY.     |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
   THE ZIG-ZAG PATH                        THE CAUSE

        o                                     ->    <-
       / \                                  ->  \  /  <-
      /   \___                            ->     (o)    <-
     /        \                             ->  /    \  <-
   o/          \____o                         ->      <-
    \          /                          hits are NOT equal on all
     \____    /                            sides at any instant, so
          \  /                              the particle is PUSHED
           \/                                 in a random direction
            o

  SMALLER particle  ->  FASTER Brownian motion
  LESS VISCOUS medium ->  FASTER Brownian motion
  HIGHER temperature  ->  FASTER Brownian motion
```

## 16.4 Charge on colloidal particles

**All colloidal particles of a given sol carry the SAME sign of charge.**
They therefore repel each other and cannot come together — which is the **main
reason a sol is stable**.

**Where does the charge come from?** From the **preferential (selective) adsorption
of ions** from the dispersion medium. A particle adsorbs the ion that is **common
to its own lattice**.

```
  EXAMPLE - AgCl sol

  If AgNO3 is added to KCl (Ag+ in excess):
      the AgCl particles adsorb Ag+  ->  POSITIVELY charged sol
                                             [AgCl] Ag+

  If KCl is added to AgNO3 (Cl- in excess):
      the AgCl particles adsorb Cl-  ->  NEGATIVELY charged sol
                                             [AgCl] Cl-

  Same substance, opposite charge - it depends on which ion is in excess.
```

| POSITIVELY charged sols | NEGATIVELY charged sols |
|---|---|
| Hydrated metallic oxides: **Fe2O3.xH2O, Al2O3.xH2O, CrO(OH).xH2O** | Metals: **Cu, Ag, Au** sols |
| **Basic dyes**: methylene blue sol | Metal sulphides: **As2S3, CdS** |
| **Haemoglobin** (blood) | **Acid dyes**: eosin, congo red |
| Fe(OH)3 sol, Al(OH)3 sol | **Starch, gum, gelatin, clay, charcoal** sols |

```
  MEMORY:  METAL and METAL SULPHIDE sols are NEGATIVE.
           METAL HYDROXIDE / OXIDE sols are POSITIVE.
           HAEMOGLOBIN is POSITIVE (blood is the odd one out).
```

## 16.5 The electrical double layer and zeta potential

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  HELMHOLTZ ELECTRICAL DOUBLE LAYER                                |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The colloidal particle adsorbs one kind of ion, forming a        |
  |  FIXED LAYER on its surface.                                      |
  |  This fixed layer then attracts ions of OPPOSITE charge from the  |
  |  medium, which form a loose, mobile DIFFUSED LAYER around it.     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The two oppositely charged layers together = ELECTRICAL DOUBLE   |
  |  LAYER.                                                           |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The POTENTIAL DIFFERENCE between the fixed layer and the         |
  |  diffused layer is the ZETA POTENTIAL (electrokinetic potential). |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
              -  -  -  -  -  -  -       <- DIFFUSED layer (mobile,
           -                       -       opposite charge, loose)
        -      + + + + + + + +       -
      -      +                 +      -
     -     +    +-----------+    +     -
     -     +    |  COLLOID  |    +     -   <- FIXED layer (adsorbed ions,
     -     +    |  PARTICLE |    +     -      tightly held)
     -     +    +-----------+    +     -
      -      +                 +      -
        -      + + + + + + + +       -
           -                       -
              -  -  -  -  -  -  -

     potential difference between the two layers = ZETA POTENTIAL
```

## 16.6 ELECTROPHORESIS

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  ELECTROPHORESIS                                                  |
  |  The MOVEMENT OF COLLOIDAL PARTICLES under an applied ELECTRIC    |
  |  FIELD towards the oppositely charged electrode.                  |
  |                                                                   |
  |     POSITIVE sol  ->  moves to the CATHODE   (= CATAPHORESIS)     |
  |     NEGATIVE sol  ->  moves to the ANODE     (= ANAPHORESIS)      |
  |                                                                   |
  |  It PROVES that colloidal particles carry a charge, and it tells  |
  |  you the SIGN of that charge.                                     |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
        (-) CATHODE                            ANODE (+)
            ||                                     ||
      +-----||-------------------------------------||-----+
      |     ||       - - -   <---- moves this way  ||     |
      |     ||     -  SOL  -                       ||     |
      |     ||       - - -                         ||     |
      +-----||-------------------------------------||-----+

      Shown here for a NEGATIVE sol: it moves to the ANODE (+).
      The sol level RISES near that electrode.
```

**ELECTRO-OSMOSIS:** if the colloidal particles are **held stationary** (by a
membrane) and the electric field is applied, the **dispersion medium itself moves**.
This is electro-osmosis.

## 16.7 COAGULATION (precipitation / flocculation)

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  COAGULATION (or FLOCCULATION or PRECIPITATION)                   |
  |  The process of SETTLING DOWN of colloidal particles as a         |
  |  precipitate, when the charge on them is NEUTRALISED.             |
  |                                                                   |
  |  MECHANISM: the added ion of OPPOSITE charge is adsorbed on the   |
  |  particle and neutralises its charge. With no charge left, the    |
  |  particles no longer repel; they collide, grow bigger, become     |
  |  too heavy for Brownian motion, and SETTLE.                       |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Ways to coagulate a sol:**

```
  1. Adding an ELECTROLYTE           <- the main one
  2. ELECTROPHORESIS (the particles reach the electrode, lose their
     charge and settle)
  3. MIXING TWO OPPOSITELY CHARGED SOLS (mutual coagulation)
  4. BOILING (increases collisions, desorbs the adsorbed ions)
  5. PERSISTENT DIALYSIS (removes ALL the electrolyte, so the
     stabilising ions go too and the sol coagulates)
```

## 16.8 THE HARDY-SCHULZE RULE — learn this word for word

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  HARDY-SCHULZE RULE                                               |
  |                                                                   |
  |  (a) The ion that causes coagulation is the one carrying the      |
  |      charge OPPOSITE to that of the colloidal particle.           |
  |                                                                   |
  |  (b) The GREATER THE VALENCY (charge) of that oppositely          |
  |      charged ion, the GREATER is its COAGULATING POWER.           |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  FOR A NEGATIVE SOL  (e.g. As2S3)  ->  CATIONS coagulate it:

       Al3+   >   Ba2+   >   Na+
       (best)              (weakest)

  FOR A POSITIVE SOL  (e.g. Fe(OH)3)  ->  ANIONS coagulate it:

       [Fe(CN)6]4-  >  PO4^3-  >  SO4^2-  >  Cl-
       (best)                                (weakest)
```

> **TRAP:** Students look at the ion with the biggest charge *of any sign*.
> **WRONG.** First decide the sign of the SOL, then look at the ion of the
> **opposite** sign only. For a negative sol, comparing AlCl3 and Na3PO4,
> it is the **Al3+** that matters in AlCl3 — the Cl- is irrelevant.

**COAGULATION VALUE (flocculation value):**

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  COAGULATION VALUE  =  the MINIMUM CONCENTRATION of an            |
  |  electrolyte, in MILLIMOLES PER LITRE, required to cause the      |
  |  coagulation of a sol in TWO HOURS.                               |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                        1                          |
  |     COAGULATING POWER   is proportional to  --------------------  |
  |                                        coagulation value          |
  |                                                                   |
  |  So a SMALL coagulation value means a STRONG coagulating agent.   |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 16.9 PROTECTION AND THE GOLD NUMBER

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  PROTECTIVE COLLOID                                               |
  |  A LYOPHILIC sol added to a LYOPHOBIC sol to make it more stable  |
  |  against coagulation by electrolytes.                             |
  |  The lyophilic particles form a protective coat around the        |
  |  lyophobic particles.                                             |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  GOLD NUMBER   (Zsigmondy)                                        |
  |  The MINIMUM MASS (in MILLIGRAMS) of a protective (lyophilic)     |
  |  colloid which, when added to 10 mL of a standard GOLD SOL, is    |
  |  JUST SUFFICIENT TO PREVENT the colour change from RED to BLUE    |
  |  on adding 1 mL of 10% NaCl solution.                             |
  |                                                                   |
  |  SMALLER GOLD NUMBER  ->  BETTER PROTECTIVE POWER.                |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                        1                          |
  |     PROTECTIVE POWER  proportional to  -----------                |
  |                                        gold number                |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

| Protective colloid | Gold number (approx.) | Protective power |
|---|---|---|
| **Gelatin** | 0.005 – 0.01 | **BEST** |
| Haemoglobin | 0.03 – 0.07 | very good |
| Egg albumin | 0.08 – 0.10 | good |
| Gum arabic | 0.15 – 0.25 | moderate |
| Sodium oleate | 0.4 – 1.0 | fair |
| Potato starch | ~25 | **WORST** |

> **TRAP:** "Which is the best protective colloid?" — the answer is the one with the
> **LOWEST** gold number, i.e. **gelatin**. Students pick the highest number. Remember:
> *"The best bodyguard needs the fewest men."*

### WORKED EXAMPLE 8

**Q.** Which of NaCl, BaCl2 and AlCl3 will coagulate an **As2S3** sol most effectively,
and why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Step 1  Identify the charge on the sol.
          As2S3 is a METAL SULPHIDE sol  ->  NEGATIVELY charged.

  Step 2  A negative sol is coagulated by the POSITIVE ion (cation).
          The cations available are:   Na+ ,  Ba2+ ,  Al3+

  Step 3  Apply the HARDY-SCHULZE RULE: the higher the charge on the
          oppositely charged ion, the greater the coagulating power.

              Al3+  (charge 3)  >  Ba2+ (charge 2)  >  Na+ (charge 1)

  ANSWER:  AlCl3 is the most effective, because it supplies Al3+ which
           has the highest positive charge. Order of coagulating power:
                AlCl3  >  BaCl2  >  NaCl
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 9

**Q.** The gold numbers of four colloids are: A = 0.005, B = 0.15, C = 25, D = 0.09.
Arrange them in order of decreasing protective power.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Protective power is INVERSELY proportional to gold number.
  So the SMALLEST gold number has the GREATEST protective power.

  Gold numbers in increasing order:
        A (0.005)  <  D (0.09)  <  B (0.15)  <  C (25)

  Therefore protective power in DECREASING order:

        A   >   D   >   B   >   C
```

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 17 — EMULSIONS
# ===============================================================

## 17.1 Definition and the two types

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  EMULSION                                                         |
  |  A colloidal system in which BOTH the dispersed phase and the     |
  |  dispersion medium are LIQUIDS.                                   |
  |  (a liquid dispersed in another IMMISCIBLE liquid)                |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

| | **TYPE 1: OIL IN WATER (O/W)** | **TYPE 2: WATER IN OIL (W/O)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Dispersed phase** | **Oil** | **Water** |
| **Dispersion medium** | **Water** | **Oil** |
| Also called | Oil-in-water emulsion | Water-in-oil emulsion |
| Conducts electricity? | **Yes** (water is continuous) | **No** |
| Diluted by | **Water** | **Oil** |
| **Examples** | **MILK** (liquid fat in water), vanishing cream, cod liver oil emulsion (medicinal) | **BUTTER**, cold cream, cheese, crude oil from a well |

```
   O/W  (milk)                          W/O  (butter)

   ~ ~ o ~ ~ o ~ ~ o ~ ~               O O ~ O O ~ O O ~ O
   ~ o ~ ~ o ~ ~ o ~ ~ o               O ~ O O ~ O O ~ O O
   ~ ~ o ~ ~ o ~ ~ o ~ ~               O O ~ O O ~ O O ~ O

   o = OIL droplets (dispersed)        ~ = WATER droplets (dispersed)
   ~ = WATER (continuous medium)       O = OIL (continuous medium)
```

> **MEMORY:** in the name, **the FIRST liquid is the DISPERSED phase**.
> "Oil in water" = oil droplets *inside* water. **Milk is O/W. Butter is W/O.**

## 17.2 Emulsifying agents (emulsifiers)

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  An EMULSION of two pure liquids is UNSTABLE - shake oil and      |
  |  water and it separates again in minutes.                         |
  |                                                                   |
  |  An EMULSIFYING AGENT (emulsifier) is a substance added in a      |
  |  small quantity to STABILISE the emulsion. It forms an            |
  |  INTERFACIAL FILM around the droplets and stops them merging.     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  FOR O/W emulsions:  proteins, gums, natural and synthetic        |
  |                      SOAPS and DETERGENTS                         |
  |                      (e.g. CASEIN is the emulsifier in MILK)      |
  |                                                                   |
  |  FOR W/O emulsions:  heavy metal salts of fatty acids,            |
  |                      long-chain alcohols, LAMPBLACK               |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 17.3 THE THREE TESTS TO DISTINGUISH O/W FROM W/O

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  TEST 1 - DILUTION TEST                                           |
  |  An emulsion can be diluted only by its own DISPERSION MEDIUM.    |
  |     Mixes freely with WATER  ->  it is O/W                        |
  |     Mixes freely with OIL    ->  it is W/O                        |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  TEST 2 - DYE TEST                                                |
  |  Add a small amount of an OIL-SOLUBLE dye (e.g. Sudan III).       |
  |     The WHOLE emulsion becomes UNIFORMLY coloured  ->  W/O        |
  |         (because the continuous oil phase takes up the dye)       |
  |     Only the scattered DROPLETS are coloured       ->  O/W        |
  |         (the dye can only get into the oil droplets)              |
  |                                                                   |
  |                                                                   |
  |  TEST 3 - CONDUCTIVITY TEST                                       |
  |  Water conducts electricity; oil does not.                        |
  |     The emulsion CONDUCTS well       ->  O/W (water continuous)   |
  |     The emulsion does NOT conduct    ->  W/O (oil continuous)     |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## 17.4 Demulsification

Breaking an emulsion back into its two liquids is **demulsification**.
Methods: **boiling, freezing, centrifuging, adding an electrolyte,
adding a large amount of the dispersed phase, or heating with an acid**.
Example: churning cream to get butter; separating water from crude oil.

### WORKED EXAMPLE 10

**Q.** A sample of cream is tested. It conducts electricity and can be diluted with
water. Which type of emulsion is it? Name a likely emulsifier.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. It CONDUCTS electricity  ->  the continuous phase must be WATER.
  2. It DILUTES with water    ->  the dispersion medium is WATER.

  Both tests agree:  it is an OIL-IN-WATER (O/W) emulsion
                     (a "vanishing cream" type).

  A suitable emulsifier:  a soap, a detergent, or a protein such as
  casein - substances which stabilise O/W emulsions.

  (A COLD cream, by contrast, is W/O: it would NOT conduct.)
```

# ===============================================================
# TOPIC 18 — APPLICATIONS OF COLLOIDS
# ===============================================================

Seven one-liners. Each may come as a 2-mark question on its own.

## 18.1 Formation of a delta at a river mouth

```
  River water carries NEGATIVELY charged colloidal CLAY particles.
  Sea water contains dissolved ELECTROLYTES (NaCl, MgCl2, CaCl2 ...)
  supplying Na+, Mg2+, Ca2+.

  Where the river meets the sea, these positive ions NEUTRALISE the
  negative charge on the clay particles (Hardy-Schulze rule).
  The clay COAGULATES and settles down, building up land at the mouth
  of the river - a DELTA.
```

## 18.2 The Cottrell smoke precipitator

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  SMOKE is an AEROSOL - solid carbon/dust particles CHARGED and    |
  |  dispersed in air. It must be cleaned before release.             |
  |                                                                   |
  |  In a COTTRELL PRECIPITATOR the smoke is passed through a         |
  |  chamber containing electrodes at a VERY HIGH POTENTIAL           |
  |  (tens of thousands of volts).                                    |
  |                                                                   |
  |  The charged carbon particles reach the oppositely charged        |
  |  electrode, LOSE THEIR CHARGE, become NEUTRAL, and are            |
  |  PRECIPITATED (they settle at the bottom of the chamber).         |
  |                                                                   |
  |  Only clean, hot AIR passes out of the chimney.                   |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
            clean air OUT
                 ^
                 |
        +--------|----------+
        |   ||       ||     |    <- electrodes at very high potential
        |   ||  . .  ||     |
        |   || .  .  ||     |    <- charged particles lose their charge
        |   ||   .    ||    |
        |   ##########      |    <- soot COLLECTS here
        +---------|---------+
                  ^
             smoke IN
```

## 18.3 Purification of drinking water using alum

```
  Natural water carries NEGATIVELY charged colloidal impurities
  (clay, mud, suspended dirt).

  ALUM  [K2SO4.Al2(SO4)3.24H2O]  supplies  Al3+ ions.

  By the HARDY-SCHULZE rule, the trivalent Al3+ neutralises the
  negative charge on the impurities very effectively.
  They COAGULATE and settle down, leaving clear water.
```

## 18.4 Artificial rain

```
  Clouds are colloidal dispersions of water droplets in air, and the
  droplets carry a CHARGE.

  Spraying ELECTRIFIED SAND (or silver iodide, AgI) of the OPPOSITE
  charge over the cloud NEUTRALISES the charge on the droplets.
  They coalesce into bigger and bigger drops until they become too
  heavy to stay suspended, and RAIN falls.
```

## 18.5 Cleansing action of soap

```
  Soap forms MICELLES in water (an associated colloid).

  The HYDROCARBON TAILS are attracted to the OIL/GREASE on the dirty
  cloth and bury themselves in it, while the IONIC -COO- HEADS stay
  pointing outwards into the water.

  The grease is thus surrounded and pulled off the cloth into the
  water as an EMULSION, and is washed away.

        cloth                cloth               water
       #######              #######
       ##oil##   + soap ->  #######      (o) <- grease now sits
       #######              #######           inside a micelle
                                              and floats away
```

## 18.6 Medicines

```
  Colloidal medicines are more EFFECTIVE because their huge surface
  area makes them ABSORBED EASILY by the body.

  ARGYROL  (colloidal silver)          - eye lotion / antiseptic
  COLLOIDAL ANTIMONY                   - used in kala-azar
  MILK OF MAGNESIA (emulsion)          - for stomach acidity
  COLLOIDAL GOLD                       - intramuscular injection
  COLLOIDAL SULPHUR                    - germicide
```

## 18.7 Tanning of leather

```
  Animal HIDE is a colloid of POSITIVELY charged particles.
  TANNIN (or a chromium salt) contains NEGATIVELY charged
  colloidal particles.

  When the hide is soaked in the tanning solution, the two
  OPPOSITELY CHARGED colloids cause MUTUAL COAGULATION.
  The hide becomes HARDER, tougher and non-putrefying - it becomes
  LEATHER.

  Using CHROMIUM salts for this is called CHROME TANNING.
```

## 18.8 A few more worth one line each

```
  PHOTOGRAPHIC PLATES AND FILMS
      an emulsion of colloidal SILVER BROMIDE in GELATIN coated on
      a glass plate or celluloid film.

  RUBBER INDUSTRY
      latex is a NEGATIVELY charged colloid of rubber; it is coagulated
      by adding acid (electrolyte) to get rubber.

  SEWAGE DISPOSAL
      sewage particles are colloidal and carry a charge; ELECTROPHORESIS
      is used to make them migrate to an electrode and settle out,
      giving manure and cleaner water.

  SMOKE SCREENS
      dense colloidal clouds of TiCl4 fumes used in warfare.

  BLUE COLOUR OF THE SKY / SEA
      Tyndall scattering of sunlight by colloidal dust and water
      particles in the atmosphere.

  FOOD ITEMS
      milk, butter, ice cream, fruit jellies and whipped cream are
      all colloids.

  ALLOYS AND STEEL
      many alloys are solid sols; carbon is colloidally dispersed in
      iron in steel.
```

### WORKED EXAMPLE 11

**Q.** Why is a delta formed where a river enters the sea? Explain using
surface chemistry.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. River water carries fine CLAY particles as a COLLOIDAL SOL.
     These clay particles carry a NEGATIVE charge, and it is this
     charge that keeps them apart and suspended.

  2. Sea water is rich in dissolved ELECTROLYTES - NaCl, MgCl2,
     CaCl2 - which give Na+, Mg2+ and Ca2+ ions.

  3. When the river meets the sea, these POSITIVE ions are adsorbed
     on the negatively charged clay particles and NEUTRALISE
     their charge (HARDY-SCHULZE rule - the higher-charged Mg2+ and
     Ca2+ are especially effective).

  4. With no charge left, the particles no longer repel one another.
     They collide, grow bigger and heavier, and COAGULATE.

  5. The coagulated clay SETTLES DOWN at the mouth of the river and,
     over centuries, builds up the triangular deposit of land called
     a DELTA.
```
