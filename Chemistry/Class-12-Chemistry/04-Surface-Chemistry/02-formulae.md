| Formula | When to use |
|---|---|
| `x/m = k P^(1/n)` | Any question giving mass adsorbed, mass of adsorbent and pressure |
| `log(x/m) = log k + (1/n) log P` | When the question mentions a **straight-line graph**, a **slope** or an **intercept** |
| slope = 1/n | To find **n** from a graph |
| intercept = log k | To find **k** — remember to take the **antilog** |
| `x/m = k C^(1/n)` | Adsorption **from solution** instead of from a gas |
| `x/m = aP/(1+bP)` | Only if the question names **Langmuir** |
| `delta G = delta H - T delta S` | To **prove adsorption is exothermic** |
| power ∝ 1/(coagulation value) | Ranking electrolytes by **coagulating power** |
| power ∝ 1/(gold number) | Ranking colloids by **protective power** |

# 1. THE CORE DEFINITIONS — write each in ONE line
# ===============================================================

| Term | One-line definition |
|---|---|
| **Adsorption** | Accumulation of a substance at the **surface** of another; a **surface** phenomenon |
| **Absorption** | Uniform uptake of a substance **throughout the bulk**; a **bulk** phenomenon |
| **Sorption** | Adsorption and absorption occurring **simultaneously** |
| **Adsorbate** | The substance that **gets adsorbed** |
| **Adsorbent** | The surface **on which** adsorption takes place |
| **Desorption** | The **reverse** of adsorption — removal of the adsorbate from the surface |
| **Occlusion** | Adsorption of **gases on the surface of metals** |
| **Physisorption** | Adsorption by **weak van der Waals** forces |
| **Chemisorption** | Adsorption by **chemical bond formation**; also called **activated adsorption** |
| **Adsorption isotherm** | Graph of **x/m against pressure at constant temperature** |
| **Catalyst** | Substance that **alters the rate** of a reaction and is itself **chemically unchanged in mass and composition** |
| **Catalysis** | The phenomenon of altering a reaction rate using a catalyst |
| **Promoter** | Substance that **increases the activity** of a catalyst (e.g. **Mo** with **Fe**) |
| **Catalytic poison** | Substance that **destroys the activity** of a catalyst (e.g. **As** on **Pt**) |
| **Autocatalysis** | When a **product of the reaction** itself acts as the catalyst |
| **Homogeneous catalysis** | Catalyst in the **same phase** as the reactants |
| **Heterogeneous catalysis** | Catalyst in a **different phase** from the reactants |
| **Shape-selective catalysis** | Catalysis depending on the **pore size** of the catalyst and the **size/shape** of the molecules |
| **Zeolite** | **Microporous aluminosilicate** with a 3-D network of SiO4 and AlO4 tetrahedra; a **molecular sieve** |
| **Enzyme** | A **protein** that acts as a **biochemical catalyst** |
| **Colloid** | Heterogeneous system with particles of size **1–1000 nm** |
| **Dispersed phase** | The **particles** in a colloid |
| **Dispersion medium** | The substance **in which** they are dispersed |
| **Lyophilic sol** | **Solvent-loving**; reversible; self-stabilised |
| **Lyophobic sol** | **Solvent-hating**; irreversible; needs a stabiliser |
| **Micelle** | Aggregate of surfactant ions formed **above the CMC and above the Kraft temperature** |
| **CMC** | **Critical Micelle Concentration** — minimum **concentration** for micelle formation |
| **Kraft temperature** | Minimum **temperature** above which micelles form |
| **Peptization** | Converting a **fresh precipitate into a sol** by adding a small amount of electrolyte |
| **Dialysis** | Removing dissolved **ions/small molecules** from a sol through a **membrane** |
| **Electrodialysis** | Dialysis **speeded up by an electric field** |
| **Ultrafiltration** | Filtering a sol through a **collodion-treated (graded) ultrafilter** |
| **Tyndall effect** | **Scattering of light** by colloidal particles, making the beam path visible |
| **Brownian movement** | Continuous **zig-zag motion** of colloidal particles |
| **Electrophoresis** | Movement of colloidal particles under an **electric field** |
| **Electro-osmosis** | Movement of the **dispersion medium** when the particles are held stationary |
| **Coagulation** | Settling of a sol as a precipitate when its **charge is neutralised** |
| **Coagulation value** | Minimum concentration of electrolyte in **millimoles per litre** to coagulate a sol in **2 hours** |
| **Gold number** | Minimum **mass in mg** of a protective colloid that just prevents the **red-to-blue** change of **10 mL gold sol** on adding **1 mL of 10% NaCl** |
| **Protective colloid** | A **lyophilic** sol added to stabilise a **lyophobic** sol |
| **Emulsion** | Colloid in which **both phases are liquids** |
| **Emulsifier** | Substance added to **stabilise** an emulsion |
| **Demulsification** | **Breaking** an emulsion back into its two liquids |

# 2. ADSORPTION vs ABSORPTION vs SORPTION
# ===============================================================

| Feature | ADSORPTION | ABSORPTION |
|---|---|---|
| Where | **Surface only** | **Whole bulk** |
| Concentration | Higher at the surface | **Uniform** throughout |
| Rate | **Fast first**, then equilibrium | **Uniform** rate |
| Heat | **Exothermic** | Usually endothermic |
| Reversible | Generally yes | Not easily |
| Example | Silica gel + water vapour; gas on charcoal | Sponge + water; CaCl2 + water |

```
  SORPTION  =  BOTH at once.   Example: dyes on cotton, H2 on palladium.

  THE CHALK-IN-INK ILLUSTRATION:
      surface turns blue   ->  the DYE is ADSORBED
      inside is white+wet  ->  the WATER is ABSORBED
```

# ===============================================================
# 3. PHYSISORPTION vs CHEMISORPTION  ***MOST ASKED 4 MARKS***
# ===============================================================

| # | Property | PHYSISORPTION | CHEMISORPTION |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Forces | **van der Waals** (weak) | **Chemical bonds** (strong) |
| 2 | Enthalpy of adsorption | **20 – 40 kJ/mol** | **80 – 240 kJ/mol** |
| 3 | Specificity | **Not specific** | **Highly specific** |
| 4 | Reversibility | **Reversible** | **Irreversible** |
| 5 | Effect of temperature | **Decreases** with rise in T (favoured by low T) | **Increases first, then decreases** (favoured by high T) |
| 6 | Effect of surface area | Increases with surface area | Increases with surface area |
| 7 | Layers | **Multimolecular (multilayer)** | **Unimolecular (monolayer)** |
| 8 | Activation energy | **Not** required | **High** — hence "activated adsorption" |
| 9 | Effect of pressure | Increases with P | Increases with P |
| 10 | Depends on | **Ease of liquefaction** (critical temperature) of the gas | **Chemical reactivity** of the gas |
| 11 | Example | H2, N2, O2 on charcoal at low T | H2 on Ni; N2 on Fe in the Haber process |

```
  THE FIVE-WORD MEMORY LINE:

  PHYSISORPTION :  WEAK - LOW HEAT - REVERSIBLE - MULTILAYER - COLD
  CHEMISORPTION :  STRONG - HIGH HEAT - IRREVERSIBLE - MONOLAYER - HOT
```

# ===============================================================
# 4. FACTORS AFFECTING ADSORPTION
# ===============================================================

| Factor | Effect | Reason / note |
|---|---|---|
| **Nature of adsorbate (gas)** | **Easily liquefiable gases adsorb more** | Higher **critical temperature** = stronger van der Waals forces. Order: `SO2 > NH3 > HCl > CH4 > CO > N2 > H2` |
| **Nature of adsorbent** | Charcoal, silica gel, alumina gel, clay, finely divided metals are good | **Activated charcoal** (heated at 650–1300 K in vacuum/steam) is the best |
| **Surface area** | **More surface area → more adsorption** | Use it **finely powdered / porous / activated**; measured as **specific surface area** (area per gram) |
| **Temperature** | Physisorption **falls**; chemisorption **rises then falls** | Adsorption is **exothermic** → Le Chatelier |
| **Pressure** | **More pressure → more adsorption**, until **saturation** | Three regions: `x/m ∝ P` (low), `x/m ∝ P^(1/n)` (medium), `x/m ∝ P^0` (high) |

```
  CRITICAL TEMPERATURE vs AMOUNT ADSORBED ON 1 g CHARCOAL

  +--------+---------------+---------------+
  |  GAS   |  Tc  (K)      |  volume (mL)  |
  +--------+---------------+---------------+
  |  SO2   |    630        |     380       |
  |  NH3   |    406        |     180       |
  |  HCl   |    324        |     165       |
  |  CH4   |    190        |      16       |
  |  CO    |    134        |      10       |
  |  N2    |    126        |       8       |
  |  H2    |     33        |       4.7     |
  +--------+---------------+---------------+

  HIGH Tc  ->  MORE ADSORPTION.  The two columns run in parallel.
```

# ===============================================================
# 5. THE FREUNDLICH ISOTHERM — THE FULL PICTURE
# ===============================================================

```
  THE GRAPHS

  x/m |                                 log |
      |            _____ saturation     x/m |                    o
      |          /                          |                 o
      |        /                            |              o
      |      /                              |           o     slope = 1/n
      |    /                                |        o
      |  /                                  |     o
      | /                            log k  |  o
      |/                                    |o
      +--------------------- P              +--------------------- log P

      THE ISOTHERM                          THE LOG PLOT
      (curved, flattens)                    (straight line)
```

| Question asks for | Read off |
|---|---|
| **n** | slope = 1/n, so **n = 1 / slope** |
| **k** | intercept = log k, so **k = antilog(intercept) = 10^(intercept)** |
| Extent of adsorption | substitute into `x/m = k P^(1/n)` |
| Limitation | **fails at high pressure** — the surface saturates so x/m becomes independent of P, and the experimental curve bends away from the straight line |

```
  VALUES OF 1/n

      1/n = 1        ->  x/m = kP           low pressure region
      0 < 1/n < 1    ->  x/m = kP^(1/n)     the useful middle region
      1/n = 0        ->  x/m = k            high pressure, SATURATED

  LANGMUIR EXPLAINS the saturation Freundlich cannot:
      assumes a MONOLAYER on a fixed number of identical sites,
      no interaction between adsorbed molecules,
      dynamic equilibrium between adsorption and desorption.
```

# 7. CATALYSIS — THE FACT BLOCK
# ===============================================================

## 7.1 What a catalyst does and does not do

```
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  A CATALYST DOES               |  A CATALYST DOES NOT           |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  Lower the ACTIVATION ENERGY   |  Change delta H                |
  |  Give an ALTERNATIVE PATH      |  Change the EQUILIBRIUM        |
  |  Speed forward AND backward    |     CONSTANT K                 |
  |     equally                    |  Change the YIELD              |
  |  Bring equilibrium SOONER      |  Start an impossible reaction  |
  |  Come out chemically unchanged |  Get consumed                  |
  +--------------------------------+--------------------------------+
```

## 7.2 Positive / negative / promoter / poison

| Term | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive catalyst** | **Increases** rate, **lowers** Ea | MnO2 in `2 KClO3 --> 2 KCl + 3 O2` |
| **Negative catalyst (inhibitor)** | **Decreases** rate, **raises** Ea | **Glycerol / acetanilide** slows `2 H2O2 --> 2 H2O + O2`; **TEL** in petrol; **alcohol** slows oxidation of chloroform |
| **Promoter (activator)** | **Increases the activity of the catalyst**; not itself a catalyst | **Mo** (also **Al2O3, K2O**) with **Fe** in the Haber process |
| **Catalytic poison** | **Destroys the activity of the catalyst** (blocks the active sites) | **As2O3** poisons **Pt** in the Contact process; **CO** poisons **Fe** in the Haber process; **S compounds** poison **Ni** |
| **Autocatalysis** | A **product** catalyses the reaction | **Mn2+** in the KMnO4–oxalic acid titration |

## 7.3 Homogeneous vs heterogeneous

| | HOMOGENEOUS | HETEROGENEOUS |
|---|---|---|
| Phases | Catalyst in the **same phase** as reactants | Catalyst in a **different phase** |
| Also called | — | **Contact / surface catalysis** |
| Ex 1 | `2 SO2(g) + O2(g) --(NO g)--> 2 SO3(g)` — lead chamber | `N2 + 3 H2 --(Fe s)--> 2 NH3` — Haber |
| Ex 2 | `CH3COOC2H5 + H2O --(H2SO4)--> acid + alcohol` | `2 SO2 + O2 --(V2O5 s)--> 2 SO3` — Contact |
| Ex 3 | `Cane sugar + H2O --(H+)--> glucose + fructose` | `4 NH3 + 5 O2 --(Pt s)--> 4 NO + 6 H2O` — Ostwald |
| Ex 4 | — | `Oil(l) + H2(g) --(Ni s)--> vanaspati(s)` |
| Separation | **Hard** to separate | **Easy** to separate (just filter) |

## 7.5 The five steps of the adsorption theory

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |   1.  DIFFUSION of reactants TO the catalyst surface              |
  |   2.  ADSORPTION of reactant molecules ON the surface             |
  |   3.  REACTION on the surface via an activated intermediate       |
  |   4.  DESORPTION of the products from the surface                 |
  |   5.  DIFFUSION of the products AWAY from the surface             |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  MEMORY:  D - A - R - D - D
```

```
  ENERGY DIAGRAM

  E |         /\
    |        /  \             <-- WITHOUT catalyst, LARGE Ea
    |       /    \
    |      /  /\  \
    |     /  /  \  \          <-- WITH catalyst, SMALL Ea
    |    /  /    \  \
    |   / /        \ \
    |  //            \\
  --|-/                \\_______________
    | REACTANTS            PRODUCTS
    |
    | delta H is THE SAME on both paths
    +---------------------------------------
              reaction progress
```

## 7.6 Zeolites

```
  ZEOLITE   =  microporous ALUMINOSILICATE
               3-D network of SiO4 and AlO4 tetrahedra joined by
               oxygen atoms, with cavities of a definite size
               General formula:  Mx/n [(AlO2)x (SiO2)y] . m H2O
               Called MOLECULAR SIEVES.
               Heated in vacuum before use to remove water of hydration.

  ZSM-5     =  the industrial zeolite that converts
               ALCOHOLS  ->  GASOLINE (PETROL)   by DEHYDRATION.

## 7.7 Enzymes

```
  CHARACTERISTICS OF ENZYME CATALYSIS  (the 4-mark answer)

# 8. COLLOIDS — THE SIZE TABLE
# ===============================================================

```
  TRUE SOLUTION   <  1 nm
  COLLOID         1 nm  to  1000 nm
  SUSPENSION      >  1000 nm  (1 micrometre)
```

| Property | TRUE SOLUTION | COLLOID | SUSPENSION |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size | < 1 nm | **1–1000 nm** | > 1000 nm |
| Nature | Homogeneous | **Heterogeneous** | Heterogeneous |
| Visible to eye | No | No | **Yes** |

| Passes filter paper | Yes | **YES** | **No** |

| Example | Sugar in water | Milk, blood, smoke | Sand in water |

> **The single distinguishing line:** a colloid **passes filter paper but NOT
> parchment**, and it **shows the Tyndall effect** while a true solution does not.

# ===============================================================
# 10. LYOPHILIC vs LYOPHOBIC  ***2nd MOST ASKED 4 MARKS***
# ===============================================================

| # | Property | LYOPHILIC | LYOPHOBIC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Affinity for medium | **Strong** | **None** |
| 2 | Preparation | **Direct** — just mix | **Special methods** needed |
| 3 | Reversibility | **REVERSIBLE** | **IRREVERSIBLE** |
| 4 | Stability | **Very stable**, self-stabilised | **Unstable**, needs a stabiliser |
| 5 | Effect of electrolyte | Coagulated only by a **large** amount | Coagulated by a **small** amount |
| 6 | Solvation | Particles are **heavily solvated** | **Not solvated** |
| 7 | Viscosity | **Much higher** than the medium | **Same** as the medium |
| 8 | Surface tension | **Lower** than the medium | **Same** as the medium |
| 9 | Tyndall effect | **Weak** | **Strong** |
| 10 | Migration in field | May go either way or not at all | **One electrode only** |
| 11 | Other names | **Intrinsic / emulsoid** | **Extrinsic / suspensoid** |
| 12 | Examples | **Gum, gelatin, starch, proteins, rubber, agar** | **Au/Ag/Cu sols, Fe(OH)3, Al(OH)3, As2S3** |

## 10.1 Classification by particle nature

| Type | What it is | Held by | Character | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Multimolecular** | Aggregate of **many small atoms/molecules** | **van der Waals** forces | Usually **lyophobic** | **Gold sol** (many Au atoms), **sulphur sol** (~1000 S8 molecules) |
| **Macromolecular** | **One big molecule** is already colloidal | Covalent bonds within the molecule | **Lyophilic**, very stable | Natural: **starch, cellulose, proteins, gelatin, rubber**. Synthetic: **polythene, nylon, polystyrene** |
| **Associated (micelles)** | **Electrolyte at low conc., colloid at high conc.** | Aggregation above CMC | Surfactants | **Soaps** (sodium stearate), **detergents** (sodium dodecyl sulphate) |

```
  MICELLE CONDITIONS - BOTH must be satisfied:

      concentration  >  CMC (critical micelle concentration)
                         for soaps: about 10^-4 to 10^-3 mol/L
      temperature    >  Tk  (Kraft temperature)

  A micelle contains 100 or more molecules.

  A SOAP MOLECULE is AMPHIPATHIC:
      C17H35 -----------------  COO- Na+
      long HYDROPHOBIC tail    polar HYDROPHILIC head
      (oil-loving)             (water-loving)

  In WATER:  heads OUT, tails IN.
  In OIL:    tails OUT, heads IN  (a REVERSE micelle).
```

# ===============================================================
# 11. PREPARATION AND PURIFICATION OF COLLOIDS
# ===============================================================

## 11.1 Preparation

| Method | Type | Details / reaction |
|---|---|---|
| **Double decomposition** | Chemical (condensation) | `As2O3 + 3 H2S --> As2S3 sol + 3 H2O` |
| **Oxidation** | Chemical | `SO2 + 2 H2S --> 3 S sol + 2 H2O` |
| **Reduction** | Chemical | `2 AuCl3 + 3 SnCl2 --> 2 Au sol + 3 SnCl4` |
| **Hydrolysis** | Chemical | `FeCl3 + 3 H2O --(boil)--> Fe(OH)3 sol + 3 HCl` |
| **Bredig's arc** | Electrical disintegration (**both dispersion AND condensation**) | Arc struck between **metal rods under water containing a trace of NaOH**, in an **ice bath**. Used for **Au, Ag, Pt, Cu** sols |
| **Peptization** | Precipitate → sol | Fresh precipitate + **small amount of electrolyte (peptizing agent)**. Fe(OH)3 precipitate + **FeCl3** → Fe(OH)3 sol; the Fe3+ is adsorbed and the particles repel each other |

## 11.2 Purification

| Method | Principle | Removes | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dialysis** | Ions diffuse through a **parchment / cellophane** membrane; colloid cannot | Electrolytes and small molecules | **Very slow** |
| **Electrodialysis** | Dialysis + an **electric field** to drag the ions across faster | **Ionic** impurities only | Much faster |
| **Ultrafiltration** | Filter through **collodion-treated graded ultrafilter paper** | Everything dissolved | Collodion = **4% nitrocellulose in alcohol + ether**, hardened with **formaldehyde**; done under **pressure/suction** |

> **Why purify at all?** A *small* amount of electrolyte **stabilises** a sol, but
> **too much coagulates** it. Even dialysis must not be continued indefinitely, or
> the sol will coagulate.

# ===============================================================
# 12. PROPERTIES OF COLLOIDS
# ===============================================================

| Property | One-line statement | Key extra fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Tyndall effect** | Scattering of light making the beam path visible as a **cone** | **Conditions:** (a) particle diameter not much smaller than the **wavelength** of light; (b) **refractive indices** of phase and medium differ greatly. Basis of the **ultramicroscope** |
| **Brownian movement** | Continuous **zig-zag** motion of colloidal particles | **Cause: unequal bombardment** by the molecules of the **dispersion medium**. It **prevents settling** → gives **stability** |
| **Charge** | All particles of one sol carry the **same charge** | Due to **preferential adsorption of ions** from the medium; the charge is the **main cause of stability** |
| **Electrical double layer** | **Fixed layer** of adsorbed ions + **diffused layer** of opposite ions | Potential difference between them = **ZETA POTENTIAL** |
| **Electrophoresis** | Particles move to the **oppositely charged electrode** | Positive sol → **cathode** (cataphoresis); negative sol → **anode** |
| **Electro-osmosis** | If the particles are **held still**, the **medium** moves instead | — |
| **Colligative properties** | **Very small**, because the number of particles is small | **Osmotic pressure** is used to find the molar mass of macromolecules |
| **Coagulation** | Settling of the sol when its **charge is neutralised** | See the Hardy-Schulze rule below |

## 12.1 Charge on common sols

| POSITIVE sols | NEGATIVE sols |
|---|---|
| Fe(OH)3, Al(OH)3, Cr(OH)3 | Metal sols: **Au, Ag, Cu, Pt** |
| Hydrated oxides: Fe2O3·xH2O, Al2O3·xH2O | Metal sulphides: **As2S3, CdS, Sb2S3** |
| **Basic dyes**: methylene blue | **Acid dyes**: eosin, congo red |
| **Haemoglobin** (blood) | **Starch, gum, gelatin, clay, charcoal** |

```
  MEMORY:  HYDROXIDES / OXIDES  ->  POSITIVE
           METALS / SULPHIDES   ->  NEGATIVE
           HAEMOGLOBIN          ->  POSITIVE  (the odd one out)
```

## 12.2 Methods of coagulation

```
  1. Adding an ELECTROLYTE                 (the main method)
  2. ELECTROPHORESIS
  3. MIXING TWO OPPOSITELY CHARGED SOLS    (mutual coagulation)
  4. BOILING
  5. PERSISTENT / PROLONGED DIALYSIS
```

## 12.3 HARDY-SCHULZE RULE

```
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                   |
  |  (a) The coagulating ion is the one with the charge OPPOSITE to   |
  |      that of the sol.                                             |
  |  (b) The HIGHER the CHARGE (valency) of that ion, the GREATER     |
  |      its COAGULATING POWER.                                       |
  |                                                                   |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------------+

  NEGATIVE sol (As2S3)  ->  use CATIONS:
        Al3+   >   Ba2+   >   Na+

  POSITIVE sol (Fe(OH)3) ->  use ANIONS:
        [Fe(CN)6]4-  >  PO4^3-  >  SO4^2-  >  Cl-

                                                1
  COAGULATING POWER   is proportional to  -------------------
                                          coagulation value

  COAGULATION VALUE = minimum concentration in MILLIMOLES PER LITRE
  needed to coagulate the sol in TWO HOURS.
  SMALL coagulation value  ->  STRONG coagulating agent.
```

## 12.4 Gold number

```
  GOLD NUMBER = the minimum MASS in MILLIGRAMS of a protective
  colloid which, added to 10 mL of a standard GOLD SOL, JUST PREVENTS
  the colour change from RED to BLUE when 1 mL of 10% NaCl is added.

                                          1
  PROTECTIVE POWER  is proportional to  -----------
                                        gold number

  SMALL gold number  =  STRONG protective power.
```

| Protective colloid | Gold number | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| **Gelatin** | **0.005 – 0.01** | **BEST** |
| Haemoglobin | 0.03 – 0.07 | 2nd |
| Egg albumin | 0.08 – 0.10 | 3rd |
| Gum arabic | 0.15 – 0.25 | 4th |
| Sodium oleate | 0.4 – 1.0 | 5th |
| **Potato starch** | **~25** | **WORST** |

# ===============================================================
# 13. EMULSIONS
# ===============================================================

| | **O/W (oil in water)** | **W/O (water in oil)** |
|---|---|---|
| Dispersed phase | **Oil** | **Water** |
| Dispersion medium | **Water** | **Oil** |
| Conductivity | **Conducts** | **Does not conduct** |
| Diluted by | **Water** | **Oil** |
| Dye test (oil-soluble dye) | Only the **droplets** coloured | **Whole** emulsion coloured |
| Emulsifiers | Soaps, detergents, proteins, gums (**casein in milk**) | Heavy-metal salts of fatty acids, long-chain alcohols, **lampblack** |
| **Examples** | **MILK**, vanishing cream, cod-liver oil emulsion | **BUTTER**, cold cream, cheese, crude oil |

```
  THE THREE TESTS

  DILUTION      mixes with WATER   -> O/W        mixes with OIL -> W/O
  DYE           droplets coloured  -> O/W        all coloured   -> W/O
  CONDUCTIVITY  conducts           -> O/W        no conduction  -> W/O

  RULE OF THE NAME: the FIRST liquid named is the DISPERSED phase.

  DEMULSIFICATION = breaking an emulsion:
      boiling, freezing, centrifuging, adding an electrolyte,
      adding excess dispersed phase, heating with acid.
```

# ===============================================================
# 14. APPLICATIONS OF COLLOIDS
# ===============================================================

| Application | Principle involved |
|---|---|
| **Delta formation** | Negative **clay** sol coagulated by the **Na+, Mg2+, Ca2+** of sea water (Hardy-Schulze) |
| **Cottrell smoke precipitator** | Charged smoke particles **lose their charge at a high-potential electrode** and settle |
| **Purification of drinking water (alum)** | **Al3+** neutralises the negatively charged colloidal impurities → coagulation |
| **Artificial rain** | **Electrified sand / AgI** of opposite charge neutralises the charged cloud droplets → coalescence → rain |
| **Cleansing action of soap** | **Micelle formation**: tails dissolve the grease, ionic heads keep it in water |
| **Medicines** | **Argyrol** (colloidal silver, eye lotion), **colloidal antimony** (kala-azar), **milk of magnesia**, colloidal gold, colloidal sulphur |
| **Tanning of leather** | Positive **hide** colloid + negative **tannin/chromium** colloid → **mutual coagulation** → leather (chrome tanning) |
| **Photographic plates** | Colloidal **AgBr in gelatin** |
| **Rubber industry** | Negative **latex** coagulated by acid |
| **Sewage disposal** | **Electrophoresis** makes the charged sewage particles settle |
| **Blue colour of the sky** | **Tyndall scattering** by colloidal dust particles |
