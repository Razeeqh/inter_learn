## Topic: Adsorption basics

### Q A1
What is adsorption? How does it differ from absorption?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ADSORPTION - the accumulation of a substance at the SURFACE of
  another substance, so that the concentration at the surface is
  higher than in the bulk. It is a SURFACE phenomenon.

  ABSORPTION - the substance is taken up UNIFORMLY THROUGHOUT the
  whole bulk of the other substance. It is a BULK phenomenon.

  Example:  water vapour on silica gel  ->  ADSORPTION
            water in a sponge           ->  ABSORPTION
```

---

### Q A2
What is sorption? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SORPTION - when ADSORPTION and ABSORPTION take place at the
  SAME TIME in the same system.

  Example:  a dye on cotton fibre; hydrogen gas on palladium.
```

---

### Q A3
Define the terms adsorbate and adsorbent with an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ADSORBATE - the substance that GETS ADSORBED on the surface.
  ADSORBENT - the substance ON WHOSE SURFACE the adsorption occurs.

  Example:  ammonia gas adsorbed on charcoal
                ADSORBATE = NH3          ADSORBENT = charcoal
```

---

### Q A4
Why is adsorption always exothermic?

**ANSWER:**
```
  When a gas molecule is adsorbed it loses its freedom of movement,
  so the randomness FALLS and delta S is NEGATIVE.

  Adsorption is spontaneous, so delta G must be NEGATIVE.

       delta G  =  delta H  -  T delta S

  With delta S negative, the term (- T delta S) is POSITIVE.
  For delta G to still be negative, delta H must be NEGATIVE.

  A negative delta H means HEAT IS RELEASED  ->  EXOTHERMIC.
```

---

### Q A5
What is desorption?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DESORPTION is the REVERSE of adsorption - the removal of the
  adsorbed substance (adsorbate) from the surface of the adsorbent.

  It is brought about by HEATING or by LOWERING THE PRESSURE.
```

## Topic: Physisorption and chemisorption

### Q A6
What is physisorption? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PHYSISORPTION (physical adsorption) is adsorption in which the
  adsorbate is held on the surface of the adsorbent by WEAK
  VAN DER WAALS FORCES only. No chemical bond is formed.

  It is reversible, non-specific, forms MULTILAYERS, has a low
  enthalpy of adsorption (20 - 40 kJ/mol) and is favoured by
  LOW TEMPERATURE.

  Example: H2, N2 or O2 adsorbed on charcoal at low temperature.
```

---

### Q A7
What is chemisorption? Why is it called activated adsorption?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CHEMISORPTION is adsorption in which the adsorbate is held on the
  surface by CHEMICAL BONDS (a surface compound is formed).
  Enthalpy of adsorption is high (80 - 240 kJ/mol), it is highly
  specific, irreversible and forms only a MONOLAYER.

  It is called ACTIVATED ADSORPTION because a chemical bond must be
  formed, which requires an ACTIVATION ENERGY to be supplied,
  usually by heating.

  Example: H2 chemisorbed on nickel; N2 on iron in the Haber process.
```

---

### Q A8
Give any two differences between physisorption and chemisorption.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. FORCES
     Physisorption - weak VAN DER WAALS forces.
     Chemisorption - strong CHEMICAL BONDS.

  2. ENTHALPY OF ADSORPTION
     Physisorption -  20 to  40 kJ/mol  (low)
     Chemisorption -  80 to 240 kJ/mol  (high)

  (Any two accepted: also reversibility, specificity, number of
   layers, activation energy, effect of temperature.)
```

---

### Q A9
How does temperature affect physisorption and chemisorption?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PHYSISORPTION  -  DECREASES with rise in temperature.
      Adsorption is exothermic; by Le Chatelier's principle heating
      pushes the equilibrium backward. Favoured by LOW temperature.

  CHEMISORPTION  -  first INCREASES with temperature, then DECREASES.
      Heating first supplies the ACTIVATION ENERGY needed to form the
      bond, so adsorption rises; at higher temperature the exothermic
      equilibrium shifts backward and it falls again.
```

## Topic: Factors and isotherms

### Q A10
Which gas is adsorbed to a greater extent on charcoal: H2 or SO2? Give the reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SO2 is adsorbed to a much greater extent.

  REASON: SO2 is far more EASILY LIQUEFIABLE than H2. Its critical
  temperature is 630 K compared with only 33 K for H2.
  Easily liquefiable gases have STRONGER van der Waals forces, and
  physisorption depends on exactly those forces.

  On 1 g of charcoal:  SO2 about 380 mL ,  H2 only about 4.7 mL.
```

---

### Q A11
Write the Freundlich adsorption isotherm and explain the terms.

**ANSWER:**
```
                 x
                ---   =   k  P^(1/n)             ( n > 1 )
                 m

     x   =  mass of the gas adsorbed
     m   =  mass of the adsorbent
     x/m =  extent of adsorption
     P   =  equilibrium pressure of the gas
     k, n=  constants for a given adsorbate-adsorbent pair at a
            given temperature

  The value of 1/n lies between 0 and 1.
```

---

### Q A12
Write the logarithmic form of the Freundlich isotherm. What do the slope
and the intercept of the graph represent?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Taking log on both sides of  x/m = k P^(1/n) :

              x                          1
         log ---   =   log k    +       ---  log P
              m                          n

  A plot of log(x/m) against log P is a STRAIGHT LINE.

         SLOPE      =   1 / n
         INTERCEPT  =   log k     (so k = antilog of the intercept)
```

---

### Q A13
State one limitation of the Freundlich adsorption isotherm.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The Freundlich isotherm is only an EMPIRICAL relation and is
  valid over a LIMITED RANGE OF PRESSURE.

  At HIGH PRESSURE the surface becomes SATURATED, so x/m becomes
  INDEPENDENT of pressure (1/n tends to 0). The Freundlich equation
  still predicts that x/m goes on increasing, so the experimental
  graph DEVIATES from the straight line and the equation FAILS at
  high pressure.
```

## Topic: Catalysis

### Q A14
What is a catalyst? What is catalysis?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CATALYST - a substance which ALTERS THE RATE of a chemical reaction
  and itself remains CHEMICALLY UNCHANGED IN MASS AND COMPOSITION
  at the end of the reaction.

  CATALYSIS - the phenomenon of altering the rate of a reaction by
  using a catalyst.

  Example: MnO2 in  2 KClO3 --(MnO2, heat)--> 2 KCl + 3 O2
```

---

### Q A15
What is a promoter and what is a catalytic poison? Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PROMOTER - a substance which is not itself a catalyst but which
  INCREASES THE ACTIVITY of a catalyst.
     Example: MOLYBDENUM (Mo) is a promoter for the IRON catalyst
              in the HABER process.

  CATALYTIC POISON - a substance which DESTROYS OR REDUCES THE
  ACTIVITY of a catalyst by getting strongly adsorbed on its
  active sites and blocking them.
     Example: ARSENIC (As2O3) poisons the PLATINUM catalyst in the
              CONTACT process.  CO poisons Fe in the Haber process.
```

---

### Q A16
Give one example each of homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysis.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HOMOGENEOUS (catalyst in the SAME phase as the reactants):

        2 SO2(g)  +  O2(g)  --(NO gas)-->  2 SO3(g)
        (lead chamber process - everything is a gas)

  HETEROGENEOUS (catalyst in a DIFFERENT phase):

        N2(g)  +  3 H2(g)  --(Fe solid)-->  2 NH3(g)
        (Haber process - solid catalyst, gaseous reactants)
```

### Q A17
What is shape-selective catalysis? Name the catalyst used.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SHAPE-SELECTIVE CATALYSIS is catalysis in which the reaction
  depends upon the PORE SIZE of the catalyst and the SIZE AND SHAPE
  of the reactant and product molecules.

  Only molecules small enough to enter the cavities can react.

  The catalysts used are ZEOLITES - microporous aluminosilicates,
  also called MOLECULAR SIEVES.
```

  ZSM-5 is a ZEOLITE catalyst used in shape-selective catalysis.

  USE: it converts ALCOHOLS DIRECTLY INTO GASOLINE (PETROL) by

        alcohols  --(ZSM-5)-->  hydrocarbons (gasoline)  +  H2O

Name the catalyst and the promoter used in the Haber process.

**ANSWER:**

        N2(g)  +  3 H2(g)  <-->  2 NH3(g)

## Topic: Colloids

### Q A21
What is a colloid? Give the range of particle size.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A COLLOID is a HETEROGENEOUS system in which one substance
  (the DISPERSED PHASE) is dispersed as particles of size
  1 nm to 1000 nm in another substance (the DISPERSION MEDIUM).

  Size:  TRUE SOLUTION  < 1 nm
         COLLOID        1 nm to 1000 nm
         SUSPENSION     > 1000 nm
```

---

### Q A22
Define dispersed phase and dispersion medium with an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DISPERSED PHASE   -  the substance present as the tiny colloidal
                       PARTICLES (like the solute).
  DISPERSION MEDIUM -  the substance in which the particles are
                       spread out (like the solvent).

  Example - MILK:   dispersed phase   = liquid FAT
                    dispersion medium = WATER

  Example - SMOKE:  dispersed phase   = SOLID carbon particles
                    dispersion medium = AIR
```

---

### Q A23
What is an aerosol? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An AEROSOL is a colloidal system in which the DISPERSION MEDIUM
  is a GAS.

  Examples:  SMOKE  (solid carbon dispersed in air)
             FOG / MIST / CLOUD  (liquid water dispersed in air)
             insecticide sprays
```

---

### Q A24
Distinguish between lyophilic and lyophobic colloids (any two points).

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. AFFINITY
     Lyophilic - the dispersed phase has a STRONG affinity for the
                 dispersion medium (solvent-loving).
     Lyophobic - it has NO affinity for the medium (solvent-hating).

  2. REVERSIBILITY AND STABILITY
     Lyophilic - REVERSIBLE and self-stabilised; can be reformed
                 by simply adding the medium back.
     Lyophobic - IRREVERSIBLE and unstable; needs a stabilising agent.

  Examples: lyophilic - gum, gelatin, starch
            lyophobic - gold sol, As2S3 sol, Fe(OH)3 sol
```

---

### Q A25
What are associated colloids? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ASSOCIATED COLLOIDS are substances which behave as NORMAL STRONG
  ELECTROLYTES at LOW concentration, but at HIGHER concentration
  their ions ASSOCIATE together into aggregates of colloidal size
  called MICELLES.

  Examples:  SOAPS (sodium stearate, C17H35COONa)
             DETERGENTS (sodium dodecyl sulphate)
```

---

### Q A26
What is the critical micelle concentration (CMC) and the Kraft temperature?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CRITICAL MICELLE CONCENTRATION (CMC) - the MINIMUM CONCENTRATION
  above which micelles begin to form.
  For soaps in water, CMC is about 10^-4 to 10^-3 mol/L.

  KRAFT TEMPERATURE (Tk) - the MINIMUM TEMPERATURE above which
  micelles are formed.

  Micelles form only when BOTH conditions are satisfied:
      concentration > CMC   AND   temperature > Tk
```

---

### Q A27
What is peptization?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PEPTIZATION is the process of converting a FRESHLY PREPARED
  PRECIPITATE into a COLLOIDAL SOL by shaking it with the dispersion
  medium in the presence of a SMALL AMOUNT OF ELECTROLYTE.

  The electrolyte used is called the PEPTIZING AGENT.

  Example: a fresh precipitate of Fe(OH)3 shaken with a little FeCl3
  adsorbs Fe3+ ions, becomes positively charged, and breaks up into
  a Fe(OH)3 SOL.
```

---

### Q A28
What is dialysis? Why is it done?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIALYSIS is the process of removing dissolved substances (ions and
  small molecules) from a colloidal solution by DIFFUSION THROUGH A
  SUITABLE MEMBRANE such as parchment paper or cellophane.

  The ions pass through the membrane; the large colloidal particles
  cannot, so they stay behind.

  WHY: a freshly prepared sol contains EXCESS ELECTROLYTE, and too
  much electrolyte would COAGULATE the sol. The excess must be removed.
```

---

### Q A29
What is the Tyndall effect? What are the conditions for it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  TYNDALL EFFECT - the SCATTERING OF LIGHT by colloidal particles,
  which makes the path of a light beam through the sol visible as a
  bright luminous CONE when viewed from the side.

  CONDITIONS:
    1. The DIAMETER of the dispersed particles must NOT be much
       SMALLER than the WAVELENGTH of the light used.
    2. The REFRACTIVE INDICES of the dispersed phase and the
       dispersion medium must DIFFER GREATLY.

  A true solution does NOT show it - so it is the test for a colloid.
```

---

### Q A30
What is Brownian movement? What causes it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BROWNIAN MOVEMENT is the continuous, rapid, ZIG-ZAG (random)
  motion of colloidal particles, observed under an ultramicroscope.

  CAUSE: the UNBALANCED / UNEQUAL BOMBARDMENT of the colloidal
  particle by the molecules of the DISPERSION MEDIUM. At any instant
  the number of collisions is not the same on all sides, so the
  particle is pushed about randomly.

  IMPORTANCE: it prevents the particles from settling under gravity,
  and so helps to STABILISE the sol.
```

---

### Q A31
What is electrophoresis?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTROPHORESIS is the MOVEMENT OF COLLOIDAL PARTICLES under an
  applied ELECTRIC FIELD towards the oppositely charged electrode.

      POSITIVELY charged sol  ->  moves to the CATHODE (cataphoresis)
      NEGATIVELY charged sol  ->  moves to the ANODE

  It proves that colloidal particles carry a charge and tells us
  the SIGN of that charge.

  (If the particles are held stationary, the MEDIUM moves instead -
   this is ELECTRO-OSMOSIS.)
```

---

### Q A32
State the Hardy-Schulze rule.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HARDY-SCHULZE RULE:

  (a) The ion that causes coagulation of a sol is the one carrying
      the charge OPPOSITE to that of the colloidal particles.

  (b) The GREATER THE VALENCY (charge) of that oppositely charged
      ion, the GREATER is its COAGULATING POWER.

  For a NEGATIVE sol :   Al3+  >  Ba2+  >  Na+
  For a POSITIVE sol :   [Fe(CN)6]4- > PO4^3- > SO4^2- > Cl-
```

### Q A33
Define coagulation value.

**ANSWER:**
```
  COAGULATION VALUE (flocculation value) is the MINIMUM
  CONCENTRATION of an electrolyte, expressed in MILLIMOLES PER LITRE,
  required to cause the COAGULATION of a sol in TWO HOURS.

                                            1
     Coagulating power  is proportional to -------------------
                                           coagulation value

  A SMALL coagulation value means a STRONG coagulating agent.
```

### Q A34
What is the gold number? What does a small gold number indicate?

**ANSWER:**
```
  GOLD NUMBER is the MINIMUM MASS (in MILLIGRAMS) of a protective
  (lyophilic) colloid which, when added to 10 mL of a standard
  GOLD SOL, is JUST SUFFICIENT TO PREVENT the colour change from
  RED to BLUE on adding 1 mL of 10% NaCl solution.

  A SMALL gold number means the colloid is a BETTER PROTECTIVE
  colloid (higher protective power).

                                          1
     Protective power  is proportional to -----------
                                          gold number

  GELATIN has the smallest gold number (0.005 - 0.01) so it is the
  best protective colloid.
```

### Q B2
Explain the factors affecting the adsorption of a gas on a solid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. NATURE OF THE GAS (ADSORBATE)
     Easily liquefiable gases are adsorbed to a greater extent,
     because they have stronger van der Waals forces. The measure is
     the CRITICAL TEMPERATURE - higher Tc means more adsorption.
        SO2 > NH3 > HCl > CH4 > CO > N2 > H2

  2. NATURE OF THE ADSORBENT
     Different solids adsorb to different extents. Activated
     charcoal, silica gel, alumina gel, clay and finely divided
     metals (Ni, Pt, Pd) are good adsorbents.

  3. SURFACE AREA OF THE ADSORBENT
     The greater the surface area, the greater the adsorption.
     Adsorbents are therefore used FINELY POWDERED, POROUS or
     ACTIVATED to increase the specific surface area (area per gram).

  4. TEMPERATURE
     Adsorption is EXOTHERMIC, so by Le Chatelier's principle a rise
     in temperature DECREASES physisorption. Chemisorption first
     increases (activation energy supplied) and then decreases.

  5. PRESSURE
     At constant temperature, adsorption INCREASES with pressure,
     until the surface becomes SATURATED and x/m becomes independent
     of pressure.
```

### Q B3
Write the Freundlich adsorption isotherm. Explain it with a graph and state
its limitation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE EQUATION
                  x
                 ---   =   k  P^(1/n)          ( n > 1 )
                  m

     x/m = extent of adsorption ,  P = equilibrium pressure
     k, n = constants for the given system at a given temperature

  LOGARITHMIC FORM
                  x                        1
             log ---   =   log k    +     ---  log P
                  m                        n

  THE GRAPHS

   x/m |                              log |
       |          ______ saturation   x/m |                  o
       |        /                         |               o
       |      /                           |            o     slope = 1/n
       |    /                             |         o
       |  /                        log k  |      o
       | /                                |   o
       |/                                 |o
       +------------------- P             +------------------- log P

  From the straight-line plot:   SLOPE = 1/n ,  INTERCEPT = log k

  LIMITATION
  It is only an EMPIRICAL relation valid over a LIMITED PRESSURE
  RANGE. At HIGH PRESSURE the surface is SATURATED and x/m becomes
  independent of pressure, so the experimental curve deviates from
  the straight line. The Freundlich isotherm FAILS at high pressure.
```

### Q B4
Write any four applications of adsorption.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. GAS MASKS
     Activated charcoal in the mask ADSORBS poisonous gases, so only
     clean air is breathed in. Used by miners and soldiers.

  2. REMOVAL OF MOISTURE AND CONTROL OF HUMIDITY
     SILICA GEL and ALUMINA GEL adsorb water vapour and keep
     instruments, medicines and packages dry.

  3. HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS
     The reactant gases are ADSORBED on the catalyst surface, where
     their bonds are weakened and they react faster - as in the
     Haber process (Fe) and hydrogenation of oils (Ni).

  4. CHROMATOGRAPHY
     Different components of a mixture are adsorbed to DIFFERENT
     EXTENTS on an alumina or silica column and so are separated.

  5. FROTH FLOTATION
     PINE OIL is preferentially adsorbed on sulphide ore particles,
     making them water-repellent so they rise with the froth while
     the gangue sinks - used to concentrate sulphide ores.

  (Also: production of high vacuum, decolourisation of raw sugar by
   animal charcoal, adsorption indicators, softening of hard water.)
```

### Q B5
Explain the adsorption theory of heterogeneous catalysis.

**ANSWER:**
```
  This modern theory explains how a SOLID catalyst speeds up a
  reaction between gases. It takes place in FIVE STEPS:

  STEP 1  DIFFUSION of the reactant molecules TO the surface of
          the catalyst.

  STEP 2  ADSORPTION of the reactant molecules on the surface. The
          surface atoms have free (unsatisfied) valencies which hold
          the molecules.

  STEP 3  REACTION on the surface. An activated intermediate complex
          is formed and then decomposes to give the products.

  STEP 4  DESORPTION of the product molecules from the surface,
          which frees the site for fresh reactant molecules.

  STEP 5  DIFFUSION of the product molecules AWAY from the surface.

  WHY IT SPEEDS THE REACTION
  (a) The reactants are CONCENTRATED on the surface, so collisions
      are much more frequent.
  (b) Adsorption WEAKENS the bonds in the reactant molecules, which
      LOWERS THE ACTIVATION ENERGY.

  ENERGY DIAGRAM

  E |         /\
    |        /  \             <-- WITHOUT catalyst : LARGE Ea
    |       /    \
    |      /  /\  \
    |     /  /  \  \          <-- WITH catalyst : SMALL Ea
    |    /  /    \  \
    |   / /        \ \
    |  //            \\
  --|-/                \\____________
    | REACTANTS            PRODUCTS
    +--------------------------------------
             reaction progress

  The catalyst LOWERS the activation energy but does NOT change
  delta H, the equilibrium constant or the yield.
```

### Q B6
What is enzyme catalysis? Write its characteristics.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ENZYME CATALYSIS (biochemical catalysis) is catalysis by ENZYMES -
  complex nitrogenous organic compounds (proteins) produced by living
  plants and animals, which form colloidal solutions in water.

  CHARACTERISTICS

  1. MOST EFFICIENT - a very small quantity brings about a large
     change. One enzyme molecule can transform about 10^6 molecules
     of the substrate per minute.

  2. HIGHLY SPECIFIC - each enzyme catalyses ONE particular reaction
     for ONE particular substrate. Urease hydrolyses only urea.

  3. OPTIMUM TEMPERATURE - they are most active between 298 K and
     310 K. Above this the protein is DENATURED and activity is lost.

  4. OPTIMUM pH - they are most active at a pH of about 5 to 7;
     activity falls sharply on either side.

  5. ACTIVATORS AND CO-ENZYMES - activity is increased by certain
     substances such as metal ions (Na+, Co2+, Cu2+). NaCl activates
     amylase.

  6. INHIBITORS AND POISONS - certain substances reduce or destroy
     the activity. Many DRUGS work by inhibiting an enzyme of the germ.

  7. Activity is increased in the presence of ULTRAVIOLET LIGHT.

  EXAMPLES
     INVERTASE : sucrose + H2O  -->  glucose + fructose
     ZYMASE    : glucose  -->  2 C2H5OH + 2 CO2
     UREASE    : urea + H2O  -->  2 NH3 + CO2
     DIASTASE  : starch  -->  maltose
```

### Q B7
Explain the lock-and-key mechanism of enzyme action.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. An enzyme molecule is very large and its surface carries
     CAVITIES of a definite SHAPE, surrounded by ACTIVE GROUPS
     such as -NH2 , -COOH , -SH and -OH.
     These cavities are the ACTIVE SITES.

  2. A substrate molecule of exactly the RIGHT SHAPE fits into the
     active site just as a KEY fits into a LOCK, and is held there
     by the active groups.

  3. This forms an ACTIVATED ENZYME-SUBSTRATE COMPLEX.

  4. The complex then breaks up to give the PRODUCTS and the FREE
     ENZYME is REGENERATED, ready to work again.

           E   +   S   <-->   [ E - S ]   -->   E   +   P
        enzyme substrate      complex        enzyme  product

  5. THIS EXPLAINS THE SPECIFICITY: a substrate of the wrong shape
     cannot fit into the cavity, so no complex forms and no reaction
     takes place. The lock accepts only its own key.

      S                                   P1   P2
     ___                                   ^    ^
    |   |                                  |    |
      |                                 +--+----+-+
   +--+----+                            |          |
   | \___/ |  <- active site            |  \___/   |  <- site now FREE
   | ENZYME|                            |  ENZYME  |
   +-------+                            +----------+
```

### Q B10
Explain the preparation of colloids by (a) chemical methods, (b) Bredig's arc
method, (c) peptization.

**ANSWER:**
```
  (a) CHEMICAL METHODS  (condensation - build the particle up)

      DOUBLE DECOMPOSITION
          As2O3  +  3 H2S   -->   As2S3 (sol)  +  3 H2O

      OXIDATION
          SO2  +  2 H2S   -->   3 S (sol)  +  2 H2O

      REDUCTION
          2 AuCl3  +  3 SnCl2   -->   2 Au (sol)  +  3 SnCl4

      HYDROLYSIS
          FeCl3  +  3 H2O  --(boiling)-->  Fe(OH)3 (sol)  +  3 HCl


  (b) BREDIG'S ARC METHOD  (electrical disintegration)

      Used for sols of the metals GOLD, SILVER, PLATINUM and COPPER.

      An ELECTRIC ARC is struck between two rods of the metal held
      UNDER WATER containing a TRACE OF NaOH as a stabiliser, with
      the whole vessel standing in an ICE BATH.

      The intense heat of the arc VAPORISES the metal (dispersion),
      and the vapour is at once CONDENSED by the cold water into
      colloidal particles (condensation).
      So it is BOTH a dispersion and a condensation method.

                  ~~ ARC ~~
      metal rod =====))((===== metal rod
        +---------------------------+
        |  water + trace of NaOH    |   <- metal SOL forms here
        +---------------------------+
        |        ICE BATH           |
        +---------------------------+


  (c) PEPTIZATION

      The process of converting a FRESHLY PREPARED PRECIPITATE into
      a colloidal sol by shaking it with the dispersion medium in
      the presence of a SMALL AMOUNT OF ELECTROLYTE, called the
      PEPTIZING AGENT.

      The precipitate ADSORBS one kind of ion from the electrolyte,
      becomes CHARGED, and the like-charged particles then REPEL
      each other and break up into colloidal particles.

      Example: a fresh precipitate of Fe(OH)3 shaken with a little
      FeCl3 adsorbs Fe3+ ions, becomes positively charged and
      disperses into a Fe(OH)3 SOL.
```

### Q B11
How are colloids purified? Explain dialysis, electrodialysis and ultrafiltration.

**ANSWER:**
```
  WHY PURIFY: a freshly prepared sol contains EXCESS ELECTROLYTE.
  A small amount stabilises the sol, but too much COAGULATES it,
  so the excess must be removed.

  1. DIALYSIS
     The removal of dissolved ions and small molecules from a sol by
     DIFFUSION THROUGH A MEMBRANE (parchment paper or cellophane).
     The sol is put in a bag of the membrane and the bag is
     suspended in a tank of continuously running water.
     The IONS pass through the membrane and are washed away; the
     large COLLOIDAL PARTICLES cannot pass and stay in the bag.
     DRAWBACK: it is very SLOW.

        water IN                              water OUT
           |                                     ^
           v      +-------------------+          |
        +---------|  SOL in the bag   |----------+
        |         |   o o o           |          |
        |         +---|-----------|---+          |
        |    ions -->  +  -    +  -   <-- washed away
        +-------------------------------------+

  2. ELECTRODIALYSIS
     Dialysis SPEEDED UP by applying an ELECTRIC FIELD across the
     membrane. The field drags the ions through the membrane much
     faster. It works only for IONIC (electrolyte) impurities.

  3. ULTRAFILTRATION
     Separating the colloid from dissolved substances by filtering
     through a specially graded ULTRAFILTER PAPER.
     Ordinary filter paper has pores too large - the colloid passes
     straight through. So the paper is soaked in COLLODION solution
     (4% nitrocellulose in alcohol and ether), HARDENED with
     FORMALDEHYDE and dried, which narrows the pores.
     The process is slow and is carried out under PRESSURE or SUCTION.
     The colloid left on the filter can be stirred into fresh medium
     to give a PURE SOL.
```

### Q B12
Explain the Tyndall effect and Brownian movement.

**ANSWER:**
```
  TYNDALL EFFECT

  The SCATTERING OF LIGHT by colloidal particles, which makes the
  path of a beam of light passing through the sol VISIBLE as a bright
  luminous CONE (the Tyndall cone) when viewed at right angles.

  CONDITIONS
    1. The DIAMETER of the dispersed particles must not be much
       SMALLER than the WAVELENGTH of the light used.
    2. The REFRACTIVE INDICES of the dispersed phase and of the
       dispersion medium must DIFFER GREATLY.

                          | eye (viewing from the SIDE)
                          v
         light      +------------------+
        ((( )))-------> . TYNDALL  .   | ----> beam continues
         source     |  .   CONE  .  .  |
                    +------------------+
                        the SOL

  A TRUE SOLUTION does NOT show the Tyndall effect, so it is the
  standard TEST to distinguish a colloid from a true solution.
  It is also the basis of the ULTRAMICROSCOPE.
  Examples: a sunbeam in a dusty room, a cinema projector beam,
  headlights in fog.


  BROWNIAN MOVEMENT

  The continuous, rapid, ZIG-ZAG (random) motion of colloidal
  particles, seen under an ultramicroscope.

  CAUSE: the UNEQUAL / UNBALANCED BOMBARDMENT of each colloidal
  particle by the molecules of the DISPERSION MEDIUM. At any instant
  the collisions on one side are not balanced by those on the other,
  so the particle is kicked about at random.

  IMPORTANCE: it PREVENTS the colloidal particles from SETTLING
  under gravity and is therefore one of the causes of the STABILITY
  of a sol. It is also direct evidence for the kinetic theory.

         o                       ->    <-
        / \                    ->  \  /  <-
       /   \___              ->     (o)    <-
      /        \               ->  /    \  <-
     o          \___o            ->      <-
```

### Q B13
What is coagulation? Explain the Hardy-Schulze rule with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  COAGULATION (flocculation / precipitation) is the process of
  SETTLING DOWN of colloidal particles as a precipitate when the
  CHARGE on them is NEUTRALISED.

  MECHANISM: the added ion of opposite charge is adsorbed on the
  colloidal particle and neutralises its charge. With no charge left
  the particles no longer repel one another; they collide, grow
  bigger and heavier and settle out.

  METHODS OF COAGULATION
     1. adding an ELECTROLYTE
     2. ELECTROPHORESIS
     3. mixing TWO OPPOSITELY CHARGED SOLS (mutual coagulation)
     4. BOILING
     5. PROLONGED DIALYSIS

  HARDY-SCHULZE RULE
  (a) The ion effective in coagulating a sol is the one carrying the
      charge OPPOSITE to that of the colloidal particles.
  (b) The GREATER the VALENCY (charge) of that oppositely charged
      ion, the GREATER is its COAGULATING POWER.

  EXAMPLES
      For a NEGATIVE sol such as As2S3, the CATIONS act:
              Al3+   >   Ba2+   >   Na+
      So AlCl3 coagulates it far more effectively than NaCl.

      For a POSITIVE sol such as Fe(OH)3, the ANIONS act:
              [Fe(CN)6]4-  >  PO4^3-  >  SO4^2-  >  Cl-

  COAGULATION VALUE is the minimum concentration of an electrolyte
  in MILLIMOLES PER LITRE needed to coagulate a sol in TWO HOURS.
  Coagulating power is INVERSELY proportional to coagulation value.
```

### Q B14
What are emulsions? Describe their types and the tests used to distinguish them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An EMULSION is a colloidal system in which BOTH the dispersed
  phase and the dispersion medium are LIQUIDS - one liquid dispersed
  in another liquid with which it is immiscible.

  THE TWO TYPES

  +--------------------+---------------------+---------------------+
  |                    |  OIL IN WATER (O/W) |  WATER IN OIL (W/O) |
  +--------------------+---------------------+---------------------+
  | Dispersed phase    |  OIL                |  WATER              |
  | Dispersion medium  |  WATER              |  OIL                |
  | Examples           |  MILK, vanishing    |  BUTTER, cold cream,|
  |                    |  cream, cod liver   |  cheese, crude oil  |
  |                    |  oil emulsion       |                     |
  +--------------------+---------------------+---------------------+

  EMULSIFYING AGENT: an emulsion of two pure liquids is unstable, so
  a small amount of an emulsifier is added to stabilise it by
  forming a film round the droplets.
     for O/W : soaps, detergents, proteins, gums (casein in milk)
     for W/O : heavy metal salts of fatty acids, long-chain
               alcohols, lampblack

  THE THREE TESTS TO DISTINGUISH THEM

  1. DILUTION TEST
     An emulsion can be diluted only by its own dispersion medium.
        mixes freely with WATER  ->  O/W
        mixes freely with OIL    ->  W/O

  2. DYE TEST
     Add a small amount of an OIL-SOLUBLE dye such as Sudan III.
        the WHOLE emulsion becomes uniformly coloured  ->  W/O
        only the scattered DROPLETS are coloured       ->  O/W

  3. CONDUCTIVITY TEST
     Water conducts electricity, oil does not.
        the emulsion CONDUCTS      ->  O/W (water is continuous)
        it does NOT conduct        ->  W/O (oil is continuous)

  DEMULSIFICATION - breaking an emulsion into its two liquids, by
  boiling, freezing, centrifuging or adding an electrolyte.
```

### Q B15
Write any four applications of colloids.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. FORMATION OF A DELTA
     River water carries NEGATIVELY charged colloidal clay particles.
     Sea water contains electrolytes supplying Na+, Mg2+ and Ca2+.
     These positive ions NEUTRALISE the charge on the clay
     (Hardy-Schulze rule), the clay COAGULATES and settles at the
     river mouth, building up a DELTA.

  2. COTTRELL SMOKE PRECIPITATOR
     Smoke is an aerosol of CHARGED carbon particles in air. It is
     passed through a chamber with electrodes at a VERY HIGH
     POTENTIAL. The charged particles reach the oppositely charged
     electrode, LOSE THEIR CHARGE and are PRECIPITATED, so only
     clean air leaves the chimney.

  3. PURIFICATION OF DRINKING WATER USING ALUM
     Natural water carries NEGATIVELY charged colloidal impurities.
     Alum supplies Al3+ ions, which by the Hardy-Schulze rule very
     effectively neutralise that charge. The impurities COAGULATE
     and settle, leaving clear water.

  4. CLEANSING ACTION OF SOAP
     Soap forms MICELLES in water. The hydrocarbon TAILS bury
     themselves in the oil and grease on the cloth while the ionic
     -COO- HEADS stay in the water. The grease is thus pulled off
     the cloth as an emulsion and washed away.

  5. ARTIFICIAL RAIN
     Cloud droplets carry a charge. Spraying ELECTRIFIED SAND or
     silver iodide of the OPPOSITE charge neutralises them; the
     droplets coalesce into large drops and fall as RAIN.

  6. TANNING OF LEATHER
     Animal hide is a POSITIVELY charged colloid and tannin (or a
     chromium salt) is NEGATIVELY charged. Soaking the hide causes
     MUTUAL COAGULATION, making the hide hard and non-putrefying.

  7. MEDICINES
     Colloidal medicines are absorbed easily because of their large
     surface area: ARGYROL (colloidal silver) as an eye lotion,
     MILK OF MAGNESIA for acidity, colloidal antimony for kala-azar.
```

### Q B16
Explain the terms multimolecular, macromolecular and associated colloids
with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. MULTIMOLECULAR COLLOIDS
     On dissolution, a LARGE NUMBER of atoms or small molecules
     (each smaller than 1 nm) AGGREGATE together to form a particle
     of colloidal size. They are held by weak VAN DER WAALS forces
     and are usually LYOPHOBIC in character.

     Examples: a GOLD SOL, in which each particle contains thousands
     of gold atoms; a SULPHUR SOL, in which each particle contains
     about a thousand S8 molecules.

  2. MACROMOLECULAR COLLOIDS
     The molecules of the substance are THEMSELVES SO LARGE
     (macromolecules or polymers) that a SINGLE molecule is already
     of colloidal size. Such sols are very STABLE and LYOPHILIC.

     Natural examples:   starch, cellulose, proteins, enzymes,
                         gelatin, natural rubber
     Synthetic examples: polythene, nylon, polystyrene

  3. ASSOCIATED COLLOIDS (MICELLES)
     Substances which behave as NORMAL STRONG ELECTROLYTES at LOW
     concentration but, above a certain concentration, their ions
     ASSOCIATE into aggregates of colloidal size called MICELLES.

     Micelles form only when BOTH conditions are satisfied:
        concentration ABOVE the CRITICAL MICELLE CONCENTRATION (CMC)
        temperature   ABOVE the KRAFT TEMPERATURE (Tk)
     For soaps the CMC is about 10^-4 to 10^-3 mol/L, and a micelle
     may contain 100 or more molecules.

     Examples: SOAPS (sodium stearate) and DETERGENTS
     (sodium dodecyl sulphate).

     In water the ionic HEADS point outward and the hydrocarbon
     TAILS point inward; in a non-polar solvent a REVERSE micelle
     is formed.
```
