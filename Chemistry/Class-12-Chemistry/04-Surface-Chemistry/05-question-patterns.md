# Surface Chemistry — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

**Chemistry 2nd Year · Chapter 4**

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps or the fixed content
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — ADSORPTION vs ABSORPTION vs SORPTION            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is adsorption?" / "Distinguish adsorption from
            absorption" / "What is sorption?"
  METHOD    1. ADSORPTION - accumulation at the SURFACE; concentration
               higher at the surface than in the bulk. SURFACE phenomenon.
            2. ABSORPTION - uniform uptake THROUGHOUT the BULK.
               BULK phenomenon.
            3. SORPTION - both happening at the same time.
            4. ALWAYS give one example of each:
                  adsorption - silica gel + water vapour, gas on charcoal
                  absorption - sponge + water, CaCl2 + water
                  sorption   - dye on cotton, H2 on palladium
  TRAP      Writing "absorbed by charcoal". Charcoal ADSORBS.
            One wrong prefix loses the whole mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — ADSORBATE AND ADSORBENT                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define adsorbate and adsorbent" or "Name the adsorbate and
            the adsorbent when NH3 is passed over charcoal."
  METHOD    ADSORBATE = the substance that GETS adsorbed (NH3)
            ADSORBENT = the surface ON WHICH it happens (charcoal)
            Memory: adsorBATE is the BAIT that gets caught.
  TRAP      Swapping them. Read the sentence: whatever is "on" or
            "over" something else is the ADSORBATE.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — WHY IS ADSORPTION EXOTHERMIC?                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is adsorption exothermic?" / "What are the signs of
            delta H and delta S in adsorption?"
  METHOD    1. Molecules lose freedom on sticking -> delta S is NEGATIVE.
            2. Adsorption is spontaneous -> delta G is NEGATIVE.
            3. delta G = delta H - T delta S
            4. With delta S negative, (-T delta S) is positive, so
               delta H must be NEGATIVE for delta G to be negative.
            5. Negative delta H = heat released = EXOTHERMIC.
  TRAP      Just writing "heat is released". You must SHOW the
            thermodynamic argument with the equation - that is the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — PHYSISORPTION vs CHEMISORPTION                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Differences between physical and chemical adsorption",
            "Give any two differences", "What is chemisorption?"
  METHOD    Draw a TABLE. The eight rows in order:
              1. FORCES         van der Waals   | chemical bond
              2. ENTHALPY       20-40 kJ/mol    | 80-240 kJ/mol
              3. SPECIFICITY    not specific    | highly specific
              4. REVERSIBILITY  reversible      | irreversible
              5. TEMPERATURE    decreases       | rises then falls
              6. SURFACE AREA   increases       | increases
              7. LAYERS         multilayer      | monolayer
              8. ACTIVATION E   not needed      | high (activated ads.)
            Finish with one EXAMPLE of each.
  TRAP      (a) Writing "chemisorption decreases with temperature".
                It INCREASES FIRST, then decreases.
            (b) Forgetting the actual kJ/mol numbers - they carry marks.
            (c) Getting multilayer/monolayer the wrong way round.
                PHYSI = MANY layers. CHEMI = ONE layer.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WHICH GAS IS ADSORBED MORE, AND WHY           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which gas is adsorbed more on charcoal, H2 or SO2?" /
            "Why is SO2 adsorbed more than H2?"
  METHOD    1. The more EASILY LIQUEFIABLE gas is adsorbed more.
            2. Easily liquefiable = STRONGER van der Waals forces,
               which is exactly what physisorption uses.
            3. The measure is the CRITICAL TEMPERATURE:
                  HIGHER critical temperature -> MORE adsorption.
            4. Quote the order:
                  SO2 > NH3 > HCl > CH4 > CO > N2 > H2
                  (Tc:  630   406   324   190  134  126   33  K)
  TRAP      Answering by molecular mass or by size. It is CRITICAL
            TEMPERATURE / ease of liquefaction, nothing else.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — FACTORS AFFECTING ADSORPTION                     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the factors affecting the adsorption of a gas
            on a solid?"
  METHOD    Give FIVE headings, one short paragraph each:
              1. NATURE OF THE GAS - easily liquefiable adsorbs more
                 (critical temperature)
              2. NATURE OF THE ADSORBENT - charcoal, silica gel,
                 alumina gel, finely divided metals
              3. SURFACE AREA - more area, more adsorption; hence
                 powdered / porous / activated adsorbents
              4. TEMPERATURE - exothermic, so physisorption falls;
                 chemisorption rises then falls
              5. PRESSURE - adsorption rises with P until saturation
  TRAP      Writing only three factors. There are FIVE headings and
            each is worth part of a mark. Write all five.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — WRITE AND EXPLAIN THE FREUNDLICH ISOTHERM     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the Freundlich adsorption isotherm" / "Explain the
            Freundlich isotherm with a graph."
  METHOD    1. x/m = k P^(1/n)   with n > 1
            2. Define x, m, P, k, n.
            3. Log form: log(x/m) = log k + (1/n) log P
            4. DRAW BOTH GRAPHS:
                  x/m against P      -> curve that flattens
                  log(x/m) vs log P  -> straight line
            5. SLOPE = 1/n , INTERCEPT = log k
            6. State the limitation: FAILS AT HIGH PRESSURE.
  TRAP      Not drawing the graph. A drawn graph is worth a mark on
            its own and takes 20 seconds.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — FIND k AND n FROM A GRAPH OR DATA             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A slope and/or intercept is given, or two (P, x/m) values.
  METHOD    1. slope = 1/n        ->   n = 1 / slope
            2. intercept = log k  ->   k = 10^(intercept)   ANTILOG IT
            3. Substitute back into x/m = k P^(1/n) if asked for a value.
            EXAMPLE  slope 0.5, intercept 0.6021:
                     n = 1/0.5 = 2
                     k = 10^0.6021 = 4
                     at P = 4:  x/m = 4 x sqrt(4) = 8
  TRAP      Reporting the intercept AS k. The intercept is LOG k.
            You must take the antilog.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — LIMITATION OF FREUNDLICH / LANGMUIR              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State a limitation of the Freundlich isotherm" or
            "What does the Langmuir isotherm assume?"
  METHOD    FREUNDLICH LIMITATION:
              it is EMPIRICAL and valid only over a LIMITED PRESSURE
              range; at HIGH pressure the surface SATURATES so x/m
              becomes INDEPENDENT of P (1/n -> 0), and the graph
              deviates from the straight line. It FAILS at high pressure.
            LANGMUIR ASSUMPTIONS:
              monolayer only; fixed number of identical sites; no
              interaction between adsorbed molecules; dynamic
              equilibrium between adsorption and desorption.
              x/m = aP / (1 + bP)
  TRAP      Saying it fails at LOW pressure. It works fine at low
            pressure. It fails at HIGH pressure.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — APPLICATIONS OF ADSORPTION                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write any four applications of adsorption" or a single
            one: "How does a gas mask work?"
  METHOD    Give the application AND the adsorbent AND what it adsorbs:
              1. GAS MASKS - activated charcoal adsorbs poison gases
              2. DRYING / HUMIDITY CONTROL - silica gel, alumina gel
                 adsorb water vapour
              3. HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS - reactants adsorbed on the
                 catalyst surface
              4. CHROMATOGRAPHY - components adsorbed to different
                 extents on an alumina/silica column
              5. FROTH FLOTATION - pine oil adsorbed on the sulphide
                 ore makes it water-repellent
              6. HIGH VACUUM, decolourising sugar, adsorption indicators
  TRAP      Naming the application without saying WHAT is adsorbed on
            WHAT. Half the mark is in that detail.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — DEFINE CATALYST AND CATALYSIS                   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a catalyst?" / "What is catalysis?"
  METHOD    A catalyst ALTERS THE RATE of a reaction and itself remains
            CHEMICALLY UNCHANGED IN MASS AND COMPOSITION at the end.
            Catalysis is the phenomenon. Give an example.
            Also know: POSITIVE catalyst speeds up (lowers Ea);
            NEGATIVE catalyst / inhibitor slows down (raises Ea),
            e.g. glycerol in the decomposition of H2O2.
  TRAP      Writing "increases the rate". It ALTERS the rate.
            And the phrase must be "chemically unchanged in MASS AND
            COMPOSITION" - the physical form may change.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — PROMOTER AND CATALYTIC POISON                   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a promoter?" / "What is a catalytic poison?" /
            "Give an example of each."
  METHOD    PROMOTER - not itself a catalyst; INCREASES the ACTIVITY
                       of a catalyst.
                       Mo (also Al2O3, K2O) with Fe in the HABER process.
            POISON   - DESTROYS or REDUCES the activity of a catalyst
                       by being strongly adsorbed on the active sites.
                       As2O3 poisons Pt in the CONTACT process;
                       CO poisons Fe in the Haber process.
  TRAP      Confusing a POISON with a NEGATIVE CATALYST.
            A negative catalyst slows the REACTION.
            A poison kills the CATALYST. Not the same thing.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — HOMOGENEOUS vs HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between homogeneous and heterogeneous
            catalysis with examples."
  METHOD    HOMOGENEOUS - catalyst in the SAME phase as the reactants.
                2 SO2(g) + O2(g) --(NO g)--> 2 SO3(g)
                ester hydrolysis with H2SO4; cane sugar with H+
            HETEROGENEOUS - catalyst in a DIFFERENT phase (usually a
            solid with gases). Also called CONTACT catalysis.
                N2 + 3H2 --(Fe s)--> 2NH3
                2SO2 + O2 --(V2O5 s)--> 2SO3
                4NH3 + 5O2 --(Pt s)--> 4NO + 6H2O
                oil + H2 --(Ni s)--> vanaspati
  TRAP      Not writing the PHYSICAL STATES. The whole distinction is
            about the states, so write (g), (l), (s) on every species.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — INDUSTRIAL CATALYSTS AND PROMOTERS              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Name the catalyst used in ..." for Haber, Contact,
            Ostwald, or the hydrogenation of oils.
  METHOD    HABER   N2 + 3H2 -> 2NH3          Fe   (promoter Mo)
            CONTACT 2SO2 + O2 -> 2SO3         V2O5
            OSTWALD 4NH3 + 5O2 -> 4NO + 6H2O  Pt gauze
            OILS    oil + H2 -> vanaspati     Ni
            KClO3   2KClO3 -> 2KCl + 3O2      MnO2
  TRAP      Mixing up Fe and V2O5. Memory line:
            "Haber has Iron, Contact has Vanadium, Ostwald has
             Platinum, Oil has Nickel."
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — ADSORPTION THEORY OF HETEROGENEOUS CATALYSIS    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the adsorption theory of heterogeneous catalysis"
            or "How does a solid catalyst work?"
  METHOD    The FIVE steps, in order:
              1. DIFFUSION of reactants TO the surface
              2. ADSORPTION of reactants ON the surface
              3. REACTION on the surface via an intermediate complex
              4. DESORPTION of the products
              5. DIFFUSION of the products AWAY
            Then say WHY it speeds up:
              (a) reactants are CONCENTRATED on the surface
              (b) adsorption WEAKENS their bonds, LOWERING Ea
            Then DRAW the energy diagram: two humps, a tall one
            (uncatalysed) and a short one (catalysed), with the SAME
            reactant and product levels.
  TRAP      In the energy diagram, drawing the products at a different
            level on the two curves. delta H MUST be identical - the
            catalyst changes only the hump height.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — WHAT A CATALYST DOES NOT DO                  2 marks / MCQ
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Does a catalyst change the yield?" / "Which of the
            following is NOT changed by a catalyst?"
  METHOD    A CATALYST CHANGES:  activation energy , reaction path ,
                                 rate (forward AND backward equally) ,
                                 the TIME taken to reach equilibrium
            A CATALYST DOES NOT CHANGE:  delta H , delta G ,
                                 the equilibrium constant K ,
                                 the POSITION of equilibrium ,
                                 the YIELD of the product
  TRAP      "A catalyst increases the yield" is FALSE. It only makes
            you reach the SAME equilibrium SOONER.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — SHAPE SELECTIVE CATALYSIS / ZEOLITES / ZSM-5    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is shape-selective catalysis?" / "What is ZSM-5
            used for?" / "What are zeolites?"
  METHOD    SHAPE SELECTIVE CATALYSIS - catalysis depending on the
            PORE SIZE of the catalyst and the SIZE AND SHAPE of the
            reactant and product molecules.
            ZEOLITES - microporous ALUMINOSILICATES, a 3-D network of
            SiO4 and AlO4 tetrahedra joined by oxygen, with cavities
            of a definite size. Called MOLECULAR SIEVES.
            ZSM-5 - converts ALCOHOLS into GASOLINE (petrol) by
            dehydrating them into a mixture of hydrocarbons.
  TRAP      Writing that zeolites are silicates. They are ALUMINO-
            silicates - the aluminium must be in the answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — ENZYME CATALYSIS: CHARACTERISTICS               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is enzyme catalysis?" / "Write the characteristics
            of enzymes."
  METHOD    Define: enzymes are complex nitrogenous organic compounds
            (PROTEINS) from living organisms acting as BIOCHEMICAL
            CATALYSTS; they form colloidal solutions.
            Characteristics (give at least five):
              1. MOST EFFICIENT - 10^6 substrate molecules per minute
              2. HIGHLY SPECIFIC - one enzyme, one substrate
              3. OPTIMUM TEMPERATURE 298-310 K (denatured above)
              4. OPTIMUM pH 5-7
              5. ACTIVATORS / CO-ENZYMES increase activity (NaCl and
                 amylase)
              6. INHIBITORS AND POISONS reduce it; many DRUGS work
                 this way
              7. UV light increases activity
            Finish with two enzyme examples and their reactions.
  TRAP      Forgetting the NUMBERS. "298-310 K", "pH 5-7" and "10^6
            per minute" are what the examiner is looking for.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — LOCK AND KEY MECHANISM                       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the mechanism of enzyme action" / "What is the
            lock-and-key model?"
  METHOD    1. The enzyme surface has CAVITIES of a definite shape,
               with ACTIVE GROUPS (-NH2, -COOH, -SH, -OH) around them.
               These are the ACTIVE SITES.
            2. A substrate of the RIGHT SHAPE fits in like a KEY in a
               LOCK.
            3. An ACTIVATED ENZYME-SUBSTRATE COMPLEX forms.
            4. It breaks up into PRODUCTS, REGENERATING the free enzyme.
                    E + S <--> [E-S] --> E + P
            5. This EXPLAINS SPECIFICITY: wrong shape, no fit, no
               reaction.
  TRAP      Not linking it back to SPECIFICITY. That link is usually
            the last mark of the question.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — NAME THE ENZYME FOR A REACTION               2 marks / MCQ
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which enzyme converts X into Y?"
  METHOD    Learn the ladder in one line:
              Starch  --DIASTASE-->  Maltose  --MALTASE-->  Glucose
              Glucose --ZYMASE-->  2 C2H5OH + 2 CO2
              Sucrose --INVERTASE--> Glucose + Fructose
              Urea    --UREASE-->  2 NH3 + CO2
              Proteins --PEPSIN / TRYPSIN--> peptides / amino acids
              Fats    --LIPASE-->  fatty acids + glycerol
  TRAP      Confusing MALTASE (maltose -> glucose) with DIASTASE
            (starch -> maltose). Diastase comes FIRST in the chain.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — CLASSIFY BY PARTICLE SIZE                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a colloid?" / "Distinguish a true solution, a
            colloid and a suspension."
  METHOD    TRUE SOLUTION  < 1 nm      homogeneous, no Tyndall,
                                       passes parchment
            COLLOID        1-1000 nm   heterogeneous, TYNDALL YES,
                                       passes filter paper but NOT
                                       parchment, does not settle
            SUSPENSION     > 1000 nm   visible, settles, blocked by
                                       filter paper
  TRAP      Giving only the sizes. Add the FILTER PAPER / PARCHMENT
            behaviour and the TYNDALL EFFECT - those are the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — NAME THE COLLOIDAL SYSTEM                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Milk is an example of ...?" / "Classify colloids on the
            basis of the physical states of the two phases."
  METHOD    Draw the 8-row table:
              solid in solid   SOLID SOL     gemstones
              solid in liquid  SOL           paints, muddy water
              solid in gas     AEROSOL       smoke, dust
              liquid in solid  GEL           cheese, butter, jellies
              liquid in liquid EMULSION      milk, hair cream
              liquid in gas    AEROSOL       fog, mist, cloud
              gas in solid     SOLID FOAM    pumice stone, foam rubber
              gas in liquid    FOAM          whipped cream, soap lather
            Add the line: GAS IN GAS IS NOT A COLLOID.
  TRAP      Missing the note about gas-in-gas. It is often the last
            part of the question, and it is a guaranteed MCQ.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — LYOPHILIC vs LYOPHOBIC                       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between lyophilic and lyophobic colloids."
  METHOD    Table, at least six rows:
              1. AFFINITY      strong (loving)   | none (hating)
              2. PREPARATION   direct mixing     | special methods
              3. REVERSIBILITY reversible        | irreversible
              4. STABILITY     stable, self-stab.| unstable, needs
                                                   a stabiliser
              5. ELECTROLYTE   large amount      | small amount
                               needed to coagulate  coagulates it
              6. VISCOSITY     much higher       | same as medium
              7. TYNDALL       weak              | strong
              8. EXAMPLES      gum, gelatin,     | Au, Ag, As2S3,
                               starch, proteins    Fe(OH)3
  TRAP      Guessing the examples. Living/polymer things are
            LYOPHILIC; metals, hydroxides and sulphides are LYOPHOBIC.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — MULTIMOLECULAR / MACROMOLECULAR / ASSOCIATED    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain multimolecular, macromolecular and associated
            colloids with examples."
  METHOD    MULTIMOLECULAR - MANY small atoms/molecules aggregate into
              one colloidal particle, held by VAN DER WAALS forces;
              usually lyophobic. GOLD SOL, SULPHUR SOL (~1000 S8).
            MACROMOLECULAR - ONE molecule is ALREADY colloidal in size
              (polymers); very stable, LYOPHILIC.
              starch, cellulose, proteins, gelatin, rubber;
              polythene, nylon, polystyrene.
            ASSOCIATED (MICELLES) - behave as normal ELECTROLYTES at
              LOW concentration but form MICELLES above the CMC.
              SOAPS, DETERGENTS.
  TRAP      Putting the gold sol under "macromolecular". A gold sol is
            an aggregate of many ATOMS, so it is MULTImolecular.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — MICELLES, CMC AND KRAFT TEMPERATURE             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is CMC?" / "Define the Kraft temperature." /
            "When are micelles formed?"
  METHOD    CMC = the MINIMUM CONCENTRATION above which micelles form.
                  For soaps: about 10^-4 to 10^-3 mol/L.
            KRAFT TEMPERATURE Tk = the MINIMUM TEMPERATURE above which
                  micelles form.
            MICELLES FORM ONLY IF BOTH:
                  concentration > CMC   AND   temperature > Tk
            A micelle contains 100 or more molecules. In water the
            ionic HEADS face out and the hydrocarbon TAILS face in.
  TRAP      Stating only one of the two conditions. The word "BOTH"
            is what the question is really testing.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — PREPARATION OF COLLOIDS                      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How are colloids prepared?" / "What is Bredig's arc
            method?" / "How is a Fe(OH)3 sol prepared?"
  METHOD    CHEMICAL (condensation) - one equation each:
              double decomposition  As2O3 + 3H2S -> As2S3 sol + 3H2O
              oxidation             SO2 + 2H2S -> 3S sol + 2H2O
              reduction             2AuCl3 + 3SnCl2 -> 2Au sol + 3SnCl4
              hydrolysis            FeCl3 + 3H2O -> Fe(OH)3 sol + 3HCl
            BREDIG'S ARC - arc between METAL RODS under water with a
              trace of NaOH, in an ICE BATH. For Au, Ag, Pt, Cu.
              It is BOTH dispersion AND condensation.
            PEPTIZATION - fresh precipitate + a SMALL amount of
              electrolyte (the peptizing agent) -> sol.
  TRAP      Forgetting that Bredig's arc is BOTH methods at once -
            that is a favourite one-mark point.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — PEPTIZATION                                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is peptization?" / "Name the peptizing agent for
            an Fe(OH)3 precipitate."
  METHOD    1. Converting a FRESHLY PREPARED PRECIPITATE into a SOL by
               shaking with the medium plus a SMALL amount of
               electrolyte (the PEPTIZING AGENT).
            2. Mechanism: the precipitate ADSORBS one kind of ion,
               becomes CHARGED, and the like-charged particles REPEL
               each other and disperse.
            3. Example: Fe(OH)3 precipitate + a little FeCl3 -> the
               Fe3+ is adsorbed, giving a POSITIVE Fe(OH)3 SOL.
  TRAP      Naming a random electrolyte. The peptizing agent must
            contain an ion COMMON to the precipitate.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — PURIFICATION OF COLLOIDS                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is dialysis?" / "How are colloids purified?"
  METHOD    WHY: excess electrolyte would COAGULATE the sol.
            1. DIALYSIS - ions diffuse out through a PARCHMENT or
               CELLOPHANE membrane into running water; the colloid
               cannot pass. SLOW.
            2. ELECTRODIALYSIS - the same, sped up by an ELECTRIC
               FIELD. Works only for IONIC impurities.
            3. ULTRAFILTRATION - filter through COLLODION-treated
               graded ultrafilter paper (4% nitrocellulose in
               alcohol+ether, hardened with formaldehyde), under
               pressure or suction.
  TRAP      Missing the "WHY". State that a small amount of
            electrolyte stabilises but excess coagulates - and that
            PROLONGED dialysis will itself coagulate the sol.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — TYNDALL EFFECT                               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the Tyndall effect?" / "Give the conditions for
            the Tyndall effect." / "How do you distinguish a colloid
            from a true solution?"
  METHOD    1. Definition: SCATTERING of light by colloidal particles,
               making the beam path visible as a luminous CONE when
               viewed from the SIDE.
            2. THE TWO CONDITIONS:
                 (a) particle DIAMETER must NOT be much SMALLER than
                     the WAVELENGTH of the light used
                 (b) the REFRACTIVE INDICES of the phase and the
                     medium must DIFFER GREATLY
            3. A true solution does NOT show it -> it is the TEST.
            4. Draw the cone diagram. Mention the ULTRAMICROSCOPE.
            5. Examples: sunbeam in a dusty room, cinema projector
               beam, headlights in fog.
  TRAP      Giving only one condition, or reversing them ("much
            smaller", "nearly equal"). Learn both exactly.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — BROWNIAN MOVEMENT                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is Brownian movement and what causes it?"
  METHOD    1. Continuous, rapid, ZIG-ZAG random motion of colloidal
               particles, seen under an ultramicroscope.
            2. CAUSE: UNEQUAL / UNBALANCED BOMBARDMENT of the particle
               by the molecules of the DISPERSION MEDIUM.
            3. IMPORTANCE: prevents settling under gravity, so it is
               a cause of the STABILITY of the sol; and it is direct
               evidence for the kinetic molecular theory.
  TRAP      Saying it is "due to the charge on the particles". It is
            not. The charge causes stability by REPULSION; Brownian
            motion is caused by COLLISIONS with medium molecules.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — CHARGE, DOUBLE LAYER, ELECTROPHORESIS        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is electrophoresis?" / "How do colloidal particles
            acquire a charge?" / "What is the zeta potential?"
  METHOD    CHARGE - from the PREFERENTIAL ADSORPTION of ions from the
              medium; the particle adsorbs the ion COMMON to its own
              lattice. All particles of a sol carry the SAME charge,
              so they REPEL - the main cause of stability.
            DOUBLE LAYER - a FIXED layer of adsorbed ions plus a
              DIFFUSED layer of opposite ions. The potential
              difference between them = ZETA POTENTIAL.
            ELECTROPHORESIS - movement of the PARTICLES to the
              oppositely charged electrode under an electric field.
              positive sol -> cathode (cataphoresis)
              negative sol -> anode
            ELECTRO-OSMOSIS - the particles are held still and the
              MEDIUM moves instead.
            KNOW THE SIGNS:
              POSITIVE: Fe(OH)3, Al(OH)3, hydrated oxides, basic dyes,
                        haemoglobin
              NEGATIVE: metal sols (Au, Ag, Cu), metal sulphides
                        (As2S3), acid dyes, starch, gum, clay, charcoal
  TRAP      Mixing up electrophoresis and electro-osmosis.
            PHORESIS = the PARTICLES move. OSMOSIS = the MEDIUM moves.
```

```
  PATTERN 32 — COAGULATION AND THE HARDY-SCHULZE RULE       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the Hardy-Schulze rule" / "Which electrolyte
            coagulates an As2S3 sol most effectively?"
  METHOD    THE THREE-STEP ROUTINE:
              STEP 1  Find the SIGN of the SOL.
                        sulphide / metal sol   -> NEGATIVE
                        hydroxide / oxide sol  -> POSITIVE
              STEP 2  Take only the ion of the OPPOSITE sign.
              STEP 3  Pick the one with the HIGHEST CHARGE.
            The rule itself, in words:
              (a) the coagulating ion is the one carrying the charge
                  OPPOSITE to that of the sol;
              (b) the GREATER its VALENCY, the GREATER its
                  COAGULATING POWER.
            Standard orders:
              negative sol:  Al3+ > Ba2+ > Na+
              positive sol:  [Fe(CN)6]4- > PO4^3- > SO4^2- > Cl-
  TRAP      Picking the highest-charged ion of EITHER sign. For a
            NEGATIVE sol, only the CATION matters - the anion of the
            same salt is irrelevant.
```

```
  PATTERN 33 — COAGULATION VALUE                            2 marks / MCQ
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define coagulation value" or a list of coagulation values
            with "which is the strongest coagulant?"
  METHOD    COAGULATION VALUE = minimum concentration of electrolyte,
            in MILLIMOLES PER LITRE, needed to coagulate a sol in
            TWO HOURS.
                coagulating power  ∝  1 / coagulation value
            So the SMALLEST value is the STRONGEST coagulant.
  TRAP      Picking the largest number. It is an INVERSE measure.
```

```
  PATTERN 34 — GOLD NUMBER AND PROTECTIVE COLLOIDS          2 marks / MCQ
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the gold number?" / "Which is the best protective
            colloid?" / a list of gold numbers to rank.
  METHOD    GOLD NUMBER = the minimum MASS in MILLIGRAMS of a
            protective colloid which, added to 10 mL of a standard
            GOLD SOL, JUST PREVENTS the colour change from RED to BLUE
            on adding 1 mL of 10% NaCl solution.
                protective power  ∝  1 / gold number
            SMALL gold number = BEST protection.
            GELATIN 0.005-0.01 (best) ... STARCH ~25 (worst).
  TRAP      Same inverse trap as coagulation value. Remember the line:
            "the best bodyguard needs the fewest men."
```

```
  PATTERN 35 — EMULSIONS: TYPES AND TESTS                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is an emulsion?" / "How do you distinguish O/W from
            W/O?" / "What is an emulsifying agent?"
  METHOD    EMULSION - a colloid in which BOTH phases are LIQUIDS.
            O/W  oil in water:  MILK, vanishing cream
            W/O  water in oil:  BUTTER, cold cream
            (the FIRST liquid named is the DISPERSED phase)
            EMULSIFIER - stabilises the emulsion by forming a film
              round the droplets. O/W: soaps, detergents, proteins,
              gums (casein in milk). W/O: heavy metal salts of fatty
              acids, long-chain alcohols, lampblack.
            THE THREE TESTS:
              DILUTION     mixes with water -> O/W ; with oil -> W/O
              DYE          only droplets coloured -> O/W ;
                           whole thing coloured   -> W/O
              CONDUCTIVITY conducts -> O/W ; does not -> W/O
            DEMULSIFICATION - breaking it, by boiling, freezing,
              centrifuging or adding an electrolyte.
  TRAP      Reversing the DYE test. The dye is OIL-SOLUBLE, so if the
            WHOLE emulsion colours, the CONTINUOUS phase must be OIL,
            i.e. W/O.
```

```
  PATTERN 36 — APPLICATIONS OF COLLOIDS                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write any four applications of colloids" or a single one:
            "Why is a delta formed?" / "How does alum purify water?"
  METHOD    Every one of these is COAGULATION BY CHARGE NEUTRALISATION.
            Say the charge, the neutralising ion, and the result.
              DELTA        negative clay + Na+/Mg2+/Ca2+ of sea water
              COTTRELL     charged smoke loses charge at a high-
                           potential electrode and settles
              ALUM         Al3+ neutralises negative impurities
              ARTIFICIAL   electrified sand / AgI neutralises the
                RAIN       charged cloud droplets -> they coalesce
              SOAP         MICELLE formation carries the grease away
              TANNING      positive hide + negative tannin ->
                           MUTUAL COAGULATION
              MEDICINES    argyrol (colloidal silver), milk of
                           magnesia, colloidal antimony
  TRAP      Naming the application without the MECHANISM. The mark is
            in the words "coagulation", "charge neutralisation" or
            "Hardy-Schulze".
```
