# ===============================================================
# PATTERN 1 — ADSORPTION vs ABSORPTION
# ===============================================================

### Q1
Which of the following is a **surface** phenomenon?
(a) Absorption  (b) Adsorption  (c) Sorption  (d) Diffusion

**ANSWER: (b) Adsorption**
```
  ADsorption = surface only.  ABsorption = whole bulk.
```

---

### Q2
When a chalk stick is dipped in ink, the surface turns coloured but the inside
stays white and wet. This shows
(a) only adsorption  (b) only absorption  (c) both — sorption  (d) neither

**ANSWER: (c) both — sorption**
```
  The DYE is ADSORBED on the surface (surface turns blue).
  The WATER is ABSORBED into the bulk (inside is wet).
  Both at once = SORPTION.
```

---

### Q3
Adsorption is accompanied by
(a) decrease in enthalpy and decrease in entropy
(b) decrease in enthalpy and increase in entropy
(c) increase in enthalpy and decrease in entropy
(d) increase in enthalpy and increase in entropy

**ANSWER: (a) decrease in enthalpy and decrease in entropy**
```
  Adsorption is EXOTHERMIC   ->  delta H is NEGATIVE (decrease)
  Molecules lose freedom     ->  delta S is NEGATIVE (decrease)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** For adsorption, **both delta H and delta S are negative**,
> and delta G is negative. Memorise the three signs: **— , — , —**.

---

### Q4
The correct statement is
(a) adsorption is always endothermic
(b) adsorption is always exothermic
(c) physisorption is endothermic, chemisorption is exothermic
(d) chemisorption is endothermic

**ANSWER: (b) adsorption is always exothermic**

---

# ===============================================================
# PATTERN 2 — PHYSISORPTION vs CHEMISORPTION
# ===============================================================

### Q5
Which is characteristic of **physisorption**?
(a) High enthalpy of adsorption  (b) Highly specific
(c) Multimolecular layers  (d) Irreversible

**ANSWER: (c) Multimolecular layers**
```
  Physisorption -> MULTILAYER , low enthalpy, non-specific, reversible.
  Chemisorption -> MONOLAYER  , high enthalpy, specific, irreversible.
```

---

### Q6
The enthalpy of adsorption in chemisorption lies in the range
(a) 20–40 kJ/mol  (b) 40–60 kJ/mol  (c) 80–240 kJ/mol  (d) 400–600 kJ/mol

**ANSWER: (c) 80–240 kJ/mol**

> **SHORTCUT:** **PHYSI = 20–40. CHEMI = 80–240.** These two numbers are
> asked directly, over and over. Just memorise them.

---

### Q7
Chemisorption is also called
(a) van der Waals adsorption  (b) activated adsorption
(c) physical adsorption  (d) reversible adsorption

**ANSWER: (b) activated adsorption**
```
  It needs ACTIVATION ENERGY to form the chemical bond.
```

---

### Q8
With increase in temperature, chemisorption
(a) always increases  (b) always decreases
(c) first increases then decreases  (d) is unaffected

**ANSWER: (c) first increases then decreases**
```
  Rising T first supplies the ACTIVATION ENERGY (adsorption rises).
  At still higher T the EXOTHERMIC equilibrium shifts back (it falls).
```

> **SHORTCUT:** The word "always" in options (a) and (b) is a giveaway.
> Chemisorption is the "hump" curve; physisorption is the "falling" curve.

---

### Q9
Which of the following is TRUE for physisorption?
(a) It requires high activation energy
(b) It is highly specific
(c) It is favoured by low temperature
(d) It forms a monolayer

**ANSWER: (c) It is favoured by low temperature**

---

### Q10
Physisorption can change into chemisorption on
(a) decreasing the temperature  (b) increasing the temperature
(c) decreasing the pressure  (d) adding a catalyst

**ANSWER: (b) increasing the temperature**
```
  Heating supplies the activation energy needed to form the chemical
  bond. Example: H2 on Ni is physisorbed at 190 K and chemisorbed
  at about 300 K.
```

# PATTERN 3 — WHICH GAS IS ADSORBED MOST
# ===============================================================

### Q11
Which gas is adsorbed to the greatest extent on 1 g of activated charcoal?
(a) H2  (b) N2  (c) CH4  (d) SO2

**ANSWER: (d) SO2**
```
  More EASILY LIQUEFIABLE gas  =  HIGHER critical temperature
                               =  MORE adsorption

  Critical temperatures:  SO2 630 K  >  CH4 190 K  >  N2 126 K > H2 33 K
```

> **SHORTCUT — the master rule of this pattern:**
> **The gas with the HIGHEST CRITICAL TEMPERATURE is adsorbed MOST.**
> If critical temperatures are not given, pick the gas that is **easiest to
> liquefy** — usually the most polar / highest boiling one.
> Standard order: `SO2 > NH3 > HCl > CH4 > CO > N2 > H2`

---

# PATTERN 4 — THE FREUNDLICH ISOTHERM
# ===============================================================

### Q14
In the Freundlich isotherm `x/m = k P^(1/n)`, the value of 1/n
(a) is always 1  (b) lies between 0 and 1
(c) is always greater than 1  (d) is always negative

**ANSWER: (b) lies between 0 and 1**
```
  n > 1  ->  0 < 1/n < 1
```

---

### Q15
A plot of log(x/m) against log P for the Freundlich isotherm gives a straight
line whose slope is
(a) k  (b) log k  (c) 1/n  (d) n

**ANSWER: (c) 1/n**
```
  log(x/m) = log k + (1/n) log P
              intercept    slope
```

> **SHORTCUT:** **SLOPE = 1/n. INTERCEPT = log k.** Two of the four options
> in this pattern are always k and log k — the intercept is **log k**, never k.

---

### Q16
For the Freundlich isotherm, the intercept of the log(x/m) vs log P plot on the
y-axis is 0.6021. The value of k is
(a) 0.6021  (b) 2  (c) 4  (d) 10

**ANSWER: (c) 4**
```
  intercept = log k = 0.6021
  k = 10^0.6021 = 4     (because log 4 = 0.6021)
```

---

### Q17
At high pressure, the Freundlich isotherm
(a) is exactly obeyed  (b) fails
(c) becomes linear in P  (d) predicts zero adsorption

**ANSWER: (b) fails**
```
  At high P the surface SATURATES, so x/m becomes independent of P
  (1/n -> 0). Freundlich predicts a continuing rise, so it deviates.
```

---

### Q18
If 1/n = 0 in the Freundlich isotherm, then
(a) x/m is proportional to P  (b) x/m is independent of P
(c) x/m is proportional to P^2  (d) adsorption is zero

**ANSWER: (b) x/m is independent of P**
```
  x/m = k P^0 = k  -> a constant. This is the SATURATION region.
```

---

### Q19
The Langmuir adsorption isotherm assumes
(a) multilayer adsorption  (b) monolayer adsorption
(c) no equilibrium  (d) interaction between adsorbed molecules

**ANSWER: (b) monolayer adsorption**

---

### Q20
0.5 g of charcoal adsorbs 0.2 g of a gas. The value of x/m is
(a) 0.1  (b) 0.4  (c) 2.5  (d) 0.25

**ANSWER: (b) 0.4**
```
  x/m = 0.2 / 0.5 = 0.4
```

> **SHORTCUT:** x/m means **adsorbed mass ÷ adsorbent mass**, in that order.
> Option (c) 2.5 is the inverted trap.

### Q24

**ANSWER: (b) decreasing the activation energy**

---

### Q25
A catalyst does NOT change

(c) the equilibrium constant  (d) the reaction path

**ANSWER: (c) the equilibrium constant**

> A catalyst changes **ONLY the activation energy and the path**.

> "does NOT change" question.

# PATTERN 14 — HARDY-SCHULZE RULE AND COAGULATION
# ===============================================================

### Q76
Which electrolyte is most effective in coagulating an **As2S3** sol?
(a) NaCl  (b) BaCl2  (c) AlCl3  (d) KCl

**ANSWER: (c) AlCl3**
```
  As2S3 is a metal SULPHIDE  ->  NEGATIVE sol  ->  the CATION acts.
  Al3+ (charge 3)  >  Ba2+ (charge 2)  >  Na+ / K+ (charge 1)
```

> **SHORTCUT — the three-second method for every Hardy-Schulze MCQ:**
> **1.** Decide the sign of the SOL (sulphide/metal = −, hydroxide = +).
> **2.** Look only at the ion of the OPPOSITE sign.
> **3.** Pick the one with the HIGHEST charge.

---

### Q77
Which is most effective in coagulating an **Fe(OH)3** sol?
(a) NaCl  (b) Na2SO4  (c) Na3PO4  (d) K4[Fe(CN)6]

**ANSWER: (d) K4[Fe(CN)6]**
```
  Fe(OH)3 is a HYDROXIDE  ->  POSITIVE sol  ->  the ANION acts.
  [Fe(CN)6]4- (charge 4) > PO4^3- (3) > SO4^2- (2) > Cl- (1)
```

---

### Q78
According to the Hardy-Schulze rule, the coagulating power of an ion
(a) decreases with increasing charge
(b) increases with increasing charge on the ion of opposite sign
(c) depends only on its size
(d) is independent of its charge

**ANSWER: (b) increases with increasing charge on the ion of opposite sign**

### Q79
The coagulation values of four electrolytes for a given sol are 50, 0.5, 12 and 100
millimoles per litre. Which is the strongest coagulating agent?
(a) 50  (b) 0.5  (c) 12  (d) 100

**ANSWER: (b) 0.5**
```
  Coagulating power is INVERSELY proportional to coagulation value.
  SMALLEST coagulation value = STRONGEST coagulating agent.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Both **coagulation value** and **gold number** are **INVERSE**
> measures — small number means strong. Two facts, one rule.

### Q80
Mixing an Fe(OH)3 sol with an As2S3 sol causes
(a) no change  (b) mutual coagulation
(c) formation of an emulsion  (d) peptization

**ANSWER: (b) mutual coagulation**
```
  Fe(OH)3 is POSITIVE, As2S3 is NEGATIVE. Opposite charges neutralise.
```

# ===============================================================
# PATTERN 18 — ASSERTION AND REASON (JEE style)
# ===============================================================

```
  In these questions:
    (a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A
    (b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A
    (c) A is true, R is false
    (d) A is false, R is true
```

### Q96
**Assertion:** Adsorption is always exothermic.
**Reason:** The entropy of the system decreases during adsorption.

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  Both are true, and the reason IS the explanation:
  delta S is negative, and since delta G must be negative for a
  spontaneous process, delta H must be negative -> exothermic.
```

---

### Q97
**Assertion:** A catalyst does not change the equilibrium constant.
**Reason:** A catalyst speeds up the forward and backward reactions equally.

**ANSWER: (a)** — both true, and R correctly explains A.

---

### Q98
**Assertion:** Lyophilic sols are more stable than lyophobic sols.
**Reason:** Lyophilic colloidal particles are heavily solvated by the medium.

**ANSWER: (a)** — both true, and the solvation sheath is exactly what
provides the extra stability.

---

### Q99
**Assertion:** Physisorption decreases with an increase in temperature.
**Reason:** Physisorption requires a high activation energy.

**ANSWER: (c)** — A is true, R is FALSE.
```
  Physisorption requires NO activation energy. It decreases with
  temperature because it is exothermic (Le Chatelier).
```

---

### Q100
**Assertion:** Gelatin is a better protective colloid than starch.
**Reason:** Gelatin has a larger gold number than starch.

**ANSWER: (c)** — A is true, R is FALSE.
```
  Gelatin has a SMALLER gold number (0.005-0.01) than starch (~25).
  Smaller gold number = better protection.
```
