# PATTERN 2 — Mineral vs ore, gangue, flux, slag

### Q7
Which statement is correct?

(a) All minerals are ores &nbsp; (b) All ores are minerals
(c) Minerals and ores are the same &nbsp; (d) No mineral is an ore

**ANSWER: (b)** — all ores are minerals, but all minerals are not ores.

---

### Q8
The substance added to the furnace charge to remove the gangue is called

(a) slag &nbsp; (b) flux &nbsp; (c) matte &nbsp; (d) collector

**ANSWER: (b) flux** — `flux + gangue -> slag`.

---

### Q9
If the gangue is **acidic**, the flux used should be

(a) acidic &nbsp; (b) basic &nbsp; (c) neutral &nbsp; (d) amphoteric

**ANSWER: (b) basic**

> **SHORTCUT:** Opposites cancel. Acidic gangue (SiO2) -> basic flux (CaO).
> Basic impurity (FeO) -> acidic flux (SiO2).

---

### Q10
In the extraction of copper, the flux used is

(a) CaCO3 &nbsp; (b) SiO2 &nbsp; (c) CaO &nbsp; (d) NaCN

**ANSWER: (b) SiO2**
```
  The impurity is FeO, which is BASIC, so the ACIDIC flux SiO2 is used:
      FeO + SiO2 --> FeSiO3   (slag)

  Do NOT confuse this with the BLAST FURNACE, where the gangue SiO2 is
  acidic and the flux CaCO3 is basic.
```

---

### Q11
The slag formed in the blast furnace is

(a) FeSiO3 &nbsp; (b) CaSiO3 &nbsp; (c) CaCO3 &nbsp; (d) Ca3(PO4)2

**ANSWER: (b) CaSiO3** — `CaO + SiO2 -> CaSiO3`.

# PATTERN 3 — Which concentration method?

### Q12
Froth flotation is used for the concentration of

(a) oxide ores &nbsp; (b) sulphide ores &nbsp; (c) carbonate ores &nbsp; (d) native metals

**ANSWER: (b) sulphide ores**

> **SHORTCUT:** See "S" in the formula (ZnS, PbS, CuFeS2) -> froth flotation.
> See a heavy oxide (Fe2O3, SnO2) -> hydraulic washing.
> See bauxite or gold -> leaching.
> See Fe3O4 or FeCr2O4 -> magnetic separation.

---

### Q13
Froth flotation works because

(a) the ore is denser than the gangue
(b) the ore is preferentially wetted by water
(c) the ore is preferentially wetted by oil
(d) the ore is magnetic

**ANSWER: (c)** — the ore is wetted by OIL and floats; the gangue is wetted
by WATER and sinks.

---

### Q14
The concentration of haematite is best done by

(a) froth flotation &nbsp; (b) hydraulic washing &nbsp; (c) leaching &nbsp; (d) poling

**ANSWER: (b) hydraulic washing** — Fe2O3 is a dense oxide ore, so gravity
separation works.

---

### Q15
Cassiterite (SnO2) containing wolframite (FeWO4) as an impurity is
concentrated by

(a) froth flotation &nbsp; (b) magnetic separation &nbsp; (c) leaching &nbsp; (d) liquation

**ANSWER: (b) magnetic separation**
```
  Here the GANGUE (FeWO4) is magnetic, not the ore. The magnet removes
  the impurity. Magnetic separation works either way round.
```

### Q16
In froth flotation, NaCN acts as a

(a) collector &nbsp; (b) froth stabiliser &nbsp; (c) depressant &nbsp; (d) frothing agent

**ANSWER: (c) depressant**

### Q17
NaCN is used to separate ZnS from PbS because

(a) it dissolves PbS &nbsp; (b) it forms a complex with ZnS so ZnS does not float
(c) it forms a complex with PbS so PbS does not float &nbsp; (d) it oxidises ZnS

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
      4 NaCN  +  ZnS  -->  Na2[Zn(CN)4]  +  Na2S

  The complex coats the ZnS, which then sinks. PbS floats in the froth.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** NaCN **D**epresses **Z**inc. Remember "CN Depresses Zn" —
> in flotation. In LEACHING, the same NaCN dissolves gold and silver.
> Two different jobs, same chemical. Read the question.

### Q19
Cresols are added in froth flotation as a

# PATTERN 5 — Calcination vs roasting

### Q20
Roasting is generally carried out for

(a) carbonate ores &nbsp; (b) sulphide ores &nbsp; (c) oxide ores &nbsp; (d) native metals

**ANSWER: (b) sulphide ores**

---

### Q21
Which process is used for the ore FeCO3?

(a) roasting &nbsp; (b) calcination &nbsp; (c) leaching &nbsp; (d) smelting

**ANSWER: (b) calcination** — it is a carbonate: `FeCO3 -> FeO + CO2`.

> **SHORTCUT:** Look at the FORMULA, not the metal.
> Contains **CO3** or **.xH2O** -> CALCINATION -> gives CO2 / H2O.
> Contains **S** -> ROASTING -> gives SO2.

### Q22
The gas evolved during the roasting of zinc blende is

(a) CO2 &nbsp; (b) SO2 &nbsp; (c) SO3 &nbsp; (d) H2S

**ANSWER: (b) SO2** — `2 ZnS + 3 O2 -> 2 ZnO + 2 SO2`.

### Q23
Which of the following is NOT true about calcination?

(a) It is carried out in the absence of air
(b) CO2 is evolved from carbonate ores
(c) It is an oxidation process
(d) It makes the ore porous

**ANSWER: (c)** — calcination is a **thermal decomposition**, not an oxidation.
Roasting is the oxidation.

---

### Q24
`4 FeS2 + 11 O2 -> 2 Fe2O3 + 8 SO2` is an example of

(a) calcination &nbsp; (b) roasting &nbsp; (c) smelting &nbsp; (d) leaching

**ANSWER: (b) roasting** — sulphide + excess O2.

# PATTERN 6 — Ellingham diagram

### Q25
In an Ellingham diagram, the Y-axis represents

(a) enthalpy of formation &nbsp; (b) Gibbs energy of formation of the oxide
(c) entropy &nbsp; (d) equilibrium constant

**ANSWER: (b)** — delta G of formation, per mole of O2, plotted against
temperature on the X-axis.

---

### Q26
Most metal oxide lines in an Ellingham diagram slope **upward** because

(a) delta H is positive &nbsp; (b) delta S is negative
(c) delta S is positive &nbsp; (d) delta G is always positive

**ANSWER: (b) delta S is negative**
```
  2 M(s) + O2(g) --> 2 MO(s)     a gas is consumed, so delta S < 0.
  Slope = ( - delta S ) = POSITIVE  ->  the line rises.
```

---

### Q27
The `2C + O2 -> 2CO` line slopes downward because

(a) it is endothermic &nbsp; (b) 1 mole of gas becomes 2 moles of gas
(c) CO is unstable &nbsp; (d) carbon is a solid

**ANSWER: (b)** — delta S is POSITIVE, so delta G becomes more negative as T rises.

> **SHORTCUT:** Count the moles of GAS on each side.
> Gas moles increase -> delta S positive -> the line falls.
> Gas moles decrease -> delta S negative -> the line rises.

---

### Q28
On the Ellingham diagram, element A can reduce the oxide of B if

(a) A's line lies above B's &nbsp; (b) A's line lies below B's
(c) the two lines are parallel &nbsp; (d) A has a higher melting point

**ANSWER: (b)** — **LOWER LINE REDUCES HIGHER LINE.**

---

### Q29
Which of the following is a limitation of the Ellingham diagram?

(a) It does not give the rate of the reaction
(b) It does not give delta G values
(c) It cannot be plotted for oxides
(d) It applies only to sulphides

**ANSWER: (a)** — it gives thermodynamic feasibility only, never kinetics.

---

### Q30
Below about 1350 deg C, which reaction is feasible?

(a) `2Al + 3MgO -> Al2O3 + 3Mg` &nbsp; (b) `3Mg + Al2O3 -> 3MgO + 2Al`
(c) both &nbsp; (d) neither

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Below 1350 deg C the MgO line lies BELOW the Al2O3 line, so MgO is the
  more stable oxide -> Mg reduces Al2O3.
  ABOVE 1350 deg C the lines have crossed, so Al reduces MgO.
```

In the Bayer process, the impurity removed as red mud is

**ANSWER: (b) Fe2O3 and TiO2** — they are basic, so they do not dissolve in

### Q38
Alumina dissolves in NaOH in the Bayer process because Al2O3 is

(a) basic &nbsp; (b) acidic &nbsp; (c) amphoteric &nbsp; (d) neutral

**ANSWER: (c) amphoteric** — it reacts with both acids and bases.

### Q39
The role of cryolite in the Hall-Heroult process is to

(a) act as the source of aluminium
(b) lower the melting point of alumina and increase conductivity
(c) act as the cathode
(d) oxidise the impurities

**ANSWER: (b)** — it brings the melting point down from ~2323 K to ~1140 K.

> **SHORTCUT:** Cryolite is a **SOLVENT**, never a source of Al. If an option
> says cryolite supplies the aluminium, it is wrong.

---

### Q40
In the Hall-Heroult cell, the cathode is

(a) the graphite rods &nbsp; (b) the graphite lining of the iron tank
(c) molten cryolite &nbsp; (d) a platinum plate

**ANSWER: (b) the graphite lining** — the graphite RODS dipping in are the anode.

### Q41
The overall reaction in the Hall-Heroult process is

(a) `Al2O3 -> 2Al + 3/2 O2` &nbsp; (b) `2 Al2O3 + 3 C -> 4 Al + 3 CO2`
(c) `Al2O3 + 3 C -> 2 Al + 3 CO` &nbsp; (d) `Al2O3 + 3 H2 -> 2 Al + 3 H2O`

**ANSWER: (b)** — the carbon anode is consumed, which is why it must be
replaced periodically.

---

### Q42
The mass of aluminium deposited by 1 faraday of charge is (Al = 27)

(a) 27 g &nbsp; (b) 13.5 g &nbsp; (c) 9 g &nbsp; (d) 3 g

**ANSWER: (c) 9 g**
```
  Al3+  +  3 e-  -->  Al        n = 3

  1 F deposits 1 equivalent mass = atomic mass / n = 27 / 3 = 9 g
```

> **SHORTCUT:** 1 F always deposits ONE EQUIVALENT MASS = atomic mass / charge.
> Ag+ (n=1) -> 108 g ; Cu2+ (n=2) -> 31.75 g ; Al3+ (n=3) -> 9 g.

> pig iron (4%) > cast iron (3%) > steel (0.2-1.5%) > wrought iron (0.2-0.5%)

### Q48
Stainless steel is an alloy of iron with

**ANSWER: (a) Cr and Ni**

### Q49
The percentage of iron in pure haematite (Fe = 56, O = 16) is

# PATTERN 11 — Refining methods

### Q54
Which metal is refined by **liquation**?

(a) Zn &nbsp; (b) Sn &nbsp; (c) Ni &nbsp; (d) Ge

**ANSWER: (b) Sn** — tin has a low melting point and runs down a sloping hearth.

> **SHORTCUT — the refining match-up table, learn it as one block:**
> ```
>   Zn , Hg    ->  DISTILLATION       (volatile metal)
>   Sn , Pb    ->  LIQUATION          (low melting point)
>   Cu , Sn    ->  POLING             (impurity is its own oxide)
>   Si , Ge    ->  ZONE REFINING      (semiconductors, ultra-pure)
>   Ni         ->  MOND process       (CO)
>   Zr , Ti    ->  VAN ARKEL          (I2 + tungsten filament)
>   Cu, Ag, Au ->  ELECTROLYTIC       (anode mud!)
>   lanthanoids->  CHROMATOGRAPHY
> ```

### Q59
The van Arkel method is particularly useful because it removes

### Q60
During the electrolytic refining of copper, the anode mud contains

(a) Zn, Fe, Ni &nbsp; (b) Ag, Au, Pt &nbsp; (c) CuSO4 &nbsp; (d) H2SO4

**ANSWER: (b) Ag, Au, Pt** (and Se, Te)
```
  These are LESS electropositive than copper, so they are not oxidised
  at the anode and simply drop off.
  Zn, Fe and Ni ARE oxidised and go into SOLUTION - they stay in the
  electrolyte, not in the mud.
```

---

### Q61
In the electrolytic refining of a metal, the impure metal is made the

(a) cathode &nbsp; (b) anode &nbsp; (c) electrolyte &nbsp; (d) salt bridge

**ANSWER: (b) anode**

> **SHORTCUT:** **I**mpure = **A**node (it dissolves). **P**ure = **C**athode
> (it grows). "The impure one wastes away."

The relation between the Gibbs energy change and the cell EMF is

### Q67
For the reaction `Zn + Cu2+ -> Zn2+ + Cu` with E = 1.10 V, delta G is
approximately (F = 96500 C)

(a) -212 kJ &nbsp; (b) -106 kJ &nbsp; (c) +212 kJ &nbsp; (d) -424 kJ

**ANSWER: (a) -212 kJ**
```
  n = 2

  delta G = - n F E = - (2)(96500)(1.10)
          = - 212 300 J
          = - 212.3 kJ
```

### Q68
Metals like Na, K, Ca and Mg are obtained by electrolysis of their FUSED
salts rather than aqueous solutions because

(a) their salts are insoluble in water
(b) water would be reduced in preference to the metal ion
(c) the fused salt is cheaper
(d) their ions are not mobile in water

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  2 H2O + 2 e- -> H2 + 2 OH-     E = - 0.83 V
  Na+ + e-     -> Na             E = - 2.71 V

  Water is far easier to reduce, so you would get H2, not Na.
```

Which of the following is NOT a method of concentration of ores?

(a) froth flotation &nbsp; (b) magnetic separation &nbsp; (c) zone refining &nbsp; (d) leaching

**ANSWER: (c) zone refining** — that is a REFINING method, not a concentration
method.

> **SHORTCUT:** Concentration happens BEFORE reduction. Refining happens AFTER.
> If the option is about purifying the METAL, it is refining. If it is about
> removing GANGUE from the ore, it is concentration.
