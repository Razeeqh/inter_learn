## Topic: Vocabulary

### Q A1
What is the difference between a **mineral** and an **ore**? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MINERAL - ANY naturally occurring compound of a metal found in the
            earth's crust.

  ORE     - a mineral from which the metal can be extracted CONVENIENTLY
            and PROFITABLY.

  ALL ORES ARE MINERALS, BUT ALL MINERALS ARE NOT ORES.

  EXAMPLE:  Bauxite  Al2O3.2H2O          ->  an ORE of aluminium
            Clay     Al2O3.2SiO2.2H2O    ->  a MINERAL, but NOT an ore
                                             (extraction is too costly)
```

---

### Q A2
What are **gangue** and **flux**?

**ANSWER:**
```
  GANGUE (or MATRIX) - the earthy / rocky / sandy unwanted impurities
                       present along with the ore. Example: SiO2, clay.

  FLUX               - a substance added to the charge which combines with
                       the gangue to form an easily fusible SLAG.

                       FLUX  +  GANGUE   ------>   SLAG
```

---

### Q A3
What is **slag**? Give the reaction that forms it in the blast furnace.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SLAG is the fusible (low melting) waste product formed when the FLUX
  combines with the GANGUE. It is LIGHTER than the molten metal, so it
  FLOATS on top and is skimmed off separately.

  In the blast furnace:

      CaCO3  --(heat)-->  CaO  +  CO2

      CaO   +   SiO2   -->   CaSiO3        <--- the SLAG
      FLUX      GANGUE        (calcium silicate)
```

---

### Q A4
Define **calcination**. Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CALCINATION is the process of heating the concentrated ore strongly in
  the ABSENCE (or limited supply) of air, at a temperature below the
  melting point of the metal.

  It is used for CARBONATE and HYDRATED OXIDE ores.

  Example:      ZnCO3  --(heat, no air)-->  ZnO  +  CO2
```

---

### Q A5
Define **roasting**. Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ROASTING is the process of heating the concentrated ore strongly in the
  PRESENCE OF EXCESS AIR, at a temperature below the melting point of the
  metal.

  It is used for SULPHIDE ores.

  Example:      2 ZnS  +  3 O2  --(heat, excess air)-->  2 ZnO  +  2 SO2
```

---

### Q A6
What is **smelting**?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SMELTING is the reduction of a metal oxide to the metal by heating it
  with a reducing agent such as carbon (coke) or carbon monoxide in a
  furnace, usually together with a FLUX which removes the gangue as slag.

  Example:      Fe2O3  +  3 CO  --(heat)-->  2 Fe  +  3 CO2
```

## Topic: Concentration

### Q A7
What is **froth flotation**? Which type of ore is it used for?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FROTH FLOTATION is a method of concentrating an ore based on the fact
  that the ORE particles are preferentially WETTED BY OIL while the
  GANGUE particles are preferentially WETTED BY WATER.

  Air is blown through a suspension of the powdered ore in water + pine
  oil; the oil-coated ore rises as a FROTH and is skimmed off, while the
  gangue sinks.

  USED FOR:  SULPHIDE ORES  -  ZnS, PbS, CuFeS2.
```

---

### Q A8
What is a **depressant**? Give an example and explain its use.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A DEPRESSANT is a substance added in froth flotation that PREVENTS ONE
  particular ore from forming a froth, so that two ores present together
  can be separated.

  EXAMPLE:  Sodium cyanide, NaCN.

  When an ore contains both ZnS and PbS:

      4 NaCN  +  ZnS  -->  Na2[Zn(CN)4]  +  Na2S

  A layer of the complex forms on the ZnS, so ZnS does NOT float.
  PbS floats in the froth. The two are thus separated.
```

---

### Q A9
Name the **collector** and the **froth stabiliser** used in froth flotation
and state their functions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  COLLECTOR         :  pine oil, fatty acids, XANTHATES
                       FUNCTION - makes the ore particles WATER-REPELLENT
                       so they attach to the air bubbles and float.

  FROTH STABILISER  :  cresols, aniline
                       FUNCTION - STABILISES the froth so the bubbles do
                       not burst before the froth is skimmed off.
```

### Q A10
What is **leaching**? Which ore is concentrated by leaching with NaOH?

**ANSWER:**
```
  LEACHING is the concentration of an ore by DISSOLVING it in a suitable
  chemical reagent in which the GANGUE is insoluble; the solution is then
  filtered and the metal recovered from it.

  BAUXITE (Al2O3.2H2O) is leached with hot concentrated NaOH:

      Al2O3 + 2 NaOH + 3 H2O --(473-523 K)--> 2 Na[Al(OH)4]

  Fe2O3 and TiO2 do not dissolve and are removed as RED MUD.
  This is the BAYER PROCESS.
```

---

### Q A11
What is **red mud**?

**ANSWER:**
```
  RED MUD is the insoluble residue left behind during the BAYER PROCESS
  when bauxite is digested with hot concentrated NaOH.

  It consists mainly of  Fe2O3  and  TiO2  (and some SiO2), which are
  NOT amphoteric and therefore do not dissolve in NaOH. It is filtered
  off and discarded. Its red colour is due to the iron(III) oxide.
```

## Topic: Thermodynamics

### Q A12
What is the criterion for a reduction reaction to be feasible?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The Gibbs energy change of the overall reaction must be NEGATIVE:

           delta G   =   delta H   -   T  delta S     <   0

  If delta G is negative the reaction is SPONTANEOUS (feasible).
  If delta G is zero the system is at equilibrium.
  If delta G is positive the reaction will not occur on its own.
```

---

### Q A13
What is an **Ellingham diagram**? What is plotted on each axis?

**ANSWER:**
```
  An ELLINGHAM DIAGRAM is a graph of the standard Gibbs energy of
  FORMATION of metal oxides against TEMPERATURE.

      Y-AXIS  :  delta G of formation of the oxide,
                 expressed PER MOLE OF O2 CONSUMED
      X-AXIS  :  TEMPERATURE

  It is used to choose a suitable REDUCING AGENT and the TEMPERATURE at
  which a given metal oxide can be reduced.
```

---

### Q A14
Why does the line for `2 C + O2 -> 2 CO` slope DOWNWARD in the Ellingham diagram?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In       2 C(s)  +  O2(g)   -->   2 CO(g)

  ONE mole of gas is converted into TWO moles of gas, so the disorder
  INCREASES  ->  delta S is POSITIVE.

      delta G  =  delta H  -  T delta S

  With delta S positive, the term ( - T delta S ) becomes more and more
  negative as T rises, so delta G becomes MORE NEGATIVE.

  Hence the line slopes DOWNWARD.
```

---

### Q A15
State any two **limitations** of the Ellingham diagram.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. It gives information only about the FEASIBILITY (thermodynamics) of
     a reduction. It says NOTHING about the RATE (kinetics) - a feasible
     reaction may still be far too slow to be useful.

  2. It assumes that the reactants and products are in EQUILIBRIUM, which
     is often not the case in an actual furnace.

  (Also acceptable: the simple interpretation breaks down when the
   products react further or form alloys/solutions with each other.)
```

---

### Q A16
Write the relation between the Gibbs energy change and the EMF of a cell.
State the condition for the reaction to be feasible.

**ANSWER:**
```
                  delta G   =   -  n  F  E

      n = number of moles of electrons transferred
      F = Faraday constant = 96500 C / mol
      E = EMF of the cell in volts

  CONDITION:  If E is POSITIVE then delta G is NEGATIVE, and the
              reaction is FEASIBLE (spontaneous).
```

## Topic: Aluminium

### Q A17
What is the role of **cryolite** in the Hall-Heroult process?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CRYOLITE, Na3AlF6, is added to the alumina for two reasons:

  1. It LOWERS THE MELTING POINT of alumina from about 2323 K to about
     1140 K, saving an enormous amount of energy.

  2. It INCREASES THE ELECTRICAL CONDUCTIVITY of the molten mixture.

  NOTE: cryolite is only a SOLVENT. The aluminium comes from the Al2O3.
```

---

### Q A18
Why is the graphite anode in the Hall-Heroult cell replaced from time to time?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The oxygen liberated at the anode

        2 O2-  -->  O2  +  4 e-

  immediately attacks the hot carbon anode:

        C  +  O2   -->  CO2
        C  +  1/2 O2  -->  CO

  So the graphite anode is gradually BURNT AWAY (consumed) and must be
  replaced periodically. (This burning is actually useful - it lowers
  the voltage needed for the electrolysis.)
```

---

### Q A19
Write the cathode and anode reactions in the Hall-Heroult process.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Ionisation:      Al2O3   -->   2 Al3+   +   3 O2-

  AT THE CATHODE (graphite lining, reduction):

                   Al3+  +  3 e-   -->   Al(l)

  AT THE ANODE (graphite rods, oxidation):

                   2 O2-   -->   O2   +   4 e-
                   C  +  O2  -->  CO2

  OVERALL:         2 Al2O3  +  3 C   -->   4 Al  +  3 CO2
```

## Topic: Iron, copper, zinc

### Q A20
What is the role of **limestone** in the blast furnace?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Limestone CaCO3 is added as a FLUX.

  1)   CaCO3   --(heat)-->   CaO  +  CO2

  2)   The CaO is BASIC and combines with the ACIDIC silica gangue:

       CaO   +   SiO2   -->   CaSiO3       <--- fusible SLAG

  The slag is lighter, floats on the molten iron, and is tapped off
  separately. The CO2 released also reacts with coke to give more CO,
  which is the reducing agent.
```

### Q A24
Why is zinc obtained as a vapour in its extraction?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The reduction  ZnO + C --> Zn + CO  is carried out at about 1673 K.

  But the BOILING POINT of zinc is only 1180 K.

  Since the reduction temperature is well ABOVE the boiling point of the
  metal, the zinc is formed as a VAPOUR and distils out of the retort.
  It is then condensed in a receiver. The product is called SPELTER.
```

## Topic: Refining

### Q A25
What is **anode mud**? What does it contain?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANODE MUD (anode sludge) is the insoluble residue that settles BELOW
  THE ANODE during the electrolytic refining of a metal.

  It contains the LESS ELECTROPOSITIVE impurities which are not oxidised
  at the working anode potential and simply fall off as the metal around
  them dissolves:

        Ag , Au , Pt , Se , Te

  It is commercially valuable - recovering these metals often pays for
  the whole refining operation.
```

---

### Q A26
What is **zone refining**? Which metals are purified by it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ZONE REFINING is based on the principle that IMPURITIES ARE MORE
  SOLUBLE IN THE MOLTEN METAL THAN IN THE SOLID METAL.

  A circular mobile heater melts a narrow zone of a rod of the metal and
  is moved slowly along the rod, always in the SAME direction. The
  impurities travel with the molten zone to one end, which is then cut
  off. The process is repeated several times.

  USED FOR:  Si , Ge , Ga , B , In  -  the SEMICONDUCTOR elements, where
             extremely high purity is required.
```

---

### Q A27
What is **Mond's process**? Write the equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MOND'S PROCESS is the VAPOUR PHASE refining of NICKEL.

  STEP 1  Impure nickel is heated in a stream of CO at 330-350 K, giving
          the volatile complex nickel tetracarbonyl:

              Ni  +  4 CO  --(330-350 K)-->  Ni(CO)4

  STEP 2  The vapour is heated to 450-470 K, where it decomposes:

              Ni(CO)4  --(450-470 K)-->  Ni  +  4 CO

  Pure nickel is obtained; the CO is recycled.
```

---

### Q A28
Describe the **van Arkel method** and name the metals refined by it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The VAN ARKEL (iodide) method is used to obtain ULTRA-PURE ZIRCONIUM
  and TITANIUM, free from OXYGEN and NITROGEN.

  STEP 1  The crude metal is heated with iodine at about 870 K:

              Zr  +  2 I2   -->   ZrI4        (volatile)

  STEP 2  The vapour is passed over a WHITE HOT TUNGSTEN FILAMENT at
          about 1800 K, where it decomposes and pure metal deposits on
          the filament:

              ZrI4  --(1800 K, W filament)-->  Zr  +  2 I2

  Metals: ZIRCONIUM and TITANIUM (nuclear grade).
```

---

### Q A29
What is **poling**? Which metals are refined by it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  POLING is a refining method used when the impurity present is the
  OXIDE OF THE METAL ITSELF.

  The molten impure metal is stirred with GREEN WOOD POLES. The wood
  gives off hydrocarbons such as CH4, which act as REDUCING AGENTS and
  reduce the oxide back to the metal:

        Cu2O  +  CH4   -->   Cu  +  CO  +  H2O   (approximately)

  USED FOR:  COPPER (to remove Cu2O)  and  TIN (to remove SnO2).
```

---

### Q A30
Which metals are refined by **distillation** and which by **liquation**? Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DISTILLATION  -  Zn , Hg , Cd
                   BECAUSE these metals are VOLATILE (low boiling point)
                   while the impurities are non-volatile. The metal is
                   boiled off and the vapour condensed.

  LIQUATION     -  Sn , Pb , Bi
                   BECAUSE these metals have a LOW MELTING POINT. On a
                   sloping hearth the metal melts and flows down, while
                   the higher melting impurities stay behind.
```

### Q B1
Explain **froth flotation** with a neat labelled diagram. Mention the role of
collectors, froth stabilisers and depressants.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE
  ---------
  Sulphide ore particles are preferentially WETTED BY OIL, while the
  gangue particles are preferentially WETTED BY WATER. When air is blown
  through, the oil-coated ore particles cling to the bubbles and rise as
  a froth; the water-wetted gangue sinks.

  DIAGRAM
  -------
              oo oo ooo oo ooo   <-- FROTH carrying the ORE (skimmed off)
             oOoOoOoOoOoOoOoOo
        +----ooooooooooooooooo----+
        |     ^   ^   ^   ^  ^    |
        |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  |
        |  ~  WATER + PINE OIL  ~ |     ||  agitator
        |  ~  + collector       ~ |    ====
        |  ~  + stabiliser      ~ |     ||
        |  ~   *  *   *   *     ~ |
        |  ~     *  *  *   *    ~ |  <-- GANGUE, water-wetted, SINKS
        |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ |
        +----- ^^^^^^^^^ ---------+
               compressed AIR blown in

        o = ore particle (oil-wetted)   * = gangue (water-wetted)

  ROLE OF THE REAGENTS
  --------------------
  COLLECTOR (pine oil, fatty acids, xanthates)
        makes the ORE surface water-repellent so it attaches to the
        air bubbles and floats.

  FROTH STABILISER (cresols, aniline)
        stabilises the froth so the bubbles do not burst before the
        froth is skimmed off.

  DEPRESSANT (NaCN, NaOH)
        prevents ONE ore from floating, allowing two ores to be
        separated:

            4 NaCN  +  ZnS  -->  Na2[Zn(CN)4]  +  Na2S

        ZnS is coated and sinks; PbS floats. So galena is separated
        from zinc blende.

  SUITABLE FOR: SULPHIDE ores - ZnS, PbS, CuFeS2.
```

### Q B3
Explain **leaching** with the Bayer process for bauxite. Write the equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LEACHING: concentrating an ore by DISSOLVING it in a reagent in which
  the gangue is insoluble, then filtering and recovering the metal.

  BAYER PROCESS - bauxite contains SiO2, Fe2O3 and TiO2 as impurities.
  Al2O3 is AMPHOTERIC, so it dissolves in hot conc. NaOH; Fe2O3 and TiO2
  are basic and do not.

  STEP 1  Digestion with 45% NaOH at 473-523 K under 35 bar:

     Al2O3(s) + 2 NaOH(aq) + 3 H2O(l) --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4](aq)
                                          sodium tetrahydroxoaluminate

     Fe2O3 and TiO2 are filtered off as RED MUD.

  STEP 2  Neutralise the filtrate with CO2 gas and seed it with a little
          freshly prepared hydrated alumina:

     2 Na[Al(OH)4](aq) + 2 CO2(g)  -->  Al2O3.x H2O(s) + 2 NaHCO3(aq)

  STEP 3  Filter, dry and heat the precipitate to 1470 K:

     Al2O3 . x H2O  --(1470 K)-->  Al2O3  +  x H2O

  RESULT: pure ALUMINA, ready for electrolysis.

     BAUXITE --NaOH--> Na[Al(OH)4] --filter--> (RED MUD out)
                              |
                            CO2 + seeding
                              v
                       Al2O3.xH2O --1470K--> PURE Al2O3
```

### Q B4
Explain the **cyanide process** for the extraction of silver / gold.
Write all the equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  This is the MacARTHUR-FORREST or CYANIDE PROCESS - a leaching method.

  STEP 1  LEACHING. The finely crushed ore is treated with a dilute
          (0.4-0.6 %) solution of NaCN with a current of AIR blown
          through. Air OXIDISES the metal so it can form the complex:

     4 Au + 8 NaCN + 2 H2O + O2  -->  4 Na[Au(CN)2]  +  4 NaOH
                                      sodium dicyanoaurate(I)

     4 Ag + 8 NaCN + 2 H2O + O2  -->  4 Na[Ag(CN)2]  +  4 NaOH

     (From argentite:  Ag2S + 4 NaCN <--> 2 Na[Ag(CN)2] + Na2S )

  STEP 2  DISPLACEMENT with ZINC DUST. Zinc is more electropositive, so
          it reduces the metal out of the complex:

     2 Na[Au(CN)2] + Zn  -->  Na2[Zn(CN)4]  +  2 Au

     2 Na[Ag(CN)2] + Zn  -->  Na2[Zn(CN)4]  +  2 Ag

  ROLE OF O2  :  OXIDISING agent - converts M(0) to M(+1) so it can complex
  ROLE OF Zn  :  REDUCING agent  - displaces the metal from the complex
  ROLE OF NaCN:  LEACHING / COMPLEXING agent
```

### Q B6
What is the **Ellingham diagram**? Explain how it helps in choosing a reducing
agent. State its limitations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  ----------
  A plot of the standard Gibbs energy of FORMATION of metal oxides
  (Y-axis, expressed PER MOLE OF O2 consumed) against TEMPERATURE
  (X-axis).

  THE SHAPE OF THE LINES
  ----------------------
  For  2 M(s) + O2(g) -> 2 MO(s)   a gas is consumed, so delta S is
  NEGATIVE, and since slope = ( - delta S ), most metal lines slope
  UPWARD.

  For  2 C(s) + O2(g) -> 2 CO(g)   1 mole of gas becomes 2 moles, so
  delta S is POSITIVE and the carbon line slopes DOWNWARD.

     delta G
       0 +-------------------------------------------
         |                                     ,-'
         |   Cu2O ......................   ,-'   <-- 2C + O2 -> 2CO
    -200 |   NiO  ____________________ ,-'
         |   FeO  __________________,-'  X
    -400 |   ZnO  ______________,-'
         |   Al2O3 __________,-'
    -600 |   MgO  ________,-'
         +-------------------------------------------> T

  HOW TO USE IT
  -------------
  1. Locate the line of the oxide to be reduced.
  2. Any element whose line lies BELOW it at some temperature can
     reduce that oxide (LOWER LINE REDUCES HIGHER LINE).
  3. The CROSSING POINT of the two lines gives the MINIMUM temperature
     at which the reduction becomes feasible.

  Examples: the Al2O3 line lies below Cr2O3 and Fe2O3, so Al reduces
  them (thermite). The carbon line drops below FeO above about 1000 K,
  so coke reduces FeO in the blast furnace. The Al2O3 and MgO lines lie
  BELOW the carbon line up to about 2000 deg C, so carbon cannot reduce
  them economically - Al and Mg must be got by ELECTROLYSIS.

  LIMITATIONS
  -----------
  1. It shows only the FEASIBILITY, not the RATE (no kinetic data).
  2. It assumes reactants and products are at EQUILIBRIUM.
  3. The simple reading fails if the products react further or form
     solutions / alloys with each other.

### Q B7
Explain the **thermite (aluminothermic) process** with equations. Why is
a magnesium ribbon used?

**ANSWER:**
```
  When a metal oxide cannot be reduced by carbon (because carbon would
  form a carbide, or the temperature needed is impractically high),
  ALUMINIUM POWDER is used as the reducing agent. Aluminium has a very
  high affinity for oxygen - the Al2O3 line lies low on the Ellingham
  diagram.

  REACTIONS
      Cr2O3    +  2 Al   -->   2 Cr  +  Al2O3
      3 Mn3O4  +  8 Al   -->   9 Mn  +  4 Al2O3
      Fe2O3    +  2 Al   -->   2 Fe  +  Al2O3     (THERMITE reaction)
      B2O3     +  2 Al   -->   2 B   +  Al2O3

  All are HIGHLY EXOTHERMIC - the heat produced (about 3000 deg C) melts
  the metal, which is collected in the molten state.

  THE MIXTURES
      Thermite mixture  =  Fe2O3 + Al powder  (about 3 : 1 by mass)
      Ignition mixture  =  BaO2 + Mg ribbon

  WHY THE Mg RIBBON?
      The reaction has a HIGH ACTIVATION ENERGY and will not start on
      its own. The burning magnesium ribbon supplies the large initial
      heat needed to ignite the mixture, and the BaO2 supplies oxygen
      to keep the magnesium burning.

  USE: THERMITE WELDING of cracked machine parts and railway rails -
       molten iron is poured into the gap between the two rails.
```

### Q B8
Explain **electrolytic refining** with the example of copper. What is anode mud?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE
  The impure metal is made the ANODE and a thin strip of the pure metal
  the CATHODE, in a solution of a soluble salt of the metal. On passing
  current, the metal dissolves from the anode and is deposited pure on
  the cathode.

  FOR COPPER
      ANODE       :  block of IMPURE copper           (+)
      CATHODE     :  thin strip of PURE copper        (-)
      ELECTROLYTE :  CuSO4 solution acidified with dilute H2SO4

          (+) ANODE                     CATHODE (-)
          impure Cu                     pure Cu strip
              ||                              ||

        |  ~~~||~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~||~~~  |
        |  ~   CuSO4 + dil. H2SO4                ~   |

### Q B9
Explain **zone refining** and **vapour phase refining** with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ZONE REFINING
  -------------
  PRINCIPLE: impurities are MORE SOLUBLE IN THE MOLTEN METAL than in the
  SOLID metal.

         ____

   |PURE|MELT|   still to be purified                     |
   =====[####]=============================================
   ^                                                      ^
   pure end                                    impurities collect HERE

      nuclear-grade Zr and Ti.

### Q B10
Explain the **self reduction** method with copper and lead as examples.
What is blister copper?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SELF REDUCTION (auto-reduction / air reduction) is used for the
  sulphide ores of the LESS reactive metals. No external reducing agent
  is needed - part of the sulphide is roasted to the oxide, and the
  remaining SULPHIDE then reduces that oxide.

  COPPER
     Partial roasting:   2 Cu2S  +  3 O2   -->   2 Cu2O  +  2 SO2
     Self reduction  :   2 Cu2O  +  Cu2S   -->   6 Cu    +    SO2

  LEAD
     Partial roasting:   2 PbS   +  3 O2   -->   2 PbO   +  2 SO2
     Self reduction  :   2 PbO   +  PbS    -->   3 Pb    +    SO2

  MERCURY (simplest - roasting alone gives the metal)
                         HgS   +   O2      -->     Hg    +    SO2

  BLISTER COPPER
     The copper obtained by self reduction in the Bessemer converter is
     about 98 % pure. As it solidifies, the dissolved SO2 escapes and
     leaves BLISTERS on the surface - hence the name BLISTER COPPER.
     It is then purified by electrolytic refining to 99.95 %.
```

### Q B11
Give the **electrochemical principles** of metallurgy. Why are highly
electropositive metals extracted by electrolysis?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE RELATION
      delta G  =  -  n  F  E

      n = moles of electrons ;  F = 96500 C ;  E = cell EMF in volts
      E(cell) = E(cathode) - E(anode)

  If E is POSITIVE, delta G is NEGATIVE and the reaction is FEASIBLE.

  WHY ELECTROLYSIS FOR ELECTROPOSITIVE METALS
      A metal such as Na, K, Ca, Mg or Al has a LARGE NEGATIVE reduction
      potential. Its ion is therefore VERY DIFFICULT to reduce. On the
      Ellingham diagram its oxide line lies very low - the oxide is
      extremely stable - so carbon, CO and H2 are not strong enough
      reducing agents at any practical temperature.

      Electricity can supply an unlimited "reducing power" (the cathode
      is simply made negative enough), so ELECTROLYSIS is used.

  WHY THE FUSED (MOLTEN) SALT AND NOT AN AQUEOUS SOLUTION
      2 H2O + 2 e-  -->  H2 + 2 OH-      E = - 0.83 V
      Na+   +   e-  -->  Na              E = - 2.71 V

      Water would be reduced in preference to the metal ion, giving
      hydrogen instead of sodium. So the ANHYDROUS MOLTEN salt is used.

  EXAMPLES
      Na  -  fused NaCl + CaCl2            (Down's process)
      Al  -  Al2O3 in molten Na3AlF6+CaF2  (Hall-Heroult)
      Mg  -  fused MgCl2 + NaCl + CaCl2    (Dow process)
```

### Q B12
Write the outline of the **extraction of zinc** from zinc blende, with equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ORE: zinc blende, ZnS

  STEP 1  CONCENTRATION by FROTH FLOTATION (it is a sulphide ore).

  STEP 2  ROASTING in excess air in a reverberatory furnace:

              2 ZnS  +  3 O2   --(heat)-->   2 ZnO  +  2 SO2

          (If the ore is calamine ZnCO3, CALCINATION is used instead:
              ZnCO3  --(heat)-->  ZnO  +  CO2 )

  STEP 3  REDUCTION (smelting) with coke in a fire-clay retort at
          about 1673 K:

              ZnO   +   C   --(1673 K)-->   Zn   +   CO

          Zinc boils at 1180 K, so it distils out as a VAPOUR and is
          condensed. Coal dust is packed in the receivers to keep the
          atmosphere reducing, otherwise the zinc vapour is re-oxidised
          by CO2. The product, about 97-98 % pure, is called SPELTER.

  STEP 4  REFINING by DISTILLATION, or by ELECTROLYTIC REFINING using
          ZnSO4 + dilute H2SO4 as the electrolyte.
```

### Q B13
Write the outline of the **extraction of copper** from copper pyrites,
with equations.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ORE: copper pyrites, CuFeS2

  STEP 1  CONCENTRATION by FROTH FLOTATION.

  STEP 2  ROASTING in a limited supply of air:

              2 CuFeS2  +  O2   -->   Cu2S  +  2 FeS  +  SO2

          Impurities are burnt off:
              S + O2 -> SO2 ;   4 As + 3 O2 -> 2 As2O3

  STEP 3  SMELTING in a blast furnace with SILICA as the flux:

              2 FeS  +  3 O2   -->   2 FeO  +  2 SO2
              FeO    +  SiO2   -->   FeSiO3            <-- SLAG removed
              BASIC     ACIDIC
              impurity  FLUX

          The molten Cu2S + FeS layer left behind is COPPER MATTE.

  STEP 4  BESSEMERISATION - the matte is placed in a silica-lined
          Bessemer converter and hot air is blown through:

              2 FeS  + 3 O2  -->  2 FeO + 2 SO2
              FeO    + SiO2  -->  FeSiO3
              2 Cu2S + 3 O2  -->  2 Cu2O + 2 SO2
              2 Cu2O + Cu2S  -->  6 Cu   +   SO2      <-- SELF REDUCTION

          Product = BLISTER COPPER, about 98 % pure.

  STEP 5  ELECTROLYTIC REFINING  ->  99.95 % pure copper,
          with valuable ANODE MUD (Ag, Au, Pt, Se, Te).

  NOTE: the flux here is SiO2 (ACIDIC) because the impurity FeO is BASIC
        - the opposite of the blast furnace, where the gangue SiO2 is
        acidic and the flux CaCO3 is basic.
```

**2. STAGE ONE — THE BAYER PROCESS (purification of bauxite)**

```

  STEP 1  Powdered bauxite is digested with 45 % NaOH at 473-523 K

     Al2O3 . x H2O(s)   --(1470 K)-->   Al2O3(s)  +  x H2O(g)

**3. STAGE TWO — THE HALL-HEROULT PROCESS**

```
  THE PROBLEM:  pure Al2O3 melts at about 2323 K - far too expensive.
  THE SOLUTION: dissolve it in molten CRYOLITE; the melting point drops
                to about 1140 K and the melt conducts well.

  THE ELECTROLYTE
     Pure alumina   Al2O3     about 20 %
     Cryolite       Na3AlF6   about 60 %
     Fluorspar      CaF2      about 20 %
     Temperature    about 1140 K       Voltage  4 - 6 V
     Current        very large (of the order of 10^5 A)

  THE CELL
     TANK      -  a steel/iron tank lined with GRAPHITE
     CATHODE   -  the GRAPHITE LINING of the tank
     ANODE     -  thick GRAPHITE RODS dipping into the melt

              O2 / CO / CO2 escape
                   ^     ^     ^
        +----------|-----|-----|-------------+
        |    ||    ||    ||    ||   ||       |  <-- GRAPHITE ANODES
        |  ############################      |
        |  #  MOLTEN ELECTROLYTE       #     |      Al2O3 + Na3AlF6
        |  #  at about 1140 K          #     |      + CaF2
        |  ############################      |
        |  ****************************      |  <-- MOLTEN ALUMINIUM
        |  * MOLTEN Al collects here  *      |      (denser, sinks)
        |####################################|  <-- GRAPHITE LINING
        +------------------------------------+      = CATHODE
                       |
                  tap hole for the molten aluminium

```

        Al2O3   -->   2 Al3+   +   3 O2-

  AT THE CATHODE (reduction - the metal is formed)
        Al3+   +   3 e-    -->    Al(l)

  AT THE ANODE (oxidation)
        2 O2-    -->    O2   +   4 e-

  The liberated oxygen attacks the hot carbon anode:
        C   +   O2      -->   CO2
        C   +   1/2 O2  -->   CO

  OVERALL
        2 Al2O3   +   3 C    -->    4 Al   +   3 CO2
```

**5. THE ROLES**

```
  CRYOLITE Na3AlF6  -  lowers the melting point of alumina from about
                       2323 K to about 1140 K, and increases the
                       electrical conductivity of the melt.
                       It is only a SOLVENT - not a source of aluminium.

  FLUORSPAR CaF2    -  lowers the melting point further and increases
                       the fluidity and conductivity.

  GRAPHITE ANODE    -  conducts the current and is deliberately consumed
                       by the liberated oxygen; this burning LOWERS the
                       voltage required. The anodes must therefore be
                       replaced periodically.

  The molten aluminium is denser than the electrolyte, so it sinks to
  the bottom and is tapped off. Purity is about 99.5 %; higher purity is
  obtained by Hoope's electrolytic refining.
```

### Q C3
Explain the **thermodynamic principles** of metallurgy. What is an Ellingham
diagram? Explain how it is used to select a reducing agent, and state its
limitations.

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT (approximate)
  ------------------------
  Gibbs energy criterion ............................ 1.5 marks
  Coupling of reactions with an example ............. 2 marks
  Definition of the Ellingham diagram + axes ........ 1 mark
  Why the lines slope as they do .................... 1.5 marks
  How to choose a reducing agent, with examples ..... 1 mark
  Limitations ....................................... 1 mark
```

**1. THE GIBBS ENERGY CRITERION**

```
      delta G   =   delta H   -   T  delta S

  A reduction is FEASIBLE (spontaneous) only when   delta G  <  0.

  delta G can be made negative by
     (a) making delta H more negative - a strongly exothermic reaction,
     (b) making T delta S large - raise T, provided delta S is positive.
```

**2. COUPLING OF REACTIONS**

```
  On its own, the decomposition of a metal oxide has a POSITIVE delta G:

        MO   -->   M   +   1/2 O2        delta G(1)  =  + 200 kJ (say)

  So it is COUPLED with a reaction having a large NEGATIVE delta G:

        C   +   1/2 O2   -->   CO        delta G(2)  =  - 250 kJ (say)
      ------------------------------------------------------------------
        MO  +   C   -->   M   +   CO     delta G  = 200 - 250 = - 50 kJ

  The total is NEGATIVE, so the coupled reaction proceeds.

  In words: the oxygen released by the metal oxide is immediately taken
  up by the reducing agent, which releases enough energy to "pay for"
  the difficult first step.

  RULE: an element A can reduce the oxide of B if A has a GREATER
        AFFINITY FOR OXYGEN than B at that temperature.
```

**3. THE ELLINGHAM DIAGRAM**

```
  DEFINITION: a plot of the standard Gibbs energy of FORMATION of metal
  oxides against TEMPERATURE.

     Y-AXIS : delta G of formation, PER MOLE OF O2 CONSUMED
              (so that different metals can be compared fairly)
     X-AXIS : TEMPERATURE

     delta G
       0 +----------------------------------------------------
         |                                         ,-'
         |  Cu2O ..........................    ,-'  <-- 2C+O2 -> 2CO
    -200 |  NiO  _______________________  ,-'         (SLOPES DOWN)
         |  FeO  ____________________ ,-'  X
    -400 |  ZnO  _________________,-'
         |  Al2O3 _____________,-'
    -600 |  MgO  ___________,-'
         +----------------------------------------------------> T
         0    500   1000   1500   2000   2500  (deg C)
```

**4. WHY THE LINES SLOPE AS THEY DO**

```
  MOST METAL LINES SLOPE UPWARD

     2 M(s)  +  O2(g)  -->  2 MO(s)

     A GAS is consumed and a SOLID formed, so disorder DECREASES:
     delta S is NEGATIVE.
     Since  delta G = delta H - T delta S , the slope of the line is
     ( - delta S ), which is POSITIVE  ->  the line rises.

  THE CARBON LINE SLOPES DOWNWARD

     2 C(s)  +  O2(g)  -->  2 CO(g)

     ONE mole of gas becomes TWO, so disorder INCREASES:
     delta S is POSITIVE  ->  slope is NEGATIVE  ->  the line falls.

  CONSEQUENCE: the carbon line must eventually CROSS every metal line.
  Above the crossing temperature carbon lies lower, so CARBON CAN
  REDUCE ALMOST ANY METAL OXIDE if the temperature is high enough.
```

**5. HOW TO SELECT A REDUCING AGENT**

```
  RULE: the element whose line lies LOWER can reduce the oxide whose
        line lies HIGHER, at that temperature. The CROSSING POINT gives
        the minimum working temperature.

  Al2O3 line is below Cr2O3 and Fe2O3   -> Al reduces them (THERMITE)
  C -> CO line drops below FeO ~1000 K  -> coke reduces FeO (blast furnace)
  Al2O3 and MgO lines lie BELOW the carbon line up to about 2000 deg C
                                        -> carbon CANNOT reduce them
                                        -> Al and Mg need ELECTROLYSIS
  MgO and Al2O3 lines cross at ~1350 deg C
                                        -> Mg reduces Al2O3 below 1350
                                        -> Al reduces MgO above 1350
  Ag2O and HgO lines are very high      -> simple heating decomposes them
```

**6. LIMITATIONS**

```
  1. It predicts only the FEASIBILITY (thermodynamics), never the RATE
     (kinetics). A feasible reaction may be far too slow to use.
  2. It assumes the reactants and products are at EQUILIBRIUM, which
     is often untrue in a working furnace.
  3. The simple interpretation of a coupled reaction breaks down when
     the products react further or form solutions / alloys.
  4. The lines BEND at melting and boiling points, because a phase
     change alters delta S.
```

### Q C4
Describe the various methods of **concentration of ores**. Give the principle,
the ores suited and a diagram for each.

```

**0. WHAT AND WHY**

```
  CONCENTRATION (dressing, benefaction) is the removal of the GANGUE
  from the crushed ore. The method chosen depends on a PHYSICAL or
  CHEMICAL difference between the ore and the gangue:

     difference in DENSITY       -> hydraulic washing
     difference in MAGNETISM     -> magnetic separation
     difference in WETTABILITY   -> froth flotation
     difference in SOLUBILITY    -> leaching
```

**1. HYDRAULIC WASHING (gravity separation / levigation)**

```
  PRINCIPLE: the ore is HEAVIER than the gangue. An upward jet of water
  carries the lighter gangue away while the heavier ore settles.

  SUITS: dense OXIDE ores - haematite Fe2O3, tinstone SnO2, native gold.

           WATER JET (upward)
                  ^
        +---------|--------------------+
        |  * o *  o  *  o              |----> LIGHT GANGUE washed off
        |  ==========================  |
        |  \  o o o o o o o o  /       |
        |   \ HEAVY ORE SETTLES/       |
        +--------|---------------------+
                 v
          CONCENTRATED ORE
```

**2. MAGNETIC SEPARATION**

```
  PRINCIPLE: one component is MAGNETIC, the other is not.

  SUITS: Fe3O4 and FeCr2O4 (the ORE is magnetic);
         SnO2 with FeWO4 impurity (the GANGUE is magnetic).

                 powdered ore
                      |||
           +----------------------+
           |     ,----------.     |
   belt -->|    |  MAGNETIC  |    |
           |    |   ROLLER   |    |
           |     `----------'     |
           +------|--------|------+
                 /          \
                v            v
          NON-MAGNETIC    MAGNETIC
          thrown further  clings, drops nearer
```

**3. FROTH FLOTATION**

```
  PRINCIPLE: sulphide ore particles are preferentially wetted by OIL,
  gangue particles by WATER. Air bubbles carry the oil-coated ore up
  as a froth; the gangue sinks.

  SUITS: SULPHIDE ores - ZnS, PbS, CuFeS2.

              oo oo ooo oo ooo   <-- FROTH with the ORE (skimmed off)
        +----ooooooooooooooooo----+
        |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  |
        |  ~ water + pine oil   ~ |     ||  agitator
        |  ~   *   *   *   *    ~ |    ====
        |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ |
        +------ ^^^^^^^^^ --------+
                 AIR blown in

  REAGENTS
     COLLECTOR (pine oil, xanthates)  - makes the ore water-repellent
     FROTH STABILISER (cresols)       - keeps the froth from bursting
     DEPRESSANT (NaCN)                - stops one ore from floating:
             4 NaCN + ZnS --> Na2[Zn(CN)4] + Na2S
             ZnS sinks, PbS floats -> the two are separated
```

**4. LEACHING**

```
  PRINCIPLE: the ore DISSOLVES in a chemical reagent in which the gangue
  is insoluble. The solution is filtered and the metal recovered.

  (a) BAYER PROCESS - bauxite
      Al2O3 + 2 NaOH + 3 H2O --(473-523 K)--> 2 Na[Al(OH)4]
                                  Fe2O3, TiO2 -> RED MUD (filtered off)
      2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 2 CO2  -->  Al2O3.xH2O + 2 NaHCO3
      Al2O3.xH2O  --(1470 K)-->  Al2O3 + x H2O

  (b) CYANIDE PROCESS - gold and silver
      4 Au + 8 NaCN + 2 H2O + O2  -->  4 Na[Au(CN)2] + 4 NaOH
      2 Na[Au(CN)2] + Zn  -->  Na2[Zn(CN)4] + 2 Au

      (the same two steps with Ag in place of Au)
```

### Q C5
Describe the various methods of **refining of metals**. Give the principle
and one example for each.

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT (approximate)
  ------------------------
  Distillation, liquation, poling ................... 2 marks
  Electrolytic refining + diagram + anode mud ....... 3 marks
  Zone refining + diagram ........................... 1.5 marks
  Vapour phase (Mond + van Arkel) with equations .... 1.5 marks
```

**1. DISTILLATION**

```
  PRINCIPLE: the METAL is volatile, the impurities are not. The impure
  metal is heated, the metal boils off, and the vapour is condensed.
  METALS: Zn , Hg , Cd
```

**2. LIQUATION**

```
  PRINCIPLE: the metal has a LOW MELTING POINT. On a sloping hearth the
  metal melts and runs down, leaving the higher melting impurities.
  METALS: Sn , Pb , Bi
```

**3. POLING**

```
  PRINCIPLE: used when the impurity is the metal's OWN OXIDE. The molten
  metal is stirred with GREEN WOOD POLES; the hydrocarbons released
  (CH4 etc.) reduce the oxide back to the metal.
  METALS: Cu (removes Cu2O) , Sn (removes SnO2)
```

**4. ELECTROLYTIC REFINING**

```
  PRINCIPLE: impure metal = ANODE ; pure metal strip = CATHODE ;
             a soluble salt of the metal = ELECTROLYTE.
             The metal dissolves from the anode and plates out pure on
             the cathode.
```

```
          (+) impure Cu ANODE          CATHODE (-) pure Cu strip
              ||                              ||
        +-----||------------------------------||-----+
        |  ~~~||  CuSO4 + dil. H2SO4  ~~~~~~~~||~~~  |
        |  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  |
        |     , ANODE MUD ,   <-- Ag, Au, Pt, Se, Te |
        +---------------------------------------------+
```

```
      ANODE   :  Cu    -->   Cu2+  +  2 e-
      CATHODE :  Cu2+  +  2 e-  -->   Cu     (99.95 % pure)

  ANODE MUD: the less electropositive impurities (Ag, Au, Pt, Se, Te)
  are not oxidised and drop off below the anode. The more
  electropositive ones (Zn, Fe, Ni) dissolve but stay in solution.

  METALS: Cu, Ag, Au, Ni, Al, Zn, Pb, Cr.
```

**5. ZONE REFINING**

```
  PRINCIPLE: impurities are MORE SOLUBLE IN THE MOLTEN metal than in the
  SOLID metal. A mobile heater melts a narrow zone and is moved slowly
  along the rod, always in the SAME direction, several times. The
  impurities are swept to one end, which is then cut off.

     heater --->
        ____
  =====[####]==========================================
  |PURE|MELT|      yet to be purified                 |
  =====[####]==========================================
                                          impurities collect at this end

  METALS: Si , Ge , Ga , B , In  (semiconductors, purity ~ 1 in 10^10)
```

**6. VAPOUR PHASE REFINING**

```
  PRINCIPLE: convert the metal into a VOLATILE compound, then DECOMPOSE
  that compound to get the pure metal.

  (a) MOND'S PROCESS - NICKEL
        Ni  +  4 CO   --(330-350 K)-->   Ni(CO)4
        Ni(CO)4       --(450-470 K)-->   Ni  +  4 CO

  (b) VAN ARKEL METHOD - ZIRCONIUM and TITANIUM
        Zr  +  2 I2   --(870 K)-->   ZrI4
        ZrI4  --(1800 K, tungsten filament)-->  Zr  +  2 I2

        Removes O and N impurities; gives nuclear-grade metal.
```

**7. CHROMATOGRAPHIC METHODS**

```
  PRINCIPLE: different components of a mixture are ADSORBED to different
  extents on an adsorbent (stationary phase), so a moving solvent
  (mobile phase) carries them at different rates and separates them.

  USED FOR: elements available only in tiny amounts, and impurities that
  are chemically very similar - e.g. the LANTHANOIDS, and germanium.
  Types: column, paper and gas chromatography.
```
