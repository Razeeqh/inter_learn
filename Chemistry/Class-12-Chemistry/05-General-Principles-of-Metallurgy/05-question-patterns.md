# General Principles of Metallurgy — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps to write
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

This chapter is entirely descriptive, so the "method" is almost always
"write these exact points in this exact order".

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — MINERAL vs ORE                                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the difference between a mineral and an ore?"
            or "Define ore" / "Is clay an ore of aluminium?"
  METHOD    1. MINERAL = any naturally occurring compound of a metal.
            2. ORE     = a mineral from which the metal can be extracted
                         CONVENIENTLY and PROFITABLY.
            3. Write the line: ALL ORES ARE MINERALS, BUT ALL MINERALS
               ARE NOT ORES.
            4. Give the standard example:
                  Bauxite Al2O3.2H2O        -> an ORE of Al
                  Clay    Al2O3.2SiO2.2H2O  -> a MINERAL only
  TRAP      Leaving out the EXAMPLE. It is worth a full mark on its own.
            Also, do not say "an ore is pure" - an ore still has gangue.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — DEFINE GANGUE / FLUX / SLAG                         2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any of the three words appearing alone in a short question.
  METHOD    GANGUE (matrix) - the earthy/rocky impurity in the ore (SiO2).
            FLUX            - added to combine with the gangue.
            SLAG            - the fusible product:  FLUX + GANGUE -> SLAG
            Always write the equation:
                  CaO  +  SiO2   -->   CaSiO3
                  FLUX    GANGUE        SLAG
  TRAP      Do not confuse SLAG with MATTE. Matte is Cu2S + FeS in copper
            extraction; slag is the waste silicate.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — WHICH FLUX SHOULD I USE?                        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which flux is used in the blast furnace / in the extraction
            of copper, and why?"
  METHOD    1. Identify the IMPURITY first.
            2. Apply the rule: OPPOSITES CANCEL.
                 acidic gangue (SiO2)  -> BASIC flux (CaO, CaCO3)
                 basic impurity (FeO)  -> ACIDIC flux (SiO2)
            3. Write the slag equation.
  TRAP      THE most common mix-up in the chapter:
                 BLAST FURNACE : gangue SiO2 is acidic -> flux CaCO3
                 COPPER        : impurity FeO is basic -> flux SiO2
            They are the OPPOSITE way round. Read the impurity, not the metal.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — WRITE THE ORE AND ITS FORMULA                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Name two ores of iron / copper / zinc / aluminium with
            their formulas."
  METHOD    Learn these blocks:
              Al : Bauxite Al2O3.2H2O , Corundum Al2O3, Cryolite Na3AlF6
              Fe : Haematite Fe2O3 , Magnetite Fe3O4 , Siderite FeCO3 ,
                   Iron pyrites FeS2
              Cu : Copper pyrites CuFeS2 , Copper glance Cu2S ,
                   Malachite CuCO3.Cu(OH)2 , Cuprite Cu2O
              Zn : Zinc blende ZnS , Calamine ZnCO3 , Zincite ZnO
  TRAP      Cryolite Na3AlF6 is a MINERAL of aluminium, but in the exam
            its role is as the SOLVENT in the Hall-Heroult cell, not as
            the ore. Bauxite is the ore.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — THE FIVE STEPS OF METALLURGY (flow chart)       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the general steps involved in metallurgy" or
            "Draw a flow chart for the extraction of a metal."
  METHOD    ORE
             -> 1. CRUSHING and GRINDING (pulverisation)
             -> 2. CONCENTRATION (dressing) - remove gangue
             -> 3. CONVERSION TO OXIDE (calcination / roasting)
             -> 4. REDUCTION to the metal
             -> 5. REFINING
            -> PURE METAL
            Name one or two techniques under each box.
  TRAP      Do not skip step 3 silently when the ore is already an oxide.
            Say so explicitly - it shows you understood.
  BONUS     If you blank out in ANY metallurgy question, write this chart.
            The sequence alone earns partial marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — WHICH CONCENTRATION METHOD, AND WHY?            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How would you concentrate an ore of ... ?" / a named ore
            is given and a method is asked for.
  METHOD    Match the DIFFERENCE to the method:
              DENSITY    -> hydraulic washing   (Fe2O3, SnO2, native Au)
              MAGNETISM  -> magnetic separation (Fe3O4, FeCr2O4)
              WETTABILITY-> froth flotation     (ALL sulphides)
              SOLUBILITY -> leaching            (bauxite, Au, Ag)
            Then state the PRINCIPLE in one line and draw the diagram.
  TRAP      Look at the FORMULA, not the metal name. ZnS -> flotation,
            but ZnCO3 -> hydraulic washing. Same metal, different method.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — FROTH FLOTATION IN FULL                             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain froth flotation with a diagram" or "What is the role
            of a depressant?"
  METHOD    1. PRINCIPLE: sulphide ore is preferentially wetted by OIL,
               gangue by WATER.
            2. Draw the cell: tank, water + pine oil, agitator, air blown
               in at the bottom, froth carrying ore at the top, gangue
               sinking.
            3. Name ALL FOUR reagents with their jobs:
                 COLLECTOR (pine oil, xanthates) - makes ore water-repellent
                 FROTH STABILISER (cresols)      - stops bubbles bursting
                 DEPRESSANT (NaCN)               - stops one ore floating
                 FROTHING AGENT (pine oil)       - produces the froth
            4. Write the depressant equation:
                 4 NaCN + ZnS --> Na2[Zn(CN)4] + Na2S
               ZnS sinks, PbS floats -> the two are separated.
            5. State it suits SULPHIDE ores.
  TRAP      Writing "NaCN depresses ZnS" without the equation and without
            the complex formula Na2[Zn(CN)4] loses half the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — LEACHING (BAYER or CYANIDE)                         4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is leaching?" / "How is bauxite purified?" /
            "How is gold extracted from its ore?"
  METHOD    Define: dissolving the ore in a reagent in which the gangue
            is insoluble, then filtering and recovering the metal.

            BAYER (bauxite):
              Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O --(473-523K, 35 bar)--> 2 Na[Al(OH)4]
                             Fe2O3, TiO2 -> RED MUD, filtered off
              2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 2 CO2 --> Al2O3.xH2O + 2 NaHCO3  (+ seeding)
              Al2O3.xH2O --(1470 K)--> Al2O3 + x H2O

            CYANIDE (Au / Ag):
              4 Au + 8 NaCN + 2H2O + O2 --> 4 Na[Au(CN)2] + 4 NaOH
              2 Na[Au(CN)2] + Zn --> Na2[Zn(CN)4] + 2 Au
              (O2 = oxidising agent ; Zn = reducing agent)
  TRAP      Forgetting the ROLE of O2 (oxidises Au so it can complex) and
            of Zn (displaces Au). Examiners ask for these directly.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — CALCINATION vs ROASTING TABLE                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between calcination and roasting."
  METHOD    Give SIX or more differences in a TABLE:
              1. no air        vs  excess air
              2. carbonates /  vs  sulphides
                 hydrated oxides
              3. CO2 evolved   vs  SO2 evolved
              4. decomposition vs  oxidation
              5. lower temp.   vs  higher temp.
              6. example ZnCO3 -> ZnO + CO2
                 vs 2 ZnS + 3 O2 -> 2 ZnO + 2 SO2
  TRAP      Give the EXAMPLES with balanced equations. A table of words
            alone scores about half.
  MEMORY    CALCINATION - C for Carbonate, C for CO2, "Closed" (no air)
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — CALCINATION OR ROASTING FOR A GIVEN ORE?           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A single ore formula is given: "How is ... converted to
            the oxide?"
  METHOD    Look at the FORMULA:
              contains CO3 or .xH2O  -> CALCINATION -> gives CO2 / H2O
              contains S             -> ROASTING    -> gives SO2
            Write the balanced equation.
  TRAP      Balance the oxygen! 2 ZnS + 3 O2 -> 2 ZnO + 2 SO2, NOT
            "ZnS + O2 -> ZnO + SO2".
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — THE FEASIBILITY / GIBBS ENERGY CRITERION           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the thermodynamic condition for a reduction to be
            feasible?"
  METHOD    delta G = delta H - T delta S
            Feasible when delta G < 0 (negative).
            delta G = 0 -> equilibrium ; delta G > 0 -> will not occur.
            Add: delta G can be made negative by raising T if delta S is
            positive.
  TRAP      Saying "delta H must be negative". It is delta G, not delta H,
            that decides.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — COUPLING OF REACTIONS                              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the coupling of reactions in metallurgy" or
            "How is a reaction with positive delta G made to occur?"
  METHOD    1. Alone:   MO -> M + 1/2 O2      delta G is POSITIVE
            2. Couple with:  C + 1/2 O2 -> CO  delta G is LARGE NEGATIVE
            3. Add them:     MO + C -> M + CO
               If the SUM of delta G is negative, the process goes.
            4. Use numbers to show it: (+200) + (-250) = -50 kJ.
            5. State the rule: the reducing agent must have a GREATER
               AFFINITY FOR OXYGEN than the metal.
  TRAP      You must ADD the delta G values, not multiply or average them.
            Show the arithmetic.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — WHAT IS AN ELLINGHAM DIAGRAM?                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define / what is an Ellingham diagram?"
  METHOD    A plot of the standard Gibbs energy of FORMATION of metal
            oxides (Y-axis) against TEMPERATURE (X-axis), with all values
            expressed PER MOLE OF O2 CONSUMED so that different metals
            can be compared. It is used to choose a reducing agent and
            the temperature.
  TRAP      Forgetting "PER MOLE OF O2". That phrase is a mark.
            Also do not say the Y-axis is enthalpy - it is Gibbs energy.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — WHY DOES A LINE SLOPE UP OR DOWN?              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why do most metal oxide lines slope upward?" or
            "Why does the carbon line slope downward?"
  METHOD    COUNT THE MOLES OF GAS.
            UP:   2 M(s) + O2(g) -> 2 MO(s)
                  gas is consumed -> delta S NEGATIVE
                  slope = ( - delta S ) = POSITIVE -> line RISES
            DOWN: 2 C(s) + O2(g) -> 2 CO(g)
                  1 mol gas -> 2 mol gas -> delta S POSITIVE
                  delta G becomes more negative as T rises -> line FALLS
            Then add the consequence: the carbon line CROSSES every metal
            line, so carbon can reduce almost any oxide if hot enough.
  TRAP      Give the SIGN of delta S explicitly, and connect it to the
            slope through delta G = delta H - T delta S. Just saying
            "entropy changes" is not enough.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — CHOOSE A REDUCING AGENT FROM THE DIAGRAM       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Can carbon reduce ... ?" / "Why is Al used to reduce Cr2O3?"
            / "At what temperature can ... be reduced?"
  METHOD    THE ONE RULE:  LOWER LINE REDUCES HIGHER LINE.
            1. Find both lines.
            2. Whichever is LOWER at that temperature is the reducing agent.
            3. The CROSSING POINT gives the minimum working temperature.
            Standard answers:
              Al2O3 line below Cr2O3, Fe2O3  -> Al reduces them (thermite)
              C->CO line below FeO ~1000 K   -> coke reduces FeO
              Al2O3, MgO below C to ~2000 C  -> carbon CANNOT reduce them
                                                -> electrolysis needed
              MgO/Al2O3 cross at ~1350 C     -> Mg reduces Al2O3 below,
                                                Al reduces MgO above
  TRAP      Never say "Al is always a stronger reducing agent than Mg".
            The whole point of the diagram is that it DEPENDS ON T.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — LIMITATIONS OF THE ELLINGHAM DIAGRAM               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the limitations of the Ellingham diagram."
  METHOD    Give at least TWO, ideally three:
            1. Gives FEASIBILITY only, NOT the RATE (no kinetics).
            2. Assumes reactants and products are at EQUILIBRIUM.
            3. Fails when the products react further or form
               solutions / alloys.
            4. Lines BEND at melting and boiling points.
  TRAP      Number them. One vague sentence is worth 1 mark, two clear
            numbered points are worth 2.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — delta G = - n F E NUMERICAL                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   An EMF value is given and delta G is asked for, or a
            displacement is to be shown feasible.
  METHOD    1. Write the reaction and identify n (moles of electrons).
            2. E(cell) = E(cathode) - E(anode).
            3. delta G = - n F E ,  F = 96500 C.
            4. Convert J to kJ.
            5. State: E positive -> delta G negative -> FEASIBLE.
  TRAP      Watch the SIGN. E(cathode) - E(anode) means
            (reduced species) - (oxidised species). Subtracting the wrong
            way gives a negative E and the wrong conclusion.
  CHECK     Zn + Cu2+ , n=2 , E=1.10 V  ->  delta G = -(2)(96500)(1.10)
                                          =  -212.3 kJ
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — WHY ELECTROLYSIS FOR ELECTROPOSITIVE METALS?   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is aluminium / sodium not extracted by carbon reduction?"
  METHOD    1. Na, K, Ca, Mg, Al have a LARGE NEGATIVE reduction potential
               -> highly electropositive -> their ions are very hard
               to reduce.
            2. Their oxide lines lie very LOW on the Ellingham diagram,
               below the carbon line up to about 2000 deg C -> C is not a
               strong enough reducing agent at any workable temperature.
            3. Electricity supplies unlimited reducing power -> electrolysis.
            4. It must be the FUSED (molten) salt, not an aqueous
               solution, because water (E = -0.83 V) is reduced far more
               easily than Na+ (E = -2.71 V) - you would get H2, not Na.
  TRAP      Point 4 is often forgotten and is a whole mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — THE THERMITE / ALUMINOTHERMIC PROCESS              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the thermite process" / "How is chromium extracted?"
            / "Why is a magnesium ribbon used?"
  METHOD    1. Used when carbon cannot be used (it would form a CARBIDE,
               or the temperature is too high). Al is the reducing agent.
            2. Equations:
                 Cr2O3   + 2 Al -> 2 Cr + Al2O3
                 3 Mn3O4 + 8 Al -> 9 Mn + 4 Al2O3
                 Fe2O3   + 2 Al -> 2 Fe + Al2O3   (thermite welding)
            3. Highly EXOTHERMIC - about 3000 deg C - the metal comes out
               MOLTEN.
            4. Thermite mixture = Fe2O3 + Al (3:1). Ignition mixture =
               BaO2 + Mg ribbon.
            5. The Mg ribbon supplies the high ACTIVATION ENERGY needed
               to start it; BaO2 supplies oxygen.
            6. Use: welding cracked rails and machine parts.
  TRAP      Do not confuse the THERMITE mixture with the IGNITION mixture.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — SELF REDUCTION                                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is self reduction?" / an equation like
            2 Cu2O + Cu2S -> 6 Cu + SO2 is given.
  METHOD    1. Definition: part of the sulphide is roasted to the oxide;
               the REMAINING SULPHIDE then reduces that oxide. No external
               reducing agent is used.
            2. Copper:  2 Cu2S + 3 O2 -> 2 Cu2O + 2 SO2
                        2 Cu2O + Cu2S -> 6 Cu + SO2
            3. Lead:    2 PbS + 3 O2 -> 2 PbO + 2 SO2
                        2 PbO + PbS  -> 3 Pb + SO2
            4. Mercury: HgS + O2 -> Hg + SO2
  TRAP      Balance 2 Cu2O + Cu2S -> 6 Cu + SO2. Copper: 4+2 = 6. Check it.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — EXTRACTION OF ALUMINIUM (FULL)             *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the extraction of aluminium from bauxite" /
            "Explain the Hall-Heroult process."
  METHOD    Split the answer into TWO clearly labelled halves.

            PART 1 - BAYER PROCESS (purification), 3 equations:
              Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O --> 2 Na[Al(OH)4]   (RED MUD out)
              2 Na[Al(OH)4] + 2 CO2 --> Al2O3.xH2O + 2 NaHCO3
              Al2O3.xH2O --(1470 K)--> Al2O3 + x H2O

            PART 2 - HALL-HEROULT (reduction):
              Electrolyte: Al2O3 20% + Na3AlF6 60% + CaF2 20%, ~1140 K,
                           4-6 V, very high current
              Anode: graphite rods ; Cathode: graphite lining of the tank
              DRAW the cell.
              Al2O3 -> 2 Al3+ + 3 O2-
              CATHODE: Al3+ + 3 e- -> Al
              ANODE  : 2 O2- -> O2 + 4 e-  ; C + O2 -> CO2
              OVERALL: 2 Al2O3 + 3 C -> 4 Al + 3 CO2
              ROLES: cryolite lowers m.p. 2323 K -> 1140 K and raises
              conductivity ; CaF2 lowers it further ; the graphite anode
              is consumed so it is replaced periodically.
  TRAP      Confusing the two halves. Bayer PURIFIES; Hall-Heroult REDUCES.
            And cryolite is only a SOLVENT, never the source of Al.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — THE BLAST FURNACE (FULL)                   *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the extraction of iron" / "Explain the reactions in
            a blast furnace" / "What is the role of limestone?"
  METHOD    1. ORE and CHARGE: Fe2O3 (roasted) : coke : limestone
               = about 8 : 4 : 1 . Hot air at 1500 K through the TUYERES.
            2. DRAW the furnace with the three zones labelled and their
               TEMPERATURES.
            3. ZONE OF COMBUSTION, 1500-2200 K (bottom):
                  C + O2 -> CO2   (exothermic - heats the furnace)
                  CO2 + C -> 2 CO (endothermic - makes the reducing agent)
            4. ZONE OF SLAG FORMATION, 900-1500 K (middle):
                  CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2
                  CaO + SiO2 -> CaSiO3   (SLAG)
                  FeO + SiO2 -> FeSiO3
                  FeO + C -> Fe + CO
            5. ZONE OF REDUCTION, 500-800 K (top):
                  3 Fe2O3 + CO -> 2 Fe3O4 + CO2
                  Fe3O4 + CO -> 3 FeO + CO2
                  FeO + CO -> Fe + CO2
                  overall Fe2O3 + 3 CO -> 2 Fe + 3 CO2
                  gives SPONGY IRON
            6. PRODUCTS: PIG IRON (about 4 % C) tapped at the bottom;
               the lighter SLAG floats and is tapped separately.
  TRAP      Getting the zone order upside down. COMBUSTION is at the
            BOTTOM (hottest, where the air enters). REDUCTION is at the
            TOP (coolest). Students often reverse them.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — ROLE OF LIMESTONE / OF COKE                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the role of limestone / coke in the blast furnace?"
  METHOD    LIMESTONE (flux):
              CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2
              CaO (basic) + SiO2 (acidic gangue) -> CaSiO3 (slag)
              The slag is light, floats, is tapped off, and protects the
              iron from re-oxidation.
            COKE:
              (a) the FUEL - C + O2 -> CO2 supplies the heat
              (b) the source of the REDUCING AGENT - CO2 + C -> 2 CO
  TRAP      Coke has TWO roles. Writing only "reducing agent" loses a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — PIG / CAST / WROUGHT IRON AND STEEL           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between cast iron and wrought iron" / "What is
            the carbon content of ... ?"
  METHOD    Table with % carbon, property and use:
              PIG IRON    ~4 %       hard, brittle, cannot be welded
              CAST IRON   ~3 %       brittle, expands on solidifying;
                                     pipes, railings, engine blocks
              STEEL       0.2-1.5 %  strong; buildings, rails, tools
              WROUGHT     0.2-0.5 %  PUREST, soft, malleable, weldable;
              IRON                   chains, bolts, electromagnet cores
            ORDER OF CARBON: pig > cast > steel > wrought
            ORDER OF PURITY: wrought > steel > cast > pig
            Wrought iron is made by PUDDLING:  Fe2O3 + 3 C -> 2 Fe + 3 CO
  TRAP      Do not say wrought iron has the MOST carbon. It has the LEAST,
            which is exactly why it is the purest and most malleable.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — EXTRACTION OF COPPER IN OUTLINE                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is copper extracted from copper pyrites?" / "What is
            copper matte / blister copper?"
  METHOD    1. Froth flotation (sulphide ore).
            2. Roasting in limited air:
                  2 CuFeS2 + O2 -> Cu2S + 2 FeS + SO2
            3. Smelting with SILICA flux:
                  2 FeS + 3 O2 -> 2 FeO + 2 SO2
                  FeO + SiO2 -> FeSiO3   (slag)
                  what remains, Cu2S + FeS = COPPER MATTE
            4. Bessemerisation:
                  2 Cu2S + 3 O2 -> 2 Cu2O + 2 SO2
                  2 Cu2O + Cu2S -> 6 Cu + SO2      (SELF REDUCTION)
                  gives BLISTER COPPER, 98 % pure (SO2 escaping leaves
                  blisters)
            5. Electrolytic refining -> 99.95 %.
  TRAP      The flux here is SiO2 (ACIDIC), not limestone. Remember the
            impurity FeO is basic.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — EXTRACTION OF ZINC IN OUTLINE                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is zinc extracted from zinc blende?" / "Why is zinc
            obtained as a vapour?"
  METHOD    1. Froth flotation.
            2. Roasting: 2 ZnS + 3 O2 -> 2 ZnO + 2 SO2
               (or calcination if the ore is ZnCO3)
            3. Smelting: ZnO + C --(1673 K)--> Zn + CO
            4. Zinc BOILS at 1180 K but the reduction needs 1673 K, so
               the zinc distils out as a VAPOUR and is condensed.
               Product = SPELTER, 97-98 % pure.
               Coal dust in the receivers keeps the atmosphere reducing.
            5. Refined by distillation or electrolysis (ZnSO4 + H2SO4).
  TRAP      The "why is it a vapour" sub-question needs BOTH numbers:
            b.p. 1180 K and reduction temperature 1673 K.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — WHICH REFINING METHOD, AND WHY?               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How would you refine ... ?" or a method is named and the
            metal is asked for.
  METHOD    Match the PROPERTY to the method:
              volatile metal          -> DISTILLATION      Zn, Hg, Cd
              low melting point       -> LIQUATION         Sn, Pb, Bi
              impurity is its own oxide-> POLING           Cu, Sn
              needs ultra purity      -> ZONE REFINING     Si, Ge, Ga, B
              forms a volatile compound-> VAPOUR PHASE     Ni, Zr, Ti
              conducts + soluble salt -> ELECTROLYTIC      Cu, Ag, Au, Ni
              very similar impurities -> CHROMATOGRAPHY    lanthanoids
            Then give the PRINCIPLE in one line.
  TRAP     Learn the metal-method pairs as one block. Most marks are lost
           by naming the right method for the wrong metal.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — ELECTROLYTIC REFINING + ANODE MUD                  4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the electrolytic refining of copper" / "What is
            anode mud?"
  METHOD    1. ANODE = impure metal ; CATHODE = thin strip of pure metal ;
               ELECTROLYTE = a soluble salt (CuSO4 + dil. H2SO4).
            2. Draw the cell with the anode mud at the bottom.
            3. ANODE  : Cu -> Cu2+ + 2 e-
               CATHODE: Cu2+ + 2 e- -> Cu   (99.95 % pure)
            4. ANODE MUD = Ag, Au, Pt, Se, Te. They are LESS
               electropositive, so they are not oxidised and drop off.
            5. MORE electropositive impurities (Zn, Fe, Ni) DO dissolve
               but stay in solution because they are harder to reduce
               than Cu2+.
  TRAP      Point 5 is the one that separates a full-mark answer from a
            half-mark one. And remember: IMPURE = ANODE.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — ZONE REFINING                                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is zone refining?" / "How is germanium purified?"
  METHOD    1. PRINCIPLE: impurities are MORE SOLUBLE IN THE MOLTEN metal
               than in the SOLID metal.
            2. A circular mobile heater melts a narrow zone and is moved
               SLOWLY along the rod, ALWAYS IN THE SAME DIRECTION.
            3. Impurities travel with the molten zone to one end.
            4. Repeated several times; the impure end is CUT OFF.
            5. METALS: Si, Ge, Ga, B, In - the SEMICONDUCTORS.
            6. Draw the rod with the heater and mark the impure end.
  TRAP      "Same direction, repeated several times" is a marking point.
            Moving the heater back and forth would undo the separation.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — MOND PROCESS / VAN ARKEL METHOD               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is vapour phase refining?" / a metal Ni, Zr or Ti is
            named.
  METHOD    State the two requirements: the metal must form a VOLATILE
            compound, and that compound must DECOMPOSE easily.

            MOND (nickel):
               Ni + 4 CO --(330-350 K)--> Ni(CO)4
               Ni(CO)4 --(450-470 K)--> Ni + 4 CO

            VAN ARKEL (zirconium, titanium):
               Zr + 2 I2 --(870 K)--> ZrI4
               ZrI4 --(1800 K, TUNGSTEN FILAMENT)--> Zr + 2 I2
               Removes O and N; gives nuclear-grade metal.
  TRAP      Quote the TEMPERATURES - they carry marks. And remember
            "Mond uses Monoxide"; van Arkel uses IODINE on a TUNGSTEN
            filament.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — USES OF A METAL                                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give any four uses of aluminium / copper / zinc."
  METHOD    Give FOUR short, distinct uses - never repeat the same idea.
            Al : aircraft alloys (duralumin) , electrical cables , foil
                 and utensils , thermite process / silver paint
            Cu : electrical wires , pipes and boilers , coins ,
                 alloys (brass Cu+Zn , bronze Cu+Sn)
            Zn : galvanising , brass , dry cell container ,
                 reducing agent / ZnO in paint
            Fe : cast iron pipes , wrought iron chains , steel
                 construction , stainless steel cutlery
  TRAP      "Used in industry" is not a use. Be specific and name the alloy.
```

```
  PATTERN 32 — DEFINE-IN-ONE-LINE QUESTIONS                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any single term: smelting, matte, blister copper, spelter,
            red mud, tuyeres, spongy iron, pulverisation, depressant.
  METHOD    One sentence of definition + one example or equation.
            SMELTING      - reduction of an oxide by C/CO in a furnace
                            with a flux.  Fe2O3 + 3 CO -> 2 Fe + 3 CO2
            MATTE         - the molten Cu2S + FeS mixture in Cu extraction
            BLISTER COPPER- 98 % Cu, blistered by escaping SO2
            SPELTER       - the 97-98 % pure zinc from smelting
            RED MUD       - Fe2O3 + TiO2 residue from the Bayer process
            TUYERES       - the pipes carrying the hot air blast into the
                            blast furnace
            SPONGY IRON   - the porous iron formed in the reduction zone
            PULVERISATION - crushing and grinding the ore to a fine powder
  TRAP      Two marks means TWO things: the definition AND an example or
            equation. One line alone usually scores one mark.
```
