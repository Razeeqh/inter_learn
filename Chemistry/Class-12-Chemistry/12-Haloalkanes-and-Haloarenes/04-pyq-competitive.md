# PATTERN 1 — REACTIVITY ORDER: SN1 or SN2
# ===============================================================

### Q1
The correct order of reactivity towards the SN1 mechanism is:

(a) CH3-X > 1deg > 2deg > 3deg
(b) 3deg > 2deg > 1deg > CH3-X
(c) 1deg > 2deg > 3deg > CH3-X
(d) 2deg > 3deg > 1deg > CH3-X

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  SN1 goes through a CARBOCATION, and the more stable the carbocation the
  faster the reaction.
     3deg(+) > 2deg(+) > 1deg(+) > CH3(+)
  So the halide order is  3deg > 2deg > 1deg > CH3-X.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** SN**1** = **one** substrate breaking = needs a **stable carbocation**
> = **tertiary wins**. SN**2** = **two** things colliding = needs **space** =
> **methyl wins**. The two orders are always exactly opposite.

---

### Q2
Which of the following reacts fastest by the SN2 mechanism?

(a) (CH3)3C-Br  (b) (CH3)2CH-Br  (c) CH3-CH2-Br  (d) CH3-Br

**ANSWER: (d) CH3-Br**
```
  SN2 is blocked by STERIC HINDRANCE. Bromomethane has three tiny
  hydrogens around the carbon, so the back face is wide open.
     SN2 order:  CH3 > 1deg > 2deg > 3deg
```

---

### Q3
The order of reactivity of alkyl halides in nucleophilic substitution is:

(a) R-F > R-Cl > R-Br > R-I
(b) R-I > R-Br > R-Cl > R-F
(c) R-Cl > R-Br > R-I > R-F
(d) R-Br > R-I > R-Cl > R-F

**ANSWER: (b) R-I > R-Br > R-Cl > R-F**
```
  The C-X bond ENTHALPY falls down the group:
     C-F (452) > C-Cl (351) > C-Br (293) > C-I (234) kJ/mol
  The weakest bond breaks most easily, and I- is the best leaving group.
```

---

### Q4
Which of the following gives a carbocation most readily?

(a) CH3-Cl  (b) C6H5-CH2-Cl  (c) C6H5-Cl  (d) CH3-CH2-Cl

**ANSWER: (b) C6H5-CH2-Cl (benzyl chloride)**
```
  The benzyl carbocation C6H5-CH2(+) is stabilised by RESONANCE - the
  positive charge is delocalised into the ring (ortho and para positions).
  It is even more stable than a tertiary carbocation.

  C6H5-Cl gives a PHENYL cation, which is the LEAST stable of all, because
  its empty orbital is in the plane of the ring and cannot overlap with
  the pi cloud.

  FULL ORDER:  benzyl , allyl > 3deg > 2deg > 1deg > CH3 >> vinyl , phenyl
```

---

### Q5
The hydrolysis of tert-butyl bromide in aqueous NaOH:

(a) is second order and gives inversion
(b) is first order and gives racemisation
(c) is first order and gives inversion
(d) is second order and gives racemisation

**ANSWER: (b) first order and gives racemisation**
```
  A tertiary halide reacts by SN1.
     rate = k [RX]                 -> FIRST order
     flat carbocation, attacked from both faces -> RACEMISATION
```

### Q6
CH3-CH2-Br reacting with AgCN gives:

(a) propanenitrile  (b) ethyl isocyanide  (c) ethanol  (d) nitroethane

**ANSWER: (b) ethyl isocyanide, CH3-CH2-NC**
```
  AgCN is COVALENT, so the carbon end is tied up in the Ag-C bond. Only
  the NITROGEN lone pair is free to attack.
     AgCN -> attack by N -> R-NC  ISOCYANIDE
     KCN  -> attack by C -> R-CN  NITRILE
```

> **SHORTCUT:** **Silver salt = attack by Nitrogen.** Applies to both AgCN → R-NC
> and AgNO2 → R-NO2. One rule covers four questions.

---

### Q7
CH3-CH2-I + KNO2 gives:

(a) nitroethane  (b) ethyl nitrite  (c) ethylamine  (d) ethanol

**ANSWER: (b) ethyl nitrite, CH3-CH2-O-N=O**
```
  KNO2 is IONIC, so the free NO2- ion attacks through OXYGEN -> R-O-N=O
  AgNO2 is covalent -> attacks through NITROGEN -> R-NO2 (nitroalkane)
```

### Q8
CH3-CH2-Br treated with **alcoholic** KOH gives mainly:

(a) ethanol  (b) ethene  (c) diethyl ether  (d) ethanal

**ANSWER: (b) ethene**
```
     AQUEOUS  KOH  ->  SUBSTITUTION  ->  alcohol
     ALCOHOLIC KOH ->  ELIMINATION   ->  alkene

     CH3-CH2-Br --alc. KOH, heat--> CH2=CH2 + KBr + H2O
```

> **SHORTCUT:** The words "aqueous" and "alcoholic" are the whole question. Circle
> them the moment you read them. Examiners put this in every single paper.

### Q9
The reaction of an alkyl halide with silver acetate gives:

(a) an acid  (b) an ester  (c) an amide  (d) an anhydride

**ANSWER: (b) an ester**
```
     CH3-CH2-Br  +  CH3COOAg   ->   CH3-COO-CH2-CH3  +  AgBr
                                    ethyl acetate (an ESTER)
```

---

### Q10
CH3-CH2-Br reacts with excess ammonia in a sealed tube to give:

(a) ethanenitrile  (b) ethylamine  (c) ethanol  (d) nitroethane

**ANSWER: (b) ethylamine, CH3-CH2-NH2**
```
     R-X + NH3 (excess) -> R-NH2 + HX
  Excess ammonia is used so that the primary amine is not further
  alkylated to secondary and tertiary amines and the quaternary salt.
```

### Q11
Chlorobenzene on treatment with CH3Cl and sodium in dry ether gives:

(a) benzene  (b) biphenyl  (c) toluene  (d) chlorotoluene

**ANSWER: (c) toluene**
```
  Aryl halide + alkyl halide + Na / dry ether = WURTZ-FITTIG reaction.
     C6H5-Cl + CH3-Cl + 2 Na --dry ether--> C6H5-CH3 + 2 NaCl
```

---

### Q12
2 C6H5-Br + 2 Na in dry ether gives:

(a) benzene  (b) biphenyl  (c) toluene  (d) phenol

**ANSWER: (b) biphenyl, C6H5-C6H5**
```
  Two ARYL halides with sodium = the FITTIG reaction.
     Wurtz        = alkyl + alkyl -> alkane
     Wurtz-Fittig = aryl  + alkyl -> alkylbenzene
     Fittig       = aryl  + aryl  -> biaryl
```

# PATTERN 3 — MARKOVNIKOV AND THE PEROXIDE EFFECT
# ===============================================================

### Q13
Propene reacts with HBr in the presence of benzoyl peroxide to give:

(a) 2-bromopropane  (b) 1-bromopropane  (c) 1,2-dibromopropane  (d) propan-2-ol

**ANSWER: (b) 1-bromopropane**
```
  Peroxide + HBr = ANTI-MARKOVNIKOV (Kharasch effect).
     CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr --peroxide--> CH3-CH2-CH2-Br
  It goes through the more stable SECONDARY free radical.
```

---

### Q14
The peroxide effect is observed with:

(a) HCl only  (b) HBr only  (c) HI only  (d) all hydrogen halides

**ANSWER: (b) HBr only**
```
  HCl : the H-Cl bond is too STRONG to be broken by the free radical.
  HI  : the H-I bond is weak, but the iodine radicals formed just
        recombine to I2 instead of adding to the alkene.
  Only HBr has the right balance.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** "Peroxide effect = **B**romine only." Any option mentioning HCl or
> HI is wrong immediately.

---

### Q15
2-methylpropene + HCl gives mainly:

(a) 1-chloro-2-methylpropane
(b) 2-chloro-2-methylpropane
(c) 1,2-dichloro-2-methylpropane
(d) 2-methylpropan-2-ol

**ANSWER: (b) 2-chloro-2-methylpropane**
```
     (CH3)2C=CH2 + HCl -> (CH3)3C-Cl
  Markovnikov: H goes to the CH2 (which has more hydrogens), so Cl ends up
  on the carbon with none. This is also the route through the stable
  TERTIARY carbocation.
```

---

### Q16
Which of the following alkenes gives the same product with HBr whether peroxide is
present or not?

(a) propene  (b) but-1-ene  (c) but-2-ene  (d) 2-methylpropene

**ANSWER: (c) but-2-ene**
```
     CH3-CH=CH-CH3  is a SYMMETRICAL alkene. Both double-bond carbons
     carry one H and one CH3, so it does not matter which end the Br goes
     to - the product is 2-bromobutane either way.

  Markovnikov and anti-Markovnikov only differ for UNSYMMETRICAL alkenes.
```

     SANDMEYER  = CUPROUS HALIDE (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN) + HX

# PATTERN 5 — OPTICAL ACTIVITY: IS IT CHIRAL?
# ===============================================================

### Q22
Which of the following is optically active?

(a) 1-chloropropane  (b) 2-chloropropane  (c) 2-chlorobutane
(d) 2-chloro-2-methylpropane

**ANSWER: (c) 2-chlorobutane**
```
  Look for ONE carbon with FOUR DIFFERENT groups.

  1-chloropropane  CH3CH2CH2Cl  -> the C-Cl carbon has two H's. NO.
  2-chloropropane  CH3CHClCH3   -> two CH3 groups. NO.
  2-chlorobutane   CH3CHClC2H5  -> Cl, H, CH3, C2H5 - ALL DIFFERENT. YES.
  2-chloro-2-methylpropane (CH3)3CCl -> three CH3 groups. NO.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Do not count the carbons in the molecule. Look at ONE carbon and its
> four attachments. Any two the same → not chiral.

---

### Q23
How many optically active isomers of C4H9Br exist?

(a) 1  (b) 2  (c) 3  (d) 4

**ANSWER: (b) 2**
```
  C4H9Br has FOUR structural isomers:
     1-bromobutane            not chiral
     2-bromobutane            CHIRAL  -> exists as (+) and (-) forms
     1-bromo-2-methylpropane  not chiral
     2-bromo-2-methylpropane  not chiral

  Only 2-bromobutane is chiral, and a chiral compound exists as TWO
  optically active forms (a pair of enantiomers).
  Answer: 2 optically active forms (from 1 chiral structure).
```

---

### Q24
A racemic mixture is:

(a) optically active and dextrorotatory
(b) optically active and laevorotatory
(c) optically inactive due to external compensation
(d) optically inactive due to internal compensation

**ANSWER: (c) optically inactive due to external compensation**
```
  50 : 50 mixture of (+) and (-). Their rotations cancel exactly.
  "External" because the cancellation is between two SEPARATE molecules.
  ("Internal compensation" is the meso compound case - one molecule
   cancelling itself - which is a different idea.)
```

---

### Q25
The SN1 hydrolysis of an optically active alkyl halide gives:

(a) a single enantiomer with inversion
(b) a single enantiomer with retention
(c) a racemic mixture
(d) a meso compound

**ANSWER: (c) a racemic mixture**
```
  The carbocation intermediate is FLAT (sp2). Attack from either face is
  equally likely, so 50 % inversion + 50 % retention = racemic mixture.
```

# ===============================================================

### Q26
Chlorobenzene is less reactive than chloromethane towards nucleophilic substitution
because:

(a) the C-Cl bond has partial double bond character
(b) the carbon is sp2 hybridised
(c) the phenyl cation is unstable
(d) all of the above

**ANSWER: (d) all of the above**
```
  All five standard reasons: resonance / partial double bond character,
  shorter and stronger bond (169 pm vs 178 pm), sp2 carbon (33 % s
  character), instability of the phenyl cation, and repulsion between the
  nucleophile and the electron-rich ring.
```

---

### Q27
The C-Cl bond length in chlorobenzene compared with chloromethane is:

(a) longer  (b) shorter  (c) the same  (d) cannot be predicted

**ANSWER: (b) shorter**
```
     C-Cl in CH3-Cl  = 178 pm
     C-Cl in C6H5-Cl = 169 pm
  Resonance gives it partial DOUBLE bond character, which shortens it.
```

---

### Q28
Halogens are ortho-para directing yet deactivating because:

(a) −I effect deactivates, +R effect directs to o and p
(b) +R effect deactivates, −I effect directs to o and p
(c) they are electron donating overall
(d) they are meta directing

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  -I effect (inductive withdrawal) makes the whole ring electron poor
     ==> slower than benzene ==> DEACTIVATING (controls the RATE)
  +R effect (resonance donation) puts extra density specifically at the
     ortho and para carbons  ==> ORTHO-PARA DIRECTING (controls the PLACE)
```

### Q29
Which is hydrolysed most easily by aqueous NaOH?

(a) chlorobenzene
(b) p-nitrochlorobenzene
(c) 2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene
(d) 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene

**ANSWER: (d) 2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene**
```
  More electron-withdrawing -NO2 groups at ORTHO and PARA = more
  stabilisation of the negatively charged intermediate = easier attack.

     chlorobenzene       NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm
     p-nitro-            NaOH, 443 K
     2,4-dinitro-        aq. NaOH, 368 K
     2,4,6-trinitro-     just WARM WATER
```

### Q30
Which of the following will NOT activate chlorobenzene towards nucleophilic
substitution?

(a) o-NO2  (b) p-NO2  (c) m-NO2  (d) 2,4-dinitro

**ANSWER: (c) m-NO2**
```
  From the META position the negative charge of the intermediate can never
  reach the carbon bearing the nitro group, so it cannot be delocalised
  onto the nitro oxygens. No stabilisation = no activation.
```

Chlorobenzene reacts with NaOH to give phenol under the conditions:

### Q32
The correct order of boiling points is:

(a) CH3F > CH3Cl > CH3Br > CH3I
(b) CH3I > CH3Br > CH3Cl > CH3F
(c) CH3Cl > CH3F > CH3Br > CH3I
(d) CH3Br > CH3I > CH3Cl > CH3F

**ANSWER: (b) CH3I > CH3Br > CH3Cl > CH3F**
```
  Bigger halogen = more electrons = more POLARISABLE = stronger van der
  Waals forces = higher boiling point.
  (Do not use polarity here - CH3I is the least polar yet boils highest.)
```

---

### Q33
The compound with the highest dipole moment is:

(a) CH3F  (b) CH3Cl  (c) CH3Br  (d) CH3I

**ANSWER: (b) CH3Cl**
```
  Dipole moment = charge x DISTANCE. Fluorine has the biggest charge
  separation but the shortest bond; chlorine gives the best combination.

     CH3Cl (1.86 D) > CH3F (1.85 D) > CH3Br (1.83 D) > CH3I (1.62 D)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Boiling point order → **I is highest**.
> Dipole moment order → **Cl is highest**. These two are the trick pair.

---

### Q34
Among the isomeric dichlorobenzenes, the one with the highest melting point is:

(a) ortho  (b) meta  (c) para  (d) all are equal

**ANSWER: (c) para**
```
  The para isomer is the most SYMMETRICAL, so it packs into the crystal
  lattice most tightly and needs the most energy to melt.
     m.p.: para 326 K >> ortho 256 K , meta 249 K
```

---

### Q35
Among n-butyl bromide, isobutyl bromide, sec-butyl bromide and tert-butyl bromide,
the highest boiling point belongs to:

(a) n-butyl bromide  (b) isobutyl  (c) sec-butyl  (d) tert-butyl

**ANSWER: (a) n-butyl bromide**
```
  BRANCHING LOWERS the boiling point. A branched molecule is more
  spherical, so it has less surface area in contact with its neighbours
  and weaker van der Waals forces.
     n- > iso- > sec- > tert-
```

### Q43
CH3MgBr reacting with acetone followed by hydrolysis gives:

(a) a primary alcohol  (b) a secondary alcohol  (c) a tertiary alcohol
(d) a carboxylic acid

**ANSWER: (c) a tertiary alcohol**
```
     CH3MgBr + CH3-CO-CH3 --> then H3O+ --> (CH3)3C-OH

     HCHO           -> 1deg alcohol
     other aldehyde -> 2deg alcohol
     KETONE         -> 3deg alcohol
     CO2            -> carboxylic acid
```

---

### Q44
CH3-CH2-MgBr + CO2 followed by acid hydrolysis gives:

(a) ethanoic acid  (b) propanoic acid  (c) propan-1-ol  (d) propanone

**ANSWER: (b) propanoic acid, CH3-CH2-COOH**
```
  The CO2 adds ONE extra carbon to the alkyl group of the Grignard
  reagent. C2H5- (2 C) + CO2 (1 C) = 3 carbons = propanoic acid.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Grignard + CO2 always gives an acid with **one carbon more** than the
> alkyl group. Count the carbons and eliminate two options instantly.

---

### Q45
A Grignard reagent must be prepared in dry ether because it reacts with water to
give:

(a) an alcohol  (b) an alkane  (c) an alkene  (d) an ether

**ANSWER: (b) an alkane**
```
     CH3-Mg-Br + H2O -> CH4 + Mg(OH)Br
  Any active hydrogen (H2O, ROH, NH3, HX, RCOOH) destroys the reagent.
```

---

### Q46
In the Grignard reagent R-Mg-X, the carbon attached to magnesium carries:

(a) a partial positive charge  (b) a partial negative charge
(c) no charge  (d) a full positive charge

**ANSWER: (b) a partial negative charge**
```
  Carbon is MORE electronegative than magnesium, so the polarity is
     C(delta-) --- Mg(delta+)
  That is why R-MgX behaves as a CARBANION: a strong nucleophile and a
  strong base. It is the only common case where the carbon of an organic
  halide-derived species is negative.
```

### Q47
2-bromobutane with alcoholic KOH gives predominantly:

(a) but-1-ene  (b) but-2-ene  (c) butan-2-ol  (d) buta-1,3-diene

**ANSWER: (b) but-2-ene**
```
  SAYTZEFF'S RULE: the more highly substituted alkene is the major
  product. But-2-ene is disubstituted, but-1-ene only monosubstituted.
     but-2-ene ~80 % , but-1-ene ~20 %
```

### Q48
Which alkyl halide gives the maximum elimination product with alcoholic KOH?

(a) CH3-CH2-Br  (b) (CH3)2CH-Br  (c) (CH3)3C-Br  (d) CH3-Br

**ANSWER: (c) (CH3)3C-Br**
```
  Elimination is favoured by TERTIARY halides (crowded carbon blocks
  substitution, and there are many beta hydrogens available).
  CH3-Br has NO beta hydrogen at all, so it cannot eliminate.
```

### Q49
Vicinal dihalide + Zn dust gives:

(a) an alkane  (b) an alkene  (c) an alkyne  (d) an alcohol

**ANSWER: (b) an alkene**
```
     CH2Br-CH2Br + Zn --> CH2=CH2 + ZnBr2
  This is DEHALOGENATION, and it is used as a test for a vicinal dihalide.
```

### Q50
Vinyl chloride does not undergo nucleophilic substitution easily because:

(a) it is a gas
(b) the C-Cl bond has partial double bond character due to resonance
(c) chlorine is too small
(d) it has no beta hydrogen

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
     CH2=CH-Cl  <-->  (-)CH2-CH=Cl(+)
  The lone pair of Cl is delocalised into the double bond, exactly as in
  chlorobenzene. The C-Cl bond gains partial double bond character and
  becomes hard to break. Vinyl and aryl halides behave alike.
```

### Q51
Which of these is a benzylic halide?

(a) C6H5-Cl  (b) C6H5-CH2-Cl  (c) CH2=CH-Cl  (d) p-Cl-C6H4-CH3

**ANSWER: (b) C6H5-CH2-Cl**
```
  Benzylic = halogen on the sp3 carbon ATTACHED TO the ring.
  Aryl     = halogen directly ON the ring (option a and d).
  Vinylic  = halogen on a double-bond carbon (option c).
```

### Q52
Chlorination of toluene in the presence of sunlight and the absence of a catalyst
gives:

(a) o-chlorotoluene  (b) p-chlorotoluene  (c) benzyl chloride  (d) benzoic acid

**ANSWER: (c) benzyl chloride, C6H5-CH2Cl**
```
     SUNLIGHT / hv, no catalyst   -> SIDE CHAIN (free radical)
     DARK + Lewis acid catalyst   -> RING (electrophilic)
  "Light hits the side, dark hits the ring."
```

### Q53
Which reagent converts an alcohol into an alkyl chloride giving only gaseous
by-products?

(a) PCl5  (b) PCl3  (c) SOCl2  (d) conc. HCl / ZnCl2

**ANSWER: (c) SOCl2**
```
     R-OH + SOCl2 --pyridine--> R-Cl + SO2 (gas) + HCl (gas)
  The gases escape, so the product is obtained PURE. This is why thionyl
  chloride is the preferred reagent (Darzen's process).
```

### Q54
The Lucas test distinguishes primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols. Which shows
turbidity immediately?

(a) primary  (b) secondary  (c) tertiary  (d) all equally

**ANSWER: (c) tertiary**
```
  Lucas reagent = conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl2.
     3deg -> turbidity IMMEDIATELY   (most stable carbocation)
     2deg -> in about 5 minutes
     1deg -> no turbidity at room temperature
```

### Q55
Which of the following will show the fastest reaction with aqueous KOH?

(a) CH3-CH2-Cl  (b) CH2=CH-Cl  (c) C6H5-Cl  (d) C6H5-CH2-Cl

**ANSWER: (d) C6H5-CH2-Cl (benzyl chloride)**
```
  Benzyl chloride forms a resonance-stabilised benzyl carbocation, so it
  is the most reactive of the four.
  Vinyl chloride and chlorobenzene are almost inert (partial double bond
  character), and chloroethane is only ordinarily reactive.

  OVERALL:  benzyl , allyl > 3deg > 2deg > 1deg >> vinyl , aryl
```
