## Topic: Classification and nomenclature

### Q A1
Write the IUPAC names of (a) `CH3-CH(Cl)-CH(Br)-CH3` (b) `(CH3)3C-CH2-Br`
(c) `CH2=CH-CH2-Cl`.

**ANSWER:**
```
  (a) CH3-CH(Cl)-CH(Br)-CH3
      4 carbons -> butane. From the left: Cl at 2, Br at 3 -> {2,3}
      From the right: Br at 2, Cl at 3 -> {2,3}. Tie, so go alphabetical:
      "bromo" comes before "chloro", so bromo gets the lower number.
      NAME:  2-bromo-3-chlorobutane

  (b) (CH3)3C-CH2-Br
      Longest chain through the C-Br carbon = 3 carbons -> propane.
      NAME:  1-bromo-2,2-dimethylpropane   (neopentyl bromide)

  (c) CH2=CH-CH2-Cl
      3 carbons with a double bond at C-1 and Cl at C-3.
      NAME:  3-chloroprop-1-ene   (allyl chloride)
```

### Q A2
What are allylic and benzylic halides? Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ALLYLIC HALIDE  - the halogen is attached to an sp3 carbon that is
                    NEXT TO a carbon-carbon double bond.
                    Example:  CH2=CH-CH2-Cl   (allyl chloride)

  BENZYLIC HALIDE - the halogen is attached to an sp3 carbon that is
                    directly joined to a benzene ring.
                    Example:  C6H5-CH2-Cl     (benzyl chloride)

  Both are VERY reactive, because they give resonance-stabilised
  carbocations.
```

### Q A3
Distinguish between a geminal dihalide and a vicinal dihalide with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  GEMINAL (gem) dihalide  - both halogens on the SAME carbon.
                            CH3-CHCl2   1,1-dichloroethane
                            (alkylidene halide)

  VICINAL (vic) dihalide  - halogens on ADJACENT carbons.
                            CH2Cl-CH2Cl 1,2-dichloroethane
                            (alkylene halide)
```

### Q A4
Why is the C-Cl bond length in chlorobenzene shorter than in chloromethane?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In chlorobenzene the lone pair on chlorine is delocalised into the
  benzene ring by RESONANCE. This gives the C-Cl bond PARTIAL DOUBLE BOND
  character, which shortens it.

     C-Cl in CH3-Cl   = 178 pm      (pure single bond)
     C-Cl in C6H5-Cl  = 169 pm      (partial double bond)
```

## Topic: Preparation

### Q A5
What is the Finkelstein reaction? Write the equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The conversion of an alkyl chloride or bromide into an alkyl IODIDE by
  heating with sodium iodide in DRY ACETONE.

     CH3-CH2-Cl  +  NaI   --dry acetone-->   CH3-CH2-I  +  NaCl

  It works because NaI dissolves in dry acetone while NaCl does not.
  The NaCl precipitates out and drives the equilibrium to the right.
```

### Q A6
What is the Swarts reaction? Write the equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The preparation of an alkyl FLUORIDE by heating an alkyl bromide or
  chloride with a metallic fluoride such as AgF, Hg2F2, CoF2 or SbF3.

     CH3-Br  +  AgF   -->   CH3-F  +  AgBr

  Industrial use:  CCl4 + 2 SbF3 --SbCl5--> CCl2F2 (Freon-12) + 2 SbCl3
```

### Q A7
Why is thionyl chloride preferred over PCl5 for converting an alcohol into an
alkyl chloride?

**ANSWER:**
```
     R-OH  +  SOCl2   --pyridine-->   R-Cl  +  SO2 (gas)  +  HCl (gas)

  Both by-products, SO2 and HCl, are GASES and escape from the reaction
  mixture. The alkyl chloride is therefore obtained in a PURE state with
  no separation step needed.

  With PCl5 the by-product POCl3 is a liquid and must be separated from
  the product, so the product is less pure.
```

### Q A8
State Markovnikov's rule with an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MARKOVNIKOV'S RULE: when an unsymmetrical reagent HX adds to an
  unsymmetrical alkene, the NEGATIVE part (X) attaches to the carbon
  carrying the SMALLER number of hydrogen atoms, and H goes to the carbon
  with more hydrogens.

     CH3-CH=CH2  +  HBr   -->   CH3-CH(Br)-CH3
     propene                    2-bromopropane   (MAJOR)

  Reason: the reaction goes through the more stable SECONDARY carbocation
  CH3-CH(+)-CH3 rather than the primary one.
```

### Q A9
What is the peroxide effect? Which halogen acid shows it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PEROXIDE (KHARASCH) EFFECT: in the presence of an organic peroxide such
  as benzoyl peroxide, HBr adds to an unsymmetrical alkene in the
  ANTI-MARKOVNIKOV way - the Br goes to the carbon with MORE hydrogens.

     CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr --benzoyl peroxide--> CH3-CH2-CH2-Br
                                            1-bromopropane

  It is shown by HBr ONLY.
     HCl - the H-Cl bond is too STRONG to be broken by the free radical.
     HI  - the iodine radicals formed simply recombine to I2 instead of
           adding to the double bond.
```

### Q A10
What is the Sandmeyer reaction? Write one equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The replacement of the diazonium group of a benzenediazonium salt by a
  halogen, using a CUPROUS (Cu(I)) HALIDE in the corresponding halogen
  acid. Nitrogen gas is evolved.

     C6H5-N2(+)Cl(-)  +  CuCl / HCl   -->   C6H5-Cl  +  N2

  Also:  with CuBr/HBr -> bromobenzene ;  with CuCN/KCN -> benzonitrile.
```

---

### Q A11
What is the Gattermann reaction? How does it differ from the Sandmeyer reaction?

**ANSWER:**
```
     C6H5-N2(+)Cl(-)  +  Cu powder / HCl   -->   C6H5-Cl  +  N2

  DIFFERENCE:
     SANDMEYER  uses a CUPROUS HALIDE  (CuCl, CuBr, CuCN) with HX.
     GATTERMANN uses COPPER POWDER with HX.

  Sandmeyer gives better yields; Gattermann is simpler and cheaper.
```

## Topic: Physical properties

### Q A12
Why do haloalkanes have higher boiling points than the corresponding alkanes?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The C-X bond is polar, so haloalkane molecules attract one another by
  DIPOLE-DIPOLE forces IN ADDITION to the usual van der Waals forces.
  Also the halogen adds a lot of mass and electrons, which strengthens the
  van der Waals attraction. More energy is needed to separate them, so the
  boiling point is higher.

     CH3-CH3 (b.p. 185 K)   <   CH3-CH2-Cl (b.p. 285 K)
```

---

### Q A13
Arrange in increasing order of boiling point: CH3Cl, CH3Br, CH3I, CH3F. Give the
reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
     CH3F  <  CH3Cl  <  CH3Br  <  CH3I

  REASON: going down the group the halogen atom becomes larger and has
  more electrons, so it is MORE POLARISABLE. This gives stronger van der
  Waals (London dispersion) forces, and hence a higher boiling point.

  NOTE: this is NOT explained by polarity - CH3I is the LEAST polar of the
  four, yet it boils highest.
```

---

### Q A14
Why are haloalkanes only very slightly soluble in water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  To dissolve, a haloalkane would have to break the strong HYDROGEN BONDS
  between water molecules. But a haloalkane cannot itself form hydrogen
  bonds with water, so the energy released by the new interactions is much
  less than the energy needed to break the water-water hydrogen bonds.
  The process is therefore energetically unfavourable and the haloalkane
  stays largely undissolved.
```

### Q A15
Why does the para isomer of a dihalobenzene have a much higher melting point than
the ortho and meta isomers?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The PARA isomer is more SYMMETRICAL, so its molecules pack into the
  crystal lattice more closely and more regularly. The lattice is
  therefore stronger and needs more energy to break.

     m.p.:  p-dichlorobenzene (326 K) >> o- (256 K) , m- (249 K)
```

## Topic: Reactions

### Q A16
Why does KCN give a nitrile with an alkyl halide while AgCN gives an isocyanide?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The cyanide ion is an AMBIDENT nucleophile - both C and N carry a lone
  pair.

     KCN is IONIC    -> a free CN(-) ion exists and attacks through
                        CARBON (the C-C bond formed is stronger)
                        -> R-CN , a NITRILE (alkyl cyanide)

     AgCN is COVALENT-> the carbon end is tied up in the Ag-C bond, so
                        only the NITROGEN lone pair is free to attack
                        -> R-NC , an ISOCYANIDE (alkyl carbylamine)
```

### Q A17
What is a Grignard reagent? How is it prepared? Why must dry ether be used?

**ANSWER:**
```
  An alkylmagnesium halide, R-Mg-X, is called a GRIGNARD REAGENT.

     R-X  +  Mg   --dry ether-->   R-Mg-X

  Dry ether is essential because a Grignard reagent reacts instantly with
  any compound containing an active hydrogen, including water:

     CH3-Mg-Br  +  H2O   -->   CH4  +  Mg(OH)Br

  Even a trace of moisture destroys the reagent as an alkane before it can
  react with the intended substrate.
```

### Q A18
Write the Wurtz reaction. What is its main limitation?

**ANSWER:**
```
     2 R-X   +   2 Na   --dry ether-->   R-R   +   2 NaX

     2 CH3-CH2-Br + 2 Na --dry ether--> CH3-CH2-CH2-CH3 + 2 NaBr
                                        n-butane

  LIMITATION: if TWO DIFFERENT alkyl halides are used, a mixture of THREE
  products (R-R, R'-R' and R-R') is obtained, which is very difficult to
  separate. So the reaction is useful only for SYMMETRICAL alkanes, and
  the product always has an EVEN number of carbon atoms.
```

State Saytzeff's rule with an example.

  SAYTZEFF'S RULE: in a dehydrohalogenation, the MAJOR product is the
  MORE HIGHLY SUBSTITUTED alkene - the one formed by removing the beta

        CH3-CH2-CH=CH2   but-1-ene   (monosubstituted) minor (~20 %)

### Q A20
What is a racemic mixture? Why is it optically inactive?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A RACEMIC MIXTURE is an EQUAL (50 : 50) mixture of the dextrorotatory
  (+) and laevorotatory (-) forms of a chiral compound. It is written as
  (+-) or dl-.

  It is optically INACTIVE because the rotation produced by one enantiomer
  is exactly cancelled by the equal and opposite rotation of the other.
  This is called EXTERNAL COMPENSATION.
```

---

### Q A21
What is racemisation? In which mechanism does it occur?

**ANSWER:**
```
  RACEMISATION is the conversion of a single pure optically active
  enantiomer into a racemic (50 : 50) mixture, so that the optical
  activity is lost.

  It occurs in the SN1 mechanism, because the intermediate carbocation is
  FLAT (sp2, trigonal planar) and the nucleophile can attack it equally
  well from either face, giving both enantiomers in equal amounts.
```

### Q A22
Why is chloroform stored in dark coloured bottles filled up to the brim?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Chloroform is slowly oxidised by atmospheric oxygen in the presence of
  SUNLIGHT to the extremely poisonous gas PHOSGENE (carbonyl chloride):

     2 CHCl3  +  O2   --sunlight-->   2 COCl2  +  2 HCl

     DARK bottle          ->  keeps SUNLIGHT out
     FILLED TO THE BRIM   ->  leaves no air (no OXYGEN) inside

  About 1 % ethanol is also added, which converts any phosgene formed into
  harmless diethyl carbonate.
```

### Q B1
Explain the SN2 mechanism with a suitable example. What is its stereochemical
outcome?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SN2 = Substitution Nucleophilic BIMOLECULAR. It has only ONE step.

  Example:  CH3-Br  +  OH(-)   -->   CH3-OH  +  Br(-)

  MECHANISM:  the nucleophile attacks the carbon from the side EXACTLY
  OPPOSITE the leaving group (back-side attack, 180 degrees). The new bond
  forms at the same time as the old one breaks.

                    H                        H
                     \                        \
     HO(-)  +         C--Br    -->   HO ------ C      +   Br(-)
                     / \                      / \
                   H    H                   H    H

  THE TRANSITION STATE:

                        +-           -+ (=|=)
                        |      H      |
       HO(-)  ->  Br    | HO---C---Br |    ->    HO-CH3  +  Br(-)
                        |     / \     |
                        |    H   H    |
                        +-           -+

     In the transition state the carbon is momentarily attached to FIVE
     groups; the three unchanged groups lie FLAT in one plane, and both
     the HO-C and C-Br bonds are PARTIAL.

  RATE LAW:   rate = k [R-X] [Nu-]     -> SECOND order, bimolecular.

  STEREOCHEMICAL OUTCOME:  INVERSION of configuration (WALDEN INVERSION).
  The three unchanged groups are turned inside out like an umbrella in a
  storm. If the reactant is optically active, the product is optically
  active too, but with the opposite configuration.

  REACTIVITY ORDER:  CH3-X > 1deg > 2deg > 3deg
  (because of STERIC HINDRANCE - a crowded carbon blocks the back attack)
```

### Q B2
Explain the SN1 mechanism with a suitable example. Why does it give a racemic
mixture?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SN1 = Substitution Nucleophilic UNIMOLECULAR. It has TWO steps.

  Example:  (CH3)3C-Br  +  H2O/OH(-)   -->   (CH3)3C-OH  +  Br(-)

  STEP 1 (SLOW, rate determining) - the C-Br bond breaks on its own:

              CH3                       CH3
               |                         |
       CH3 --- C --- Br   --slow-->  CH3-C(+)      +    Br(-)
               |                         |
              CH3                       CH3
        tert-butyl bromide        TERTIARY CARBOCATION
                                  FLAT, sp2, trigonal planar,
                                  bond angles 120 degrees

  STEP 2 (FAST) - the nucleophile attacks the flat carbocation:

                     OH(-) from ABOVE
                          |
                          v
                        CH3
                         |
             CH3 ----- C(+) ----- CH3       <-- planar
                         |
                         ^
                         |
                     OH(-) from BELOW

  RATE LAW:  rate = k [R-X]   -> FIRST order. The nucleophile does NOT
  appear, because it is not involved until after the slow step.

  WHY A RACEMIC MIXTURE:
     The carbocation is FLAT, so the nucleophile can attack it from either
     face with EQUAL ease. Attack from one face gives retention, attack
     from the other gives inversion, and the two are equally probable.
     The result is a 50 : 50 mixture of the two enantiomers, i.e. a
     RACEMIC MIXTURE, which is optically INACTIVE.

  REACTIVITY ORDER:  3deg > 2deg > 1deg > CH3
  (because a tertiary carbocation is the most stable, thanks to the +I
   effect and hyperconjugation of the three alkyl groups)
```

Compare the SN1 and SN2 mechanisms (any eight points).

**ANSWER:**

  +----+---------------------+---------------------+---------------------+
  | No | POINT               | SN1                 | SN2                 |
  +----+---------------------+---------------------+---------------------+
  | 1  | Number of steps     | TWO                 | ONE                 |

  |    |                     |                     | transition state)   |

### Q B4
Why are haloarenes much less reactive than haloalkanes towards nucleophilic
substitution? Give any four reasons.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. RESONANCE / PARTIAL DOUBLE BOND CHARACTER
     The lone pair on the halogen is delocalised into the benzene ring:

        Cl(..)         Cl(+)          Cl(+)          Cl(+)
         |              ||             ||             ||
        / \            / \            / \            / \
       |   |  <-->    |   |(-) <-->  (-)  |  <-->    |   |
        \ /            \ /            \ /            \ /
                                                      (-)

     In three of these structures the C-Cl bond is a DOUBLE bond, so the
     real bond has partial double bond character and is hard to break.

  2. SHORTER, STRONGER BOND
     C-Cl in CH3Cl = 178 pm ;  C-Cl in C6H5Cl = 169 pm.
     A shorter bond is a stronger bond, and needs more energy to break.

  3. sp2 HYBRIDISED CARBON
     The carbon holding the halogen is sp2 (33 % s character) in a
     haloarene but sp3 (25 % s character) in a haloalkane. Higher s
     character means the carbon is more electronegative and holds the
     halogen more tightly.

  4. INSTABILITY OF THE PHENYL CATION
     An SN1 route would need a phenyl cation, C6H5(+). Its empty orbital
     lies IN the plane of the ring, at right angles to the pi cloud, so it
     gets no resonance stabilisation. The cation never forms.

  5. REPULSION BY THE ELECTRON-RICH RING
     The pi cloud of the ring is electron rich, and so is the incoming
     nucleophile. Like charges repel, so the nucleophile is pushed away.

  CONSEQUENCE: chlorobenzene needs NaOH at 623 K and 300 atm to be
  hydrolysed, whereas chloroethane reacts with dilute NaOH at room
  temperature.
```

### Q B5
Explain why halogens are ortho-para directing but deactivating in electrophilic
substitution.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The halogen exerts TWO OPPOSITE effects at the same time.

  (1) -I EFFECT (inductive withdrawal)
      The halogen is highly electronegative, so it pulls electron density
      OUT of the ring through the sigma bond. The whole ring becomes
      electron poor, so it is attacked by an electrophile more SLOWLY than
      benzene.
      ==>  The halogen is DEACTIVATING.

  (2) +R EFFECT (resonance donation)
      The halogen's lone pair is pushed INTO the ring. From the resonance
      structures, the extra electron density (the negative charge) appears
      only at the ORTHO and PARA carbons, never at meta.
      ==>  The ortho and para positions are richer than the meta position,
           so the electrophile attacks there.

  RESULT:  the -I effect controls the RATE  ->  DEACTIVATING
           the +R effect controls the PLACE ->  ORTHO-PARA DIRECTING

  In one line: "slower than benzene, but when it does happen it happens at
  ortho and para."

  Example:  C6H5-Cl + conc. HNO3/conc. H2SO4 -> o- and p-nitrochlorobenzene
```

### Q B6
Describe the reactions of a Grignard reagent with (a) water (b) formaldehyde
(c) acetone (d) carbon dioxide.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Preparation:   R-X  +  Mg  --dry ether-->  R-Mg-X
  In R-Mg-X the carbon carries a partial NEGATIVE charge, so it acts as a
  carbanion: a strong nucleophile and a strong base.

  (a) WITH WATER  ->  an ALKANE
        CH3-Mg-Br  +  H2O   -->   CH4  +  Mg(OH)Br

  (b) WITH FORMALDEHYDE  ->  a PRIMARY alcohol
        CH3-MgBr + HCHO --> CH3-CH2-OMgBr --H3O+--> CH3-CH2-OH

  (c) WITH ACETONE (a ketone)  ->  a TERTIARY alcohol
        CH3-MgBr + CH3-CO-CH3 --> (CH3)3C-OMgBr --H3O+--> (CH3)3C-OH

  (d) WITH CARBON DIOXIDE  ->  a CARBOXYLIC ACID
        CH3-MgBr + CO2 --> CH3-COOMgBr --H3O+--> CH3-COOH

  SUMMARY RULE:
        HCHO           ->  1deg alcohol
        other aldehyde ->  2deg alcohol
        ketone         ->  3deg alcohol
        CO2            ->  carboxylic acid (one carbon MORE than R)
```

### Q B7
Explain the peroxide effect with the mechanism.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In the presence of benzoyl peroxide, HBr adds to an unsymmetrical alkene
  in the ANTI-MARKOVNIKOV manner:

     CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr --benzoyl peroxide--> CH3-CH2-CH2-Br

  MECHANISM (free radical, four steps):

  1. The peroxide breaks up:
        C6H5-CO-O-O-CO-C6H5 --heat--> 2 C6H5-COO. --> 2 C6H5. + 2 CO2

  2. The radical pulls an H off HBr:
        C6H5. + H-Br  ->  C6H6 + Br.

  3. The bromine radical adds to the double bond. It adds to the TERMINAL
     carbon, because that produces the MORE STABLE SECONDARY radical:

        CH3-CH=CH2 + Br.  ->  CH3-.CH-CH2-Br   [2deg radical, STABLE]
                          not CH3-CHBr-.CH2    [1deg radical, unstable]

  4. The radical takes an H from another HBr, regenerating Br.:
        CH3-.CH-CH2-Br + H-Br  ->  CH3-CH2-CH2-Br + Br.

  RESULT: the Br ends up on the carbon with MORE hydrogens, the opposite
  of Markovnikov's rule.

  IMPORTANT: the peroxide effect is shown by HBr ONLY. The H-Cl bond is
  too strong to be broken in step 2, and with HI the iodine radicals
  recombine to I2 instead of adding to the alkene.
```

### Q B8
How are haloalkanes prepared from alcohols? Write all the methods.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. WITH A HALOGEN ACID
        R-OH + HCl --anhyd. ZnCl2--> R-Cl + H2O   (Lucas reagent)
        R-OH + HBr --> R-Br + H2O
        R-OH + HI  --> R-I  + H2O
     Order of acid reactivity:    HI > HBr > HCl
     Order of alcohol reactivity: 3deg > 2deg > 1deg

  2. WITH PHOSPHORUS PENTACHLORIDE
        R-OH + PCl5  ->  R-Cl + POCl3 + HCl

  3. WITH PHOSPHORUS TRICHLORIDE
        3 R-OH + PCl3  ->  3 R-Cl + H3PO3

  4. WITH THIONYL CHLORIDE (Darzen's process) - PREFERRED
        R-OH + SOCl2 --pyridine--> R-Cl + SO2 + HCl
     Both by-products are GASES, so the product is obtained PURE.

  5. FOR BROMIDES AND IODIDES
        R-OH + P + Br2  ->  R-Br      (PBr3 formed in situ)
        R-OH + red P + I2 -> R-I      (PI3 formed in situ)
```

### Q B9
What is optical isomerism? Explain chirality, enantiomers and asymmetric carbon
with an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  OPTICAL ISOMERISM is the type of stereoisomerism shown by compounds that
  can rotate the plane of plane-polarised light.

  CHIRALITY - an object is CHIRAL if it cannot be superimposed on its
  mirror image, like your left and right hands. Such a molecule is
  optically active.

  ASYMMETRIC (CHIRAL) CARBON - an sp3 carbon attached to FOUR DIFFERENT
  groups. It is marked with a star, C*.

     Example: 2-chlorobutane

                    Cl
                    |
            CH3 --- C* --- H
                    |
                   C2H5

             |            |            |

### Q B11
Explain the effect of the nitro group at the ortho and para positions on the
reactivity of chlorobenzene towards nucleophilic substitution.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An electron-withdrawing group such as -NO2 at the ORTHO or PARA position
  makes the chlorine much easier to replace.

  +--------------------------------+------------------------------------+

  +--------------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  chlorobenzene                 |  NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm              |

           |                        |

  stabilised and the reaction proceeds easily.

  From the META position the negative charge can never reach the carbon
  bearing the nitro group, so a meta nitro group gives NO activation.

### Q C1
Explain the mechanisms of nucleophilic substitution in haloalkanes. Compare SN1 and
SN2 with respect to the number of steps, kinetics, effect of the substrate,
stereochemistry, solvent and nucleophile.

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown):**
```
  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1 mark
  INTRODUCTION
  In R-X the halogen is more electronegative than carbon, so the C-X bond
  is polar and the carbon carries a partial positive charge. A nucleophile
  (a species with a lone pair) attacks this carbon and the halide ion
  leaves. This is NUCLEOPHILIC SUBSTITUTION. It happens by two different
  mechanisms, SN1 and SN2.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  THE SN2 MECHANISM   (Substitution Nucleophilic Bimolecular)

  It is a ONE-STEP reaction. The nucleophile attacks from the side exactly
  opposite the leaving group.

     Example:  CH3-Br  +  OH(-)  ->  CH3-OH  +  Br(-)

                    H                        H
                     \                        \
     HO(-)  +         C--Br    -->   HO ------ C      +   Br(-)
                     / \                      / \
                   H    H                   H    H

     TRANSITION STATE:
                        +-              -+ (=|=)
                        |       H        |
        HO ---------    | HO --- C --- Br|    ---------> products
                        |      /   \     |
                        |     H     H    |
                        +-              -+
        carbon momentarily bonded to FIVE groups; the three unchanged
        groups lie flat in a plane; both HO-C and C-Br bonds are PARTIAL.

     rate = k [R-X][Nu-]    ->  SECOND order, bimolecular
     There is NO intermediate, only a transition state.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  THE SN1 MECHANISM   (Substitution Nucleophilic Unimolecular)

  It is a TWO-STEP reaction with a carbocation intermediate.

     Example:  (CH3)3C-Br + H2O -> (CH3)3C-OH + HBr

     STEP 1 (SLOW, rate determining):
              CH3                        CH3
               |                          |
       CH3 --- C --- Br   --slow-->  CH3- C(+)   +   Br(-)
               |                          |
              CH3                        CH3
                                   FLAT carbocation (sp2, planar)

     STEP 2 (FAST):
                       OH(-) attacks from the TOP
                            |
                            v
                          CH3
                           |
              CH3 ------ C(+) ------ CH3
                           |
                            ^
                            |
                       OH(-) attacks from the BOTTOM

     rate = k [R-X]   ->  FIRST order, unimolecular.
     The nucleophile does not appear in the rate law.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 1 mark
  STEREOCHEMISTRY

     SN2 : the umbrella of three groups is turned inside out.
           ==> 100 % INVERSION of configuration (WALDEN INVERSION).
           The product is optically ACTIVE.

     SN1 : the carbocation is flat, so attack from both faces is equally
           likely, giving 50 % retention and 50 % inversion.
           ==> RACEMISATION. The product is optically INACTIVE.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  THE FULL COMPARISON

  +---------------------+---------------------+----------------------+
  |  POINT              |  SN1                |  SN2                 |
  +---------------------+---------------------+----------------------+
  |  Steps              |  two                |  one                 |
  |  Intermediate       |  carbocation        |  none                |
  |  Rate law           |  k[RX]              |  k[RX][Nu-]          |
  |  Order              |  first              |  second              |
  |  Molecularity       |  unimolecular       |  bimolecular         |
  |  Effect of [Nu-]    |  none               |  directly proportional|
  |  Substrate order    |  3deg>2deg>1deg>CH3 |  CH3>1deg>2deg>3deg  |
  |  Reason for order   |  carbocation        |  steric hindrance    |
  |                     |  stability          |                      |
  |  Stereochemistry    |  racemisation       |  inversion           |
  |  Product optically  |  inactive           |  active              |
  |  Solvent            |  polar PROTIC       |  polar APROTIC       |
  |  Nucleophile        |  weak is enough     |  must be strong      |
  |  Rearrangement      |  possible           |  never               |
  +---------------------+---------------------+----------------------+

  The two substrate orders are EXACTLY OPPOSITE, because SN1 needs a
  stable carbocation (favoured by many alkyl groups) while SN2 needs an
  open, uncrowded carbon (spoiled by many alkyl groups).
```

### Q C3
Explain in detail why haloarenes are far less reactive than haloalkanes towards
nucleophilic substitution. What conditions and what substituents make the
substitution possible? Also explain the orientation in electrophilic substitution
of haloarenes.

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown):**
```
  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 4 marks
  PART 1 — THE FIVE REASONS FOR THE LOW REACTIVITY

  (i) RESONANCE / PARTIAL DOUBLE BOND CHARACTER

        Cl(..)          Cl(+)          Cl(+)         Cl(+)
         |               ||             ||            ||
        / \             / \            / \           / \
       |   |   <-->    |   |(-) <-->  (-)  |  <-->   |   |
        \ /             \ /            \ /           \ /
                                                      (-)
      The lone pair on chlorine is delocalised into the ring. In three of
      these structures the C-Cl bond is DOUBLE, so the real bond has
      partial double bond character and is very hard to break.

  (ii) SHORTER AND STRONGER BOND
      C-Cl in CH3-Cl = 178 pm ; C-Cl in C6H5-Cl = 169 pm.
      Shorter bond = stronger bond = more energy needed to break it.

  (iii) sp2 HYBRIDISED CARBON
      In C6H5-Cl the carbon holding Cl is sp2 (33 % s character); in
      CH3-Cl it is sp3 (25 %). Greater s character makes the carbon more
      electronegative, so it grips the halogen more tightly.

  (iv) INSTABILITY OF THE PHENYL CATION
      An SN1 pathway would require C6H5(+). Its empty orbital lies IN the
      plane of the ring, perpendicular to the pi cloud, so there is no
      overlap and no resonance stabilisation. The cation never forms.

  (v) REPULSION BY THE ELECTRON-RICH RING
      The pi cloud of the ring is electron rich, and the incoming
      nucleophile is also electron rich. Like repels like, so the
      nucleophile is pushed away before it can reach the carbon.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  PART 2 — THE CONDITIONS THAT DO FORCE SUBSTITUTION

     C6H5-Cl --(i) NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm (ii) dil. HCl--> C6H5-OH
     (This is the DOW process for making phenol.)

     Compare: CH3-CH2-Cl reacts with dilute NaOH at room temperature.

     ELECTRON-WITHDRAWING GROUPS AT ORTHO AND PARA make it far easier:

     +--------------------------------+---------------------------------+
     |  chlorobenzene                 |  NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm           |
     |  o- or p-nitrochlorobenzene    |  NaOH, 443 K                    |
     |  2,4-dinitrochlorobenzene      |  aq. NaOH, 368 K                |
     |  2,4,6-trinitrochlorobenzene   |  warm water alone               |
     +--------------------------------+---------------------------------+

     REASON: the attack of the nucleophile puts a NEGATIVE charge on the
     ring. From the ortho and para positions this charge can be
     delocalised onto the oxygen atoms of the -NO2 group, stabilising the
     intermediate. From the META position the charge can never reach the
     nitro-bearing carbon, so a meta -NO2 group provides NO activation.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  PART 3 — ELECTROPHILIC SUBSTITUTION: DEACTIVATING YET ORTHO-PARA
           DIRECTING

     The halogen does two opposite things at once.

     -I EFFECT : it is very electronegative, so it pulls electron density
                 out of the whole ring through the sigma bond. The ring
                 becomes electron poor and reacts more SLOWLY than
                 benzene.  ==>  DEACTIVATING.

     +R EFFECT : its lone pair is donated into the ring by resonance, and
                 the extra density appears specifically at the ORTHO and
                 PARA carbons (never at meta).
                 ==>  ORTHO-PARA DIRECTING.

     The -I effect controls the RATE; the +R effect controls the POSITION.

     Examples:
        C6H5Cl + Cl2 / anhyd. FeCl3   -> o- and p-dichlorobenzene
        C6H5Cl + conc.HNO3/conc.H2SO4 -> o- and p-nitrochlorobenzene
        C6H5Cl + conc. H2SO4          -> o- and p-chlorobenzenesulphonic
                                         acid
        C6H5Cl + CH3Cl / anhyd. AlCl3 -> o- and p-chlorotoluene
```

### Q C6
Explain optical isomerism. Define chirality, enantiomers, racemic mixture,
racemisation, and retention and inversion of configuration. How are these connected
to the SN1 and SN2 mechanisms?

**MODEL ANSWER (mark split shown):**
```
  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  OPTICAL ISOMERISM AND CHIRALITY

  Compounds that rotate the plane of PLANE-POLARISED LIGHT are said to be
  OPTICALLY ACTIVE, and the isomerism is OPTICAL ISOMERISM.

  A molecule is CHIRAL if it cannot be superimposed on its mirror image,
  just as your left hand cannot be superimposed on your right.

  A molecule is chiral if it contains an ASYMMETRIC (CHIRAL) CARBON - an
  sp3 carbon attached to FOUR DIFFERENT groups, marked C*.

                       Cl
                       |
              CH3 ---- C* ---- H          2-chlorobutane: the starred
                       |                  carbon carries Cl, H, CH3 and
                      C2H5                C2H5 - all different.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  ENANTIOMERS, d AND l, AND SPECIFIC ROTATION

                          MIRROR
               Cl           |           Cl
               |            |            |
        H3C -- C -- H       |     H -- C -- CH3
               |            |            |
              C2H5          |          C2H5
             (+) form       |        (-) form

  The two non-superimposable mirror images are ENANTIOMERS. They have
  identical melting point, boiling point, density and solubility, and
  differ only in the direction in which they rotate polarised light:

     DEXTROROTATORY  d- or (+)  : rotates the plane to the RIGHT
     LAEVOROTATORY   l- or (-)  : rotates the plane to the LEFT

  Ordinary light vibrates in all planes; a Nicol prism or polaroid lets
  through only one plane. The rotation is measured in a POLARIMETER, and
  reported as SPECIFIC ROTATION:

                            observed rotation (degrees)
      specific rotation = -------------------------------
                           path length (dm) x conc (g/mL)

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  RACEMIC MIXTURE AND RACEMISATION

  A RACEMIC MIXTURE is an equal (50 : 50) mixture of the (+) and (-)
  forms, written (+-) or dl-. The rotations cancel exactly, so the mixture
  is OPTICALLY INACTIVE. This is called EXTERNAL COMPENSATION.

  RACEMISATION is the conversion of a single pure enantiomer into such a
  racemic mixture, with loss of optical activity.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------- 2 marks
  RETENTION, INVERSION, AND THE LINK TO SN1 AND SN2

  RETENTION of configuration: the spatial arrangement of the four groups
     around the chiral carbon is unchanged in the product.
  INVERSION of configuration: the arrangement is turned inside out, like
     an umbrella caught in the wind. Also called WALDEN INVERSION.

     SN2 : the nucleophile attacks from the BACK, exactly opposite the
           leaving group, so the umbrella is inverted.
           ==>  100 % INVERSION.  The product is optically ACTIVE.

     SN1 : the intermediate carbocation is FLAT (sp2, trigonal planar), so
           the nucleophile attacks from either face with equal ease.
           50 % retention + 50 % inversion.
           ==>  RACEMISATION.  The product is optically INACTIVE.

  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |   SN2  ->  INVERSION     ->  product optically ACTIVE               |
  |   SN1  ->  RACEMISATION  ->  product optically INACTIVE             |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```
