# Coordination Compounds — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The complex changes every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — DOUBLE SALT versus COORDINATION COMPOUND            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between a double salt and a coordination
            compound" , or two formulae are given, one with a DOT and
            one with SQUARE BRACKETS.
  METHOD    1. Double salt  -> loses identity in water, gives ALL its
                               simple ions.  Mohr's salt, potash alum.
            2. Complex      -> retains identity, the [ ] ion does not
                               break.  K4[Fe(CN)6] , [Cu(NH3)4]SO4.
            3. Give the TEST: Mohr's salt gives free Fe2+ so NaOH gives
               a green precipitate; K4[Fe(CN)6] does not.
  TRAP      Give ONE EXAMPLE OF EACH. The example carries a mark on its own.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — OXIDATION NUMBER OF THE CENTRAL METAL               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the oxidation state / oxidation number of the metal in ..."
  METHOD    1. Read the OUTSIDE ions to get the charge on the bracket.
                 K4[...] -> 4-      [...]Cl3 -> 3+
                 K3[...] -> 3-      [...]SO4 -> 2+
                 nothing outside    -> 0
            2. Add up the ligand charges.
                 ZERO :  NH3 H2O CO NO en py
                 -1   :  Cl- Br- I- F- CN- OH- NO2- SCN- NO3-
                 -2   :  C2O4 2- CO3 2- SO4 2-
                 +1   :  NO+
            3. x + (ligand total) = bracket charge . Solve for x.
            4. Write it in ROMAN numerals: Co(III).
  TRAP      CO (carbon monoxide) is ZERO.  C2O4 2- (oxalate) is MINUS TWO.
            Read the formula character by character.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — COORDINATION NUMBER                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the coordination number of the metal in ...?"
  METHOD    Count DONOR ATOMS, not ligand molecules.
                 unidentate  x 1     (NH3 , H2O , Cl- , CN-)
                 bidentate   x 2     (en , ox , gly , dmg)
                 tridentate  x 3     (dien)
                 hexadentate x 6     (EDTA)
            [Co(en)3]3+  ->  3 x 2  =  6
            [Co(en)2Cl2]+ -> (2x2) + (2x1) = 6
  TRAP      [Co(en)3]3+ has C.N. = 6 , NOT 3.
            This is the single most common mistake in the chapter.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — DEFINE THE BASIC TERMS                              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define ligand / coordination sphere / coordination
            polyhedron / homoleptic / heteroleptic."
  METHOD    Give the DEFINITION IN ONE SENTENCE, then an EXAMPLE.
              LIGAND      - an ion or molecule that donates a lone pair
                            to the central metal (a Lewis base).
              COORD. SPHERE - metal + ligands inside [ ] ; does not ionise.
              COORD. POLYHEDRON - the 3-D shape of the donor atoms.
              HOMOLEPTIC  - only ONE kind of ligand.   [Ni(CO)4]
              HETEROLEPTIC- MORE THAN ONE kind.        [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+
  TRAP      A definition without an example rarely gets the full 2 marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — TYPES OF LIGANDS                                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are unidentate / bidentate / polydentate / ambidentate /
            chelating ligands? Give examples."
  METHOD    Define by the NUMBER OF DONOR ATOMS, and always give examples:
              unidentate  1 donor     NH3 , H2O , Cl- , CN-
              bidentate   2 donors    en , ox , gly , dmg
              polydentate >1 donor    en , dien , trien , EDTA
              ambidentate 2 DIFFERENT donors but only ONE used at a time
                                      NO2- , SCN- , CN-
              chelating   polydentate that forms a RING with the metal
  TRAP      AMBIDENTATE is NOT the same as BIDENTATE.
            Ambidentate = "either end, one at a time" (still C.N. 1).
            Bidentate   = "both ends at once" (C.N. 2).
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — WERNER'S THEORY                                  4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the postulates of Werner's theory" / "primary and
            secondary valency" / "explain the CoCl3.nNH3 series".
  METHOD    1. The 6 postulates.
            2. The comparison table: primary = oxidation number,
               ionisable, outside the bracket, dotted line;
               secondary = coordination number, non-ionisable, inside
               the bracket, solid line, decides the shape.
            3. The CoCl3.nNH3 TABLE with colours, moles of AgCl,
               Werner formula and number of ions.
            4. One diagram with solid and dotted lines.
  TRAP      Do not forget to say that the primary valency is 3 and the
            secondary valency is 6 in EVERY member of the series -
            that is the whole point of the answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — THE AgNO3 / NUMBER-OF-IONS QUESTION                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many moles of AgCl are precipitated ...?" /
            "How many ions does it give in solution?"
  METHOD    1. Only the ions OUTSIDE the bracket are free.
            2. moles of AgCl = number of free Cl-.
            3. number of ions = free ions + ONE for the complex ion itself.
               [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2 -> 2 AgCl , but 3 ions.
  SHORTCUT  For the CoCl3.nNH3 series:
               moles AgCl = n - 3        number of ions = n - 2
  TRAP      "Number of ions" is always ONE MORE than "moles of AgCl".
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — WRITE THE IUPAC NAME FROM THE FORMULA            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the IUPAC name of [ ... ]"
  METHOD    1. Cation first, anion second.
            2. Work out the metal's oxidation number.
            3. List the ligands ALPHABETICALLY (ignore di/tri/tetra).
            4. Anionic ligands -> -O  (chlorido, cyanido, oxalato).
               Neutral: aqua , ammine , carbonyl , nitrosyl.
            5. Prefix: di/tri/tetra , or bis/tris for en , ox , dmg.
            6. Metal name + (Roman numeral).
               ANION -> add -ATE (ferrate, cuprate, argentate,
                        plumbate, stannate, aurate).
            7. Write the complex ion as ONE WORD.
  TRAP      Alphabetise the LIGAND NAME, not the prefix:
            tetraaquadichlorido... , not dichloridotetraaqua...
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — WRITE THE FORMULA FROM THE IUPAC NAME               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the formula of potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)."
  METHOD    1. Metal symbol inside [ ] .
            2. Add the ligands in the order given by the name.
            3. Bracket charge = oxidation number + sum of ligand charges.
            4. Balance with the outside ion.
                 potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)
                 -> Fe +2 , 6 CN- = -6 , bracket 4-  ->  K4[Fe(CN)6]
  TRAP      Balance the charges properly. tris(en)cobalt(III) sulphate is
            [Co(en)3]2(SO4)3 , not [Co(en)3]SO4.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM                               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain structural isomerism with examples" or a specific
            pair of formulae is given and you must NAME the isomerism.
  METHOD    Learn the four types with ONE example each:
              IONISATION   - inside/outside ions swap
                             [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br  /  [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4
              HYDRATE      - water in/out of the sphere
                             [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3 / [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2.H2O /
                             [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl.2H2O
              LINKAGE      - ambidentate ligand turns round
                             [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2 / [Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2
              COORDINATION - both ions are complexes, ligands swap
                             [Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6] / [Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]
  TRAP      Add the LAB TEST for the extra mark: AgNO3 / BaCl2 for
            ionisation isomers, colours for linkage isomers.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — "NAME THIS ISOMERISM"                              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two formulae are given side by side. Name the relationship.
  METHOD    Ask three questions in order:
            1. Is the difference INSIDE-versus-OUTSIDE the bracket?
                 water involved -> HYDRATE
                 other ion      -> IONISATION
            2. Is an AMBIDENTATE ligand (NO2- , SCN- , CN-) written
               two different ways (NO2 vs ONO , SCN vs NCS)?
                                -> LINKAGE
            3. Are BOTH ions complexes with swapped ligands?
                                -> COORDINATION
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — GEOMETRICAL ISOMERISM (cis / trans)                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain geometrical isomerism" / "draw the cis and trans
            forms of ..."
  METHOD    1. Square planar Ma2b2 -> cis and trans. DRAW BOTH.
               cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] is CISPLATIN.
            2. Octahedral Ma4b2 -> cis (90 deg) and trans (180 deg).
            3. Octahedral Ma3b3 -> facial (one face) and meridional.
            4. Say clearly: TETRAHEDRAL complexes show NONE, because all
               four positions are adjacent.
  TRAP      You must actually DRAW the structures. A written description
            alone loses half the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — FACIAL versus MERIDIONAL                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are fac and mer isomers?" or Ma3b3 is given.
  METHOD    fac - the three identical ligands occupy one triangular FACE
                  of the octahedron (all three 90 degrees apart)
            mer - the three identical ligands lie on a MERIDIAN, a plane
                  passing through the metal
            Example: [Co(NH3)3Cl3] , [Cr(NH3)3Cl3]
  TRAP      fac/mer appears ONLY for Ma3b3. For Ma4b2 it is cis/trans.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — OPTICAL ISOMERISM                               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which of these is optically active?" / "explain optical
            isomerism in coordination compounds".
  METHOD    A complex with NO plane of symmetry is not superimposable on
            its mirror image -> optically active, exists as d and l forms.
            THE THREE ONE-LINE TESTS:
               CIS with 2 or 3 chelate rings   ->  ACTIVE
               TRANS                           ->  inactive
               SQUARE PLANAR                   ->  inactive (always)
            Examples: [Co(en)3]3+ , cis-[Co(en)2Cl2]+ , [Cr(ox)3]3-
  TRAP      "How many geometrical isomers?" for [Co(en)2Cl2]+ is 2.
            "How many isomers in total?" is 3 (trans + d-cis + l-cis).
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — COUNT THE ISOMERS                               objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The number of isomers of ... is"
  METHOD    Memorise the counting table:
               Ma2b2  square planar  ->  2
               Mabcd  square planar  ->  3
               Ma4b2  octahedral     ->  2  (cis , trans)
               Ma3b3  octahedral     ->  2  (fac , mer)
               M(AA)2b2 octahedral   ->  3  (trans , d-cis , l-cis)
               M(AA)3   octahedral   ->  2  (d , l)
               ANY tetrahedral       ->  0 geometrical isomers
  TRAP      Read whether the question wants GEOMETRICAL isomers only, or
            ALL isomers including optical forms.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — VBT FOR ONE COMPLEX (orbital diagram)           4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "On the basis of valence bond theory, explain the hybridisation,
            geometry and magnetic behaviour of [ ... ]"
  METHOD    THE SIX FIXED STEPS - do them in this order every time:
            1. Oxidation number of the metal.
            2. Electronic configuration of the ION
               (REMOVE 4s ELECTRONS FIRST, then 3d).
            3. Draw the 3d , 4s , 4p (and 4d if needed) BOXES.
            4. Is the ligand STRONG or WEAK field?
                  STRONG (CN- , CO , NH3 , en , NO2-) -> PAIR UP the 3d
                  WEAK   (F- , Cl- , Br- , I- , H2O , OH-) -> do NOT pair
            5. Count the empty orbitals -> name the hybridisation
                  6 orbitals with inner 3d -> d2sp3 (INNER , low spin)
                  6 orbitals with outer 4d -> sp3d2 (OUTER , high spin)
                  4 with one 3d            -> dsp2  (square planar)
                  4 with none              -> sp3   (tetrahedral)
            6. Count unpaired electrons -> mu = sqrt(n(n+2)).
  TRAP      The BOXES themselves carry marks. Draw them; do not just write
            "d2sp3". Label 3d , 4s , 4p and mark the ligand pairs.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — THE SIX STANDARD VBT COMPLEXES               *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   The long-answer question naming two or four of these complexes.
  METHOD    Learn this block cold - it IS the 8-mark answer:

             [Co(NH3)6]3+  d6  NH3 strong  d2sp3  octa   n=0  diamagnetic
             [CoF6]3-      d6  F-  weak    sp3d2  octa   n=4  4.90 BM
             [Fe(CN)6]4-   d6  CN- strong  d2sp3  octa   n=0  diamagnetic
             [Fe(H2O)6]2+  d6  H2O weak    sp3d2  octa   n=4  4.90 BM
             [Ni(CN)4]2-   d8  CN- strong  dsp2   square n=0  diamagnetic
             [NiCl4]2-     d8  Cl- weak    sp3    tetra  n=2  2.83 BM

            Finish EVERY VBT long answer with the LIMITATIONS OF VBT.
  TRAP      Notice that [Co(NH3)6]3+ and [CoF6]3- contain the SAME ION.
            The difference is entirely the LIGAND. Say that sentence.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — "WHY IS X DIAMAGNETIC AND Y PARAMAGNETIC?"      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two complexes of the SAME metal ion with different ligands.
  METHOD    ONE SENTENCE ANSWERS ALL OF THEM:
            "Both contain the same ion, but ___ is a STRONG field ligand
             and pairs the d electrons (inner orbital, low spin, n = 0,
             diamagnetic), while ___ is a WEAK field ligand and does not
             (outer orbital, high spin, n = ___, paramagnetic)."
            Then draw both orbital diagrams and compute mu.
  TRAP      Always state the d-electron count first (both are 3d6, etc.)
            so the examiner sees you know the metal ion is the same.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — INNER versus OUTER ORBITAL COMPLEX                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are inner orbital and outer orbital complexes?"
  METHOD    INNER  - uses (n-1)d orbitals , d2sp3 , strong field ligand ,
                     LOW SPIN , fewer unpaired electrons , more stable
                     [Co(NH3)6]3+ , [Fe(CN)6]4-
            OUTER  - uses  n d  orbitals , sp3d2 , weak field ligand ,
                     HIGH SPIN , more unpaired electrons
                     [CoF6]3- , [Fe(H2O)6]2+
  SHORTCUT  d written FIRST = INNER.  d written LAST = OUTER.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — LIMITATIONS OF VBT                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the drawbacks / limitations of VBT?"
  METHOD    Write at least FIVE:
            1. too many assumptions
            2. no quantitative interpretation of magnetic data
            3. does not explain COLOUR
            4. no quantitative account of stability
            5. cannot predict tetrahedral versus square planar
            6. does not explain why some ligands are strong field
            7. cannot explain temperature dependence of magnetism
  TRAP      These 2 marks sit at the end of the 8-mark question and are
            the marks most often left on the table. Write them.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — CFT OCTAHEDRAL SPLITTING DIAGRAM               4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the splitting of d orbitals in an octahedral field."
  METHOD    1. In the free ion the five d orbitals are DEGENERATE.
            2. Six ligands approach ALONG the axes.
            3. dx2-y2 and dz2 point AT the ligands -> repelled more
               -> raised -> eg set (+0.6 delta-o)
               dxy, dyz, dzx point BETWEEN -> lowered -> t2g (-0.4 delta-o)
            4. DRAW the diagram with t2g below, eg above, and the gap
               labelled delta-o.
            5. State that 2(+0.6) + 3(-0.4) = 0 , so the average energy
               is unchanged (the barycentre rule).
  TRAP      Label the ORBITAL NAMES on the levels, not just "t2g" and "eg".
            Show the +0.6 and -0.4 values - they carry a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — HIGH SPIN versus LOW SPIN                       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "When is a complex high spin?" / "predict the configuration
            of a d5 ion in a strong field."
  METHOD    Compare delta-o with the PAIRING ENERGY P:
                 delta-o > P  ->  LOW SPIN  (strong field ligand)
                 delta-o < P  ->  HIGH SPIN (weak field ligand)
            Then fill the boxes and count n.
            The choice exists ONLY for d4 , d5 , d6 , d7.
            The full table:
                 d4  high t2g3 eg1 n=4  |  low t2g4 eg0 n=2
                 d5  high t2g3 eg2 n=5  |  low t2g5 eg0 n=1
                 d6  high t2g4 eg2 n=4  |  low t2g6 eg0 n=0
                 d7  high t2g5 eg2 n=3  |  low t2g6 eg1 n=1
  TRAP      For d1-d3 and d8-d10 there is only ONE answer. Do not invent
            a "low spin d3".
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — TETRAHEDRAL SPLITTING                              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the splitting of d orbitals in a tetrahedral field."
  METHOD    1. The picture is UPSIDE DOWN compared with octahedral:
                 e  (dx2-y2 , dz2)   LOWER   , - 0.6 delta-t
                 t2 (dxy,dyz,dzx)    HIGHER  , + 0.4 delta-t
            2. delta-t = (4/9) delta-o , because there are only 4 ligands
               and they point BETWEEN the orbitals.
            3. Therefore ALL tetrahedral complexes are HIGH SPIN.
            4. The subscript "g" is dropped (no centre of symmetry).
  TRAP      Do NOT write t2g and eg for a tetrahedral complex.
            It is t2 and e.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — SPECTROCHEMICAL SERIES                             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the spectrochemical series?" / "arrange these
            ligands in increasing field strength."
  METHOD    Define it as the arrangement of ligands in order of the
            crystal field splitting energy they produce, then write:
              I- < Br- < SCN- < Cl- < F- < OH- < ox < H2O
                 < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO
  SHORTCUT  You only need the two ENDS:
              WEAK   -> halides , OH- , oxalate , water
              STRONG -> NH3 , en , NO2- , CN- , CO
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — SPIN-ONLY MAGNETIC MOMENT NUMERICAL                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of ..." or
            "mu = 3.87 BM, how many unpaired electrons?"
  METHOD    1. Find the oxidation number -> d-electron count.
            2. Decide strong / weak field from the ligand.
            3. Fill the boxes and count the SINGLE arrows = n.
            4. mu = sqrt( n(n+2) )  B.M.
  SHORTCUT  n  =  0     1      2      3      4      5
            mu =  0   1.73   2.83   3.87   4.90   5.92
            Works forwards AND backwards.
  TRAP      Write the UNIT: B.M. (Bohr Magneton). No unit = lost mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — COLOUR OF COMPLEXES                             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why are coordination compounds coloured?" / "Why is
            [Ti(H2O)6]3+ violet?" / "Why is [Zn(NH3)4]2+ colourless?"
  METHOD    1. The d orbitals split by delta-o.
            2. An electron absorbs a photon of energy delta-o and jumps
               from t2g to eg - a d-d TRANSITION.
            3. That wavelength is removed from white light; we SEE the
               COMPLEMENTARY colour.
            4. [Ti(H2O)6]3+ (d1) absorbs near 500 nm and looks VIOLET;
               on heating it loses water and becomes colourless.
            5. d0 and d10 ions have NO possible d-d transition, so
               Sc3+ , Ti4+ , Zn2+ , Ag+ , Cu+ complexes are COLOURLESS.
  TRAP      Say "d-d transition" and "complementary colour" explicitly.
            Those two phrases are what the examiner is looking for.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — LIMITATIONS OF CFT                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the limitations / drawbacks of crystal field theory?"
  METHOD    1. It treats the bond as purely IONIC and the ligands as point
               charges, ignoring the COVALENT character.
            2. It therefore predicts that anionic ligands should split
               most - but I- and Br- are actually WEAK field, while the
               neutral CO and NH3 are STRONG field.
            3. It cannot explain why CO and CN- are so strong (pi-bonding
               is ignored).
            4. It ignores the LIGAND orbitals entirely, so it cannot
               explain charge-transfer spectra.
  TRAP      Do not confuse these with the limitations of VBT. CFT DOES
            explain colour and magnetism; VBT does not.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — STABILITY AND THE CHELATE EFFECT                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the chelate effect?" / "Why is [Ni(en)3]2+ more
            stable than [Ni(NH3)6]2+?"
  METHOD    1. State the effect: a chelate complex is far more stable than
               the complex of the same number of similar unidentate ligands.
            2. Give the ENTROPY reason with the two equations:
                 [Ni(H2O)6]2+ + 6 NH3 -> [Ni(NH3)6]2+ + 6 H2O  (7 -> 7)
                 [Ni(H2O)6]2+ + 3 en  -> [Ni(en)3]2+  + 6 H2O  (4 -> 7)
               More particles -> higher entropy -> more negative delta-G.
            3. Mention that 5- and 6-membered rings are the most stable.
            4. Other stability factors: higher charge on the metal, smaller
               ion, stronger field ligand, more basic ligand.
  TRAP      The reason is ENTROPY, not "stronger bonds". Say the word.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — EAN RULE CALCULATION                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the effective atomic number of the metal in ..."
  METHOD    EAN = Z - (oxidation number) + 2 x (coordination number)
            K4[Fe(CN)6] : 26 - 2 + 12 = 36  (krypton , obeys the rule)
            [Ni(CO)4]   : 28 - 0 + 8  = 36  (krypton , obeys)
            [Cu(NH3)4]2+: 29 - 2 + 8  = 35  (does NOT obey)
            Noble gas numbers: Ne 10 , Ar 18 , Kr 36 , Xe 54 , Rn 86.
  TRAP      Each ligand donates a PAIR, so multiply the coordination
            number by 2, not by 1.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — APPLICATIONS OF COORDINATION COMPOUNDS             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the importance / applications of coordination
            compounds" / "role in biological systems".
  METHOD    Cover FIVE areas with one named example each:
            1. ANALYSIS - EDTA for hardness of water ; DMG gives a red
               precipitate with Ni2+ ; Fe3+ + SCN- gives blood red.
            2. EXTRACTION - cyanide process for Ag and Au ; Mond's
               process for Ni via [Ni(CO)4].
            3. ELECTROPLATING - [Ag(CN)2]- and [Au(CN)2]- give smooth,
               even, adherent deposits.
            4. MEDICINE - cisplatin cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] for cancer ;
               EDTA for lead poisoning.
            5. BIOLOGY - haemoglobin (Fe, porphyrin, carries O2) ,
               chlorophyll (Mg, porphyrin, photosynthesis) ,
               vitamin B12 (Co, corrin, anti-anaemia).
  TRAP      Haemoglobin = IRON. Chlorophyll = MAGNESIUM. B12 = COBALT.
            Swapping them is the commonest lost mark in this pattern.
```
