## Topic: Basic terms

### Q A1
What is a ligand? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A LIGAND is an ion or a molecule that is bonded to the central metal
  atom or ion in a complex by DONATING a lone pair of electrons.
  A ligand acts as a LEWIS BASE, the metal as a LEWIS ACID.

  Examples:   NH3 (ammine) ,  H2O (aqua) ,  Cl- (chlorido) ,  CN- (cyanido)
```

---

### Q A2
Define coordination number. What is the coordination number of the metal in
[Co(en)3]3+?

**ANSWER:**
```
  COORDINATION NUMBER  =  the number of ligand DONOR ATOMS directly bonded
  to the central metal ion, i.e. the number of sigma bonds it forms.

  In [Co(en)3]3+ :  en is BIDENTATE , so  3 x 2  =  6

  Coordination number  =  6      (NOT 3)
```

---

### Q A3
What is a coordination sphere? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The central metal ion together with the ligands attached to it, written
  inside SQUARE BRACKETS, is called the COORDINATION SPHERE.
  It behaves as a single unit and does NOT ionise in solution.

  In  K4[Fe(CN)6]  ,  the coordination sphere is  [Fe(CN)6]4-
                       and  4 K+  form the ionisation sphere.
```

---

### Q A4
What are homoleptic and heteroleptic complexes? Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HOMOLEPTIC    -  the metal is bonded to only ONE KIND of ligand.
                   Example:  [Co(NH3)6]3+  ,  [Ni(CO)4]  ,  [Fe(CN)6]4-

  HETEROLEPTIC  -  the metal is bonded to MORE THAN ONE KIND of ligand.
                   Example:  [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+  ,  [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]+
```

---

### Q A5
Distinguish between a double salt and a coordination compound with one example
of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DOUBLE SALT              -  loses its identity in water and gives ALL its
                              simple ions.
                              Example: Mohr's salt FeSO4.(NH4)2SO4.6H2O
                              -> gives free Fe2+ , NH4+ , SO4 2-

  COORDINATION COMPOUND    -  retains its identity in water; the complex ion
                              does NOT break up.
                              Example: K4[Fe(CN)6]
                              -> gives 4 K+ and [Fe(CN)6]4- , no free Fe2+
```

## Topic: Ligands

### Q A6
What is an ambidentate ligand? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An AMBIDENTATE ligand has TWO DIFFERENT donor atoms and can attach to the
  metal through EITHER of them, but only ONE at a time.

  NO2-   ->  through N  =  nitrito-N       through O  =  nitrito-O
  SCN-   ->  through S  =  thiocyanato-S   through N  =  thiocyanato-N
  CN-    ->  through C  =  cyanido         through N  =  isocyanido

  They are the cause of LINKAGE ISOMERISM.
```

---

### Q A7
What is a chelate ligand? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A CHELATE (chelating) ligand is a POLYDENTATE ligand that attaches to the
  metal through two or more donor atoms, so that a RING is formed with the
  metal. The ring is called a CHELATE RING.

  Example:  ethane-1,2-diamine (en) in [Co(en)3]3+ forms three
            five-membered chelate rings.
            Oxalate (C2O4 2-) and EDTA 4- are also chelating ligands.
```

### Q A8
What is the denticity of EDTA? Name its donor atoms.

**ANSWER:**
```
  EDTA 4-  is  HEXADENTATE  (denticity = 6).

  Donor atoms:  2 NITROGEN atoms (from the two amine groups)
              + 4 OXYGEN atoms  (one from each of the four COO- groups)

  A single EDTA molecule therefore gives a coordination number of 6.
```

## Topic: Isomerism

### Q A16
What is linkage isomerism? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LINKAGE ISOMERISM arises when an AMBIDENTATE ligand bonds to the metal
  through one donor atom in one isomer and through its other donor atom
  in the second isomer.

  [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2   nitrito-N , bonded through N , YELLOW
  [Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2   nitrito-O , bonded through O , RED
```

### Q A17
What is ionisation isomerism? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  IONISATION ISOMERS have the same molecular formula but give DIFFERENT
  IONS in solution, because an ion inside the coordination sphere has
  exchanged places with the ion outside it.

  [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br   ->  gives free Br-      (BaCl2 gives no precipitate)
  [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4   ->  gives free SO4 2-   (BaCl2 gives white BaSO4)
```

### Q A18
Give an example of a complex that shows optical isomerism.

**ANSWER:**
```
  [Co(en)3]3+  exists as two non-superimposable mirror images,
  the d-form and the l-form, and is therefore OPTICALLY ACTIVE.

  cis-[Co(en)2Cl2]+ is also optically active;
  the trans form is NOT (it has a plane of symmetry).
```

---

### Q A19
Why do tetrahedral complexes not show geometrical isomerism?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In a tetrahedron, ALL FOUR positions are adjacent to one another
  (every bond angle is 109 degrees 28 minutes).
  There are no "opposite" positions, so there can be no cis form and no
  trans form. Hence tetrahedral complexes show no geometrical isomerism.
```

What is the spectrochemical series?

**ANSWER:**

  The SPECTROCHEMICAL SERIES is the arrangement of ligands in the order of
  the CRYSTAL FIELD SPLITTING ENERGY (delta-o) that they produce.

     I- < Br- < SCN- < Cl- < F- < OH- < ox < H2O < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO
     <-------- weak field --------->        <-------- strong field ------->
```

### Q A21
Calculate the spin-only magnetic moment of a metal ion having 3 unpaired
electrons.

**ANSWER:**
```
   mu  =  sqrt( n ( n + 2 ) )    with  n = 3

       =  sqrt( 3 x 5 )  =  sqrt(15)  =  3.87  B.M.
```

---

### Q A22
Why is [Ni(CN)4]2- diamagnetic while [NiCl4]2- is paramagnetic?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Both contain Ni2+ (3d8).

  CN- is a STRONG field ligand -> it pairs the two unpaired 3d electrons
       -> dsp2 hybridisation , SQUARE PLANAR , n = 0 -> DIAMAGNETIC

  Cl- is a WEAK field ligand -> no pairing occurs
       -> sp3 hybridisation , TETRAHEDRAL , n = 2 -> PARAMAGNETIC
          (mu = 2.83 B.M.)
```

---

### Q A23
Why are [Zn(NH3)4]2+ and [Sc(H2O)6]3+ colourless?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Colour in a complex comes from a d-d TRANSITION, which needs
  PARTLY FILLED d orbitals.

  Zn2+  is  d10  -> the d subshell is COMPLETELY FULL , no d-d transition.
  Sc3+  is  d0   -> the d subshell is EMPTY , no d-d transition.

  Both are therefore COLOURLESS.
```

---

### Q A24
What is the crystal field splitting energy? What symbol is used for it in an
octahedral complex?

**ANSWER:**
```
  When ligands approach the metal, the five degenerate d orbitals split into
  two sets of different energy. The ENERGY GAP between these two sets is the
  CRYSTAL FIELD SPLITTING ENERGY.

  For an OCTAHEDRAL complex it is written  delta-o  (also 10 Dq),
  the gap between the lower  t2g  set and the upper  eg  set.
```

### Q B1
State the postulates of Werner's theory of coordination compounds.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  In coordination compounds a metal shows TWO types of valency:
      PRIMARY valency and SECONDARY valency.

  2.  The PRIMARY valency is IONISABLE, is satisfied by NEGATIVE IONS and
      is equal to the OXIDATION NUMBER of the metal. It is shown by dotted
      lines and these ions are written OUTSIDE the square bracket.

  3.  The SECONDARY valency is NON-IONISABLE, is satisfied by LIGANDS
      (neutral or negative) and is equal to the COORDINATION NUMBER.
      It is shown by solid lines and written INSIDE the bracket.

  4.  The secondary valencies are DIRECTED in space towards fixed positions
      around the metal, and therefore decide the GEOMETRY of the complex.

  5.  Every metal tries to satisfy BOTH its primary and secondary valencies.

  6.  A negative ion may satisfy BOTH valencies at the same time.
```

### Q B2
How did Werner explain the series CoCl3.6NH3 , CoCl3.5NH3 , CoCl3.4NH3 and
CoCl3.3NH3?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Werner treated each compound with excess AgNO3 and counted the AgCl formed.
  Only the CHLORIDES OUTSIDE the bracket are precipitated.

  +---------------+----------+------------+---------------------+--------+
  | COMPOUND      | COLOUR   | moles AgCl | WERNER'S FORMULA    | IONS   |
  +---------------+----------+------------+---------------------+--------+
  | CoCl3.6NH3    | yellow   |     3      | [Co(NH3)6]Cl3       |   4    |
  | CoCl3.5NH3    | purple   |     2      | [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2     |   3    |
  | CoCl3.4NH3    | green    |     1      | [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl     |   2    |
  | CoCl3.3NH3    | violet   |     0      | [Co(NH3)3Cl3]       |   0    |
  +---------------+----------+------------+---------------------+--------+

  In EVERY compound:
     PRIMARY   valency of Co  =  3   (Co is always +3)
     SECONDARY valency of Co  =  6   (always 6 groups inside the bracket)

  As one NH3 leaves the coordination sphere, one Cl- enters it. That Cl-
  then becomes NON-IONISABLE, so one fewer mole of AgCl is precipitated.
  This is exactly what is observed, and it proved Werner's two-valency idea.
```

### Q B3
Explain the different types of ligands with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A LIGAND is a Lewis base that donates a lone pair to the central metal.
  Ligands are classified by the NUMBER OF DONOR ATOMS (denticity).

  1. UNIDENTATE (monodentate) - ONE donor atom.
        NH3 (N) , H2O (O) , Cl- , CN- (C) , OH- , CO (C) , py (N)

  2. BIDENTATE - TWO donor atoms, forms a ring.
        en , ethane-1,2-diamine  (2 N)
        ox , oxalate C2O4 2-     (2 O)
        gly , glycinate          (1 N + 1 O)

  3. TRIDENTATE - THREE donor atoms.       dien (diethylenetriamine)

  4. TETRADENTATE - FOUR donor atoms.      trien

  5. HEXADENTATE - SIX donor atoms.        EDTA 4-  (2 N + 4 O)

     (2 to 6 donor atoms are all called POLYDENTATE.)

  6. AMBIDENTATE - has two DIFFERENT donor atoms but uses only one at a
     time.  NO2- (through N or O) , SCN- (through S or N) , CN-

  7. CHELATING ligand - a polydentate ligand that forms a RING with the
     metal, e.g. en in [Co(en)3]3+ forms three five-membered rings.
     Chelate complexes are unusually STABLE (the chelate effect).
```

### Q B4
State the IUPAC rules for naming coordination compounds and name any three
complexes.

**ANSWER:**
```
  RULES
  1. Name the CATION first, then the ANION.
  2. Inside the bracket, name the LIGANDS first, then the METAL.
  3. Ligands are named in ALPHABETICAL order (ignore di, tri, tetra).
  4. Anionic ligands end in -O : chlorido , cyanido , oxalato , sulphato.
  5. Neutral ligands keep their names, EXCEPT
        H2O = aqua , NH3 = ammine , CO = carbonyl , NO = nitrosyl.
  6. Cationic ligands end in -ium :  NO+ = nitrosonium.
  7. Use di, tri, tetra for simple ligands; bis, tris, tetrakis (with the
     ligand in brackets) when the ligand's own name contains di / tri.
  8. Write the oxidation number of the metal in ROMAN numerals in round
     brackets right after the metal's name.
  9. If the complex ion is an ANION, end the metal name in -ATE
        (ferrate , cuprate , argentate , plumbate , stannate , aurate).
 10. The whole complex ion is written as ONE WORD.

  EXAMPLES
     [Co(NH3)6]Cl3      ->  hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride
     K4[Fe(CN)6]        ->  potassium hexacyanidoferrate(II)
     [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl    ->  tetraaquadichloridochromium(III) chloride
     [Ni(CO)4]          ->  tetracarbonylnickel(0)
     [Co(en)3]Cl3       ->  tris(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) chloride
```

### Q B5
Explain structural isomerism in coordination compounds with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STRUCTURAL ISOMERS have the same molecular formula but DIFFERENT BONDS.
  There are four types.

  1. IONISATION ISOMERISM
     An ion inside the bracket exchanges with the ion outside it, so the
     two isomers give different ions in solution.
        [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br   ->  free Br-    (AgNO3 gives pale yellow AgBr)
        [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4   ->  free SO4 2- (BaCl2 gives white BaSO4)

  2. HYDRATE (SOLVATE) ISOMERISM
     Water shifts between being a LIGAND and being water of crystallisation.
        [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3            violet      , gives 3 AgCl
        [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2 . H2O    blue-green  , gives 2 AgCl
        [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl . 2H2O   dark green  , gives 1 AgCl

  3. LINKAGE ISOMERISM
     An AMBIDENTATE ligand bonds through its other donor atom.
        [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2   nitrito-N , yellow
        [Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2   nitrito-O , red

  4. COORDINATION ISOMERISM
     Both cation and anion are complexes, and the ligands are interchanged
     between the two metals.
        [Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6]     and     [Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]
```

### Q B6
Explain geometrical isomerism in coordination compounds with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  GEOMETRICAL ISOMERS have the same bonds but the ligands occupy DIFFERENT
  POSITIONS in space relative to each other.

  A. SQUARE PLANAR  Ma2b2   ->  cis and trans      e.g. [Pt(NH3)2Cl2]

        CIS  (identical ligands 90 deg apart)     TRANS (180 deg apart)

           NH3       Cl                              NH3       Cl
              \     /                                   \     /
                Pt                                        Pt
              /     \                                   /     \
           NH3       Cl                              Cl        NH3

        cis-[Pt(NH3)2Cl2] is CISPLATIN, an anti-cancer drug;
        the trans form has no anti-cancer activity.

  B. OCTAHEDRAL  Ma4b2   ->  cis and trans     e.g. [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+

        CIS   : the two Cl are ADJACENT (90 degrees apart)   - green
        TRANS : the two Cl are OPPOSITE (180 degrees apart)  - violet

  C. OCTAHEDRAL  Ma3b3   ->  facial and meridional  e.g. [Co(NH3)3Cl3]

        FACIAL (fac)      : the three identical ligands occupy one
                            triangular FACE of the octahedron
        MERIDIONAL (mer)  : the three identical ligands lie on a
                            MERIDIAN (a plane through the metal)

  NOTE: TETRAHEDRAL complexes show NO geometrical isomerism, because all
  four positions are adjacent to one another.
```

### Q B7
What is optical isomerism? Explain with the example of [Co(en)2Cl2]+.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A complex is OPTICALLY ACTIVE when it has NO plane of symmetry, so that
  its mirror image cannot be superimposed on it. The two forms are called
  ENANTIOMERS:
        d (dextro) rotates plane-polarised light to the RIGHT
        l (laevo)  rotates plane-polarised light to the LEFT

  [Co(en)2Cl2]+ has TWO geometrical isomers:

     TRANS  - the two Cl are opposite each other.
              This form HAS a plane of symmetry  ->  OPTICALLY INACTIVE.

     CIS    - the two Cl are adjacent.
              This form has NO plane of symmetry ->  OPTICALLY ACTIVE.
              It exists as a d-form and an l-form, which are mirror images.

  So the compound has 2 geometrical isomers, but 3 forms in all
  (trans , d-cis , l-cis).

  Other optically active complexes:  [Co(en)3]3+ , [Cr(ox)3]3-
  Square planar complexes are NEVER optically active.
```

### Q B8
Using VBT, explain why [Co(NH3)6]3+ is diamagnetic while [CoF6]3- is
paramagnetic.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Both contain Co3+ =  [Ar] 3d6.

  (a)  [Co(NH3)6]3+     NH3 is a STRONG field ligand -> pairing occurs

           3 d                            4s         4p
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
   | ^v | ^v | ^v | xx | xx |        | xx |   | xx | xx | xx |
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
                    \_______ d2sp3 (2 x 3d + 4s + 3 x 4p) ________/

       INNER orbital , d2sp3 , OCTAHEDRAL , LOW SPIN
       n = 0   ->   mu = 0   ->   DIAMAGNETIC


  (b)  [CoF6]3-         F- is a WEAK field ligand -> NO pairing

        3 d                        4s        4p                 4d
  +----+----+----+----+----+   +----+  +----+----+----+   +----+----+
  | ^v | ^  | ^  | ^  | ^  |   | xx |  | xx | xx | xx |   | xx | xx |
  +----+----+----+----+----+   +----+  +----+----+----+   +----+----+
       4 unpaired                \______________ sp3d2 _____________/

       OUTER orbital , sp3d2 , OCTAHEDRAL , HIGH SPIN
       n = 4   ->   mu = sqrt(4 x 6) = 4.90 B.M.  ->  PARAMAGNETIC
```

### Q B10
What are the limitations of Valence Bond Theory?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  It involves a large number of ASSUMPTIONS.

  2.  It gives no QUANTITATIVE interpretation of magnetic data; it can only
      say "diamagnetic" or "paramagnetic".

  3.  It does not explain the COLOUR of coordination compounds at all.

  4.  It gives no quantitative account of the THERMODYNAMIC or KINETIC
      stability of complexes.

  5.  It does not PREDICT whether a four-coordinate complex will be
      tetrahedral or square planar; the answer must be known beforehand.

  6.  It does not explain WHY some ligands are strong field and others are
      weak field.

  7.  It cannot explain the TEMPERATURE DEPENDENCE of magnetic behaviour.
```

### Q B11
Explain the crystal field splitting of d orbitals in an octahedral complex.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In a free metal ion the five d orbitals are DEGENERATE (equal in energy).
  In an octahedral complex the six ligands approach ALONG the x, y, z axes.

     dx2-y2 and dz2  point ALONG the axes    -> face the ligands head on
                                             -> repelled STRONGLY
                                             -> energy RAISED  = eg set

     dxy , dyz , dzx point BETWEEN the axes  -> repelled LESS
                                             -> energy LOWERED = t2g set

   E
   ^                                              +----+  +----+
   |                                              |    |  |    |    eg
   |                                              +----+  +----+
   |                                                  ^
   |   +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+             |  + 0.6 delta-o
   |   |    | |    | |    | |    | |    |    ....-----+-----.... barycentre
   |   +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+             |  - 0.4 delta-o
   |   five degenerate d orbitals                     v
   |   (free ion)                              +----+ +----+ +----+
   |                                           |    | |    | |    |   t2g
   |                                           +----+ +----+ +----+
   +--------------------------------------------------------------->

  The energy gap between t2g and eg is the CRYSTAL FIELD SPLITTING ENERGY,
  delta-o (or 10 Dq).

     eg  is raised  by  + 0.6 delta-o   (+ 3/5)
     t2g is lowered by  - 0.4 delta-o   (- 2/5)

  Check:   2(+0.6) + 3(-0.4)  =  0 , so the average energy is unchanged.
```

### Q B12
Distinguish between high-spin and low-spin octahedral complexes using the
pairing-energy criterion.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Let  P  =  the PAIRING ENERGY (the cost of forcing two electrons into
             the same orbital)

     delta-o  >  P    ->  the electron PAIRS UP in t2g  ->  LOW SPIN
                          (produced by STRONG field ligands: CN- , NH3 , CO)

     delta-o  <  P    ->  the electron GOES UP into eg  ->  HIGH SPIN
                          (produced by WEAK field ligands: F- , Cl- , H2O)

  EXAMPLE  d6 ion:

     HIGH SPIN [CoF6]3-              LOW SPIN [Co(NH3)6]3+

     +----+  +----+                  +----+  +----+
     | ^  |  | ^  |    eg            |    |  |    |    eg

     | ^v | | ^  | | ^  |   t2g      | ^v | | ^v | | ^v |   t2g
     +----+ +----+ +----+            +----+ +----+ +----+

     t2g4 eg2 , n = 4                t2g6 eg0 , n = 0
     mu = 4.90 B.M. , paramagnetic   mu = 0 , diamagnetic

  The high-spin / low-spin choice exists ONLY for d4 , d5 , d6 and d7 ions.
```

### Q B13
Explain the colour of coordination compounds on the basis of crystal field
theory.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  In a complex the d orbitals are split into t2g (lower) and eg (higher),
      separated by delta-o.

  2.  When white light falls on the complex, an electron in a t2g orbital
      ABSORBS a photon whose energy is exactly equal to delta-o and is
      promoted to an eg orbital. This is called a  d-d TRANSITION.

  3.  The wavelength corresponding to that energy is REMOVED from the white
      light. What is transmitted is the rest of the spectrum.

  4.  The colour we SEE is the COMPLEMENTARY colour of the light absorbed.

        absorbs green-yellow  ->  looks VIOLET
        absorbs blue          ->  looks YELLOW
        absorbs red           ->  looks GREEN

  5.  EXAMPLE: [Ti(H2O)6]3+ is d1. It absorbs light near 500 nm
      (green-yellow) and appears VIOLET. On heating, the water ligands are
      removed, the splitting disappears and the compound becomes COLOURLESS.

  6.  A complex is COLOURLESS if the d subshell is EMPTY (d0, e.g. Sc3+)
      or COMPLETELY FULL (d10, e.g. Zn2+, Ag+), because no d-d transition
      is then possible.

  7.  A STRONGER field ligand gives a LARGER delta-o, so the complex absorbs
      shorter-wavelength light and the colour changes:
        [Cu(H2O)4]2+  pale blue   ->  [Cu(NH3)4]2+  deep blue
```

### Q B14
What is the chelate effect? Why is [Ni(en)3]2+ more stable than [Ni(NH3)6]2+?

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE CHELATE EFFECT: a complex formed with a POLYDENTATE (chelating)
  ligand is far more stable than the corresponding complex formed with the
  same number of similar UNIDENTATE ligands.

  Both [Ni(en)3]2+ and [Ni(NH3)6]2+ have six NITROGEN donor atoms around
  Ni2+, yet [Ni(en)3]2+ is much more stable. The reason is ENTROPY:

     [Ni(H2O)6]2+ + 6 NH3  ->  [Ni(NH3)6]2+ + 6 H2O
          7 particles       ->      7 particles     (no entropy gain)

     [Ni(H2O)6]2+ + 3 en   ->  [Ni(en)3]2+  + 6 H2O
          4 particles       ->      7 particles     (entropy INCREASES)

  The increase in the number of free particles increases the entropy,
  which makes delta-G more negative, so the chelate complex is more stable.
  Five- and six-membered chelate rings are the most stable.
```

### Q C1  *** THE MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED LONG ANSWER ***
Discuss the Valence Bond Theory of coordination compounds. Explain the
hybridisation, geometry and magnetic behaviour of [Co(NH3)6]3+ , [CoF6]3- ,
[Ni(CN)4]2- and [NiCl4]2-. State the limitations of the theory.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PART 1 - THE MAIN POSTULATES OF VBT                          (2 marks)

  1. The central metal ion makes available a number of EMPTY orbitals equal
     to its coordination number.
  2. These empty orbitals HYBRIDISE to give an equal number of equivalent
     hybrid orbitals of definite geometry.
  3. Each ligand donates a LONE PAIR of electrons into one empty hybrid
     orbital, forming a COORDINATE (dative) sigma bond.
  4. If the metal uses INNER (n-1)d orbitals, the complex is called an
     INNER ORBITAL or LOW-SPIN complex (d2sp3).
     If it uses OUTER nd orbitals, it is an OUTER ORBITAL or HIGH-SPIN
     complex (sp3d2).
  5. STRONG field ligands force the d electrons to PAIR UP;
     WEAK field ligands do not.
  6. The number of unpaired electrons left decides the magnetic behaviour:
        mu  =  sqrt( n (n+2) )   B.M.

  +-------------------+------------------+-------------------------------+
  | HYBRIDISATION     | GEOMETRY         | EXAMPLE                       |
  +-------------------+------------------+-------------------------------+
  | sp                | linear           | [Ag(NH3)2]+                   |
  | sp3               | tetrahedral      | [NiCl4]2- , [Ni(CO)4]         |
  | dsp2              | square planar    | [Ni(CN)4]2-                   |
  | d2sp3  (inner)    | octahedral       | [Co(NH3)6]3+ , [Fe(CN)6]4-    |
  | sp3d2  (outer)    | octahedral       | [CoF6]3- , [Fe(H2O)6]2+       |
  +-------------------+------------------+-------------------------------+


  PART 2 - [Co(NH3)6]3+                                       (1.5 marks)

  Co (Z=27) = [Ar] 3d7 4s2 ,   Co3+ = [Ar] 3d6
  NH3 is a STRONG field ligand -> the 3d electrons pair up:

           3 d                            4s         4p
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
   | ^v | ^v | ^v | xx | xx |        | xx |   | xx | xx | xx |
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
                    \____ d2sp3 : 2 x 3d + 1 x 4s + 3 x 4p ______/

     Hybridisation : d2sp3        Geometry : OCTAHEDRAL
     INNER orbital complex , LOW SPIN
     n = 0     mu = 0     ->   DIAMAGNETIC


  PART 3 - [CoF6]3-                                           (1.5 marks)

  Co3+ = [Ar] 3d6 (the same ion), but F- is a WEAK field ligand,
  so NO pairing takes place and OUTER 4d orbitals must be used:

        3 d                        4s        4p                 4d
  +----+----+----+----+----+   +----+  +----+----+----+   +----+----+
  | ^v | ^  | ^  | ^  | ^  |   | xx |  | xx | xx | xx |   | xx | xx |
  +----+----+----+----+----+   +----+  +----+----+----+   +----+----+
       4 unpaired                \_____________ sp3d2 _____________/

     Hybridisation : sp3d2        Geometry : OCTAHEDRAL
     OUTER orbital complex , HIGH SPIN
     n = 4     mu = sqrt(4 x 6) = 4.90 B.M.  ->  PARAMAGNETIC


  PART 4 - [Ni(CN)4]2-                                        (1.5 marks)

  Ni (Z=28) = [Ar] 3d8 4s2 ,   Ni2+ = [Ar] 3d8
  CN- is a STRONG field ligand -> the two unpaired electrons pair up,
  freeing ONE 3d orbital:

           3 d                            4s         4p
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
   | ^v | ^v | ^v | ^v | xx |        | xx |   | xx | xx |    |
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
                       \___ dsp2 : 1 x 3d + 1 x 4s + 2 x 4p ___/

     Hybridisation : dsp2         Geometry : SQUARE PLANAR
     n = 0     mu = 0     ->   DIAMAGNETIC


  PART 5 - [NiCl4]2-                                          (1.5 marks)

  Ni2+ = [Ar] 3d8 , but Cl- is a WEAK field ligand -> NO pairing:

           3 d                            4s         4p
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
   | ^v | ^v | ^v | ^  | ^  |        | xx |   | xx | xx | xx |
   +----+----+----+----+----+        +----+   +----+----+----+
              2 unpaired              \__________ sp3 ________/

     Hybridisation : sp3          Geometry : TETRAHEDRAL
     n = 2     mu = sqrt(2 x 4) = 2.83 B.M.  ->  PARAMAGNETIC


  PART 6 - LIMITATIONS OF VBT                                   (1 mark)

  1. It involves too many assumptions.
  2. It gives no quantitative interpretation of magnetic data.
  3. It does not explain the COLOUR of complexes.
  4. It gives no quantitative account of thermodynamic or kinetic stability.
  5. It cannot PREDICT whether a 4-coordinate complex will be tetrahedral
     or square planar.
  6. It does not explain why some ligands are strong field and others weak.
  7. It cannot explain the temperature dependence of magnetic behaviour.
```

### Q C2  *** THE OTHER MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED LONG ANSWER ***
Explain Crystal Field Theory. Describe the splitting of d orbitals in
octahedral and tetrahedral fields, high-spin and low-spin complexes, the
spectrochemical series, and the colour and magnetic behaviour of complexes.
Give the limitations of the theory.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PART 1 - THE POSTULATES OF CFT                                (1 mark)

  1. The bond between the metal and the ligand is purely IONIC
     (electrostatic). The ligands are treated as NEGATIVE POINT CHARGES
     (anions) or as DIPOLES (neutral ligands).
  2. In the free metal ion the five d orbitals are DEGENERATE.
  3. When the ligands approach, they repel the d electrons. This repulsion
     is NOT the same for all five orbitals, because the orbitals point in
     different directions.
  4. The d orbitals therefore SPLIT into sets of different energy.
     The pattern of the splitting depends on the GEOMETRY.


  PART 2 - OCTAHEDRAL SPLITTING                                (2 marks)

  The six ligands approach ALONG the x, y and z axes.

     dx2-y2 and dz2   point ALONG the axes  -> repelled MORE -> energy UP
                                            -> the  eg  set (2 orbitals)
     dxy , dyz , dzx  point BETWEEN axes    -> repelled LESS -> energy DOWN
                                            -> the  t2g set (3 orbitals)

   E
   ^                                              +----+  +----+
   |                                              |    |  |    |    eg
   |                                              +----+  +----+
   |                                                  ^
   |   +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+             |  + 0.6 delta-o
   |   |    | |    | |    | |    | |    |    ....-----+-----.... barycentre
   |   +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+             |  - 0.4 delta-o
   |   five degenerate d orbitals                     v
   |   (free metal ion)                        +----+ +----+ +----+
   |                                           |    | |    | |    |   t2g
   |                                           +----+ +----+ +----+
   +--------------------------------------------------------------->

     The energy gap  =  delta-o  =  CRYSTAL FIELD SPLITTING ENERGY (10 Dq)
     eg  raised  by  + 0.6 delta-o
     t2g lowered by  - 0.4 delta-o
     Check:  2(+0.6) + 3(-0.4) = 0 , so the average energy is unchanged.


  PART 3 - THE SPECTROCHEMICAL SERIES                           (1 mark)

  The size of delta-o depends on the ligand. Arranging ligands in the order
  of increasing delta-o gives the SPECTROCHEMICAL SERIES:

     I- < Br- < S2- < SCN- < Cl- < F- < OH- < C2O4 2- < H2O
        < NH3 < en < NO2- < CN- < CO
     <------ WEAK FIELD ------>        <------ STRONG FIELD ------>


  PART 4 - HIGH SPIN AND LOW SPIN                              (2 marks)

  Let P = PAIRING ENERGY.

     delta-o  >  P   ->  cheaper to PAIR UP in t2g   ->  LOW SPIN
                         (strong field ligand)
     delta-o  <  P   ->  cheaper to GO UP into eg    ->  HIGH SPIN
                         (weak field ligand)

  EXAMPLE - a d6 ion:

     HIGH SPIN  [CoF6]3-             LOW SPIN  [Co(NH3)6]3+

     +----+  +----+                  +----+  +----+
     | ^  |  | ^  |    eg            |    |  |    |    eg
     +----+  +----+                  +----+  +----+
     +----+ +----+ +----+            +----+ +----+ +----+
     | ^v | | ^  | | ^  |   t2g      | ^v | | ^v | | ^v |   t2g
     +----+ +----+ +----+            +----+ +----+ +----+
     t2g4 eg2 ,  n = 4               t2g6 eg0 ,  n = 0
     mu = 4.90 B.M. , PARAMAGNETIC   mu = 0 , DIAMAGNETIC

  The choice arises ONLY for d4 , d5 , d6 , d7 ions.


  PART 5 - TETRAHEDRAL SPLITTING                                (1 mark)

  Here the four ligands approach BETWEEN the axes, so the order is REVERSED.

   E
   ^                            +----+ +----+ +----+
   |                            |    | |    | |    |    t2  (dxy,dyz,dzx)
   |                            +----+ +----+ +----+
   |                                       ^   + 0.4 delta-t
   |   +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+  |
   |   |    | |    | |    | |    | |    |  |  .... barycentre ....
   |   +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+ +----+  |   - 0.6 delta-t
   |   five degenerate d orbitals          v
   |                                  +----+ +----+
   |                                  |    | |    |         e  (dx2-y2,dz2)
   |                                  +----+ +----+
   +--------------------------------------------------------------->

               delta-t  =  ( 4 / 9 )  x  delta-o

  Because delta-t is always SMALLER than the pairing energy, ALL
  tetrahedral complexes are HIGH SPIN. The subscript "g" is dropped
  because a tetrahedron has no centre of symmetry.


  PART 6 - COLOUR AND MAGNETIC BEHAVIOUR                        (1 mark)

  COLOUR: an electron in t2g absorbs a photon of energy equal to delta-o
  and jumps to eg (a d-d TRANSITION). That colour is removed from white
  light and we SEE the COMPLEMENTARY colour.
     [Ti(H2O)6]3+ (d1) absorbs near 500 nm and appears VIOLET.
     d0 and d10 complexes have no possible d-d transition and are
     COLOURLESS (Sc3+ , Zn2+ , Ag+).

  MAGNETISM:      mu  =  sqrt( n (n+2) )  B.M.
     n = 0 -> diamagnetic ; n = 1,2,3,4,5 -> 1.73, 2.83, 3.87, 4.90, 5.92


  PART 7 - LIMITATIONS OF CFT                                   (1 mark)

  1. It assumes the metal-ligand bond is purely IONIC and treats ligands
     as point charges, ignoring the COVALENT character of the bond.
  2. Because of that, it wrongly predicts that anionic ligands should give
     the largest splitting; in fact anions such as I- and Br- are at the
     WEAK end of the series while neutral CO and NH3 are at the STRONG end.
  3. It cannot explain why CO and CN- are such strong field ligands
     (this requires pi-bonding, which CFT ignores).
  4. It takes no account of the LIGAND orbitals, so it cannot explain
     charge-transfer spectra.
```

### Q C3
Discuss Werner's theory of coordination compounds, explaining primary and
secondary valencies, and show how it accounts for the compounds of the series
CoCl3.nNH3. Also explain the terms coordination number, coordination sphere
and coordination polyhedron.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PART 1 - WERNER'S POSTULATES                                 (3 marks)

  1. In coordination compounds the metal shows TWO types of valency -
     PRIMARY and SECONDARY.
  2. PRIMARY VALENCY is IONISABLE, is satisfied by NEGATIVE IONS, equals
     the OXIDATION NUMBER of the metal, is shown by DOTTED lines, and the
     ions are written OUTSIDE the square bracket.
  3. SECONDARY VALENCY is NON-IONISABLE, is satisfied by LIGANDS (neutral
     or negative), equals the COORDINATION NUMBER, is shown by SOLID lines,
     and the ligands are written INSIDE the bracket.
  4. Secondary valencies are DIRECTED in space, so they fix the GEOMETRY of
     the complex (octahedral for 6, square planar or tetrahedral for 4).
  5. Every metal satisfies both its primary and its secondary valencies.
  6. A negative ion may satisfy both valencies simultaneously.


  PART 2 - THE CoCl3.nNH3 SERIES                               (3 marks)

  Each compound was dissolved in water and treated with excess AgNO3.
  Only the FREE chloride outside the bracket is precipitated as AgCl.

  +---------------+----------+------------+---------------------+--------+
  | COMPOUND      | COLOUR   | moles AgCl | WERNER'S FORMULA    | IONS   |
  +---------------+----------+------------+---------------------+--------+
  | CoCl3.6NH3    | yellow   |     3      | [Co(NH3)6]Cl3       |   4    |
  | CoCl3.5NH3    | purple   |     2      | [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2     |   3    |
  | CoCl3.4NH3    | green    |     1      | [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl     |   2    |
  | CoCl3.3NH3    | violet   |     0      | [Co(NH3)3Cl3]       |   0    |
  +---------------+----------+------------+---------------------+--------+

  In EVERY member of the series:
       primary valency of Co   =  3   (Co is always +3)
       secondary valency of Co =  6   (always six groups inside the bracket)

  As one NH3 leaves the coordination sphere, one Cl- takes its place inside
  the bracket. That Cl- becomes non-ionisable, so one fewer mole of AgCl is
  precipitated and the electrical conductivity of the solution falls. The
  last member is a NEUTRAL complex and does not conduct at all.
  These observations exactly match the experiments, which is why Werner's
  theory was accepted. Werner's representation of [Co(NH3)4Cl2]Cl :

                        NH3
                         |
              NH3 ------ Co ------ NH3       SIX solid lines
                       / |  \                = secondary valency = 6
                    Cl   |   Cl
                        NH3                  the DOTTED line
                         :                   = primary valency, ionisable
                        Cl-


  PART 3 - THE TERMS                                           (2 marks)

  COORDINATION NUMBER    - the number of ligand DONOR ATOMS bonded to the
                           central metal, i.e. the number of sigma bonds.
                           In [Co(NH3)6]3+ it is 6; in [Co(en)3]3+ it is
                           also 6 because en is bidentate.

  COORDINATION SPHERE    - the central metal together with its ligands,
                           written inside square brackets. It behaves as
                           a single unit and does NOT ionise in solution.
                           In K4[Fe(CN)6] it is [Fe(CN)6]4-.

  COORDINATION POLYHEDRON- the three-dimensional shape formed by the donor
                           atoms around the metal.
                              C.N. 2 -> linear
                              C.N. 4 -> tetrahedral or square planar
                              C.N. 6 -> octahedral
```

### Q C4
Explain isomerism in coordination compounds. Describe structural isomerism and
stereoisomerism with suitable examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Isomers are compounds with the SAME molecular formula but DIFFERENT
  arrangements of atoms, and therefore different properties.

                            ISOMERISM
                                |
              +-----------------+-----------------+
              |                                   |
        STRUCTURAL                          STEREOISOMERISM
     (the bonds differ)               (bonds same, positions differ)
              |                                   |
   +------+---+----+-------+              +-------+--------+
   |      |        |       |              |                |
ionisation hydrate linkage coordination GEOMETRICAL     OPTICAL


  PART A - STRUCTURAL ISOMERISM                                (4 marks)

  1. IONISATION ISOMERISM - an ion inside the sphere swaps with the ion
     outside, so the isomers give different ions in solution.
        [Co(NH3)5SO4]Br    gives free Br-    -> AgNO3 gives pale yellow AgBr
        [Co(NH3)5Br]SO4    gives free SO4 2- -> BaCl2 gives white BaSO4

  2. HYDRATE (SOLVATE) ISOMERISM - water moves between being a ligand and
     being water of crystallisation.
        [Cr(H2O)6]Cl3           violet     , 3 moles AgCl
        [Cr(H2O)5Cl]Cl2 . H2O   blue-green , 2 moles AgCl
        [Cr(H2O)4Cl2]Cl . 2H2O  dark green , 1 mole AgCl

  3. LINKAGE ISOMERISM - shown by AMBIDENTATE ligands, which can bond
     through either of two different donor atoms.
        [Co(NH3)5(NO2)]Cl2   nitrito-N (through N) , yellow
        [Co(NH3)5(ONO)]Cl2   nitrito-O (through O) , red

  4. COORDINATION ISOMERISM - both the cation and the anion are complexes,
     and the ligands are exchanged between the two metal centres.
        [Co(NH3)6][Cr(CN)6]     and     [Cr(NH3)6][Co(CN)6]


  PART B - GEOMETRICAL ISOMERISM                               (2 marks)

  SQUARE PLANAR Ma2b2  ->  cis and trans , e.g. [Pt(NH3)2Cl2]

        CIS (90 deg apart)                TRANS (180 deg apart)
           NH3       Cl                      NH3       Cl
              \     /                           \     /
                Pt                                Pt
              /     \                           /     \
           NH3       Cl                      Cl        NH3
      cis form = CISPLATIN , anti-cancer      trans form = inactive

  OCTAHEDRAL Ma4b2  ->  cis and trans , e.g. [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+
        cis  : the two Cl are adjacent (90 degrees)
        trans: the two Cl are opposite (180 degrees)

  OCTAHEDRAL Ma3b3  ->  facial (fac) and meridional (mer),
        e.g. [Co(NH3)3Cl3]
        fac : the three identical ligands occupy one triangular FACE
        mer : the three identical ligands lie on a MERIDIAN

  TETRAHEDRAL complexes show NO geometrical isomerism, because all four
  positions are adjacent to one another.


  PART C - OPTICAL ISOMERISM                                   (2 marks)

  A complex with NO plane of symmetry is not superimposable on its mirror
  image and is OPTICALLY ACTIVE. The two forms are ENANTIOMERS:
        d-form rotates plane-polarised light to the RIGHT
        l-form rotates plane-polarised light to the LEFT

  EXAMPLES
     [Co(en)3]3+           optically active , exists as d and l
     cis-[Co(en)2Cl2]+     optically active
     trans-[Co(en)2Cl2]+   NOT active (it has a plane of symmetry)
     ALL square planar complexes are optically INACTIVE.

  So [Co(en)2Cl2]+ has 2 geometrical isomers but 3 forms in all
  (trans , d-cis , l-cis).
```

### Q C5
Write the IUPAC nomenclature rules for coordination compounds and name at
least eight complexes. Also write the formulae for four given names.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PART 1 - THE RULES                                           (4 marks)

  1. Name the CATION first and the ANION second.
  2. Inside the coordination sphere, name the LIGANDS first, then the METAL.
  3. Ligands are named in ALPHABETICAL order; the multiplying prefixes
     (di, tri, tetra, bis, tris) are IGNORED while alphabetising.
  4. ANIONIC ligands end in -O :
        chloride -> chlorido , cyanide -> cyanido ,
        oxalate  -> oxalato  , sulphate -> sulphato , nitrite -> nitrito
  5. NEUTRAL ligands keep their own names, with four exceptions:
        H2O -> aqua , NH3 -> ammine , CO -> carbonyl , NO -> nitrosyl
  6. CATIONIC ligands end in -ium :  NO+ -> nitrosonium
  7. Use di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa for simple ligands. Use bis, tris,
     tetrakis with the ligand in brackets when the ligand's own name
     already contains di / tri  (2 en -> bis(ethane-1,2-diamine)).
  8. Write the metal's oxidation number in ROMAN NUMERALS in round
     brackets immediately after its name, with no space: cobalt(III).
  9. If the complex ion is an ANION, add -ATE to the metal name:
        Fe -> ferrate , Cu -> cuprate , Ag -> argentate ,
        Pb -> plumbate , Sn -> stannate , Au -> aurate
 10. If the complex is a cation or is neutral, use the ordinary English
     name of the metal.
 11. The complex ion is written as ONE WORD.
