# Solutions — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

This chapter has only about 28 shapes in total, and most of them are numericals
with three or four steps. **That is why it is the easiest chapter to score in.**

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — NAME THE TYPE OF SOLUTION / GIVE AN EXAMPLE          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give an example of a solution of a gas in a liquid",
            "Brass is an example of ...", "What are the nine types?"
  METHOD    Fix these three in memory and derive the rest:
                 gas in gas       ->  AIR
                 solid in liquid  ->  SUGAR IN WATER
                 solid in solid   ->  BRASS (an alloy)
            The other six:
                 liquid in gas    ->  water vapour in air (humidity)
                 solid in gas     ->  camphor vapour in nitrogen
                 gas in liquid    ->  CO2 in soda water
                 liquid in liquid ->  ethanol in water
                 gas in solid     ->  hydrogen in palladium
                 liquid in solid  ->  AMALGAM (mercury in sodium)
  TRAP      The SOLVENT is the one present in the LARGER amount, not
            "the liquid one". In 90 % alcohol, alcohol is the solvent.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — DEFINE A CONCENTRATION TERM                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define molarity / molality / mole fraction / ppm /
            mass percentage / normality."
  METHOD    Give THREE things every time:
              1. the definition in words
              2. the formula
              3. the UNIT
            One mark is for the formula, one for the definition+unit.
                 mass %   = (mass solute / mass SOLUTION) x 100 , no unit
                 volume % = (vol solute / vol SOLUTION) x 100   , no unit
                 w/v %    = (g solute / mL SOLUTION) x 100      , no unit
                 ppm      = (mass solute / mass SOLUTION) x 10^6, no unit
                 x2       = n2 / (n1 + n2)                      , no unit
                 M        = mol solute / L of SOLUTION          , mol/L
                 m        = mol solute / kg of SOLVENT          , mol/kg
                 N        = gram eq / L of SOLUTION             , eq/L
  TRAP      Molarity uses the SOLUTION. Molality uses the SOLVENT.
            Getting these two the wrong way round is the single most
            common mistake in the whole chapter.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — SIMPLE MOLARITY / MOLALITY / MOLE FRACTION SUM       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the molarity of a solution containing X g of ...
            in Y mL of solution."
  METHOD    1. moles  n = w / M
            2. MOLARITY : divide by the VOLUME OF SOLUTION in LITRES
               MOLALITY : divide by the MASS OF SOLVENT in KILOGRAMS
               MOLE FRACTION : divide by the TOTAL moles
            3. Write the unit.
  TRAP      Convert mL to L (divide by 1000) and g to kg (divide by 1000)
            BEFORE dividing. Half the lost marks in this pattern are
            factor-of-1000 errors.
  CHECK     Mole fractions must add up to exactly 1.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — NORMALITY AND n-FACTOR                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the normality of ..." or "Define normality."
  METHOD    1. n-factor:  acid  -> number of replaceable H+
                          base  -> number of OH-
                          salt  -> total positive charge
            2. equivalent mass = molar mass / n-factor
            3. N = gram equivalents / litres of SOLUTION
               or simply    N  =  M  x  n-factor
  TRAP      H2SO4 has n-factor 2, so 1 M H2SO4 is 2 N, NOT 1 N.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WHY MOLALITY, NOT MOLARITY?                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is molality preferred over molarity?" /
            "Which concentration term is independent of temperature?"
  METHOD    Write BOTH halves - one mark each:
              MOLARITY uses VOLUME. Volume expands on heating, so the
              same moles occupy more volume and molarity FALLS.
                        ->  MOLARITY DEPENDS ON TEMPERATURE.
              MOLALITY uses MASS. Mass is unaffected by temperature.
                        ->  MOLALITY IS INDEPENDENT OF TEMPERATURE.
            Finish with: "that is why colligative-property formulae
            use molality."
  NOTE      Mole fraction, mass % and ppm are ALSO temperature
            independent. Volume % and normality are NOT.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — "x % w/w, DENSITY d" -> MOLARITY, MOLALITY, x        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any question that gives a PERCENTAGE and a DENSITY.
            *** THE MOST COMMON 4-MARK QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
  METHOD    1. ASSUME 100 g of SOLUTION.
                  solute = x g ,  solvent = (100 - x) g
            2. moles of solute = x / M2
            3. MOLALITY = moles / [(100-x)/1000] kg
            4. volume of solution = 100 / d  mL  ->  litres
            5. MOLARITY = moles / volume in litres
            6. moles of solvent = (100-x)/M1 ;  x2 = n2/(n1+n2)
  SHORTCUT  Molarity in ONE line:   M = 10 (mass %) d / M2
  TRAP      Density is of the SOLUTION, so it converts the 100 g of
            SOLUTION into volume - never the mass of solvent.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — HENRY'S LAW: STATEMENT AND APPLICATIONS         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Henry's law", "Why do soft drinks fizz?",
            "What are bends?", "Why do climbers feel weak?"
  METHOD    STATEMENT: "The partial pressure of a gas in the vapour phase
            is directly proportional to the mole fraction of that gas in
            the solution."           p  =  K(H) . x
            THREE APPLICATIONS (one each, with the reason):
              1. SODA WATER  - sealed under high CO2 pressure; opening
                 drops the pressure, so CO2 fizzes out.
              2. SCUBA DIVING / BENDS - high pressure dissolves N2 in
                 blood; fast ascent releases it as bubbles in the
                 capillaries. Prevented by diluting the air with HELIUM.
              3. HIGH ALTITUDE SICKNESS (ANOXIA) - low partial pressure
                 of O2 means less O2 dissolves in blood; climbers become
                 weak and cannot think clearly.
  TRAP      K(H) has the unit of PRESSURE, not mol/L. And a BIG K(H)
            means a POORLY soluble gas, not a highly soluble one.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — HENRY'S LAW NUMERICAL                                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A value of K(H) is given, with a pressure, and you are asked
            for solubility, mole fraction, moles or mass of dissolved gas.
  METHOD    1. Put the pressure and K(H) into the SAME unit.
            2. x  =  p / K(H)
            3. Moles of water: 1 L of water = 1000 g = 55.5 mol
               (or use the mass of water actually given).
            4. Since x is tiny,   n(gas)  =  x  x  n(water)
            5. mass of gas = moles x molar mass
  TRAP      K(H) is often quoted in kbar (1 kbar = 1000 bar) or in Pa.
            Convert first. A missed "k" makes the answer 1000x wrong.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — EFFECT OF TEMPERATURE AND PRESSURE ON SOLUBILITY     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Effect of temperature on the solubility of a gas / solid",
            "Why do bubbles form before water boils?"
  METHOD    GAS IN LIQUID
              dissolving is EXOTHERMIC  ->  heating drives the gas OUT
              ->  SOLUBILITY DECREASES with temperature ; K(H) increases
              ->  pressure has a LARGE effect (Henry's law)
            SOLID IN LIQUID
              ENDOTHERMIC dissolving (KNO3, KCl) -> solubility INCREASES
              EXOTHERMIC dissolving (Ce2(SO4)3)  -> solubility DECREASES
              ->  pressure has NO significant effect (solids are
                  incompressible)
  TRAP      Do not say "solubility always increases with temperature".
            For GASES it always DECREASES.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — STATE RAOULT'S LAW                                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Raoult's law."
  METHOD    Give BOTH forms - the examiner expects both.
            (a) TWO VOLATILE LIQUIDS: "the partial vapour pressure of
                each component is directly proportional to its mole
                fraction in the solution."
                     p(A) = p0(A) x(A)  ,  p(B) = p0(B) x(B)
                     p(total) = p0(A)x(A) + p0(B)x(B)
            (b) NON-VOLATILE SOLUTE: "the relative lowering of vapour
                pressure equals the mole fraction of the solute."
                     (p0 - p)/p0  =  x2      and     p = p0 x1
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — TWO VOLATILE LIQUIDS NUMERICAL                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two p0 values are given, plus masses or moles of the two
            liquids. "Find the total vapour pressure / the composition
            of the vapour."
  METHOD    1. moles of each = w / M
            2. mole fractions in the LIQUID: x(A), x(B). Check x(A)+x(B)=1
            3. p(A) = p0(A) x(A) ,  p(B) = p0(B) x(B)
            4. p(total) = p(A) + p(B)
            5. VAPOUR composition:  y(A) = p(A)/p(total)
  SHORTCUT  For an EQUIMOLAR mixture, p(total) is simply the AVERAGE
            of p0(A) and p0(B).
  TRAP      x is in the LIQUID, y is in the VAPOUR. The vapour is always
            RICHER in the more volatile liquid (higher p0). If your y
            for that liquid is smaller than its x, you slipped.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — RAOULT'S LAW AND HENRY'S LAW - THE LINK             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the relationship between Raoult's law and
            Henry's law?"
  METHOD    RAOULT :  p = p0 . x        HENRY :  p = K(H) . x
            Both say the partial pressure is proportional to the mole
            fraction in solution; they differ only in the constant.
            ==> RAOULT'S LAW IS A SPECIAL CASE OF HENRY'S LAW in which
                K(H) becomes equal to p0.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — IDEAL vs NON-IDEAL SOLUTIONS                        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between ideal and non-ideal solutions",
            "What is an ideal solution? Give examples."
  METHOD    Draw a two-column table with these five rows:
               obeys Raoult's law at all concentrations?  yes / no
               delta H (mixing)     = 0  /  not 0
               delta V (mixing)     = 0  /  not 0
               A-B force vs A-A,B-B same / different
               forms an azeotrope?  no / yes
            THEN give examples of each - the examples carry a mark.
               IDEAL: benzene + toluene , n-hexane + n-heptane ,
                      bromoethane + chloroethane
  TRAP      "Ideal" needs BOTH delta H = 0 AND delta V = 0. Writing only
            one loses half the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — POSITIVE AND NEGATIVE DEVIATION (with graphs)       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain positive and negative deviation from Raoult's law",
            "Why does chloroform + acetone show negative deviation?"
  METHOD    For EACH type give FOUR things:
              1. observed VP higher (positive) / lower (negative) than
                 Raoult predicts
              2. CAUSE: A-B force weaker (positive) / stronger, usually
                 a new H-bond (negative)
              3. SIGNS: delta H and delta V both + (positive),
                        both - (negative)
              4. TWO EXAMPLES
            Then DRAW the graph: the observed curve BULGES ABOVE the
            straight ideal line for positive, SAGS BELOW for negative.
            Label the axes (vapour pressure vs mole fraction) and mark
            p0(A) and p0(B).
  TRAP      Positive deviation means MORE vapour, so a LOWER boiling
            point. Students wrongly write "higher boiling point".
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — AZEOTROPES                                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is an azeotrope?", "Why can't pure ethanol be obtained
            by distillation?"
  METHOD    DEFINITION: a binary mixture that boils at a constant
            temperature and distils without change of composition, so it
            cannot be separated by fractional distillation.
            MINIMUM BOILING - from a large POSITIVE deviation
                              95 % ethanol + water , 351.1 K
            MAXIMUM BOILING - from a large NEGATIVE deviation
                              68 % nitric acid + water , 393.5 K
  MEMORY    POSITIVE -> boils easier -> MINIMUM boiling -> ethanol/water
            NEGATIVE -> boils harder -> MAXIMUM boiling -> HNO3/water
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — RELATIVE LOWERING OF VAPOUR PRESSURE                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two vapour pressures (p0 and p), or "find the vapour pressure
            of the solution", or "find the molar mass from vapour
            pressure data".
  METHOD    CASE A - find the new vapour pressure p:
              1. n2 = w2/M2 , n1 = w1/M1
              2. x2 = n2 / (n1 + n2)
              3. (p0 - p)/p0 = x2   ->   p0 - p = p0 x2
              4. p = p0 - (p0 x2)
            CASE B - find the molar mass M2:
              1. compute (p0 - p)/p0
              2. for a dilute solution set it equal to n2/n1
              3. n1 = w1/M1  ->  n2 = (relative lowering) x n1
              4. M2 = w2 / n2
                 or in one line:  M2 = w2 M1 p0 / [ w1 (p0 - p) ]
  TRAP      Relative lowering equals the mole fraction of the SOLUTE,
            not the solvent. And it has NO UNIT.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — ELEVATION OF BOILING POINT                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "At what temperature will the solution boil?",
            "Calculate the elevation of boiling point ..."
  METHOD    1. moles of solute = w2/M2
            2. molality m = moles / (mass of SOLVENT in kg)
            3. delta Tb = Kb x m       (put i in front if it is a salt)
            4. new boiling point = Tb(0) + delta Tb        <-- ADD
  DATA      Kb(water) = 0.52 , Kb(benzene) = 2.53
            b.p. water = 373.15 K , b.p. benzene = 353.23 K
  TRAP      ADD for boiling. Subtracting here is an automatic zero.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — DEPRESSION OF FREEZING POINT                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the freezing point of ...", antifreeze / ethylene
            glycol / salt-on-icy-roads questions.
  METHOD    1. moles of solute = w2/M2
            2. molality m = moles / (mass of SOLVENT in kg)
            3. delta Tf = Kf x m       (put i in front if it is a salt)
            4. new freezing point = Tf(0) - delta Tf     <-- SUBTRACT
  DATA      Kf(water) = 1.86 , Kf(benzene) = 5.12
            f.p. water = 273.15 K (0 C)
  TRAP      SUBTRACT for freezing. Also note delta Tf is defined as
            Tf(0) - Tf, so it is a POSITIVE number.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — MOLAR MASS FROM delta Tb OR delta Tf                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the molar mass of the solute" together with a
            temperature change and a value of Kb or Kf.
            *** THE MOST COMMON NUMERICAL IN THE WHOLE CHAPTER ***
  METHOD    1. Find delta T from the two temperatures given.
            2. Apply the ONE formula:

                            1000   x   K   x   w2
                   M2   =  --------------------------
                            delta T    x    w1

               K = Kb if the question mentions BOILING
               K = Kf if the question mentions FREEZING or MELTING
            3. w1 is the mass of the SOLVENT in GRAMS.
  TRAP      Do NOT convert w1 to kg - the 1000 in the formula has
            already done that. This one slip makes the answer 1000x out.
  CHECK     Molar masses of real substances are between about 20 and 400
            (except polymers). An answer of 0.058 or 58000 means you
            slipped a factor of 1000.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — OSMOTIC PRESSURE NUMERICAL                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the osmotic pressure of ..."
  METHOD    1. C = moles of solute / volume of solution in LITRES
            2. T in KELVIN
            3. pi = C R T          (put i in front if it is a salt)
               R = 0.0821 L atm/K mol  for an answer in ATM
               R = 0.083  L bar/K mol  for an answer in BAR
  TRAP      Volume in LITRES, temperature in KELVIN. And match R to the
            pressure unit the question wants.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — MOLAR MASS FROM OSMOTIC PRESSURE                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A protein, polymer, enzyme or haemoglobin, with an osmotic
            pressure given.
  METHOD                w2   R   T
              M2   =  ---------------
                        pi   x   V
            1. convert cm3 or mL to LITRES
            2. temperature to KELVIN
            3. substitute and divide carefully - the answer will be a
               LARGE number (tens of thousands)
  NOTE      Be ready for the follow-up theory question: osmotic pressure
            is preferred for proteins and polymers because it is measured
            at ROOM TEMPERATURE (so the sample is not destroyed) and
            because pi is large and measurable even in a very dilute
            solution.
  TRAP      pi in the denominator is often given as 10^-3 bar. Dividing
            by a small number gives a big answer - that is correct here.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — ISOTONIC / HYPERTONIC / HYPOTONIC              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define isotonic solutions", "Why is 0.9 % NaCl used for
            injections?", "What happens to a cell placed in ..."
  METHOD    ISOTONIC   - same osmotic pressure, so no osmosis.
                         0.9 % w/v NaCl is isotonic with blood cells,
                         which is why it is used for IV drips.
            HYPERTONIC - higher pi ; the cell loses water and SHRINKS
                         (plasmolysis). Salt preserves food this way.
            HYPOTONIC  - lower pi ; the cell gains water and SWELLS,
                         and may burst. A raisin swells in water.
            NUMERICAL FORM:  pi(1) = pi(2)  ==>  C1 = C2
                             w1/(M1 V) = w2/(M2 V)
  TRAP      "Hyper" pulls water OUT and shrivels the cell. Students often
            reverse this.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — OSMOSIS, SPM AND REVERSE OSMOSIS               4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain osmosis and osmotic pressure with a diagram",
            "What is reverse osmosis? Give its application."
  METHOD    1. Define SEMIPERMEABLE MEMBRANE + one natural and one
               artificial example (parchment / Cu2[Fe(CN)6]).
            2. Define OSMOSIS (solvent flows from dilute to concentrated).
            3. Define OSMOTIC PRESSURE and write pi = C R T.
            4. DRAW the labelled diagram: two compartments, the membrane
               between them, an arrow showing water entering the
               solution, the raised level, and the applied pressure pi.
            5. REVERSE OSMOSIS: applied pressure GREATER than pi reverses
               the flow. Used for DESALINATION of sea water (pi of sea
               water is about 30 atm) with a CELLULOSE ACETATE membrane.
  TRAP      In reverse osmosis the pressure must EXCEED the osmotic
            pressure, not equal it. And the SOLVENT moves, never the
            solute.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — FIND THE VAN'T HOFF FACTOR i                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the value of i for ...", or an observed and a normal
            molar mass are both given.
  METHOD    COUNT THE IONS in the formula:
                 NaCl , KCl , MgSO4        ->  i = 2
                 CaCl2 , MgCl2 , K2SO4     ->  i = 3
                 AlCl3 , K3[Fe(CN)6]       ->  i = 4
                 Al2(SO4)3 , K4[Fe(CN)6]   ->  i = 5
                 glucose , urea , sucrose  ->  i = 1
                 a DIMERISING acid         ->  i = 0.5
            FROM DATA:
                 i = normal molar mass / observed molar mass
                 i = observed delta T   / calculated delta T
  TRAP      i > 1 means DISSOCIATION, i < 1 means ASSOCIATION.
            i can never exceed the number of ions in the formula.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — DEGREE OF DISSOCIATION FROM i                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A salt or a weak acid in water, with an experimental freezing
            point, boiling point or osmotic pressure. "Find the degree
            of dissociation."
  METHOD    1. delta T (observed) from the temperatures given.
            2. delta T (calculated) = K x m , assuming NO dissociation.
            3. i = observed / calculated
            4. n = number of ions the formula gives.
            5.        i  -  1
               alpha = ---------          then multiply by 100 for %
                        n  -  1
  TRAP      n is the number of particles ONE formula unit produces,
            not the number of moles taken. For CaCl2, n = 3.
  CHECK     alpha must lie between 0 and 1. If not, recheck step 3.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — DEGREE OF ASSOCIATION (the dimer question)          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   BENZOIC ACID or ACETIC ACID dissolved in BENZENE. The
            observed molar mass is roughly DOUBLE the true one.
  METHOD    1. Observed molar mass from  M2 = 1000 Kf w2 / (delta Tf w1)
            2. Normal molar mass from the formula
                  benzoic acid C6H5COOH = 122 , acetic acid = 60
            3. i = normal / observed        (this will be less than 1)
            4. n = 2 for a dimer
            5.          1  -  i
               alpha = ---------------      then x 100 for %
                        1  -  ( 1/n )
  REASON    Benzene is non-polar and cannot solvate the -COOH group, so
            two acid molecules hydrogen bond to each other and behave as
            ONE particle.
  TRAP      Use the ASSOCIATION formula, not (i-1)/(n-1). With i < 1 the
            dissociation formula gives a negative answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — RANK SOLUTIONS BY A COLLIGATIVE PROPERTY         objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which 0.1 M solution has the highest boiling point / lowest
            freezing point / highest osmotic pressure / lowest vapour
            pressure?"
  METHOD    ONE LINE ANSWERS ALL OF THEM:  COUNT THE IONS.
                 glucose, urea    1     NaCl, KCl       2
                 CaCl2, K2SO4     3     AlCl3           4
                 Al2(SO4)3        5

              MOST particles  ->  HIGHEST boiling point
                              ->  LOWEST  freezing point
                              ->  HIGHEST osmotic pressure
                              ->  LOWEST  vapour pressure

              FEWEST particles ->  the exact opposite
  TRAP      If molalities differ, multiply:  effect is proportional to
            i x m , not to i alone.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — THE FOUR COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES              *** 8 marks ***
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are colligative properties? Explain all four and show
            how each gives the molar mass of a solute."
  METHOD    Write FIVE parts:
              1. DEFINITION - depends only on the NUMBER of solute
                 particles, not on their nature. Give the glucose /
                 urea / sucrose comparison as proof.
              2. RELATIVE LOWERING OF VP:  (p0-p)/p0 = x2
                 M2 = w2 M1 p0 / [w1 (p0 - p)]
              3. ELEVATION OF BOILING POINT: delta Tb = Kb m
                 M2 = 1000 Kb w2 / (delta Tb w1) ; define Kb
              4. DEPRESSION OF FREEZING POINT: delta Tf = Kf m
                 M2 = 1000 Kf w2 / (delta Tf w1) ; define Kf
              5. OSMOTIC PRESSURE: pi = C R T
                 M2 = w2 R T / (pi V) ; say why it is best for proteins
            Finish with the summary table of the four formulae.
  NOTE      *** THE MOST FREQUENTLY ASKED LONG ANSWER FROM THIS
            CHAPTER. If the van't Hoff factor is also asked, add the
            four modified formulae with i in front. ***
```

# GUARANTEED-MARKS PLAN FOR THIS CHAPTER

Learn these six things and you comfortably secure **10 to 14 marks out of 60**:

```
  1.  The definitions of molarity, molality, mole fraction, ppm,
      normality - with FORMULA and UNIT for each.
      Plus the one-line reason why molality is temperature independent.
          ->  2 to 4 marks of Section A, every single year

  2.  The "x % w/w + density -> M, m, x" sum (PATTERN 6).
      Practise it ten times with different numbers.
          ->  one whole 4-mark question

  3.  Henry's law:  p = K(H) x , the meaning of K(H), the temperature
      effect, and the three applications (soda, bends, anoxia).
          ->  2 or 4 marks

  4.  ONE formula that answers every molar-mass numerical:

                        1000   x   K   x   w2
               M2   =  --------------------------
                        delta T    x    w1

      with  Kb(water) = 0.52 ,  Kf(water) = 1.86 ,
            Kb(benzene) = 2.53 , Kf(benzene) = 5.12
```
