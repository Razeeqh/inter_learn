## Topic: What is a solution, and the nine types

### Q A1
Define a solution. What is a binary solution?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SOLUTION  -  a HOMOGENEOUS mixture of two or more substances, whose
               composition is the same throughout.
               Example: sugar dissolved in water.

  BINARY SOLUTION  -  a solution made of exactly TWO components,
                      one solute and one solvent.
                      Example: NaCl in water.
```

### Q A8
Why is molality preferred over molarity in expressing the concentration
of a solution? (**asked very often**)

**ANSWER:**
```
  MOLARITY is defined per unit VOLUME of solution. Volume EXPANDS on
  heating, so the same moles occupy a larger volume and the molarity
  FALLS.  ->  molarity CHANGES with temperature.

  MOLALITY is defined per unit MASS of solvent, and mass is completely
  independent of temperature.
                     ->  molality DOES NOT CHANGE with temperature.

  That is why molality is preferred, and why every colligative-property
  formula uses molality.
```

## Topic: Solubility and Henry's law

### Q A14
State Henry's law. Write its mathematical expression.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HENRY'S LAW  -  "The partial pressure of a gas in the vapour phase is
  directly proportional to the mole fraction of that gas dissolved in
  the solution."

              p   =   K(H)   .   x

   p    =  partial pressure of the gas above the liquid
   x    =  mole fraction of the gas in the solution (its solubility)
   K(H) =  Henry's constant, whose unit is a unit of PRESSURE
```

---

### Q A15
What is the effect of temperature on the solubility of a gas in a liquid?
Give one everyday example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The solubility of a gas in a liquid DECREASES as the temperature
  INCREASES, because dissolving a gas is an EXOTHERMIC process and
  heating shifts the equilibrium backwards.
  (Equivalently, K(H) increases with temperature.)

  EXAMPLE:  bubbles of dissolved air appear in a pan of water long
  before it boils. Also, warm soda goes flat much faster than cold soda.
```

---

### Q A16
Why do soft drink bottles fizz when they are opened?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A soft drink is sealed under a HIGH pressure of CO2, so by Henry's law
  a large amount of CO2 dissolves in it.

  When the bottle is opened, the pressure above the liquid suddenly falls
  to atmospheric pressure. The solubility of CO2 falls with it, and the
  excess dissolved CO2 escapes as bubbles - the fizz.
```

---

### Q A17
What is meant by "bends"? How is it avoided?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BENDS (decompression sickness) - a deep-sea diver breathes air at high
  pressure, so a large amount of NITROGEN dissolves in the blood.
  If the diver surfaces too fast, the pressure drops suddenly and the
  dissolved nitrogen comes out as BUBBLES inside the blood capillaries,
  blocking them and causing severe pain.

  AVOIDED by filling the diver's tanks with air DILUTED WITH HELIUM
  (about 11.7 % He, 56.2 % N2, 32.1 % O2), because helium is much less
  soluble in blood than nitrogen.
```

---

### Q A18
Why do climbers at high altitude become weak and unable to think clearly?

**ANSWER:**
```
  At high altitude the PARTIAL PRESSURE OF OXYGEN is low. By Henry's law
  less oxygen dissolves in the blood, so the blood and tissues are
  starved of oxygen. The climber feels weak and cannot think clearly.

  This condition is called ANOXIA (or high altitude sickness).
```

---

### Q A19
Why does the solubility of most solids in a liquid increase with
temperature, while for some it decreases?

**ANSWER:**
```
   SOLID  +  SOLVENT  <-->  SOLUTION

  If dissolving is ENDOTHERMIC (delta H positive), heating pushes the
  equilibrium FORWARD, so the SOLUBILITY INCREASES.
      Example:  KNO3 , KCl

  If dissolving is EXOTHERMIC (delta H negative), heating pushes the
  equilibrium BACKWARD, so the SOLUBILITY DECREASES.
      Example:  Ce2(SO4)3
```

## Topic: Raoult's law, ideal and non-ideal solutions

### Q A20
State Raoult's law for a solution of two volatile liquids.

**ANSWER:**
```
  "For a solution of two volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of
  each component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the
  solution."

          p(A)  =  p0(A) . x(A)          p(B)  =  p0(B) . x(B)

          p(total)  =  p0(A) x(A)  +  p0(B) x(B)
```

---

### Q A21
What is the relationship between Raoult's law and Henry's law?

**ANSWER:**
```
   RAOULT :   p  =  p0   . x
   HENRY  :   p  =  K(H) . x

  Both state that the partial pressure of a component is directly
  proportional to its mole fraction in the solution. They differ only in
  the proportionality constant.

  ==>  RAOULT'S LAW IS A SPECIAL CASE OF HENRY'S LAW, in which the Henry
       constant K(H) becomes equal to the vapour pressure of the pure
       component, p0.
```

---

### Q A22
Define an ideal solution. Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An IDEAL SOLUTION obeys Raoult's law over the ENTIRE range of
  concentration, and for it

         delta H (mixing)  =  0        delta V (mixing)  =  0

  because the A-B force equals the A-A and B-B forces.

  EXAMPLES:   benzene + toluene
              n-hexane + n-heptane
              (also bromoethane + chloroethane)
```

### Q A23
What is an azeotrope? Name its two types with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AZEOTROPE (constant boiling mixture) - a binary mixture that boils at a
  CONSTANT temperature and distils over WITHOUT any change in composition,
  so it CANNOT be separated by fractional distillation.

  1. MINIMUM BOILING AZEOTROPE  -  formed by solutions showing a large
     POSITIVE deviation.
     Example:  95 % ethanol + water , boiling at 351.1 K

  2. MAXIMUM BOILING AZEOTROPE  -  formed by solutions showing a large
     NEGATIVE deviation.
     Example:  68 % nitric acid + water , boiling at 393.5 K
```

## Topic: Colligative properties

### Q A25
What are colligative properties? Name them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES are the properties of a solution that depend only
  on the NUMBER of solute particles present, and NOT on the nature of
  those particles.

  The FOUR colligative properties are:
      1.  Relative lowering of vapour pressure
      2.  Elevation of boiling point
      3.  Depression of freezing point
      4.  Osmotic pressure
```

Define the molal elevation constant (Kb). What is its unit?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Kb (molal elevation constant / ebullioscopic constant) is the ELEVATION
  OF BOILING POINT produced when ONE MOLE of a solute is dissolved in
  ONE KILOGRAM of the solvent.

          delta Tb  =  Kb  x  m           UNIT :   K kg / mol

          For water,  Kb  =  0.52  K kg/mol
```

### Q A29
Define osmosis and osmotic pressure.

**ANSWER:**
```
  OSMOSIS  -  the spontaneous flow of SOLVENT molecules through a
  semipermeable membrane from a solution of LOWER concentration to a
  solution of HIGHER concentration.

  OSMOTIC PRESSURE (pi)  -  the extra pressure that must be applied on
  the solution side to JUST STOP osmosis.

              pi   =   C R T
```

---

### Q A30
What is a semipermeable membrane? Give one natural and one artificial
example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A SEMIPERMEABLE MEMBRANE is a membrane whose pores allow SOLVENT
  molecules to pass through but are too small for SOLUTE particles.

  NATURAL     :  animal bladder, parchment paper, cell membrane
  ARTIFICIAL  :  copper ferrocyanide  Cu2[Fe(CN)6]
                 cellulose acetate film (used in RO plants)
```

---

### Q A31
Define isotonic, hypertonic and hypotonic solutions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ISOTONIC    -  two solutions having the SAME osmotic pressure at the
                 same temperature. No osmosis occurs between them.
                 Example: 0.9 % w/v NaCl is isotonic with blood cells.

  HYPERTONIC  -  a solution having a HIGHER osmotic pressure than the one
                 it is compared with. A cell placed in it SHRINKS.

  HYPOTONIC   -  a solution having a LOWER osmotic pressure. A cell placed
                 in it SWELLS and may burst.
```

---

### Q A32
What is reverse osmosis? Give its main use.

**ANSWER:**
```
  REVERSE OSMOSIS - if a pressure GREATER than the osmotic pressure is
  applied on the solution side, the solvent flows in the OPPOSITE
  direction, i.e. OUT of the solution and into the pure solvent side.

  MAIN USE :  DESALINATION OF SEA WATER - producing drinking water from
  sea water. The membrane used is cellulose acetate. Sea water has an
  osmotic pressure of about 30 atm, so more than 30 atm must be applied.
```

### Q A35
What is meant by abnormal molar mass? Give one cause.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ABNORMAL MOLAR MASS - a molar mass determined from a colligative
  property that differs from the true (theoretical) molar mass.

  CAUSES:
   (a) DISSOCIATION of the solute into ions (NaCl -> Na+ + Cl-)
```

### Q B3
State Henry's law and write any three of its important applications.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT: "The partial pressure of a gas in the vapour phase is
  directly proportional to the mole fraction of that gas in the solution."

              p   =   K(H)  .  x

  K(H) is Henry's constant; its unit is a unit of pressure.
  A LARGE K(H) means a POORLY soluble gas.
  K(H) increases with temperature, so gas solubility falls on heating.

  APPLICATIONS

  1.  SOFT DRINKS AND SODA WATER
      Bottles are sealed under high CO2 pressure so that a large amount
      of CO2 dissolves. On opening, the pressure falls, solubility falls,
      and the excess CO2 escapes as fizz.

  2.  SCUBA DIVING AND BENDS
      Under water a diver breathes air at high pressure, so more nitrogen
      dissolves in the blood. Surfacing too quickly releases it as
      bubbles in the capillaries - a painful condition called BENDS.
      Divers therefore use tanks of air diluted with HELIUM, which is far
      less soluble in blood.

  3.  HIGH ALTITUDE SICKNESS (ANOXIA)
      At high altitude the partial pressure of oxygen is low, so less
      oxygen dissolves in the blood. Climbers become weak and cannot
      think clearly.
```

### Q B4
Henry's law constant for CO2 in water is 1.67 x 10^8 Pa at 298 K.

```

          p  =  2.5  x  1.01325 x 10^5   =   2.533 x 10^5  Pa

  STEP 3  Moles of water (500 mL of water = 500 g).

                        500
          n(water) =  -------  =  27.78 mol
                         18

  STEP 4  Since x is very small,   n(CO2) = x  x  n(water)

          n(CO2)  =  1.517 x 10^-3  x  27.78   =   0.0421 mol

  STEP 5  mass of CO2  =  0.0421  x  44   =   1.85  g

  ANSWER:  about 1.85 g of CO2
```

### Q B8
Explain, with reasons and graphs, the positive and negative deviations
from Raoult's law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  POSITIVE DEVIATION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  The observed vapour pressure is HIGHER than Raoult's law predicts.

  REASON: the A-B attraction is WEAKER than the A-A and B-B
  attractions, so molecules escape into the vapour MORE easily.

         delta H (mixing)  is POSITIVE  (mixture cools)
         delta V (mixing)  is POSITIVE  (volume increases)

  EXAMPLES: ethanol + water ; acetone + carbon disulphide ;
            carbon tetrachloride + chloroform

  GRAPH - the total pressure curve BULGES UPWARD:

   V |
```

### Q B16
Why is osmotic pressure preferred over the other colligative properties
for determining the molar mass of proteins and polymers?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  It is measured at ROOM TEMPERATURE. Boiling point and freezing
      point methods require heating or cooling, which can DESTROY
      delicate biomolecules such as proteins.

  2.  The magnitude of osmotic pressure is LARGE and easy to measure
      accurately, even for a very dilute solution. Proteins and polymers
      have huge molar masses, so their solutions are always extremely
      dilute; for such solutions delta Tb and delta Tf would be far too
      small to measure reliably.

  3.  It uses MOLARITY, so the concentration is obtained directly from
      the volume of the solution.

  4.  For polymers, only a very small quantity of sample is available,
      and the osmotic pressure method works with a small quantity.
```

### Q C1
What are colligative properties? Explain all four colligative properties
with their formulae, and show how each is used to determine the molar mass
of a solute.

**MODEL ANSWER:**

```
  DEFINITION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  COLLIGATIVE PROPERTIES are those properties of a dilute solution that
  depend ONLY on the NUMBER of solute particles present, and NOT on the
  nature, size or mass of those particles.

  For example, 1 mole of glucose, 1 mole of urea and 1 mole of sucrose
  in 1 kg of water all lower the freezing point by exactly the same
  amount, even though their molar masses are 180, 60 and 342.

  There are FOUR colligative properties.
```

**1. RELATIVE LOWERING OF VAPOUR PRESSURE**

```
  A non-volatile solute occupies part of the surface of the liquid, so
  fewer solvent molecules can escape and the vapour pressure FALLS.

         p0  -  p                                n2
       -------------  =  x2 (of solute)  =  -----------
            p0                                n1 + n2

  For a dilute solution this becomes

         p0  -  p           w2  x  M1
       -------------   =  --------------
            p0              M2  x  w1

  MOLAR MASS:
                       w2  x  M1  x  p0
          M2   =   --------------------------
                       w1  x  ( p0  -  p )
```

**2. ELEVATION OF BOILING POINT**

```
  Because the vapour pressure is lowered, a higher temperature is needed
  before the vapour pressure equals atmospheric pressure. So the boiling
  point RISES.

          delta Tb   =   Tb  -  Tb(0)   =   Kb  x  m

  Kb = molal elevation constant (ebullioscopic constant), K kg/mol
       = the rise in boiling point when 1 mole of solute is dissolved
         in 1 kg of solvent.       For water, Kb = 0.52 K kg/mol

  MOLAR MASS:
                       1000  x  Kb  x  w2
          M2   =   ---------------------------
                       delta Tb   x   w1
```

**3. DEPRESSION OF FREEZING POINT**

```
  A solution freezes when the vapour pressure of the liquid equals that
  of the solid solvent. Since the solute has lowered the vapour pressure,
  this happens at a LOWER temperature.

          delta Tf   =   Tf(0)  -  Tf   =   Kf  x  m

  Kf = molal depression constant (cryoscopic constant), K kg/mol
       For water, Kf = 1.86 K kg/mol

  MOLAR MASS:
                       1000  x  Kf  x  w2
          M2   =   ---------------------------
                       delta Tf   x   w1
```

**4. OSMOTIC PRESSURE**

```
  OSMOSIS is the flow of solvent through a semipermeable membrane from
  a dilute solution into a concentrated one.
  OSMOTIC PRESSURE (pi) is the extra pressure that must be applied on the
  solution side to just stop osmosis.

          pi   =   C R T           (van't Hoff equation)

          pi V  =  n2 R T

  MOLAR MASS:
                       w2   R   T
          M2   =   -------------------
                        pi   x   V

  R = 0.0821 L atm/K mol , T in kelvin , V in litres.
```

```
  SUMMARY TABLE

  +-------------------------+------------------+----------------------+
  |  PROPERTY               |  FORMULA         |  MOLAR MASS FROM     |
  +-------------------------+------------------+----------------------+
  | Relative lowering of VP | (p0-p)/p0 = x2   | w2 M1 p0/(w1(p0-p))  |
  | Elevation of b.p.       | delta Tb = Kb m  | 1000 Kb w2/(dTb w1)  |
  | Depression of f.p.      | delta Tf = Kf m  | 1000 Kf w2/(dTf w1)  |
  | Osmotic pressure        | pi = C R T       | w2 R T /(pi V)       |
  +-------------------------+------------------+----------------------+

  Osmotic pressure is the BEST method for PROTEINS and POLYMERS,
  because it works at room temperature and gives a large, easily
  measured value even for very dilute solutions.
```

```
  ISOTONIC, HYPERTONIC AND HYPOTONIC
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  ISOTONIC    -  two solutions with the SAME osmotic pressure at the
                 same temperature. No osmosis occurs between them.
                 A 0.9 % w/v NaCl solution (normal saline) is isotonic
                 with the fluid inside blood cells, which is why it is
                 used for intravenous injections.

  HYPERTONIC  -  a solution of HIGHER osmotic pressure. A cell placed
                 in it loses water and SHRINKS (plasmolysis).
                 This is why raw mango shrivels in a strong brine and
                 why salt and sugar preserve food.

  HYPOTONIC   -  a solution of LOWER osmotic pressure. A cell placed in
                 it takes in water, SWELLS and may burst.
                 This is why a dried raisin swells up in water.


  REVERSE OSMOSIS
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  If a pressure GREATER than the osmotic pressure is applied on the
  solution side, the direction of solvent flow REVERSES: pure solvent is
  squeezed OUT of the solution through the membrane.

          CONDITION :   applied pressure   >   osmotic pressure

        HUGE applied pressure (more than about 30 atm)
                          | |
                          v v
                     +---------+
                     |  SEA    |
       +---------+   | WATER   |
       |  PURE   |   | (salty) |
       | WATER   |   |         |
       | comes   |   |         |
       | out     |   |         |
       +----+----+---+----+----+
            |    || SPM ||    |
            +====++=====++====+

  APPLICATION - DESALINATION OF SEA WATER
     Sea water has an osmotic pressure of about 30 atm. If a pressure
     greater than 30 atm is applied to sea water held against a
     CELLULOSE ACETATE membrane, pure water passes through the membrane
     while the dissolved salts are left behind. The pure water collected
     is fit for drinking. The same principle is used in domestic
     "RO" water purifiers.
```

### Q C5
What is abnormal molar mass? Explain the van't Hoff factor. How is it
related to the degree of dissociation and the degree of association?
Illustrate with the case of NaCl in water and benzoic acid in benzene.

**MODEL ANSWER:**

```
  ABNORMAL MOLAR MASS
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  A molar mass calculated from a colligative property that is DIFFERENT
  from the true (theoretical) molar mass of the solute is called an
  ABNORMAL MOLAR MASS.

  It arises for two reasons.

  1.  DISSOCIATION
      The solute breaks up into ions in solution, so the number of
      particles INCREASES.
              NaCl   ->   Na+   +   Cl-
      The colligative effect is BIGGER than expected, so the calculated
      molar mass comes out TOO LOW (about 29.25 instead of 58.5).

  2.  ASSOCIATION
      Solute molecules join together, so the number of particles
      DECREASES.
              2 C6H5COOH   ->   ( C6H5COOH )2      a DIMER
      The colligative effect is SMALLER than expected, so the calculated
      molar mass comes out TOO HIGH (about 244 instead of 122).


  VAN'T HOFF FACTOR  i
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  To correct for this, van't Hoff introduced a factor i.

            number of particles ACTUALLY present in solution
    i  =  ----------------------------------------------------
            number of particles EXPECTED before dissoc./assoc.

            OBSERVED colligative property         normal molar mass
       =  ---------------------------------  =  -------------------
            CALCULATED colligative property       observed molar mass

       i = 1   ->  normal, no dissociation or association
       i > 1   ->  DISSOCIATION
       i < 1   ->  ASSOCIATION


  THE MODIFIED COLLIGATIVE FORMULAE
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
        p0  -  p
      -------------  =  i . x2          delta Tb  =  i . Kb . m
           p0

      delta Tf  =  i . Kf . m           pi  =  i . C . R . T


  DEGREE OF DISSOCIATION AND ASSOCIATION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  DISSOCIATION - one molecule gives n particles:

                     i  -  1
        alpha   =  ------------          i  =  1 + ( n - 1 ) alpha
                     n  -  1

  ASSOCIATION - n molecules give one particle:

                        1  -  i
        alpha   =  -------------------
                     1  -  ( 1 / n )


  ILLUSTRATION 1 - NaCl IN WATER (dissociation)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
     NaCl  ->  Na+  +  Cl-        n = 2 ,  i = 2 when complete

     For a 1.00 molal solution of NaCl:

        delta Tf  =  i  x  Kf  x  m  =  2  x  1.86  x  1.00  =  3.72 K

        freezing point  =  273.15  -  3.72   =   269.43  K

     Without dissociation it would have been only 1.86 K. The salt gives
     DOUBLE the effect because it produces DOUBLE the particles.


  ILLUSTRATION 2 - BENZOIC ACID IN BENZENE (association)
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
     2 g of benzoic acid in 25 g of benzene gives delta Tf = 1.62 K,
     Kf(benzene) = 4.9 K kg/mol.

                       1000 x 4.9 x 2         9800
        M2(obs)  =  --------------------  =  --------  =  241.98 g/mol
                       1.62  x  25             40.5

        normal molar mass of C6H5COOH  =  122 g/mol

                    122
        i   =   ----------   =   0.504        ( i < 1 -> association )
                  241.98

        n = 2  (two molecules form one dimer)

                      1  -  0.504         0.496
        alpha   =  ----------------  =  ---------  =  0.992
                      1  -  0.5            0.500

     So 99.2 % of the benzoic acid exists as the DIMER, because benzene
     cannot solvate the -COOH group and two molecules hydrogen bond to
     each other instead.
```
