### Q1
Which of the following is an example of a solid dissolved in a solid?

(a) sugar in water &nbsp; (b) brass &nbsp; (c) soda water &nbsp; (d) camphor in nitrogen

**ANSWER: (b)** — brass is copper dissolved in zinc, an alloy, so it is a
solid solution of a solid in a solid.

---

### Q2
An amalgam is an example of a solution of

(a) solid in liquid &nbsp; (b) liquid in solid &nbsp; (c) gas in solid &nbsp; (d) liquid in liquid

**ANSWER: (b)** — an amalgam is mercury (a LIQUID) dissolved in a metal
such as sodium (a SOLID).

The molarity of a solution containing 5.85 g of NaCl in 500 mL of solution
is (NaCl = 58.5)

(a) 0.1 M &nbsp; (b) 0.2 M &nbsp; (c) 0.5 M &nbsp; (d) 1.0 M

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
                     5.85
   moles  =  ---------------  =  0.100 mol
                     58.5

                  0.100
   Molarity  =  ---------  =  0.200  M
                  0.500
```

### Q4
Which of the following concentration terms does NOT change with
temperature?

(a) molarity &nbsp; (b) normality &nbsp; (c) molality &nbsp; (d) volume percentage

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  ANYTHING BUILT ON VOLUME  ->  changes with temperature
       molarity , normality , volume %

  ANYTHING BUILT ON MASS OR MOLES  ->  does NOT change
       molality , mole fraction , mass % , ppm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** the exam only ever asks this one way round.
> **"Which is temperature independent?" -> MOLALITY (or mole fraction).**

### Q5
The mole fraction of the solute in a 1 molal aqueous solution is

(a) 0.0177 &nbsp; (b) 0.177 &nbsp; (c) 0.0344 &nbsp; (d) 1.0

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  1 molal  =  1 mol of solute in 1000 g of water

                        1000
   moles of water  =  --------  =  55.5
                         18

                  1                 1
   x2  =  ---------------   =   --------   =   0.0177
            1  +  55.5            56.5
```
> **SHORTCUT worth memorising:**
> ```
>                       m
>       x2   =   ----------------      for any AQUEOUS solution
>                  m   +   55.5
> ```

### Q6
The molality of a 1 M aqueous NaOH solution of density 1.04 g/mL is
(NaOH = 40)

(a) 0.5 m &nbsp; (b) 1.0 m &nbsp; (c) 1.04 m &nbsp; (d) 2.0 m

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  1 M  ->  1 mol NaOH (40 g) in 1 LITRE of SOLUTION

  mass of solution  =  1000 mL  x  1.04  =  1040 g

  mass of WATER     =  1040  -  40   =   1000 g   =   1 kg

                  1 mol
  molality  =  -----------  =  1.0  mol/kg
                  1 kg
```

### Q7
The normality of a 0.1 M H2SO4 solution is

(a) 0.05 N &nbsp; (b) 0.1 N &nbsp; (c) 0.2 N &nbsp; (d) 0.4 N

**ANSWER: (c)** — `N = M x n-factor = 0.1 x 2 = 0.2 N`
(H2SO4 has two replaceable H+ ions.)

### Q9
1 ppm of a solute in water is the same as

(a) 1 g per litre &nbsp; (b) 1 mg per litre &nbsp; (c) 1 microgram per litre &nbsp; (d) 1 kg per litre

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  1 ppm  =  1 g in 10^6 g of solution

         =  1 g in 1000 L of water          (1 L of water = 1 kg)

# PATTERN 3 — Henry's law

### Q11
The unit of Henry's constant K(H) is

(a) mol/L &nbsp; (b) no unit &nbsp; (c) unit of pressure &nbsp; (d) L atm/K mol

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   p  =  K(H)  x        and  x  has NO unit

   ==>  K(H) must carry the unit of PRESSURE  (bar, atm, Pa, mm Hg)
```

---

### Q12
Among He, N2, O2 and CO2, the gas with the HIGHEST solubility in water is

(a) He &nbsp; (b) N2 &nbsp; (c) O2 &nbsp; (d) CO2

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   K(H) at 293 K:   He 144.97 kbar > N2 76.48 > O2 34.86 > CO2 1.67

   SMALLEST K(H)  ->  HIGHEST solubility  ->  CO2
```
> **SHORTCUT:** `K(H)` and solubility move in OPPOSITE directions.
> **Big K(H) = the gas hates the water.**

---

### Q13
The solubility of a gas in a liquid, as the temperature increases,

(a) increases &nbsp; (b) decreases &nbsp; (c) stays the same &nbsp; (d) first rises then falls

**ANSWER: (b)** — dissolving a gas is exothermic, so heating drives the gas
back out. This is why bubbles appear before water boils and why warm soda
goes flat quickly.

---

### Q14
If Henry's constant for a gas is 1.0 x 10^5 bar, the mole fraction of the
gas dissolved in water under a partial pressure of 2 bar is

(a) 2 x 10^-5 &nbsp; (b) 2 x 10^5 &nbsp; (c) 5 x 10^4 &nbsp; (d) 2 x 10^-3

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
              p            2
      x  =  -------  =  ---------  =  2 x 10^-5
             K(H)        1 x 10^5
```

---

### Q15
Divers use tanks of air diluted with helium instead of ordinary air in
order to avoid

(a) anoxia &nbsp; (b) bends &nbsp; (c) osmosis &nbsp; (d) plasmolysis

**ANSWER: (b)** — helium is much less soluble in blood than nitrogen, so
fewer bubbles form when the diver surfaces.

# PATTERN 4 — Raoult's law and vapour pressure

### Q16
Two volatile liquids A and B have pure vapour pressures 100 mm and 60 mm.
The total vapour pressure of an EQUIMOLAR mixture is

(a) 160 mm &nbsp; (b) 80 mm &nbsp; (c) 40 mm &nbsp; (d) 100 mm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   x(A) = x(B) = 0.5

   p(total)  =  100 x 0.5  +  60 x 0.5   =   50 + 30   =   80  mm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** for an EQUIMOLAR mixture the total vapour pressure is
> simply the **AVERAGE** of the two pure vapour pressures.
> `(100 + 60)/2 = 80`. No working needed.

---

### Q17
For the mixture in Q16, the mole fraction of A in the VAPOUR phase is

(a) 0.500 &nbsp; (b) 0.625 &nbsp; (c) 0.375 &nbsp; (d) 0.750

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
              p(A)          50
   y(A)  =  ---------  =  ------  =  0.625
             p(total)       80
```
> **RULE:** the vapour is ALWAYS richer in the MORE VOLATILE component
> (the one with the higher `p0`). If your `y` for the more volatile liquid
> comes out SMALLER than its `x`, you have made a mistake.

---

### Q18
The vapour pressure of a pure solvent is 100 mm Hg. On adding a
non-volatile solute the mole fraction of the solute becomes 0.2. The
vapour pressure of the solution is

(a) 20 mm &nbsp; (b) 80 mm &nbsp; (c) 100 mm &nbsp; (d) 120 mm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   x(solvent)  =  1  -  0.2  =  0.8

   p  =  p0  x(solvent)  =  100  x  0.8   =   80  mm Hg
```

---

### Q19
Raoult's law becomes a special case of Henry's law when

(a) K(H) = p0 &nbsp; (b) K(H) = 1 &nbsp; (c) x = 1 &nbsp; (d) p = 0

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   RAOULT :  p = p0 . x            HENRY : p = K(H) . x

   The SAME equation. They coincide when   K(H)  =  p0.
```

# PATTERN 5 — Ideal and non-ideal solutions, azeotropes

### Q20
For an ideal solution,

(a) delta H(mix) = 0, delta V(mix) = 0 &nbsp; (b) delta H(mix) > 0
(c) delta V(mix) < 0 &nbsp; (d) it forms an azeotrope

**ANSWER: (a)** — an ideal solution obeys Raoult's law at all
concentrations, with no heat change and no volume change on mixing.

---

### Q21
Which of the following pairs forms an IDEAL solution?

(a) ethanol + water &nbsp; (b) chloroform + acetone
(c) benzene + toluene &nbsp; (d) nitric acid + water

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   IDEAL             benzene + toluene , n-hexane + n-heptane ,
                     bromoethane + chloroethane

   POSITIVE dev.     ethanol + water , acetone + CS2 , CCl4 + CHCl3

   NEGATIVE dev.     chloroform + acetone , HNO3 + water ,
                     phenol + aniline
```

---

### Q22
A mixture of chloroform and acetone shows

(a) ideal behaviour &nbsp; (b) positive deviation
(c) negative deviation &nbsp; (d) no vapour pressure

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   A NEW hydrogen bond forms between the H of chloroform and the O of
   acetone. The molecules are held together more strongly, so FEWER
   escape and the observed vapour pressure is LOWER than Raoult predicts.

        Cl                        CH3
         \                        /
    Cl -- C -- H  . . . . .  O = C
         /                        \
        Cl                        CH3
```
> **SHORTCUT for every deviation MCQ:**
> ```
>   Does mixing them create a NEW hydrogen bond?
>        YES  ->  molecules stick together  ->  NEGATIVE deviation
>        NO, it BREAKS existing hydrogen bonds
>             ->  molecules escape easier   ->  POSITIVE deviation
> ```

---

### Q23
A minimum boiling azeotrope is formed by a solution showing

(a) ideal behaviour &nbsp; (b) positive deviation
(c) negative deviation &nbsp; (d) zero vapour pressure

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   POSITIVE deviation -> abnormally HIGH vapour pressure
                      -> boils EASILY, at a LOW temperature
                      -> MINIMUM boiling azeotrope
                      -> ethanol (95 %) + water , 351.1 K

   NEGATIVE deviation -> abnormally LOW vapour pressure
                      -> boils with DIFFICULTY, at a HIGH temperature
                      -> MAXIMUM boiling azeotrope
                      -> HNO3 (68 %) + water , 393.5 K
```

---

### Q24
Absolutely pure (100 %) ethanol cannot be obtained by fractional
distillation of an ethanol-water mixture because

(a) ethanol decomposes &nbsp; (b) they form an azeotrope at 95 % ethanol
(c) ethanol is heavier than water &nbsp; (d) ethanol does not boil

**ANSWER: (b)** — the 95 % ethanol / 5 % water mixture is a minimum boiling
azeotrope; it boils as a single substance without change of composition.

# PATTERN 7 — Elevation of boiling point and depression of freezing point

### Q28
The elevation of boiling point of a 1 molal aqueous solution of a
non-electrolyte is (Kb for water = 0.52 K kg/mol)

(a) 0.52 K &nbsp; (b) 1.86 K &nbsp; (c) 0.052 K &nbsp; (d) 5.2 K

**ANSWER: (a)** — `delta Tb = Kb m = 0.52 x 1 = 0.52 K`
> **This is what Kb MEANS** — the elevation for a 1 molal solution.
> Same for Kf: a 1 molal aqueous solution freezes 1.86 K lower.

---

### Q29
The depression of freezing point of a 0.5 molal aqueous glucose solution
is (Kf for water = 1.86 K kg/mol)

(a) 0.93 K &nbsp; (b) 1.86 K &nbsp; (c) 3.72 K &nbsp; (d) 0.26 K

**ANSWER: (a)** — `delta Tf = 1.86 x 0.5 = 0.93 K`

---

### Q30
Which of the following 0.1 M aqueous solutions has the HIGHEST boiling
point?

(a) glucose &nbsp; (b) NaCl &nbsp; (c) CaCl2 &nbsp; (d) urea

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Colligative properties count PARTICLES, so count the ions:

      glucose  ->  1 particle        i = 1
      urea     ->  1 particle        i = 1
      NaCl     ->  2 particles       i = 2
      CaCl2    ->  3 particles       i = 3    <-- MOST particles

   MOST particles  ->  HIGHEST boiling point
```
> **SHORTCUT — this MCQ appears every single year in some form:**
> ```
>   HIGHEST boiling point   ->  the one with the MOST ions
>   LOWEST  freezing point  ->  the one with the MOST ions
>   HIGHEST osmotic pressure->  the one with the MOST ions
>   HIGHEST vapour pressure ->  the one with the FEWEST ions
>
>   Just count the ions in the formula. That's the whole question.
> ```

---

### Q31
Which of the following 0.1 m aqueous solutions has the LOWEST freezing
point?

(a) K2SO4 &nbsp; (b) NaCl &nbsp; (c) Al2(SO4)3 &nbsp; (d) glucose

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Al2(SO4)3  ->  2 Al3+  +  3 SO4 2-   =  5 particles   i = 5
   K2SO4      ->  2 K+    +  SO4 2-     =  3 particles   i = 3
   NaCl       ->  2 particles                            i = 2
   glucose    ->  1 particle                             i = 1

   MOST particles -> BIGGEST delta Tf -> LOWEST freezing point
```

The unit of the cryoscopic constant Kf is

### Q34
Ethylene glycol is added to the water in a car radiator because it

(a) lowers the freezing point and raises the boiling point
(b) lowers both &nbsp; (c) raises both &nbsp; (d) has no effect

**ANSWER: (a)** — as a non-volatile solute it depresses the freezing point

# PATTERN 8 — Osmotic pressure and osmosis

### Q35
The osmotic pressure of a 0.1 M solution of a non-electrolyte at 300 K is
(R = 0.0821 L atm / K mol)

(a) 0.246 atm &nbsp; (b) 2.46 atm &nbsp; (c) 24.6 atm &nbsp; (d) 246 atm

**ANSWER: (b)** — `pi = CRT = 0.1 x 0.0821 x 300 = 2.463 atm`

---

### Q36
Two solutions are isotonic when they have the same

(a) mass &nbsp; (b) volume &nbsp; (c) osmotic pressure &nbsp; (d) density

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   pi(1) = pi(2)     and since  pi = CRT ,  this means   C1 = C2

   Isotonic solutions therefore have the same MOLAR CONCENTRATION
   (of particles). 0.9 % w/v NaCl is isotonic with blood.
```

---

### Q37
The best method for determining the molar mass of a polymer or a protein is

(a) elevation of boiling point &nbsp; (b) depression of freezing point
(c) osmotic pressure &nbsp; (d) relative lowering of vapour pressure

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   1. Measured at ROOM temperature - no heating, so the protein is not
      destroyed.
   2. pi is LARGE and easy to measure even for a very dilute solution.
      Polymer solutions are always very dilute, so delta Tb and delta Tf
      would be far too small to measure.
```

---

### Q38
Reverse osmosis is used for

(a) making soda water &nbsp; (b) desalination of sea water
(c) preserving food &nbsp; (d) separating azeotropes

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Apply a pressure GREATER than the osmotic pressure of sea water
   (about 30 atm) across a CELLULOSE ACETATE membrane. Pure water is
   squeezed out and the salts are left behind.
```

---

### Q39
A cell placed in a HYPERTONIC solution will

(a) swell &nbsp; (b) shrink &nbsp; (c) burst &nbsp; (d) stay the same

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   HYPERtonic  ->  HIGHER osmotic pressure outside
               ->  water flows OUT of the cell
               ->  the cell SHRINKS  (plasmolysis)

   HYPOtonic   ->  water flows IN  ->  the cell SWELLS and may burst

   MEMORY:  "hyper" = high = pulls water OUT = shrivels
            (a mango in strong brine shrivels)
```

# PATTERN 9 — van't Hoff factor and abnormal molar mass

### Q40
The van't Hoff factor for K4[Fe(CN)6], assuming complete dissociation, is

(a) 2 &nbsp; (b) 3 &nbsp; (c) 4 &nbsp; (d) 5

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   K4[Fe(CN)6]   ->   4 K+   +   [Fe(CN)6] 4-

   4 cations  +  1 anion   =   5 particles     ->   i = 5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** `i` is just the number of ions you can count in the
> formula. NaCl 2, CaCl2 3, K2SO4 3, AlCl3 4, K4[Fe(CN)6] 5,
> Al2(SO4)3 5.

---

### Q41
For a solute that COMPLETELY dimerises in solution, the van't Hoff factor
is

(a) 2 &nbsp; (b) 1 &nbsp; (c) 0.5 &nbsp; (d) 0

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   2 molecules  ->  1 dimer

            particles present         1
   i  =  -----------------------  =  ----  =  0.5
            particles expected        2
```

### Q42
For a 0.1 m NaCl solution the van't Hoff factor is found to be 1.9. The
degree of dissociation of NaCl is

(a) 0.45 &nbsp; (b) 0.90 &nbsp; (c) 1.90 &nbsp; (d) 0.19

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   NaCl -> Na+ + Cl-        so   n = 2

                i  -  1        1.9  -  1        0.9
   alpha  =  ------------  =  -----------  =  -------  =  0.90
                n  -  1          2  -  1         1

   ->  90 % dissociated
```

### Q43
The observed molar mass of NaCl determined from the depression of freezing
point is about 29.25 instead of 58.5. This is because NaCl

(a) associates &nbsp; (b) dissociates into two ions
(c) is volatile &nbsp; (d) reacts with water

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   NaCl -> Na+ + Cl-        i = 2       so the colligative effect is
                                        DOUBLE what was expected.

                normal M              58.5
   i  =  -------------------    ->   -------  =  2       correct
               observed M             29.25

   MORE particles  ->  molar mass comes out TOO SMALL.
   FEWER particles ->  molar mass comes out TOO LARGE.
```

---

### Q44
Benzoic acid in benzene shows a molar mass of about 244 instead of 122
because it

(a) dissociates &nbsp; (b) forms a dimer &nbsp; (c) evaporates &nbsp; (d) decomposes

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Benzene is non-polar and cannot solvate the -COOH group, so two
   benzoic acid molecules hydrogen bond to each other and act as ONE
   particle (a DIMER). Half the number of particles -> half the
   colligative effect -> double the apparent molar mass.

              i  =  122 / 244  =  0.5     ( i < 1  ->  ASSOCIATION )
```

### Q45
Which of the following expressions is correct for the depression of
freezing point of an electrolyte solution?

(a) delta Tf = Kf m &nbsp; (b) delta Tf = i Kf m
(c) delta Tf = Kf / m &nbsp; (d) delta Tf = i m / Kf

**ANSWER: (b)** — for any solute that dissociates or associates, `i` is
placed in FRONT of every colligative formula.

### Q46
The freezing point of a 1 molal aqueous NaCl solution (complete
dissociation, Kf = 1.86) is

(a) -1.86 C &nbsp; (b) -3.72 C &nbsp; (c) +1.86 C &nbsp; (d) -0.93 C

**ANSWER: (b)**
