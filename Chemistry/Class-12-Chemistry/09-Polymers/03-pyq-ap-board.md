## Topic: Basic definitions

### Q A1
What are polymers? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A POLYMER is a very high molecular mass substance (a macromolecule)
  formed by joining together a large number of simple repeating
  structural units through covalent bonds.

  Example:  polythene, formed from ethene.
```

---

### Q A2
What is a monomer? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A MONOMER is the simple, small molecule from which a polymer is made.

  Examples:  ethene (monomer of polythene)
             vinyl chloride (monomer of PVC)
```

Define polymerisation.

**ANSWER:**

```
  POLYMERISATION is the process in which a large number of monomer
  molecules combine with one another to form a polymer.
```

### Q A6
Distinguish between a homopolymer and a copolymer with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HOMOPOLYMER - a polymer made from only ONE kind of monomer.
                Example: polythene (only ethene).

  COPOLYMER   - a polymer made from TWO OR MORE different monomers.
                Example: Buna-S (1,3-butadiene + styrene).
```

```
  NATURAL         -  starch, cellulose  (also natural rubber, proteins)

  SEMI-SYNTHETIC  -  cellulose acetate, cellulose nitrate
```

### Q A8
What are elastomers? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELASTOMERS are polymers in which the chains are held together by the
  WEAKEST intermolecular forces. The randomly coiled chains have a few
  cross-links, so the polymer can be stretched to several times its
  length and it returns to its original shape when released.

  Examples:  vulcanised rubber, Buna-S   (also Buna-N, neoprene)
```

---

### Q A9
What are fibres? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  FIBRES are polymers whose chains are held together by the STRONGEST
  intermolecular forces - hydrogen bonding and dipole-dipole attraction.
  The chains pack closely, giving high tensile strength, high melting
  point and a crystalline, thread-like solid.

  Examples:  nylon 6,6, terylene (dacron)   (also Orlon, silk)
```

---

### Q A10
What are thermoplastic polymers? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THERMOPLASTIC polymers are linear or slightly branched polymers with
  NO cross-links. They SOFTEN on heating and HARDEN on cooling, and so
  can be remoulded and reused again and again.

  Examples:  polythene, polystyrene   (also PVC, polypropene)
```

---

### Q A11
What are thermosetting polymers? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THERMOSETTING polymers are heavily CROSS-LINKED, three dimensional
  network polymers. On heating in a mould they set permanently into a
  hard, rigid, infusible mass and CANNOT be remoulded.

  Examples:  Bakelite, urea-formaldehyde resin
             (also melamine-formaldehyde resin)
```

---

### Q A12
What is addition polymerisation? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ADDITION POLYMERISATION is the repeated addition of UNSATURATED
  monomer molecules (containing a double or triple bond) to one another,
  with NO elimination of any small molecule.

  Example:  n CH2 = CH2  ---->  -[- CH2 - CH2 -]-
                                                  n
            ethene                polythene
```

---

### Q A13
What is condensation polymerisation? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CONDENSATION POLYMERISATION is the repeated condensation of two
  BI-FUNCTIONAL or TRI-FUNCTIONAL monomers, accompanied by the
  ELIMINATION of a small molecule such as water, ammonia or HCl.

  Example:  adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine  ---->  nylon 6,6 + H2O
```

### Q A25
What is vulcanisation of rubber?

**ANSWER:**
```
  VULCANISATION is the process of heating raw natural rubber with
  SULPHUR, along with a suitable additive, at a temperature of
  373 K to 415 K.

  Sulphur forms CROSS-LINKS between the rubber chains at their reactive
  double-bond sites, making the rubber harder, stronger and elastic over
  a much wider temperature range.
```

---

### Q A26
Why is natural rubber elastic?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In natural rubber the double bonds are all in the CIS configuration.
  The cis kinks stop the chains from packing closely, so the
  intermolecular forces are weak and the chains stay randomly coiled.

  On stretching, the coiled chains straighten out; on release they
  return to the coiled shape. Hence rubber is elastic.
```

### Q B1
Classify polymers on the basis of their SOURCE, with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  On the basis of source, polymers are of THREE types.

  1. NATURAL POLYMERS
     Polymers that occur in nature, in plants and animals.
     Examples: starch, cellulose, natural rubber, proteins,
               nucleic acids, silk, wool.

  2. SEMI-SYNTHETIC POLYMERS
     Natural polymers that have been chemically modified in the
     laboratory.
     Examples: cellulose acetate (rayon), cellulose nitrate
               (gun cotton), vulcanised rubber.

  3. SYNTHETIC POLYMERS
     Polymers prepared entirely in the laboratory from simple
     chemicals.
     Examples: polythene, PVC, nylon 6,6, Buna-S, Bakelite, Teflon.
```

### Q B2
Classify polymers on the basis of STRUCTURE. Explain how the structure
affects the properties.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. LINEAR POLYMERS
     Monomers joined in a long straight chain with no branches.

        ---O---O---O---O---O---O---O---O---
        ---O---O---O---O---O---O---O---O---

     The chains pack CLOSELY, so the intermolecular forces are strong.
     PROPERTIES: high density, high tensile strength, high melting point.
     EXAMPLES: high density polythene (HDPE), PVC, nylon 6,6, polyesters.

  2. BRANCHED CHAIN POLYMERS
     A long main chain carrying side branches.

                  O           O
                  |           |
        ---O---O---O---O---O---O---O---
                  |
                  O

     The branches keep the chains apart, so packing is LOOSE and the
     intermolecular forces are weak.
     PROPERTIES: low density, low tensile strength, low melting point,
     flexible.
     EXAMPLES: low density polythene (LDPE), amylopectin, glycogen.

  3. CROSS-LINKED (NETWORK) POLYMERS
     Chains joined to one another by strong COVALENT bridges, forming a
     three dimensional network.

        ---O---O---O---O---O---
               |         |
        ---O---O---O---O---O---
               |         |
        ---O---O---O---O---O---

     PROPERTIES: hard, rigid, brittle; do not melt or dissolve; cannot
     be remoulded.
     EXAMPLES: Bakelite, melamine-formaldehyde, urea-formaldehyde,
     vulcanised rubber.
```

### Q B4
Classify polymers on the basis of MOLECULAR FORCES.

**ANSWER:**
```
  On the basis of the magnitude of the intermolecular forces between the
  chains, polymers are of FOUR types.

  1. ELASTOMERS
     WEAKEST intermolecular forces (weak van der Waals). Chains are
     randomly coiled with a few cross-links, so the polymer stretches
     to several times its length and returns on release.
     Examples: vulcanised rubber, Buna-S, Buna-N, neoprene.

  2. FIBRES
     STRONGEST intermolecular forces - hydrogen bonding and dipole-
     dipole attraction. Chains are close packed and crystalline,
     giving high tensile strength and a high melting point, and can be
     drawn into long threads.
     Examples: nylon 6, nylon 6,6, terylene, Orlon, silk.

  3. THERMOPLASTIC POLYMERS
     INTERMEDIATE forces, linear or slightly branched chains with no
     cross-links. They soften on heating and harden on cooling, and so
     can be remoulded repeatedly.
     Examples: polythene, polystyrene, PVC, polypropene.

  4. THERMOSETTING POLYMERS
     Chains are joined by covalent CROSS-LINKS into a three
     dimensional network. On heating in a mould they set permanently
     and cannot be remoulded.
     Examples: Bakelite, urea-formaldehyde, melamine-formaldehyde.
```

### Q B6
Explain the free radical mechanism of addition polymerisation, taking
ethene as the example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Ethene polymerises to polythene in the presence of a free radical
  initiator such as BENZOYL PEROXIDE. The reaction has three steps.

  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1 - INITIATION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  The peroxide breaks at the weak O-O bond on heating:

        O               O                        O
        ||              ||                       ||
   C6H5-C-O - O - C-C6H5    --heat-->      2 C6H5-C-O *
                  ||
                  O

  This loses carbon dioxide to give a phenyl free radical:

        O
        ||
   C6H5-C-O *      ----->     C6H5 *   +   CO2

  The phenyl radical attacks the double bond of ethene, leaving a new
  free radical:

   C6H5 *  +  CH2 = CH2   ----->   C6H5 - CH2 - CH2 *

  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 2 - PROPAGATION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  The new radical adds to the next ethene molecule, and so on, the
  radical always sitting at the growing end of the chain:

   C6H5-CH2-CH2 *  +  CH2 = CH2  --->  C6H5-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2 *

              ... repeated many thousands of times ...

   ---->   C6H5-(CH2-CH2)n-CH2-CH2 *

  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 3 - TERMINATION
  ------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two growing radical chains combine; their odd electrons pair up and
  both chains stop growing:

   C6H5-(CH2-CH2)n-CH2-CH2 *  +  * CH2-CH2-(CH2-CH2)m-C6H5

        ---->  C6H5-(CH2-CH2)n-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2-(CH2-CH2)m-C6H5

                            POLYTHENE
```

### Q B7
How are low density polythene and high density polythene prepared?
How do they differ?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Both are made from the SAME monomer, ETHENE, but under different
  conditions, which gives them different STRUCTURES.

  LOW DENSITY POLYTHENE (LDPE)
     Ethene is heated at 350 - 570 K under a very high pressure of
     1000 - 2000 atmospheres in the presence of traces of dioxygen or
     a peroxide initiator - a FREE RADICAL mechanism.
     The chain transfers to itself, so the polymer is highly BRANCHED.
     It is of LOW DENSITY, chemically inert, tough but FLEXIBLE, and a
     poor conductor of electricity.
     USES: insulation of electric wires, squeeze bottles, toys,
           flexible pipes.

  HIGH DENSITY POLYTHENE (HDPE)
     Ethene is polymerised at 333 - 343 K under only 6 - 7 atmospheres
     in the presence of a ZIEGLER-NATTA catalyst
     (triethylaluminium + titanium tetrachloride).
     The chains are LINEAR and pack closely.
     It is of HIGH DENSITY, chemically inert, and much harder and
     tougher, with a higher melting point.
     USES: buckets, dustbins, bottles, pipes, house-ware articles.
```

### Q B10
How is Bakelite prepared? Give its uses.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Bakelite is a phenol-formaldehyde CONDENSATION polymer, prepared in
  two stages.

  STAGE 1
  Phenol reacts with formaldehyde in the presence of an acid or a base
  catalyst to give ortho- or para-hydroxymethylphenol:

        OH                          OH
        |                           |
      (   )  +  HCHO   ------->   (   ) - CH2OH
      (   )                       (   )

  STAGE 2
  This condenses with more phenol, losing water, to give a LINEAR
  polymer called NOVOLAC, which is used in paints:

        OH        OH        OH
        |         |         |
      (   )-CH2-(   )-CH2-(   )- ...          NOVOLAC
      (   )     (   )     (   )

  STAGE 3
  On heating NOVOLAC with EXCESS formaldehyde, cross-links form at the
  free ortho and para positions, giving a three dimensional network:

        OH        OH
        |         |
   -CH2-(   )-CH2-(   )-CH2-
        (   )     (   )
          |         |
         CH2       CH2
          |         |
   -CH2-(   )-CH2-(   )-CH2-
        (   )     (   )
          |         |
        OH        OH

                    BAKELITE   (cross-linked, THERMOSETTING)

  USES: combs, phonograph records, electrical switches, handles of
        cooking utensils.
```

### Q B12
What is vulcanisation of rubber? How does it improve the properties of
natural rubber?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  Vulcanisation is the process of heating raw natural rubber with
  SULPHUR and a suitable additive at a temperature of 373 K to 415 K.
  It was discovered by Charles Goodyear in 1839.

  WHAT HAPPENS
  Sulphur atoms attack the reactive sites next to the C=C double bonds
  of the rubber chains and form sulphur CROSS-LINKS (bridges) between
  neighbouring chains:

     ~~~/\~~~/\~~~/\~~~/\~~~
            |         |
            S         S            <-- sulphur cross-links
            |         |
     ~~~/\~~~/\~~~/\~~~/\~~~

  The chains can still uncoil a little, so the rubber stays elastic,
  but they can no longer slide apart, so the rubber becomes strong.

  IMPROVEMENTS
  1. Much higher tensile strength.
  2. Elasticity is controlled - it no longer deforms permanently.
  3. Useful over a much wider temperature range; it does not become
     soft and sticky in summer or brittle in winter.
  4. Greater hardness and much better resistance to abrasion.
  5. Better resistance to oxidation and to organic solvents.
  6. Absorbs much less water.

  AMOUNT OF SULPHUR
     about  5 % sulphur  ->  tyre rubber
     about 30 % sulphur  ->  battery case rubber (ebonite)
```

### Q B13
Describe the structure of natural rubber and explain its drawbacks.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STRUCTURE
  Natural rubber is obtained as latex from the rubber tree. It is a
  natural addition polymer of ISOPRENE (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) and is
  chemically CIS-1,4-POLYISOPRENE:

     -[- CH2 - C = CH - CH2 -]-
               |               n
               CH3

  All the double bonds are in the CIS configuration. The cis kinks
  prevent the chains from packing closely, so the intermolecular forces
  are weak and the chains remain randomly coiled. On stretching the
  coils straighten out and on release they spring back - hence rubber
  is elastic.

  (The TRANS isomer, gutta percha, packs closely and is hard and
  non-elastic.)

  DRAWBACKS OF RAW NATURAL RUBBER
  1. Useful only between 283 K and 335 K; it becomes soft and sticky
     above 335 K and hard and brittle below 283 K.
  2. Low tensile strength.
  3. Low resistance to abrasion - it wears out quickly.
  4. It absorbs a large amount of water.
  5. It is soluble in non-polar solvents such as petrol and benzene.
  6. It is easily attacked by oxidising agents, so it perishes.
```

### Q B15
In a polymerisation reaction, 30% of the molecules have a molecular mass

### Q B16
Define number average and weight average molecular mass. Why are the
two different?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A polymer sample contains chains of MANY different lengths, so it has
  no single molecular mass - only an average. There are two averages.

  NUMBER AVERAGE MOLECULAR MASS
  Every chain is counted equally:

                SUM ( Ni Mi )
       Mn  =  -----------------
                 SUM ( Ni )

  WEIGHT AVERAGE MOLECULAR MASS
  Each chain is weighted by its own mass, so heavy chains count more:

                SUM ( Ni Mi^2 )
       Mw  =  --------------------
                SUM ( Ni Mi )

  WHY THEY DIFFER
  Because the heavier chains carry more weight in Mw than in Mn,
  Mw is always greater than or equal to Mn. Their ratio Mw/Mn is the
  POLYDISPERSITY INDEX. It equals 1 only when every chain has exactly
  the same length, which is true for natural polymers but never for
  synthetic ones.
```

### Q C2
(a) Explain the free radical mechanism of the polymerisation of ethene.
(b) Distinguish between addition and condensation polymerisation.

```
      INITIATOR: benzoyl peroxide.

      INITIATION                                              (2 marks)
            O           O                     O

       C6H5-C-O - O - C-C6H5  --heat-->  2 C6H5-C-O *
                      ||
                      O
```

### Q C3
Write an account of natural rubber and synthetic rubbers.

**ANSWER — mark split**
```
  (a) NATURAL RUBBER                                           3 marks
      Obtained as latex from the rubber tree. It is a natural addition
      polymer of ISOPRENE (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene) and is chemically
      CIS-1,4-POLYISOPRENE:

           -[- CH2 - C = CH - CH2 -]-
                     |               n
                     CH3

      coiled and the intermolecular forces are weak. The coils
      straighten on stretching and spring back on release - hence the

  (b) DRAWBACKS                                                2 marks

      oxidising agents.

      controlling the elasticity, widening the useful temperature

                   mineral oils. Used in conveyor belts, gaskets, hoses.
      BUNA - S   - copolymer of 1,3-butadiene and styrene, sodium
                   catalyst. Tough and abrasion resistant. Used for
                   automobile tyres, floor tiles, footwear, cable
                   insulation.

                   Resists petrol, oil and organic solvents.
                   Used for oil seals and tank lining.
```

### Q C5

```
  (a) THE PROBLEM                                              3 marks
      Most synthetic polymers such as polythene, PVC and polystyrene
      are chemically inert and NON-BIODEGRADABLE, so:
      1. they remain in the soil for hundreds of years;
      2. they choke drains and sewers, causing water logging and

      thermoplastics by melting and remoulding; segregate waste and
```
