# Polymers — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The polymer named in the question changes.
The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — DEFINE POLYMER / MONOMER / POLYMERISATION           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are polymers?"  "Define polymerisation."
            "What is a monomer?"
  METHOD    1. Give the definition in one full sentence.
            2. ALWAYS add an example. The example carries half the mark.

            POLYMER  - a high molecular mass macromolecule formed by
                       joining a large number of simple repeating
                       structural units through covalent bonds.
                       Example: polythene.
            MONOMER  - the simple small molecule from which the polymer
                       is made. Example: ethene.
            POLYMERISATION - the process in which many monomer molecules
                       combine to form a polymer.
  TRAP      Writing the definition without an example. In a 2-mark
            question that is 1 mark thrown away.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — DEGREE OF POLYMERISATION (definition or numerical)   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the degree of polymerisation?"  or a number is
            given for the molecular mass of a polymer.
  METHOD    Definition: the NUMBER of monomer units present in one
                        polymer chain.

                        molecular mass of the POLYMER
            n   =   ------------------------------------
                        molecular mass of the MONOMER

            For a numerical:
              1. Identify the monomer from the polymer's name.
              2. Work out the monomer's molecular mass.
              3. Divide.
  TRAP      This works cleanly only for ADDITION polymers.
            Handy masses: ethene 28, propene 42, vinyl chloride 62.5,
            styrene 104, tetrafluoroethene 100, acrylonitrile 53,
            isoprene 68, chloroprene 88.5.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — HOMOPOLYMER vs COPOLYMER                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a copolymer?"  "Which of these is a copolymer?"
  METHOD    HOMOPOLYMER - ONE kind of monomer.  Example: polythene.
            COPOLYMER   - TWO OR MORE kinds.    Example: Buna-S.

            COPOLYMERISATION - polymerisation of a MIXTURE of more than
            one kind of monomer, so the chain contains units of each.
  TRAP      NEOPRENE is a HOMOpolymer (chloroprene alone), even though
            it is a synthetic rubber. Buna-S and Buna-N are copolymers.
            Nylon 6 is a HOMOpolymer; nylon 6,6 is a copolymer.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — CLASSIFY ON THE BASIS OF SOURCE                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Classify polymers based on source."  "Give an example of a
            semi-synthetic polymer."
  METHOD    THREE classes, each with TWO examples:
              NATURAL        - starch, cellulose, natural rubber,
                               proteins, nucleic acids
              SEMI-SYNTHETIC - cellulose acetate (rayon), cellulose
                               nitrate, vulcanised rubber
              SYNTHETIC      - polythene, PVC, nylon 6,6, Bakelite,
                               Buna-S
  TRAP      RAYON is SEMI-SYNTHETIC, not natural - it is made from
            cellulose acetate. Vulcanised rubber is also semi-synthetic,
            NOT natural, because sulphur has been added.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — CLASSIFY ON THE BASIS OF STRUCTURE                  4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Classify polymers based on structure."  "Why is HDPE denser
            than LDPE?"
  METHOD    THREE classes. DRAW EACH ONE - the diagram earns marks.

            LINEAR        ---O---O---O---O---O---
                          ---O---O---O---O---O---
                          close packing -> high density, high tensile
                          strength, high m.p.   HDPE, PVC, nylon 6,6

            BRANCHED             O
                                 |
                          ---O---O---O---O---
                                 |
                                 O
                          loose packing -> low density, low tensile
                          strength, low m.p.    LDPE, amylopectin,
                                                glycogen

            CROSS-LINKED  ---O---O---O---O---
                                 |      |
                          ---O---O---O---O---
                          covalent bridges -> hard, rigid, brittle,
                          cannot be remoulded.  Bakelite, melamine-
                          formaldehyde, vulcanised rubber
  TRAP      You must SAY the link between structure and property
            ("branches keep the chains apart, so the forces are weak").
            Just listing the three names gets half marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — ADDITION vs CONDENSATION POLYMERISATION             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between addition and condensation
            polymerisation."  "Classify the following as addition or
            condensation."
  METHOD    Draw a TWO-COLUMN table. Give at least SIX points:
              1. monomer unsaturated (C=C)  vs  bi/tri-functional
              2. monomers ADD                vs  monomers CONDENSE
              3. NOTHING eliminated          vs  SMALL MOLECULE
                                                 eliminated (H2O, NH3,
                                                 HCl, CH3OH)
              4. mass = exact multiple       vs  not an exact multiple
              5. free radical / ionic chain  vs  functional group
                 carriers                        reactions, in steps
              6. examples: polythene, PVC,   vs  nylon 6,6, terylene,
                 Teflon, Buna-S                  Bakelite, glyptal
  TRAP      One-second test for classification questions:
            DOES THE MONOMER HAVE A C=C ?   yes -> addition
            Has -OH / -COOH / -NH2 instead? -> condensation
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — CLASSIFY ON THE BASIS OF MOLECULAR FORCES           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Classify polymers based on the magnitude of intermolecular
            forces."  "What are elastomers / fibres?"
  METHOD    FOUR classes. For each give FORCE + PROPERTY + 2 EXAMPLES:

            ELASTOMER      weakest van der Waals; coiled chains with a
                           few cross-links; highly elastic.
                           vulcanised rubber, Buna-S, Buna-N, neoprene

            FIBRE          strongest - H-bonding / dipole-dipole;
                           close packed, crystalline, high tensile
                           strength and m.p.
                           nylon 6, nylon 6,6, terylene, Orlon, silk

            THERMOPLASTIC  intermediate; linear/slightly branched, no
                           cross-links; soften on heating, remouldable.
                           polythene, polystyrene, PVC, polypropene

            THERMOSETTING  covalent cross-links, 3-D network; set
                           permanently, cannot be remoulded.
                           Bakelite, urea- and melamine-formaldehyde
  TRAP      The order of forces is ELASTOMER (weakest) < thermoplastic
            < FIBRE (strongest). Do not say a thermosetting polymer has
            "the strongest intermolecular force" - it is held by
            COVALENT CROSS-LINKS, which is a different thing.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — THERMOPLASTIC vs THERMOSETTING                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between thermoplastic and thermosetting
            polymers."  "Why is Bakelite used for switches?"
  METHOD    Two-column table, at least SIX points:
              linear/branched      vs  cross-linked 3-D network
              no cross-links       vs  covalent cross-links
              SOFTEN on heating    vs  SET PERMANENTLY on heating
              remoulded many times vs  moulded only ONCE
              recyclable           vs  not recyclable, they char
              soft, less brittle   vs  hard, rigid, brittle
              polythene, PVC       vs  Bakelite, urea-formaldehyde
  TRAP      Do NOT say thermosetting polymers melt on heating. Heat is
            what CREATES the cross-links; after that more heat only
            burns them.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — FREE RADICAL MECHANISM OF ADDITION POLYMERISATION   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the mechanism of addition polymerisation."
            "How is polythene formed from ethene? Give the mechanism."
  METHOD    Name the initiator: BENZOYL PEROXIDE.
            Write THREE HEADINGS IN CAPITALS.

            INITIATION
              (C6H5COO)2  --heat-->  2 C6H5COO *
              C6H5COO *   ---->      C6H5 *  +  CO2
              C6H5 * + CH2=CH2 ----> C6H5-CH2-CH2 *

            PROPAGATION
              C6H5-CH2-CH2 * + CH2=CH2 -> C6H5-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH2 *
                       ... repeated n times ...
              ----> C6H5-(CH2-CH2)n-CH2-CH2 *

            TERMINATION
              two growing radicals combine:
              C6H5-(CH2-CH2)n-CH2-CH2 * + * CH2-CH2-(CH2-CH2)m-C6H5
              ----> one dead polythene chain
  TRAP      Losing the marks for the HEADINGS. Write the words
            INITIATION, PROPAGATION and TERMINATION explicitly.
            Also: mark every free radical with a star or a dot.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — NAME THE MONOMER(S) OF A GIVEN POLYMER             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the monomers of ..."  "... is obtained from which
            monomer?"
  METHOD    Use the naming rules first, memory second:
              "poly + X"          ->  monomer is X
              two chemical names  ->  those two ARE the monomers
              "Buna-S"            ->  butadiene + styrene
              "Buna-N"            ->  butadiene + acrylonitrile
              "nylon 6,6"         ->  adipic acid + hexamethylenediamine
              "nylon 6"           ->  caprolactam
              "TERylene"          ->  ethylene glycol + TEREphthalic acid
              "GLYptal"           ->  ethylene GLYcol + phthalic acid
              "neoprene"          ->  chloroprene
              natural rubber      ->  isoprene
  TRAP      Write the STRUCTURE as well as the name where you can -
            it is often worth the extra mark, and it takes 5 seconds.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — PREPARATION AND USES OF A NAMED POLYMER            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is nylon 6,6 prepared? Give its uses."
  METHOD    Answer in FOUR fixed parts, always in this order:
              1. NAME THE MONOMERS (with structures)
              2. WRITE THE EQUATION with the repeating unit in
                 square brackets and the subscript n
              3. GIVE THE CONDITIONS (temperature, pressure, catalyst)
              4. GIVE TWO OR THREE USES
  TRAP      Forgetting the "+ n H2O" on the right hand side of a
            condensation equation. It is a marked item.
            Also forgetting the subscript n on the repeating unit.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — LDPE vs HDPE                                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How are low density and high density polythene prepared?"
            "Why is HDPE harder than LDPE?"
  METHOD    Say clearly: SAME MONOMER (ethene), DIFFERENT CONDITIONS,
            DIFFERENT STRUCTURE.

            LDPE   350-570 K, 1000-2000 atm, traces of O2 / peroxide,
                   free radical -> BRANCHED -> low density, flexible.
                   Uses: electric wire insulation, squeeze bottles,
                   toys, flexible pipes.

            HDPE   333-343 K, 6-7 atm, ZIEGLER-NATTA catalyst
                   (Al(C2H5)3 + TiCl4) -> LINEAR -> high density,
                   hard and tough.
                   Uses: buckets, dustbins, bottles, pipes.
  TRAP      Students say the monomers are different. They are the same.
            The difference is the CATALYST and hence the STRUCTURE.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — BAKELITE AND NOVOLAC                              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is Bakelite prepared?"  "What is Novolac?"
  METHOD    STAGE 1  phenol + HCHO (acid or base) ->
                     ortho- / para-hydroxymethylphenol
            STAGE 2  it condenses with more phenol ->
                     NOVOLAC, a LINEAR resin -> used in PAINTS
            STAGE 3  heat Novolac with EXCESS formaldehyde ->
                     cross-links at the free ortho and para positions
                     -> BAKELITE, a CROSS-LINKED THERMOSETTING resin
            USES of Bakelite: electrical switches, combs, phonograph
                     records, handles of cooking utensils.
  TRAP      Novolac and Bakelite have the SAME monomers. The examiner
            wants you to say that Novolac is LINEAR and Bakelite is
            CROSS-LINKED, and that heating with excess formaldehyde is
            what converts one into the other.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — COPOLYMERISATION AND THE BUNA RUBBERS              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is copolymerisation? Give examples."
  METHOD    1. Define copolymerisation.
            2. Show the difference:
                 homopolymer  -A-A-A-A-A-
                 copolymer    -A-B-A-A-B-B-
            3. BUNA-S : 1,3-butadiene + styrene, sodium catalyst.
                        Uses: automobile tyres, floor tiles, footwear,
                        cable insulation.
            4. BUNA-N : 1,3-butadiene + acrylonitrile.
                        Resists petrol, oil and organic solvents.
                        Uses: oil seals, tank lining.
            Write the repeating unit of at least one of them.
  TRAP      Do not offer neoprene as your example of a copolymer.
            It is a homopolymer.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — STRUCTURE OF NATURAL RUBBER / WHY IT IS ELASTIC   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is natural rubber elastic?"  "What is the structure of
            natural rubber?"
  METHOD    1. Monomer: ISOPRENE (2-methyl-1,3-butadiene).
            2. Structure: CIS-1,4-POLYISOPRENE.

                 -[- CH2 - C = CH - CH2 -]-
                           |               n
                           CH3

            3. The CIS double bonds prevent close packing.
            4. So the intermolecular forces are weak and the chains
               stay randomly coiled.
            5. Stretching straightens the coils; releasing lets them
               spring back. Hence elasticity.
            6. Bonus line: the TRANS isomer, gutta percha, packs
               closely and is hard and non-elastic.
  TRAP      The word CIS must appear. Without it the answer is
            incomplete no matter how long it is.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — DRAWBACKS OF NATURAL RUBBER                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is raw natural rubber not used as such?"
  METHOD    Give at least FOUR:
              1. useful only from 283 K to 335 K - soft and sticky
                 above, hard and brittle below
              2. low tensile strength
              3. low abrasion resistance
              4. absorbs a large amount of water
              5. soluble in non-polar solvents (petrol, benzene)
              6. attacked by oxidising agents
  TRAP      Quote the TEMPERATURES. "It is affected by temperature" on
            its own is a weak answer.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — VULCANISATION                                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is vulcanisation?"  "How does vulcanisation improve
            rubber?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION - heating raw natural rubber with SULPHUR and
               an additive at 373-415 K.
            2. WHAT HAPPENS - sulphur forms CROSS-LINKS between the
               chains at the reactive double-bond sites. Draw it:

                    ~~~/\~~~/\~~~/\~~~
                           |      |
                           S      S
                           |      |
                    ~~~/\~~~/\~~~/\~~~

            3. IMPROVEMENTS - higher tensile strength, controlled
               elasticity, wider useful temperature range, harder,
               better resistance to oxidation, abrasion and solvents,
               less water absorption.
            4. AMOUNTS - about 5% sulphur for tyres, about 30% for
               battery cases.
  TRAP      Three keywords must appear: SULPHUR, 373-415 K,
            CROSS-LINKS. Missing any one costs a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — SYNTHETIC RUBBERS AND THEIR SPECIAL USES           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write about synthetic rubbers."  "Which rubber is used for
            oil seals and why?"
  METHOD    For each: MONOMER(S) -> HOMO or CO -> SPECIAL PROPERTY -> USE

            NEOPRENE  chloroprene; homopolymer; resists VEGETABLE and
                      MINERAL oils; conveyor belts, gaskets, hoses.
            BUNA-N    butadiene + acrylonitrile; copolymer; resists
                      PETROL and LUBRICATING OIL; oil seals, tank lining.
            BUNA-S    butadiene + styrene; copolymer; tough, abrasion
                      resistant; automobile tyres, floor tiles,
                      footwear, cable insulation.
  TRAP      Buna-N and neoprene are both "oil resistant" - the examiner
            distinguishes them by the USE. Oil SEALS and TANK LINING ->
            Buna-N. Conveyor belts, gaskets, hoses -> neoprene.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — BIODEGRADABLE POLYMERS                            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are biodegradable polymers? Give examples."
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION - polymers broken down by micro-organisms in
               the environment into simple harmless substances, so they
               do not accumulate as waste.
            2. PHBV - condensation copolymer of 3-hydroxybutanoic acid
               and 3-hydroxypentanoic acid. Uses: speciality packaging,
               orthopaedic devices, CONTROLLED RELEASE OF DRUGS.
            3. NYLON-2-NYLON-6 - alternating polyamide copolymer of
               GLYCINE and AMINO CAPROIC ACID. Uses: biodegradable
               plastics, sutures.
  TRAP      Do not confuse nylon-2-nylon-6 (biodegradable, from glycine)
            with nylon 6 (caprolactam) or nylon 6,6 (adipic acid).
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — POLYMER POLLUTION AND ITS REMEDIES                 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why are plastics a pollution problem? Suggest remedies."
  METHOD    PROBLEM (any four):
              non-biodegradable, survives for centuries; chokes drains
              and sewers; animals swallow bags and die; burning gives
              toxic dioxins and HCl; huge solid waste mountains.
            REMEDIES:
              reduce, reuse, recycle thermoplastics, replace with
              biodegradable polymers such as PHBV and nylon-2-nylon-6,
              segregate and dispose of waste scientifically instead of
              open burning.
  TRAP      "Recycle" only works for THERMOPLASTICS. Say so - it shows
            you understand the chapter and not just the slogan.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — Mn , Mw AND PDI (numerical)                        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Percentages or counts of molecules with different molecular
            masses are given.
  METHOD    1. If percentages are given, take a sample of 100 molecules
               so that the percentages ARE the values of Ni.
            2. Build a four-column table: Ni , Mi , Ni Mi , Ni Mi^2 .
            3. Mn  = SUM(Ni Mi)   / SUM(Ni)
            4. Mw  = SUM(Ni Mi^2) / SUM(Ni Mi)
            5. PDI = Mw / Mn
  TRAP      Mn divides by the plain COUNT.
            Mw divides by SUM(Ni Mi), a MASS - not by the count.
            Final check: Mw must be BIGGER than Mn, and PDI >= 1.
            If not, you have made an arithmetic slip.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — DEFINE Mn / Mw / PDI (theory)                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define the polydispersity index."  "Why does a polymer have
            two average molecular masses?"
  METHOD    A polymer sample contains chains of MANY DIFFERENT LENGTHS,
            so it has no single molecular mass, only averages.
              Mn counts every chain equally.
              Mw gives the heavier chains a larger say.
              PDI = Mw / Mn.
            PDI = 1 for natural polymers (monodisperse);
            PDI > 1 for all synthetic polymers (polydisperse).
  TRAP      PDI can never be less than 1. If a question offers 0.8 as
            an option, it is wrong by definition.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — WHICH POLYMER FOR WHICH ARTICLE                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Name the polymer used in ..."  or a matching question.
  METHOD    Memorise the short list of "signature uses":
              non-stick cookware        Teflon
              electrical switches       Bakelite
              unbreakable crockery      melamine-formaldehyde
              laminated sheets, glues   urea-formaldehyde
              paints and lacquers       glyptal, Novolac
              automobile tyres          Buna-S (and vulcanised rubber)
              oil seals, tank lining    Buna-N
              conveyor belts, gaskets   neoprene
              tyre cords, ropes         nylon 6
              brush bristles, fabrics   nylon 6,6
              safety helmets, blends    terylene / dacron
              substitute for wool       polyacrylonitrile (Orlon)
              buckets, dustbins         HDPE
              squeeze bottles, wire     LDPE
              insulation
              raincoats, water pipes    PVC
              controlled drug release   PHBV
  TRAP      Teflon and PVC are both "plastic pipes" in daily life, but
            in the exam Teflon means NON-STICK and PVC means
            RAINCOATS / WATER PIPES.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — CLASSIFY THE FOLLOWING POLYMERS                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A list of 4-6 polymers with "classify as elastomer / fibre /
            thermoplastic / thermosetting" or "addition / condensation".
  METHOD    Go one at a time and use the decoders:
              rubber-sounding name (Buna, neoprene, vulcanised)
                                                       -> ELASTOMER
              nylon / terylene / Orlon / silk          -> FIBRE
              "poly-" something                        -> THERMOPLASTIC
              Bakelite or a "-formaldehyde" resin      -> THERMOSETTING

              monomer has C=C                          -> ADDITION
              monomer has -OH / -COOH / -NH2           -> CONDENSATION
  TRAP      Answer EVERY item in the list even if unsure. Each item
            carries its own mark and there is no negative marking on
            the board paper.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — DRAW THE REPEATING UNIT                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the structure of ..."  "Give the repeating unit of ..."
  METHOD    1. Write the monomer.
            2. Open the double bond (addition) or join the functional
               groups and drop the small molecule (condensation).
            3. Put SQUARE BRACKETS around the repeating unit.
            4. Put the SUBSCRIPT n outside the bracket.

               polythene   -[- CH2 - CH2 -]-n
               PVC         -[- CH2 - CH(Cl) -]-n
               nylon 6,6   -[- CO-(CH2)4-CO-NH-(CH2)6-NH -]-n
               terylene    -[- O-CH2-CH2-O-CO-C6H4-CO -]-n
  TRAP      NO BRACKETS AND NO n = NOT A POLYMER. This is the single
            most common silly mistake in the whole chapter.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — GIVE REASONS / ASSERTION-STYLE                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is ...?"  "Explain the reason for ..."
  METHOD    The reason is always ONE of these five ideas:
              1. STRUCTURE  - linear packs closely, branched does not,
                              cross-linked cannot move.
              2. FORCES     - weak forces -> elastic; strong forces ->
                              strong fibre.
              3. CIS/TRANS  - cis rubber is elastic, trans is hard.
              4. CROSS-LINKS- they make a polymer hard, rigid and
                              non-remouldable.
              5. FUNCTIONAL GROUP - ester and amide links can be
                              hydrolysed, so those polymers are
                              biodegradable.
            Pick the right one and write TWO sentences.
  TRAP      Answer the "why", not the "what". If asked why rubber is
            elastic, do not just describe rubber.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — TRUE / FALSE AND ODD-ONE-OUT                       objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which statement is incorrect?"  "Which does not belong?"
  METHOD    Test each option against these five facts:
              Bakelite is THERMOSETTING (not thermoplastic).
              Neoprene is a HOMOpolymer (not a copolymer).
              Nylon 6,6 is a POLYAMIDE; terylene is a POLYESTER.
              LDPE is BRANCHED; HDPE is LINEAR.
              Rayon is SEMI-SYNTHETIC (not natural).
            Those five statements are the ones examiners flip.
```
