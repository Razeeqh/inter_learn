## Topic: Carbohydrates — definitions and classification

### Q A1
Define carbohydrates. How are they classified?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
     Carbohydrates are optically active polyhydroxy ALDEHYDES or
     polyhydroxy KETONES, or compounds which give these on hydrolysis.

  CLASSIFICATION (on hydrolysis)
     1. MONOSACCHARIDES   - cannot be hydrolysed further.   e.g. glucose
     2. OLIGOSACCHARIDES  - give 2 to 10 units.             e.g. sucrose
     3. POLYSACCHARIDES   - give a large number of units.   e.g. starch
```

---

### Q A2
What are reducing sugars? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Carbohydrates that REDUCE Tollens' reagent (giving a silver mirror)
  and Fehling's solution (giving a red precipitate of Cu2O) are called
  REDUCING SUGARS. They have a free aldehyde or ketone group
  (a free anomeric carbon).

  Examples :  GLUCOSE , MALTOSE   (also fructose, lactose, galactose)
```

---

### Q A3
What are non-reducing sugars? Give an example and the reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Sugars that do NOT reduce Tollens' or Fehling's reagent are
  NON-REDUCING SUGARS.

  Example : SUCROSE

  Reason  : in sucrose the glycosidic linkage uses the anomeric carbon
            of BOTH units - C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose - so neither
            ring can open to give a free -CHO or >C=O group.
```

---

### Q A4
Define (a) aldoses and (b) ketoses, with one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ALDOSE  -  a monosaccharide containing an ALDEHYDE ( -CHO ) group.
             Example : GLUCOSE  (an aldohexose)

  KETOSE  -  a monosaccharide containing a KETO ( >C=O ) group.
             Example : FRUCTOSE (a ketohexose)
```

---

### Q A5
What is a glycosidic linkage?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The oxygen bridge that joins two monosaccharide units in a di- or
  polysaccharide, formed by the loss of a water molecule between the
  -OH groups of the two units, is called a GLYCOSIDIC LINKAGE.

       Sugar1 - OH  +  HO - Sugar2  --->  Sugar1 - O - Sugar2  +  H2O

  Example : in maltose it is an alpha-1,4 glycosidic linkage.
```

---

### Q A6
What is mutarotation?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MUTAROTATION is the spontaneous change in the specific optical rotation
  of a freshly prepared solution of a sugar until it reaches a constant
  equilibrium value.

  For glucose:
       pure alpha form  +111    ---> falls
       pure beta  form  +19.2   ---> rises      both settle at + 52.5

  It happens because the alpha and beta anomers interconvert through the
  small amount of OPEN CHAIN form present in solution.
```

---

### Q A7
What is invert sugar? Why is it so called?

**ANSWER:**
```
  When sucrose is hydrolysed (by dilute acid or by the enzyme invertase)
  it gives an equimolar mixture of glucose and fructose. This mixture is
  called INVERT SUGAR.

  WHY "INVERT" :
       sucrose             [a] = + 66.5   (dextrorotatory)
       glucose + fructose  [a] = (+52.5 - 92.4)/2 = about - 20
                                          (laevorotatory)

  The SIGN of the optical rotation is INVERTED, from (+) to (-).
  This is called the inversion of cane sugar.
```

---

### Q A8
Why is fructose a reducing sugar even though it is a ketone?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Tollens' and Fehling's reagents are ALKALINE.
  In an alkaline medium fructose isomerises through an ENEDIOL
  intermediate into GLUCOSE (and mannose), which contain a free -CHO
  group. That -CHO group then reduces the reagent.

  Hence fructose, though a ketose, is a reducing sugar.
```

---

### Q A9
What are anomers? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANOMERS are two cyclic forms of a sugar that differ ONLY in the
  configuration at the ANOMERIC carbon (the carbon that was the
  carbonyl carbon before the ring closed).

  Example : alpha-D-glucose  ( -OH at C1 points DOWN )
            beta -D-glucose  ( -OH at C1 points UP )
```

---

### Q A10
Name the two components of starch and give one difference between them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Starch = AMYLOSE (15-20%) + AMYLOPECTIN (80-85%)

  DIFFERENCE:
     AMYLOSE     - a LINEAR (unbranched) chain, only alpha-1,4 linkages,
                   soluble in hot water
     AMYLOPECTIN - a BRANCHED chain, alpha-1,4 in the chain plus
                   alpha-1,6 at the branch points, insoluble in water
```

---

### Q A11
Why can humans digest starch but not cellulose?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Starch has ALPHA-1,4 glycosidic linkages and cellulose has BETA-1,4
  glycosidic linkages.

  Human digestive enzymes (amylase) can hydrolyse alpha linkages but
  cannot hydrolyse beta linkages, because the enzyme's active site does
  not fit the beta linkage.

  So cellulose passes through us undigested, as dietary fibre.
```

### Q A13
What is a zwitter ion? Give the zwitter ion form of glycine.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A ZWITTER ION (dipolar ion) is a species carrying a POSITIVE charge and
  a NEGATIVE charge on different atoms of the SAME molecule, so that the
  molecule is overall electrically neutral.

  In an amino acid the -COOH gives its proton to the -NH2 group:

           R                              R
           |                              |
    H2N -- CH -- COOH   ---->      H3N -- CH -- COO
                                   (+)           (-)

  For GLYCINE :          H3N -- CH2 -- COO
                         (+)            (-)
```

---

### Q A14
Define the isoelectric point of an amino acid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The ISOELECTRIC POINT (pI) is the pH at which an amino acid exists
  entirely as the ZWITTER ION, its net charge is ZERO, and it does NOT
  migrate towards either electrode in an electric field.

  At the isoelectric point the solubility of the amino acid is MINIMUM.
  For glycine, pI = 6.0.
```

### Q A15
What is a peptide bond? Show its formation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The -CO-NH- linkage formed between the -COOH group of one amino acid
  and the -NH2 group of another, with the elimination of a molecule of
  water, is called a PEPTIDE BOND (an amide linkage).

       R1                          R2
        |                           |
  H2N - CH - COOH  +  H2N -------- CH - COOH
                             |
                             |  - H2O
                             v
       R1       O   H    R2
        |       ||  |     |
  H2N - CH - C ----- N -- CH - COOH        (a dipeptide)
              \_______/
              peptide bond
```

**ANSWER:**

  DENATURATION is the loss of the biological activity of a protein
  caused by the destruction of its SECONDARY and TERTIARY structures,
  while the PRIMARY structure (the sequence of amino acids) remains

## Topic: Nucleic acids

### Q A21
What is the difference between a nucleoside and a nucleotide?

**ANSWER:**
```
  NUCLEOSIDE  =  pentose SUGAR  +  nitrogenous BASE

  NUCLEOTIDE  =  pentose SUGAR  +  nitrogenous BASE  +  PHOSPHATE
              =  nucleoside + phosphoric acid

  So a nucleotide is a phosphorylated nucleoside.
  Example : adenosine is a nucleoside ; adenosine monophosphate (AMP)
            is the corresponding nucleotide.
```

### Q B1
Classify carbohydrates with suitable examples. *(a guaranteed 4 marks)*

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
     Carbohydrates are optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones,
     or compounds that give these on hydrolysis.

  1.  ON THE BASIS OF HYDROLYSIS

      (a) MONOSACCHARIDES  - cannot be hydrolysed into simpler sugars

      NON-SUGARS - tasteless, amorphous, insoluble  : starch, cellulose

  3.  ON THE BASIS OF REDUCING PROPERTY
      REDUCING     - all monosaccharides, maltose, lactose
      NON-REDUCING - sucrose, starch, cellulose

  4.  ON THE BASIS OF THE FUNCTIONAL GROUP
      ALDOSE - contains -CHO  : glucose  (an aldohexose)
      KETOSE - contains >C=O  : fructose (a ketohexose)
```

### Q B2
How is glucose prepared (a) from sucrose and (b) from starch?

**ANSWER:**
```
  (a) FROM SUCROSE  (laboratory method)

      Sucrose is boiled with dilute HCl or dilute H2SO4 in an
      ALCOHOLIC solution:

         C12H22O11  +  H2O  --(dil. HCl / H+)-->  C6H12O6 + C6H12O6
          sucrose                                 GLUCOSE   FRUCTOSE

      Glucose is insoluble in alcohol, so it crystallises out first
      and is filtered off; fructose stays in solution.

  (b) FROM STARCH  (commercial method)

      Starch (from maize or potato) is hydrolysed with dilute H2SO4 at
      393 K under a pressure of 2 to 3 atmospheres:

         (C6H10O5)n  +  n H2O  --(dil.H2SO4, 393 K, 2-3 atm)--> n C6H12O6
            starch                                                GLUCOSE
```

### Q B3
Give the reactions which establish the open chain structure of glucose.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  MOLECULAR FORMULA
      Elemental analysis and molecular mass give  C6H12O6.

  2.  HI, PROLONGED HEATING  ->  n-HEXANE
         C6H12O6  --(HI, red P, heat)-->  CH3(CH2)4CH3
      All six carbons are in a STRAIGHT, unbranched chain.

  3.  NH2OH  ->  OXIME ;  HCN  ->  CYANOHYDRIN (only one molecule adds)
      Glucose contains ONE CARBONYL group ( >C=O ).

  4.  Br2 WATER (mild oxidant)  ->  GLUCONIC ACID
         CHO-(CHOH)4-CH2OH  --(Br2/H2O)-->  COOH-(CHOH)4-CH2OH
      A six-carbon MONOcarboxylic acid, so the carbonyl group is an
      ALDEHYDE and it is at the END of the chain (C1).

  5.  ACETIC ANHYDRIDE  ->  GLUCOSE PENTAACETATE
      FIVE acetyl groups enter, so glucose has FIVE -OH groups; and
      because the product is stable they must be on FIVE DIFFERENT
      carbon atoms.

  6.  conc. HNO3 (strong oxidant)  ->  SACCHARIC ACID
         CHO-(CHOH)4-CH2OH  --(conc.HNO3)-->  COOH-(CHOH)4-COOH
      A DIcarboxylic acid, so there is a primary -CH2OH group at the
      other end of the chain (C6).

  CONCLUSION - the open chain structure of glucose is

           CHO - CHOH - CHOH - CHOH - CHOH - CH2OH
            C1     C2     C3     C4     C5     C6

      i.e.  D-(+)-glucose , an aldohexose.
```

### Q B4
Which facts about glucose could not be explained by its open chain
structure? How were they explained?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FACTS THE OPEN CHAIN COULD NOT EXPLAIN

  1.  Glucose does NOT give the SCHIFF'S test, though a free aldehyde
      should.
  2.  Glucose does NOT form the hydrogensulphite addition product with
      NaHSO3, though ordinary aldehydes do.
  3.  Glucose does NOT react with NH3 in the way an aldehyde does.
  4.  GLUCOSE PENTAACETATE does not react with hydroxylamine, showing
      that once the -OH groups are blocked there is no free -CHO group.
  5.  Glucose exists in TWO crystalline forms, alpha (m.p. 419 K,
      [a] = +111) and beta (m.p. 423 K, [a] = +19.2).
  6.  MUTAROTATION - the rotation of a fresh solution changes until it
      becomes constant at +52.5.

  THE EXPLANATION - THE CYCLIC STRUCTURE

  The -OH group on C5 attacks the -CHO group on C1 within the same
  molecule, forming a cyclic HEMIACETAL with a six-membered ring
  (a PYRANOSE ring). The aldehyde group is therefore NOT free, which
  explains points 1 to 4.

  Carbon C1 becomes a new chiral centre (the ANOMERIC carbon). Its -OH
  can lie below the ring (ALPHA) or above the ring (BETA), which gives
  the two crystalline forms and, through their interconversion via the
  open chain, mutarotation.
```

### Q B5
Explain the cyclic structure of glucose. What is mutarotation?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CYCLIC STRUCTURE

  In solution the -OH on C5 of glucose adds across the -CHO on C1,
  giving a cyclic HEMIACETAL with a six-membered PYRANOSE ring
  (5 carbons + 1 oxygen). C1 becomes a new chiral centre - the
  ANOMERIC CARBON - so two forms are possible.

              a l p h a - D - G L U C O P Y R A N O S E

                     6 CH2OH
                        |
                        C5 --------------- O
                       /                     \
                      /                       \
                 4  C4                         C1 ---- H
                    /  \                      /   \
                  OH    \                    /     OH   <-- alpha :
                         C3 -------------- C2          OH DOWN
                        /  \              /  \
                      OH    H            H    OH

  In the BETA form everything is the same except that the -OH on C1
  points UP.

  MUTAROTATION

  The change in the specific optical rotation of a freshly prepared
  sugar solution until it reaches a constant value is called
  MUTAROTATION.

      alpha-D-glucose  [a] = +111    ---> decreases
      beta -D-glucose  [a] = +19.2   ---> increases
      both reach the equilibrium value  [a] = + 52.5

  It occurs because the two anomers interconvert through the small
  amount of open chain form present in solution, until an equilibrium
  mixture of about 36% alpha and 64% beta is set up.
```

### Q B6
Describe the structure of sucrose. Why is it non-reducing? What is
inversion of cane sugar?

**ANSWER:**
```
  STRUCTURE OF SUCROSE   C12H22O11

     Sucrose is a disaccharide made of ONE alpha-D-GLUCOSE unit
     (pyranose) and ONE beta-D-FRUCTOSE unit (furanose), joined by a
     glycosidic linkage between C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose
     (an alpha-1,2 glycosidic linkage).

        [ alpha-D-GLUCOSE ] -- C1 -- O -- C2 -- [ beta-D-FRUCTOSE ]

  WHY IT IS NON-REDUCING

     The linkage uses the ANOMERIC carbon of BOTH units (C1 of glucose
     and C2 of fructose). Neither ring can therefore open to give a
     free -CHO or >C=O group, so sucrose cannot reduce Tollens' reagent
     or Fehling's solution.

  INVERSION OF CANE SUGAR

     C12H22O11 + H2O --(dil. acid or invertase)--> glucose + fructose

         sucrose    [a] = + 66.5
         glucose    [a] = + 52.5
         fructose   [a] = - 92.4
         mixture    [a] = ( +52.5 - 92.4 ) / 2  =  about - 20

     The rotation changes from dextro (+) to laevo (-). This change of
     SIGN is called INVERSION, and the product mixture is INVERT SUGAR.
     Honey is chiefly invert sugar.
```

### Q B7
Write about the structure and functions of starch, cellulose and glycogen.

**ANSWER:**
```
  All three have the formula (C6H10O5)n and are polysaccharides of glucose.

  1.  STARCH
      * a MIXTURE of AMYLOSE (15-20%) and AMYLOPECTIN (80-85%)
      * AMYLOSE - linear, only alpha-1,4 linkages, soluble in hot water
      * AMYLOPECTIN - branched, alpha-1,4 in the chains plus alpha-1,6
        at the branch points, insoluble
      * FUNCTION : the reserve food of PLANTS; the main dietary source
        of carbohydrate for humans. Gives a blue colour with iodine.

  2.  CELLULOSE
      * a STRAIGHT chain polymer of BETA-D-GLUCOSE joined by
        BETA-1,4 glycosidic linkages
      * neighbouring chains are held to each other by hydrogen bonds,
        which makes strong fibres
      * FUNCTION : STRUCTURAL - it forms the CELL WALL of plants.
        The most abundant organic substance in the plant world
        (cotton is ~90% cellulose). Humans cannot digest it.

  3.  GLYCOGEN
      * "animal starch"; a polymer of alpha-D-glucose with alpha-1,4
        and alpha-1,6 linkages, MORE HIGHLY BRANCHED than amylopectin
      * FUNCTION : the reserve carbohydrate of ANIMALS, stored in the
        LIVER, MUSCLES and BRAIN. Excess glucose is stored as glycogen
        and broken down again when the body needs energy.
```

### Q B9
Explain the amphoteric behaviour of amino acids. What is the
isoelectric point?

**ANSWER:**
```
  An amino acid contains an ACIDIC -COOH group and a BASIC -NH2 group
  in the same molecule. A proton passes from the -COOH to the -NH2 and
  the molecule exists as a ZWITTER ION:

           R                              R
           |                              |
    H2N -- CH -- COOH   ---->      H3N -- CH -- COO
                                   (+)           (-)

  BECAUSE OF THIS IT REACTS WITH BOTH ACIDS AND BASES (amphoteric):

  With an ACID (extra H+) - the -COO(-) takes the proton:

       H3N(+)-CHR-COO(-)  +  H+   --->   H3N(+)-CHR-COOH
                                          a CATION -> moves to the CATHODE

  With a BASE (extra OH-) - the -NH3(+) gives up its proton:

       H3N(+)-CHR-COO(-)  +  OH-  --->   H2N-CHR-COO(-)  +  H2O
                                          an ANION -> moves to the ANODE

  ISOELECTRIC POINT

     The pH at which the amino acid exists entirely as the zwitter ion,
     with a net charge of ZERO, and does not migrate towards either
     electrode, is called the ISOELECTRIC POINT (pI).
     At this pH the solubility of the amino acid is minimum.
     For glycine, pI = 6.0.
```

### Q B10
Describe the structure of proteins (the four levels).
*(the single most likely 4-mark question)*

**ANSWER:**
```
  Proteins are polymers of alpha-amino acids joined by PEPTIDE BONDS
  ( -CO-NH- ). Their structure is described at FOUR levels.

  1.  PRIMARY STRUCTURE
      The exact NUMBER and SEQUENCE (order) of the amino acids in the
      polypeptide chain, from the free -NH2 end to the free -COOH end.
      Held by PEPTIDE BONDS (covalent). Changing even one amino acid
      changes the protein (as in sickle cell anaemia).

  2.  SECONDARY STRUCTURE
      The shape taken up locally by the chain, held by HYDROGEN BONDS
      between the C=O of one peptide bond and the N-H of another.
      Two types:
        (a) ALPHA HELIX - the chain coils into a right-handed spiral,
            held by INTRAmolecular hydrogen bonds.  e.g. keratin
        (b) BETA PLEATED SHEET - the chains lie stretched side by side
            and are held by INTERmolecular hydrogen bonds; the sheet
            looks pleated.  e.g. fibroin (silk)

  3.  TERTIARY STRUCTURE
      The overall THREE-DIMENSIONAL folding of the whole chain, giving
      either a FIBROUS or a GLOBULAR shape. Stabilised by hydrogen
      bonds, DISULPHIDE bridges (-S-S-), IONIC (salt) bridges,
      van der Waals and hydrophobic interactions.

  4.  QUATERNARY STRUCTURE
      The way TWO OR MORE polypeptide sub-units are arranged with
      respect to one another. Held by the same weak forces.
      Example : HAEMOGLOBIN, which has four sub-units.
```

### Q B11
What is denaturation of proteins? Give the causes, everyday examples,
and say what is lost.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
     Denaturation is the loss of the biological activity of a protein
     brought about by the destruction of its SECONDARY and TERTIARY
     structures, the PRIMARY structure remaining intact.

     A protein in its natural functional form is a NATIVE protein;
     after this change it is a DENATURED protein.

  CAUSES
     heat (boiling) , change of pH (acid or alkali) , heavy metal ions
     (Pb2+, Hg2+) , alcohol , urea , strong salt solutions ,
     UV radiation , violent shaking

  WHAT IS LOST
     * the alpha helix uncoils and the globular shape unfolds
     * the hydrogen bonds and other weak forces holding the 2nd and
       3rd structures break
     * the protein loses its BIOLOGICAL ACTIVITY
     * solubility decreases and the protein coagulates
     * the change is usually IRREVERSIBLE

  WHAT SURVIVES
     * the PEPTIDE BONDS, and hence the PRIMARY structure (the sequence
       of amino acids), are unaffected

  EXAMPLES
     1. Boiling an egg : the soluble globular protein ALBUMIN of the egg
        white coagulates into a hard white insoluble solid.
     2. Curdling of milk : lactic acid formed by bacteria lowers the pH
        and the milk protein CASEIN coagulates into curd.
```

### Q B12
What are enzymes? Explain the mechanism of enzyme action.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ENZYMES are BIOCATALYSTS - protein molecules (globular proteins)
  produced by living cells that catalyse the biochemical reactions of
  the body. They are named after the substrate plus "ase"
  (maltose -> maltase, urea -> urease).

  MECHANISM OF ENZYME ACTION

  Each enzyme has a small region on its surface, the ACTIVE SITE, whose
  shape and chemical groups exactly match one particular substrate -
  like a lock and its key.

  STEP 1  The substrate fits into the active site and an
          ENZYME-SUBSTRATE COMPLEX is formed.

  STEP 2  Within the complex the substrate bonds are strained and the
          ACTIVATION ENERGY of the reaction is LOWERED, so the reaction
          takes place rapidly.

  STEP 3  The product no longer fits the active site and is RELEASED,
          leaving the enzyme free to accept another substrate molecule.

              E   +   S   <---->   ES   ---->   E   +   P
           enzyme substrate      complex      enzyme  product

     SUBSTRATE                                     PRODUCTS
        /\                                          /  \
    ____\/____          ___________            ____|__|____
   |  active  |   ->   |    \/     |    ->    |            |
   |   site   |        |  complex  |          |  enzyme    |
   |__________|        |___________|          |  free      |
      ENZYME             ES COMPLEX           |____________|
```

### Q B13
Give the characteristics of enzyme catalysis.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  HIGHLY EFFICIENT - one enzyme molecule can transform about a
      million substrate molecules per minute; rates are raised by
      factors of 10^6 to 10^20.

  2.  HIGHLY SPECIFIC - each enzyme catalyses only ONE reaction or one
      type of reaction. Urease hydrolyses urea only, and no other amide.
      (The "lock and key" fit of the active site.)

  3.  OPTIMUM TEMPERATURE - activity is maximum at about 298-310 K
      (body temperature). At higher temperatures the enzyme is
      DENATURED and destroyed; at lower temperatures it is inactive.

  4.  OPTIMUM pH - most enzymes work best between pH 5 and 7; activity
      falls sharply on either side. (Pepsin is an exception, working
      at about pH 2 in the stomach.)

  5.  REQUIRED IN VERY SMALL AMOUNTS and are not consumed.

  6.  ACTIVITY INCREASED BY ACTIVATORS AND CO-ENZYMES - many enzymes
      need a small non-protein helper such as a vitamin or a metal ion
      (Na+, Mn2+, Co2+, Cu2+).

  7.  INHIBITED BY POISONS - heavy metal ions and certain chemicals
      block the active site and destroy the activity. Many drugs act
      as enzyme inhibitors.
```

### Q B16
Describe the double helix structure of DNA.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The Watson-Crick model (1953):

  1.  DNA consists of TWO polynucleotide chains coiled round a common
      axis to form a RIGHT-HANDED DOUBLE HELIX, like a twisted ladder.

  2.  The two strands are ANTIPARALLEL - one runs in the 5' to 3'
      direction and the other in the 3' to 5' direction.

  3.  The SUGAR-PHOSPHATE backbones lie on the OUTSIDE; the nitrogenous
      BASES point INWARDS and are stacked like the rungs of a ladder.

  4.  The two strands are held together by HYDROGEN BONDS between
      complementary bases:
              ADENINE == THYMINE     -  2 hydrogen bonds
              GUANINE == CYTOSINE    -  3 hydrogen bonds
      A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine, keeping the width of
      the helix constant (about 2 nm).

  5.  One complete turn of the helix contains about 10 base pairs and
      is about 3.4 nm long.

  6.  Because the pairing is fixed, %A = %T and %G = %C
      (CHARGAFF'S RULE).

         5'                                        3'
          P                                         P
          |                                         |
          S ---- A  = = = = = = = = = T ---- S
          |                                         |
          P                                         P
          |                                         |
          S ---- G  = = = = = = = = = C ---- S
          |                                         |
         3'                                        5'

         S = deoxyribose ,  P = phosphate
```

### Q B17
Write a short note on the biological functions of nucleic acids.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  REPLICATION - DNA copies itself
      Before cell division the double helix UNZIPS; each strand acts as
      a TEMPLATE and free nucleotides pair with it (A with T, G with C).
      Two identical DNA molecules result, so hereditary characters are
      passed on unchanged.

  2.  TRANSCRIPTION - DNA to messenger RNA
      The message in one DNA strand is copied into a strand of m-RNA,
      with URACIL taking the place of thymine.
         DNA   :  A  T  G  C
         m-RNA :  U  A  C  G

  3.  TRANSLATION - protein synthesis
      The m-RNA travels to a ribosome, where it is read three bases at
      a time. Each triplet (a CODON) codes for one amino acid, and
      t-RNA brings that amino acid. The amino acids are joined by
      peptide bonds into a polypeptide, which folds into the protein.

  4.  Through the enzymes and proteins so produced, nucleic acids
      control every chemical process in the cell.

  THE CENTRAL DOGMA :   DNA ---> RNA ---> PROTEIN
```

### Q C1
Explain the structure of glucose. Give the reactions that establish the
open chain structure, the facts it fails to explain, and the cyclic
structure. **(8 marks)**

**ANSWER — mark split:**
```
  (a)  PREPARATION and molecular formula .................... 1 mark
  (b)  The six structure-proving reactions .................. 3 marks
  (c)  The open chain structure drawn ....................... 1 mark
  (d)  The facts the open chain cannot explain .............. 1.5 marks
  (e)  The cyclic (pyranose) structure + anomers ............ 1 mark
  (f)  Mutarotation ......................................... 0.5 mark
```

```
  (a)  Glucose, C6H12O6, molar mass 180, is also called dextrose or
       grape sugar. It is prepared commercially by hydrolysing starch
       with dilute H2SO4 at 393 K under 2-3 atm pressure.

  (b)  EVIDENCE FOR THE OPEN CHAIN STRUCTURE

       1. HI, prolonged heating  ->  n-HEXANE
          Six carbons in a straight, unbranched chain.

       2. NH2OH -> an OXIME ;  HCN -> a CYANOHYDRIN (one molecule only)
          One CARBONYL group is present.

       3. Br2 water (mild) -> GLUCONIC ACID, COOH-(CHOH)4-CH2OH
          A six-carbon monocarboxylic acid, so the carbonyl group is an
          ALDEHYDE at the end of the chain.

       4. Acetic anhydride -> GLUCOSE PENTAACETATE
          FIVE -OH groups, one on each of five different carbons.

       5. conc. HNO3 (strong) -> SACCHARIC ACID, COOH-(CHOH)4-COOH
          A dicarboxylic acid, so a primary -CH2OH exists at C6.

       6. It reduces Tollens' reagent (silver mirror) and Fehling's
          solution (red Cu2O), confirming the free -CHO group.

  (c)  OPEN CHAIN STRUCTURE

              CHO
               |
             H-C-OH
               |
            HO-C-H            D-(+)-GLUCOSE
               |              an ALDOHEXOSE
             H-C-OH
               |              CHO-(CHOH)4-CH2OH
             H-C-OH
               |
              CH2OH

  (d)  WHAT THE OPEN CHAIN COULD NOT EXPLAIN

       * glucose does not give the Schiff's test
       * it does not form the addition product with NaHSO3
       * it does not react with NH3 like an ordinary aldehyde
       * glucose pentaacetate does not react with NH2OH
       * two crystalline forms exist:
              alpha  m.p. 419 K , [a] = + 111
              beta   m.p. 423 K , [a] = + 19.2
       * mutarotation occurs

  (e)  THE CYCLIC STRUCTURE

       The -OH on C5 attacks the -CHO on C1 to give a cyclic HEMIACETAL
       with a six-membered PYRANOSE ring. C1 becomes a new chiral centre,
       the ANOMERIC carbon, whose -OH may point down (ALPHA) or up (BETA).
       These two anomers are the two crystalline forms.

                     6 CH2OH
                        |
                        C5 -------------- O
                       /                    \
                  4  C4                      C1 ---- H
                    /  \                    /   \
                  OH    \                  /     OH   <-- alpha
                         C3 ------------ C2
                        /  \            /  \
                      OH    H          H    OH

  (f)  MUTAROTATION

       In solution the two anomers interconvert through the open chain
       form until an equilibrium mixture (36% alpha, 64% beta) is set up,
       whose rotation is +52.5. This change in rotation of a fresh
       solution is called MUTAROTATION.
```

### Q C2
Write an essay on proteins: amino acids, the peptide bond, the four
levels of structure, classification and denaturation. **(8 marks)**

**ANSWER — mark split:**
```
  (a)  Amino acids + zwitter ion + essential/non-essential ..... 2 marks
  (b)  Peptide bond and formation of the protein ............... 1 mark
  (c)  Fibrous and globular classification ..................... 1 mark
  (d)  The FOUR levels of structure ............................ 3 marks
  (e)  Denaturation ............................................ 1 mark
```

```
  (a)  AMINO ACIDS
       An amino acid contains both an -NH2 and a -COOH group. All the
       naturally occurring ones are ALPHA-amino acids:

                        R
                        |
                 H2N -- C -- COOH
                        |
                        H

       About 20 occur in proteins. All except glycine are optically
       active. They exist as ZWITTER IONS,  H3N(+)-CHR-COO(-),  so they
       are high-melting crystalline solids, soluble in water and
       AMPHOTERIC. The pH at which the net charge is zero is the
       ISOELECTRIC POINT.

       ESSENTIAL amino acids (valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine,
       methionine, phenylalanine, tryptophan, lysine, arginine,
       histidine) cannot be made by the body and must come from food.
       NON-ESSENTIAL ones (glycine, alanine, serine, cysteine, tyrosine,
       proline, aspartic acid, glutamic acid, asparagine, glutamine)
       are made by the body.

  (b)  THE PEPTIDE BOND
       The -COOH of one amino acid condenses with the -NH2 of another,
       losing water and forming the -CO-NH- PEPTIDE BOND.

              R1       O   H    R2
               |       ||  |     |
         H2N - CH - C ----- N - CH - COOH

       2 units = dipeptide, 3 = tripeptide, more than 10 = polypeptide;
       a polypeptide of molar mass above ~10000 with a definite shape
       is a PROTEIN.

  (c)  CLASSIFICATION
       FIBROUS proteins - thread-like, insoluble in water, structural.
            keratin (hair, nails), myosin (muscle), collagen, fibroin
       GLOBULAR proteins - spherical, soluble in water, functional.
            insulin, albumin, haemoglobin, all enzymes

  (d)  STRUCTURE OF PROTEINS
       PRIMARY    - the number and SEQUENCE of amino acids in the chain.
                    Held by PEPTIDE BONDS.
       SECONDARY  - the local shape, held by HYDROGEN BONDS.
                    (i) ALPHA HELIX : a right-handed coil held by
                        INTRAmolecular H-bonds (keratin)
                    (ii) BETA PLEATED SHEET : stretched chains side by
                        side held by INTERmolecular H-bonds (silk fibroin)
       TERTIARY   - the overall 3-D folding of the whole chain, giving
                    the fibrous or globular shape. Held by H-bonds,
                    disulphide (-S-S-) bridges, ionic bonds and van der
                    Waals forces.
       QUATERNARY - the arrangement of two or more sub-units, e.g. the
                    four sub-units of haemoglobin.

  (e)  DENATURATION
       The loss of biological activity of a protein by destruction of
       its secondary and tertiary structures, the primary structure
       remaining intact. Caused by heat, change of pH, heavy metal ions,
       alcohol or UV light; usually irreversible.
       Examples: coagulation of egg albumin on boiling; curdling of milk.
```
