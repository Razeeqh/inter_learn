# PATTERN 1 — Classification of a given carbohydrate

### Q1
Which of the following is a **non-reducing** sugar?

(a) glucose &nbsp; (b) maltose &nbsp; (c) lactose &nbsp; (d) sucrose

**ANSWER: (d) sucrose**
```
  In sucrose the linkage uses the anomeric carbon of BOTH units
  (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose), so neither ring can open.
```

> **SHORTCUT:** Only ONE common disaccharide is non-reducing — **sucrose**.
> If "sucrose" is in the options of a "non-reducing" question, it is the answer.

---

### Q2
Which of the following is a ketohexose?

(a) glucose &nbsp; (b) fructose &nbsp; (c) ribose &nbsp; (d) galactose

**ANSWER: (b) fructose** — 6 carbons and a keto group at C2.

---

### Q3
The number of monosaccharide units obtained on complete hydrolysis of
one molecule of sucrose is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 2 &nbsp; (c) 3 &nbsp; (d) many

**ANSWER: (b) 2** — one glucose and one fructose. Sucrose is a disaccharide.

---

### Q4
Which of the following is **not** a carbohydrate?

(a) glucose &nbsp; (b) sucrose &nbsp; (c) glycine &nbsp; (d) starch

**ANSWER: (c) glycine** — glycine is an amino acid, not a carbohydrate.

---

### Q5
Glucose and fructose are

(a) chain isomers &nbsp; (b) position isomers
(c) functional isomers &nbsp; (d) optical isomers

**ANSWER: (c) functional isomers**
```
  Both are C6H12O6, but glucose has an ALDEHYDE group and fructose has
  a KETONE group - different functional groups, same formula.
```

---

### Q6
Which of the following pairs are anomers?

(a) glucose and fructose &nbsp; (b) glucose and galactose
(c) alpha-D-glucose and beta-D-glucose &nbsp; (d) starch and cellulose

**ANSWER: (c)** — they differ only at the anomeric carbon C1.

# PATTERN 2 — Reactions of glucose: which reagent gives which product

### Q7
Glucose on prolonged heating with HI gives

(a) n-hexane &nbsp; (b) hexanol &nbsp; (c) gluconic acid &nbsp; (d) saccharic acid

**ANSWER: (a) n-hexane** — HI removes every oxygen, proving a straight six-carbon chain.

---

### Q8
Oxidation of glucose with **bromine water** gives

(a) saccharic acid &nbsp; (b) gluconic acid &nbsp; (c) glucaric acid &nbsp; (d) oxalic acid

**ANSWER: (b) gluconic acid** — bromine water is MILD, so only the -CHO is oxidised.

---

### Q9
Oxidation of glucose with **concentrated HNO3** gives

(a) gluconic acid &nbsp; (b) saccharic acid &nbsp; (c) n-hexane &nbsp; (d) glucose oxime

**ANSWER: (b) saccharic acid** — HNO3 is STRONG, so both the -CHO and the -CH2OH become -COOH.

> **SHORTCUT:**
> ```
>   MILD   reagent (Br2 water)  ->  ONE  -COOH  ->  GLUCONIC acid
>   STRONG reagent (conc. HNO3) ->  TWO  -COOH  ->  SACCHARIC acid
> ```

---

### Q10
Glucose reacts with acetic anhydride to give

(a) mono-acetate &nbsp; (b) tri-acetate &nbsp; (c) **penta**-acetate &nbsp; (d) hexa-acetate

**ANSWER: (c) pentaacetate** — glucose has FIVE -OH groups.

---

### Q11
The number of hydroxyl groups present in glucose is

(a) 3 &nbsp; (b) 4 &nbsp; (c) 5 &nbsp; (d) 6

**ANSWER: (c) 5**

---

### Q12
Which reagent will distinguish glucose from fructose?

(a) Tollens' reagent &nbsp; (b) Fehling's solution
(c) bromine water &nbsp; (d) NH2OH

**ANSWER: (c) bromine water**
```
  Tollens' and Fehling's are ALKALINE, so fructose isomerises to glucose
  and BOTH give a positive test - they cannot distinguish.
  Bromine water is NEUTRAL, so no isomerisation occurs:
        glucose  -> decolourises bromine water (gives gluconic acid)
        fructose -> NO reaction
```

---

### Q13
Glucose does not give the Schiff's test and does not form a
hydrogensulphite addition product with NaHSO3. This indicates that

(a) glucose has no -OH groups
(b) glucose has no free -CHO group in its cyclic structure
(c) glucose is a ketone
(d) glucose is not optically active

**ANSWER: (b)** — the evidence for the cyclic hemiacetal structure.

# PATTERN 3 — Anomers, mutarotation and ring size

### Q14
Mutarotation of glucose occurs because of

(a) hydrolysis &nbsp; (b) interconversion of alpha and beta anomers
(c) oxidation &nbsp; (d) polymerisation

**ANSWER: (b)** — through the small amount of open chain form present in solution.

---

### Q15
The specific rotation of an equilibrium aqueous solution of D-glucose is

(a) +111 &nbsp; (b) +19.2 &nbsp; (c) +52.5 &nbsp; (d) −92.4

**ANSWER: (c) +52.5**

> **SHORTCUT:** Remember three numbers: alpha **+111**, beta **+19.2**,
> equilibrium **+52.5**. And fructose is **−92.4**.

---

### Q16
The ring in glucose and the ring in fructose are respectively

(a) furanose, pyranose &nbsp; (b) pyranose, furanose
(c) pyranose, pyranose &nbsp; (d) furanose, furanose

**ANSWER: (b) pyranose, furanose**

> **SHORTCUT:** **F**ructose → **F**uranose (5-membered). Glucose → pyranose (6-membered).

---

### Q17
In alpha-D-glucopyranose the -OH group at C1

(a) lies above the plane of the ring
(b) lies below the plane of the ring
(c) is absent
(d) is replaced by -H

**ANSWER: (b) below the plane** — that is the definition of the alpha anomer.

---

### Q18
The anomeric carbon in fructose is

(a) C1 &nbsp; (b) C2 &nbsp; (c) C5 &nbsp; (d) C6

**ANSWER: (b) C2** — the keto carbon becomes the anomeric carbon on ring closure.

# PATTERN 4 — Glycosidic linkages in di- and polysaccharides

### Q19
The linkage present in maltose is

(a) alpha-1,4 &nbsp; (b) beta-1,4 &nbsp; (c) alpha-1,2 &nbsp; (d) alpha-1,6

**ANSWER: (a) alpha-1,4**

---

### Q20
Lactose on hydrolysis gives

(a) glucose + glucose &nbsp; (b) glucose + fructose
(c) galactose + glucose &nbsp; (d) galactose + fructose

**ANSWER: (c) galactose + glucose** — joined by a beta-1,4 linkage.

---

### Q21
The linkage present in cellulose is

(a) alpha-1,4 &nbsp; (b) **beta-1,4** &nbsp; (c) alpha-1,6 &nbsp; (d) beta-1,6

**ANSWER: (b) beta-1,4**

> **SHORTCUT — the linkage table in one line:**
> ```
>   sucrose  alpha-1,2      maltose   alpha-1,4
>   lactose  beta-1,4       cellulose BETA-1,4
>   amylose  alpha-1,4      amylopectin/glycogen alpha-1,4 + alpha-1,6
> ```

Branching in amylopectin is due to

(a) alpha-1,4 linkages &nbsp; (b) **alpha-1,6 linkages**

### Q23
Which of the following is the **most highly branched** polysaccharide?

Cellulose cannot be digested by humans because

(a) it is insoluble

### Q25
Invert sugar is

(a) sucrose &nbsp; (b) an equimolar mixture of glucose and fructose

# PATTERN 5 — Amino acids, zwitter ions and the isoelectric point

### Q26
The zwitter ion form of an amino acid is

(a) H2N-CHR-COOH &nbsp; (b) H3N(+)-CHR-COOH
(c) **H3N(+)-CHR-COO(−)** &nbsp; (d) H2N-CHR-COO(−)

**ANSWER: (c)**

---

### Q27
At its isoelectric point an amino acid

(a) moves to the cathode &nbsp; (b) moves to the anode
(c) does not migrate to either electrode &nbsp; (d) decomposes

**ANSWER: (c)** — the net charge is zero, and its solubility is minimum.

---

### Q28
Which amino acid is **not** optically active?

(a) alanine &nbsp; (b) **glycine** &nbsp; (c) valine &nbsp; (d) leucine

**ANSWER: (b) glycine** — R = H, so the alpha carbon carries two hydrogens
and is not chiral.

---

### Q29
Which of the following is an **essential** amino acid?

(a) glycine &nbsp; (b) alanine &nbsp; (c) **valine** &nbsp; (d) serine

**ANSWER: (c) valine**

> **SHORTCUT:** "PVT TIM HALL" = Phenylalanine, Valine, Threonine,
> Tryptophan, Isoleucine, Methionine, Histidine, Arginine, Leucine, Lysine.
> Anything NOT in that list is non-essential.

---

### Q30
An amino acid placed in a solution of pH lower than its isoelectric point
will migrate towards the

(a) **cathode** &nbsp; (b) anode &nbsp; (c) neither &nbsp; (d) both

**ANSWER: (a) cathode**
```
  pH below pI -> the molecule picks up H+ -> becomes a CATION
              -> moves to the negative electrode, the CATHODE
```

The linkage present in proteins is

(a) glycosidic &nbsp; (b) **peptide (-CO-NH-)** &nbsp; (c) ester &nbsp; (d) ether

### Q32
The number of peptide bonds in a tripeptide is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) **2** &nbsp; (c) 3 &nbsp; (d) 4

**ANSWER: (b) 2** — peptide bonds = (number of amino acids) − 1.

---

### Q33
The alpha helix of a protein is stabilised by

(a) peptide bonds &nbsp; (b) **hydrogen bonds** &nbsp;
(c) ionic bonds &nbsp; (d) van der Waals forces

**ANSWER: (b) hydrogen bonds** — INTRAmolecular, between C=O and N-H.

### Q34
The primary structure of a protein refers to

(a) the alpha helix &nbsp; (b) the sequence of amino acids

**ANSWER: (b) the sequence of amino acids** — held by peptide bonds.

### Q35
Which protein shows quaternary structure?

(a) insulin &nbsp; (b) keratin &nbsp; (c) **haemoglobin** &nbsp; (d) fibroin

**ANSWER: (c) haemoglobin** — four sub-units (two alpha, two beta).

### Q36
During denaturation of a protein, which structure is **retained**?

(a) secondary &nbsp; (b) tertiary &nbsp; (c) quaternary &nbsp; (d) **primary**

**ANSWER: (d) primary** — the peptide bonds are not broken.

---

### Q37
Which of the following is a fibrous protein?

**ANSWER: (c) keratin**

> **SHORTCUT:** Fibrous = hair/silk/muscle/tendon = keratin, fibroin, myosin,

> enzymes) is globular.

### Q38
The beta pleated sheet structure is present in

(a) wool &nbsp; (b) **silk (fibroin)** &nbsp; (c) haemoglobin &nbsp; (d) insulin

# PATTERN 9 — Nucleic acids: components and pairing

### Q48
A nucleotide consists of

(a) sugar + base
(b) **sugar + base + phosphate**
(c) base + phosphate
(d) sugar + phosphate

**ANSWER: (b)**

> **SHORTCUT:** nucleo**T**ide has the **T** of phospha**T**e.
> Nucleo**S**ide is **S**impler — no phosphate.

---

### Q49
Which of the following is a purine base?

(a) cytosine &nbsp; (b) thymine &nbsp; (c) uracil &nbsp; (d) **guanine**

**ANSWER: (d) guanine**

> **SHORTCUT:** "**PURE As Gold**" → **PUR**ines are **A**denine and **G**uanine.
> Everything else (C, T, U) is a pyrimidine.

---

### Q50
The base present in RNA but **not** in DNA is

(a) adenine &nbsp; (b) guanine &nbsp; (c) **uracil** &nbsp; (d) cytosine

**ANSWER: (c) uracil**

---

### Q51
The number of hydrogen bonds between guanine and cytosine is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 2 &nbsp; (c) **3** &nbsp; (d) 4

**ANSWER: (c) 3** — A–T has 2, G–C has 3.

---

### Q52
The sugar present in DNA is

(a) D-ribose &nbsp; (b) **2-deoxy-D-ribose** &nbsp; (c) glucose &nbsp; (d) fructose

**ANSWER: (b) 2-deoxy-D-ribose**

---

### Q53
In a sample of double-stranded DNA, if adenine is 20%, the percentage of
guanine is

(a) 20 &nbsp; (b) **30** &nbsp; (c) 40 &nbsp; (d) 60

**ANSWER: (b) 30**
```
  %A = %T = 20      ->  A + T = 40
  G + C = 100 - 40  =  60
  %G = %C           ->  %G = 60 / 2 = 30
```

> **SHORTCUT:** `%G = (100 − 2 x %A) / 2 = 50 − %A`.
> Here 50 − 20 = 30. Do it in your head.

---

### Q54
The two strands of the DNA double helix are

(a) parallel &nbsp; (b) **antiparallel** &nbsp; (c) identical &nbsp; (d) unconnected

**ANSWER: (b) antiparallel** — one runs 5'→3', the other 3'→5'.

---

### Q55
The linkage that joins the nucleotides in a nucleic acid chain is

(a) peptide &nbsp; (b) glycosidic &nbsp; (c) **phosphodiester** &nbsp; (d) hydrogen bond

**ANSWER: (c) phosphodiester** — between C5' of one sugar and C3' of the next.

---

### Q56
The process by which the message of DNA is copied into m-RNA is called

(a) replication &nbsp; (b) **transcription** &nbsp; (c) translation &nbsp; (d) mutation

**ANSWER: (b) transcription**

> **SHORTCUT:**
> ```
>   DNA -> DNA      REPLICATION    ("re-plicate" = copy itself)
>   DNA -> RNA      TRANSCRIPTION  ("scribe" = write it down)
>   RNA -> PROTEIN  TRANSLATION    ("translate" into another language)
> ```
