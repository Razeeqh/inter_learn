# Biomolecules — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps, and exactly what to write
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — DEFINE AND CLASSIFY CARBOHYDRATES                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are carbohydrates?" / "How are carbohydrates
            classified?" / "Give examples of each class."
  METHOD    1. Definition first:
               optically active POLYHYDROXY ALDEHYDES or KETONES,
               or compounds giving these on HYDROLYSIS.
            2. Classify on hydrolysis:
               MONO  (glucose) / OLIGO (sucrose) / POLY (starch)
            3. For 4 marks, add the other three classifications:
               sugars vs non-sugars ,
               reducing vs non-reducing ,
               aldose vs ketose.
            4. EVERY class gets an EXAMPLE.
  TRAP      Do NOT define them as "hydrates of carbon, Cx(H2O)y".
            Rhamnose is a carbohydrate that does not fit that formula,
            and formaldehyde fits it but is not a carbohydrate.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — REDUCING OR NON-REDUCING?                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are reducing sugars?" / "Why is sucrose non-reducing?"
            / "Which of these reduces Fehling's solution?"
  METHOD    1. Definition: a sugar that reduces Tollens' reagent (silver
               mirror) and Fehling's solution (red Cu2O precipitate),
               because it has a FREE anomeric carbon.
            2. REDUCING     : all monosaccharides + maltose + lactose
               NON-REDUCING : sucrose , starch , cellulose
            3. If asked WHY sucrose is not: BOTH anomeric carbons
               (C1 of glucose and C2 of fructose) are used in the linkage.
  TRAP      Fructose IS reducing even though it is a ketose. Never write
            "ketones are not reducing".
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — WHY IS FRUCTOSE A REDUCING SUGAR?                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Fructose is a ketose, yet it reduces Tollens' reagent. Why?"
  METHOD    1. Tollens' and Fehling's reagents are ALKALINE.
            2. In alkali fructose ISOMERISES through an ENEDIOL into
               GLUCOSE (and mannose).
            3. Glucose has a free -CHO group, which reduces the reagent.
  TRAP      Add the distinguishing fact for the extra mark: fructose does
            NOT react with BROMINE WATER, because bromine water is
            NEUTRAL and no isomerisation occurs. That is how glucose and
            fructose are told apart.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — PREPARATION OF GLUCOSE                           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is glucose prepared from sucrose / from starch?"
  METHOD    FROM SUCROSE :
               C12H22O11 + H2O --(dil. HCl, ALCOHOLIC solution)-->
                                             glucose + fructose
               glucose is insoluble in alcohol so it crystallises out
            FROM STARCH :
               (C6H10O5)n + n H2O --(dil.H2SO4, 393 K, 2-3 atm)-->
                                             n C6H12O6
  TRAP      The CONDITIONS carry a mark of their own. Always write
            "in alcoholic solution" and "393 K, 2-3 atm".
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — REACTIONS PROVING THE OPEN CHAIN STRUCTURE       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the reactions which establish the structure of glucose"
            / "How was the open chain structure of glucose arrived at?"
  METHOD    Write each as REAGENT -> PRODUCT -> CONCLUSION:
            1. HI, prolonged heating -> n-HEXANE
                  -> 6 carbons, STRAIGHT chain
            2. NH2OH -> OXIME ; HCN -> CYANOHYDRIN
                  -> ONE carbonyl group
            3. Br2 water -> GLUCONIC ACID (6C mono-acid)
                  -> the carbonyl is an ALDEHYDE at C1
            4. (CH3CO)2O -> PENTAACETATE
                  -> FIVE -OH groups on five different carbons
            5. conc. HNO3 -> SACCHARIC ACID (6C di-acid)
                  -> a primary -CH2OH at C6
            Finish by DRAWING the open chain, CHO-(CHOH)4-CH2OH.
  TRAP      The CONCLUSION carries the mark, not just the product name.
            Also: Br2 water is MILD (one -COOH), HNO3 is STRONG (two).
            Swapping those two loses two marks at once.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — WHAT THE OPEN CHAIN COULD NOT EXPLAIN            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which reactions of glucose cannot be explained by its open
            chain structure?" / "Why was the cyclic structure proposed?"
  METHOD    List them:
            1. no SCHIFF'S test
            2. no addition product with NaHSO3
            3. does not react with NH3 like an aldehyde
            4. glucose PENTAACETATE does not react with NH2OH
            5. TWO crystalline forms exist (alpha m.p. 419 K [a]+111 ,
               beta m.p. 423 K [a]+19.2)
            6. MUTAROTATION
            Then: all are explained by the CYCLIC HEMIACETAL (pyranose)
            structure, in which the -CHO is not free.
  TRAP      Do not stop at the list. The examiner wants the sentence
            "hence the cyclic structure was proposed".
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — CYCLIC STRUCTURE / HAWORTH PROJECTION            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the cyclic structure of glucose" / "Draw the Haworth
            projection of alpha- and beta-D-glucose."
  METHOD    1. The -OH on C5 attacks the -CHO on C1 -> cyclic HEMIACETAL.
            2. Six-membered ring = PYRANOSE. C1 becomes the ANOMERIC
               carbon, a new chiral centre.
            3. Draw the hexagon with the ring oxygen at the top right.
                  C1 -OH DOWN = ALPHA
                  C1 -OH UP   = BETA
               Other groups: C2 -OH down, C3 -OH up, C4 -OH down,
               C5 -CH2OH up.
            4. Label the anomeric carbon.
  TRAP      Forgetting to put the CH2OH on C5 (pointing UP) - it is a
            standard half-mark. And label "alpha" / "beta" clearly.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — MUTAROTATION                                     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is mutarotation?" / "A fresh solution of alpha-D-glucose
            has [a] = +111 which falls to +52.5. Explain."
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: the spontaneous change in the specific optical
               rotation of a freshly prepared sugar solution until it
               becomes constant.
            2. NUMBERS: alpha +111 falls , beta +19.2 rises ,
               both settle at +52.5.
            3. REASON: the alpha and beta anomers interconvert THROUGH
               THE OPEN CHAIN FORM until equilibrium (36% alpha,
               64% beta) is reached.
  TRAP      Do not confuse it with INVERSION of cane sugar.
            Mutarotation = ONE sugar changing between its own anomers.
            Inversion    = sucrose being HYDROLYSED into two sugars.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — SUCROSE, INVERSION AND INVERT SUGAR              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is invert sugar?" / "Explain the inversion of cane
            sugar" / "Describe the structure of sucrose."
  METHOD    1. STRUCTURE: alpha-D-glucose C1 -O- C2 beta-D-fructose,
               an alpha-1,2 glycosidic linkage. NON-REDUCING because
               both anomeric carbons are used.
            2. HYDROLYSIS: sucrose + H2O --(dil.acid or invertase)-->
               glucose + fructose
            3. NUMBERS: sucrose +66.5 ; glucose +52.5 ; fructose -92.4
               mixture = (+52.5 - 92.4)/2 = about -20
            4. The SIGN changes from (+) to (-) -> INVERSION.
               The product mixture is INVERT SUGAR. Honey is invert sugar.
  TRAP      You must quote the rotation values. "It becomes laevorotatory"
            without the numbers loses half the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — THE LINKAGE IN A GIVEN DI- OR POLYSACCHARIDE    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a glycosidic linkage?" / "Name the linkage in
            maltose / lactose / cellulose."
  METHOD    Definition: the oxygen bridge joining two monosaccharide
            units, formed by the loss of water between two -OH groups.
            Then quote the table:
               sucrose      alpha-1,2   glucose + fructose  NON-reducing
               maltose      alpha-1,4   glucose + glucose   reducing
               lactose      beta-1,4    galactose + glucose reducing
               amylose      alpha-1,4
               amylopectin  alpha-1,4 + alpha-1,6 (branches)
               cellulose    BETA-1,4
               glycogen     alpha-1,4 + alpha-1,6 (most branched)
  TRAP      Cellulose is BETA. Everything else you meet is alpha.
            This one letter is the whole question.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — STARCH vs CELLULOSE / AMYLOSE vs AMYLOPECTIN    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Differentiate between ..." / "Write about the structure and
            function of starch, cellulose and glycogen."
  METHOD    DRAW A TABLE with the rows:
               monomer , linkage , structure (linear/branched) ,
               solubility , function , digestibility by humans
            Key contrasts:
               starch    alpha-D-glucose , alpha-1,4 , food reserve
               cellulose BETA-D-glucose  , beta-1,4  , structural
               glycogen  alpha , most branched , animal food reserve
               amylose 15-20% linear ; amylopectin 80-85% branched
  TRAP      Do not forget the FUNCTION row - it is usually a full mark.
            And mention that starch gives a BLUE colour with iodine.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — WHY HUMANS CANNOT DIGEST CELLULOSE              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why can we digest starch but not cellulose?"
  METHOD    1. Starch has ALPHA-1,4 linkages; cellulose has BETA-1,4.
            2. Human enzymes can hydrolyse alpha linkages but not beta
               linkages (the active site does not fit).
            3. Cellulose therefore passes through as dietary FIBRE.
               Ruminants and termites can digest it because gut bacteria
               supply CELLULASE.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — AMINO ACIDS: STRUCTURE AND CLASSIFICATION       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are alpha-amino acids?" / "Essential and non-essential
            amino acids - define and give examples."
  METHOD    1. Draw the general structure with the alpha carbon carrying
               R , H , -NH2 , -COOH.
            2. ESSENTIAL - cannot be made by the body, must be eaten.
               (valine, leucine, isoleucine, threonine, methionine,
                phenylalanine, tryptophan, lysine, arginine, histidine)
               NON-ESSENTIAL - the body makes them.
               (glycine, alanine, serine, cysteine, tyrosine, proline,
                aspartic acid, glutamic acid, asparagine, glutamine)
            3. Add: all except GLYCINE are optically active.
  TRAP      Give at least ONE named example from each list. A definition
            without an example is half marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — ZWITTER ION AND AMPHOTERIC BEHAVIOUR            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a zwitter ion?" / "Explain the amphoteric nature of
            amino acids."
  METHOD    1. DRAW it:      H3N(+) - CHR - COO(-)
            2. Definition: positive and negative charges on different
               atoms of the SAME molecule, overall neutral.
            3. With ACID  : COO(-) takes H+ -> CATION -> CATHODE
               With BASE  : NH3(+) loses H+ -> ANION  -> ANODE
            4. It explains high melting points, water solubility and
               amphoteric behaviour.
  TRAP      You must SHOW BOTH CHARGES in the drawing. Writing
            "H2N-CHR-COOH" gets zero for this question.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — ISOELECTRIC POINT                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define the isoelectric point" / "In which direction will
            glycine migrate at pH 2?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: the pH at which the amino acid exists entirely
               as the zwitter ion, net charge ZERO, and does NOT migrate
               to either electrode.
            2. Add: solubility is MINIMUM at this pH.
            3. For the migration version:
                  pH < pI -> CATION  -> CATHODE
                  pH = pI -> no movement
                  pH > pI -> ANION   -> ANODE
  TRAP      The isoelectric point is a pH VALUE, not a temperature and
            not a concentration. Say "the pH at which ...".
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — THE PEPTIDE BOND                                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a peptide bond?" / "How is a dipeptide formed?"
  METHOD    1. Definition: the -CO-NH- linkage formed between the -COOH
               of one amino acid and the -NH2 of another with the
               ELIMINATION OF WATER.
            2. Draw the two amino acids, the arrow labelled "- H2O", and
               the product with the peptide bond marked.
            3. If asked to count: peptide bonds = (amino acids) - 1.
  TRAP      Show the loss of H2O. A drawing without it loses a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — THE FOUR LEVELS OF PROTEIN STRUCTURE            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the structure of proteins" / "What are the primary
            and secondary structures of a protein?"    *** MOST LIKELY ***
  METHOD    One paragraph each, and NAME THE FORCE each time:
            PRIMARY    - number and SEQUENCE of amino acids.
                         held by PEPTIDE BONDS
            SECONDARY  - alpha HELIX (right-handed coil, INTRAmolecular
                         H-bonds, e.g. keratin) or BETA PLEATED SHEET
                         (stretched chains, INTERmolecular H-bonds,
                         e.g. silk fibroin). held by HYDROGEN BONDS
            TERTIARY   - overall 3-D folding; fibrous or globular shape.
                         held by H-bonds, DISULPHIDE (-S-S-), IONIC and
                         van der Waals forces
            QUATERNARY - arrangement of two or more SUB-UNITS,
                         e.g. HAEMOGLOBIN (4 sub-units)
  TRAP      alpha helix = INTRAmolecular H-bonds.
            beta sheet  = INTERmolecular H-bonds.
            Getting these the wrong way round is the most common error
            in the whole chapter.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — FIBROUS vs GLOBULAR PROTEINS                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between fibrous and globular proteins."
  METHOD    Table with rows: shape , water solubility , forces ,
            function , examples.
               FIBROUS  - thread-like, INSOLUBLE, structural
                          keratin, myosin, collagen, fibroin
               GLOBULAR - spherical, SOLUBLE, functional
                          insulin, albumin, haemoglobin, all ENZYMES
  TRAP      Enzymes are GLOBULAR. Never write "enzymes are fibrous".
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — DENATURATION OF PROTEINS                        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is denaturation?" / "What happens when an egg is
            boiled?" / "Why does milk curdle?"
  METHOD    1. DEFINITION: loss of biological activity of a protein by
               destruction of its SECONDARY and TERTIARY structure, the
               PRIMARY structure remaining INTACT.
            2. CAUSES: heat, change of pH, heavy metal ions, alcohol,
               urea, UV light.
            3. WHAT IS LOST: helix uncoils, H-bonds break, solubility
               falls, the protein coagulates, activity is lost.
            4. EXAMPLES: boiling an egg (albumin coagulates),
               curdling of milk (casein, by lactic acid).
            5. It is usually IRREVERSIBLE.
  TRAP      The examiner is specifically waiting for the words
            "the PRIMARY structure remains intact". Without them the
            answer is incomplete.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — ENZYMES: NATURE AND MECHANISM                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are enzymes?" / "Explain the mechanism of enzyme
            action."
  METHOD    1. Definition: BIOCATALYSTS, chemically GLOBULAR PROTEINS,
               produced by living cells.
            2. Naming: substrate + "ase" (maltose -> maltase).
            3. Mechanism in three steps:
                  (i)   substrate binds at the ACTIVE SITE ->
                        ENZYME-SUBSTRATE COMPLEX (lock and key)
                  (ii)  the ACTIVATION ENERGY is lowered and the
                        reaction occurs
                  (iii) the product is RELEASED, the enzyme is free again
                  E + S <-> ES -> E + P
            4. Draw the lock-and-key diagram.
  TRAP      Write "lowers the ACTIVATION ENERGY", not "gives energy to
            the reaction". A catalyst supplies no energy.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — CHARACTERISTICS OF ENZYME CATALYSIS             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the characteristics of enzyme catalysis."
  METHOD    Number them, one line each:
            1. highly EFFICIENT (10^6 to 10^20 times faster)
            2. highly SPECIFIC (one enzyme, one reaction)
            3. optimum TEMPERATURE 298-310 K; denatured above it
            4. optimum pH 5-7 (pepsin ~2)
            5. needed in very small amounts, not consumed
            6. activity increased by ACTIVATORS and CO-ENZYMES
            7. destroyed by INHIBITORS / poisons
  TRAP      Give the actual NUMBERS for the optimum temperature and pH.
            Vague answers ("works best at body temperature") score less.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — VITAMINS: CLASSIFICATION AND DEFICIENCY         2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Classify vitamins" / "Name the deficiency disease of
            vitamin X" / "Why must vitamin C be taken daily?"
  METHOD    1. FAT SOLUBLE  A, D, E, K - stored in liver and fat,
               excess is toxic, need not be taken daily.
               WATER SOLUBLE B-complex and C - not stored (excreted in
               urine), must be supplied regularly.
            2. Table: vitamin / source / deficiency disease.
               A - night blindness, xerophthalmia
               B1 - beri beri        B2 - cheilosis
               B6 - convulsions      B12 - pernicious anaemia
               C - scurvy            D - rickets / osteomalacia
               E - sterility, fragile RBCs
               K - increased blood clotting time
  TRAP      Vitamin D deficiency is RICKETS in children and OSTEOMALACIA
            in adults - write both if the question says "rickets and ...".
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — NUCLEOSIDE vs NUCLEOTIDE                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a nucleotide?" / "Difference between a nucleoside
            and a nucleotide."
  METHOD    NUCLEOSIDE = sugar + base
            NUCLEOTIDE = sugar + base + PHOSPHATE
                       = nucleoside + phosphoric acid
            Add: the base is joined at C1' by a beta-N-glycosidic linkage,
            the phosphate at C5'; nucleotides join through
            PHOSPHODIESTER linkages.
  TRAP      nucleoTide has the T of phosphaTe. Use that hook and you will
            never swap them.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — PURINE AND PYRIMIDINE BASES                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Name the purine bases" / "Which base is present in RNA but
            not in DNA?"
  METHOD    PURINES (two rings)     : ADENINE , GUANINE   (in both)
            PYRIMIDINES (one ring)  : CYTOSINE (in both) ,
                                      THYMINE (DNA only) ,
                                      URACIL  (RNA only)
  TRAP      "PURE As Gold" = purines A and G. Everything else is a
            pyrimidine.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — DNA DOUBLE HELIX AND BASE PAIRING               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the double helix structure of DNA" / "Which bases
            pair, and by how many hydrogen bonds?"
  METHOD    1. TWO strands, RIGHT-HANDED double helix, ANTIPARALLEL.
            2. Sugar-phosphate backbone OUTSIDE, bases INSIDE.
            3. Held by HYDROGEN BONDS:
                  A == T   2 hydrogen bonds
                  G == C   3 hydrogen bonds
               A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine, so the diameter
               stays constant (2 nm).
            4. Hence %A = %T and %G = %C (Chargaff's rule).
            5. DRAW the flat ladder with S, P, and the base pairs.
  TRAP      "Antiparallel" and "the backbone is outside" are each worth
            a mark. Do not leave them out.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — CHARGAFF NUMERICAL                              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A DNA sample contains x% adenine. Find the % of G."
  METHOD    %A = %T and %G = %C , and the four add to 100.
                %G = %C = ( 100 - 2 x %A ) / 2  =  50 - %A
            Example: %A = 20  ->  %G = 50 - 20 = 30
  TRAP      This only works for DOUBLE-stranded DNA. Single-stranded RNA
            does NOT obey Chargaff's rule.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — DNA vs RNA COMPARISON                           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the differences between DNA and RNA."
  METHOD    Table, at least SIX rows:
               sugar     : 2-deoxy-D-ribose  |  D-ribose
               bases     : A,G,C,THYMINE     |  A,G,C,URACIL
               strands   : double helix      |  single
               location  : nucleus           |  cytoplasm, ribosomes
               stability : more stable       |  less stable
               function  : stores genetic    |  protein synthesis
                           information
               types     : one               |  m-RNA, t-RNA, r-RNA
  TRAP      Get the two "only" bases right: THYMINE only in DNA,
            URACIL only in RNA. That is the row the examiner checks first.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — BIOLOGICAL FUNCTIONS OF NUCLEIC ACIDS           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the biological functions of nucleic acids?" /
            "Explain replication and transcription."
  METHOD    REPLICATION   DNA -> DNA. The helix unzips, each strand is a
                          template, base pairing gives two identical
                          copies. Heredity is transmitted.
            TRANSCRIPTION DNA -> m-RNA. The message is copied, with
                          URACIL replacing thymine.
            TRANSLATION   m-RNA -> PROTEIN. The ribosome reads CODONS of
                          three bases; t-RNA brings the matching amino
                          acid; peptide bonds join them.
            End with the CENTRAL DOGMA:  DNA ---> RNA ---> PROTEIN
  TRAP      Do not mix up transcription and translation. "Trans-CRIPTION
            = writing (DNA to RNA)". "Trans-LATION = into another
            language (RNA to protein)".
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — HORMONES                                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are hormones? Give examples" / "Name the hormone whose
            deficiency causes diabetes."
  METHOD    1. Definition: chemical messengers secreted directly into the
               BLOOD by the ENDOCRINE (ductless) glands, which control
               and coordinate the activity of target organs.
            2. Types: STEROID (testosterone, estradiol),
               POLYPEPTIDE (insulin, glucagon),
               AMINO-ACID DERIVED (adrenaline, thyroxine).
            3. Key facts: insulin lowers blood glucose, a deficiency
               causes DIABETES MELLITUS; thyroxine needs IODINE, a
               deficiency causes GOITRE.
  TRAP      Hormones are NOT enzymes. An enzyme is a catalyst made where
            it acts; a hormone is a messenger carried by the blood.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — "DISTINGUISH BETWEEN X AND Y" (any pair)        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any question containing "differentiate", "distinguish",
            "compare" or "differences between".
  METHOD    1. ALWAYS draw a two-column table. Never write paragraphs.
            2. Give at least FOUR rows for 4 marks.
            3. Finish with an EXAMPLE row.
            The pairs that get asked:
               reducing / non-reducing sugar
               aldose / ketose
               alpha / beta anomer
               sucrose / maltose
               starch / cellulose
               amylose / amylopectin
               essential / non-essential amino acid
               fibrous / globular protein
               nucleoside / nucleotide
               purine / pyrimidine
               DNA / RNA
               enzyme / hormone
  TRAP      A table with only two rows cannot earn four marks. Count your
            rows before you move on.
```
