## Topic: Crystalline and amorphous solids

### Q A1
What are crystalline and amorphous solids? Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CRYSTALLINE SOLID - a solid in which the constituent particles are
  arranged in a definite, regularly repeating pattern that extends
  over a LONG RANGE.
       Example:  NaCl  (also diamond, quartz, ice, metals)

  AMORPHOUS SOLID   - a solid in which the particles show only
  SHORT RANGE order; there is no long range regularity.
       Example:  GLASS  (also rubber, plastic, starch)
```

---

### Q A2
Give any two differences between crystalline and amorphous solids.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  MELTING POINT
         Crystalline - has a SHARP, definite melting point.
         Amorphous   - has NO sharp melting point; it softens over
                       a range of temperature.

  2.  DIRECTIONAL PROPERTIES
         Crystalline - ANISOTROPIC (properties differ with direction)
         Amorphous   - ISOTROPIC   (properties are the same in all
                       directions)
```

---

### Q A3
What is meant by the term "amorphous"? Why are amorphous solids called
pseudo solids?

**ANSWER:**
```
  "Amorphous" comes from the Greek "amorphos" = NO FORM, i.e. having
  no definite geometrical shape.

  They are called PSEUDO SOLIDS (or SUPERCOOLED LIQUIDS) because they
  have only short range order like a LIQUID, no sharp melting point,
  and they can FLOW very slowly - yet they are rigid like a solid.
```

---

### Q A4
Define anisotropy. Which type of solid shows it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANISOTROPY - the property of having DIFFERENT physical properties
  (refractive index, electrical conductivity, thermal expansion) in
  DIFFERENT directions.

  Shown by CRYSTALLINE solids, because the arrangement of particles
  is different along different directions.
```

### Q A6
Why do ionic solids conduct electricity in the molten state but not in the
solid state?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In the SOLID state the ions are held at fixed lattice sites by strong
  electrostatic forces, so they cannot move -> no conduction.

  On MELTING these forces are overcome, the ions become FREE to move
  towards the electrodes, and the melt conducts electricity.
```

---

### Q A7
Why is graphite soft and a good conductor while diamond is hard and an insulator?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIAMOND  - each carbon is sp3 hybridised and uses ALL FOUR valence
             electrons in covalent bonds forming a rigid 3-D network.
             No free electron  ->  hard and INSULATOR.

  GRAPHITE - each carbon is sp2 hybridised and uses only THREE
             electrons in bonds; the fourth is DELOCALISED -> CONDUCTS.
             The layers are held by weak van der Waals forces and
             can SLIDE over one another  ->  SOFT and slippery.
```

## Topic: Unit cell and crystal lattice

### Q A8
Define crystal lattice and unit cell.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CRYSTAL LATTICE (space lattice) - the regular three dimensional
  arrangement of points in space, each point showing the position of
  a constituent particle. Every point has identical surroundings.

  UNIT CELL - the SMALLEST repeating portion of the crystal lattice
  which, when repeated in three dimensions, generates the whole lattice.
```

### Q A16
What are tetrahedral and octahedral voids? How many of each are present per atom
in a close packed structure?

**ANSWER:**
```
  TETRAHEDRAL VOID - the empty space surrounded by FOUR spheres whose
                     centres form a tetrahedron.
  OCTAHEDRAL VOID  - the empty space surrounded by SIX spheres whose
                     centres form an octahedron.

        tetrahedral voids  =  2 N     (2 per atom)
        octahedral voids   =    N     (1 per atom)
```

## Topic: Defects

### Q A19
What is a Schottky defect? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SCHOTTKY DEFECT - a stoichiometric point defect of ionic solids in
  which an EQUAL number of CATIONS and ANIONS are MISSING from their
  lattice sites, so electrical neutrality is maintained.

  It DECREASES the density of the crystal.

  Example:  NaCl   (also KCl, KBr, CsCl, AgBr)
```

---

### Q A20
What is a Frenkel defect? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  FRENKEL DEFECT (dislocation defect) - a stoichiometric point defect
  in which the SMALLER ion (usually the cation) leaves its own lattice
  site and occupies an INTERSTITIAL site.

  The density REMAINS UNCHANGED because no ion leaves the crystal.

  Example:  ZnS   (also AgCl, AgBr, AgI)
```

---

### Q A21
Which point defect lowers the density of a crystal? Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The SCHOTTKY defect.

  Because a cation and an anion actually LEAVE the crystal, the mass
  of the unit cell decreases while its volume stays the same.
        d = mass / volume    ->    d DECREASES.
```

---

### Q A22
What are F-centres? What is their effect?

**ANSWER:**
```
  F-CENTRE - an ANION VACANCY in a crystal that is occupied by an
  UNPAIRED ELECTRON.  ("F" is from the German Farbenzentre = colour
  centre.)

  EFFECT:  the trapped electron absorbs visible light, so the crystal
  becomes COLOURED, and because the electron can move, the solid
  becomes an n-type semiconductor.

  Example:  NaCl heated in sodium vapour turns YELLOW.
```

---

### Q A23
Why does ZnO turn yellow on heating?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ZnO  --(heat)-->  Zn2+  +  (1/2) O2  +  2 e-

  The Zn2+ ions move into interstitial sites and the released electrons
  occupy neighbouring interstitial sites. This METAL EXCESS defect
  traps electrons which absorb visible light, so ZnO turns YELLOW.
  It becomes white again on cooling.
```

---

### Q A24
Why does NaCl doped with SrCl2 develop cation vacancies?

**ANSWER:**
```
  One Sr2+ ion replaces TWO Na+ ions to keep the charge balanced, but
  it occupies only ONE of those two sites. The other site is left
  EMPTY.

  Hence the number of CATION VACANCIES created equals the number of
  Sr2+ ions added.
```

## Topic: Electrical and magnetic properties

### Q A25
What are n-type and p-type semiconductors?

**ANSWER:**
```
  n-TYPE - a semiconductor doped with a GROUP 15 element (P, As, Sb).
           The extra fifth electron is free, so the current is carried
           by NEGATIVE electrons.

  p-TYPE - a semiconductor doped with a GROUP 13 element (B, Al, Ga).
           An electron HOLE is created, so the current is carried by
           POSITIVE holes.
```

---

### Q A26
What is doping? Why is it done?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DOPING - the deliberate addition of a very small, controlled amount
  of a suitable impurity to a pure semiconductor.

  It is done to INCREASE the electrical conductivity enormously, by
  supplying either extra free electrons (n-type) or electron holes
  (p-type).
```

---

### Q A27
Give two examples each of 13-15 and 12-16 compound semiconductors.

**ANSWER:**
```
  13-15 compounds :   InSb , GaAs   (also AlP, GaP)
  12-16 compounds :   ZnS  , CdS    (also CdSe, HgTe)

  In both, the AVERAGE number of valence electrons per atom is 4,
  like silicon.
```

---

### Q A28
Define ferromagnetic and ferrimagnetic substances with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  FERROMAGNETIC  - substances whose magnetic moments in the domains
  are all aligned in the SAME direction, giving a very large net
  moment. They are strongly attracted and can be permanently
  magnetised.        Example:  Fe  (also Co, Ni, CrO2)

  FERRIMAGNETIC  - moments aligned in OPPOSITE directions but in
  UNEQUAL numbers, so a SMALL net moment remains. Weakly attracted.
                     Example:  Fe3O4  (also MgFe2O4)
```

---

### Q A29
What is an antiferromagnetic substance? Give an example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A substance in which the magnetic moments of the domains are
  aligned in OPPOSITE directions in EQUAL numbers, so they cancel
  each other EXACTLY and the net magnetic moment is ZERO.

  Example:  MnO   (also MnO2, FeO, NiO, Cr2O3)
```

---

### Q A30
Distinguish between paramagnetic and diamagnetic substances.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PARAMAGNETIC - have UNPAIRED electrons, are WEAKLY ATTRACTED by a
                 magnetic field.        Example:  O2 , Cu2+

  DIAMAGNETIC  - have ALL electrons PAIRED, are WEAKLY REPELLED by a
                 magnetic field.        Example:  NaCl , H2O , TiO2
```

### Q B1
Explain the classification of crystalline solids on the basis of the nature of the
constituent particles and the forces between them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  There are FOUR types.

  1. IONIC SOLIDS
       particles - positive and negative IONS
       force     - strong electrostatic (coulombic) force
       nature    - hard but brittle, high melting point
       conduct   - only when molten or in aqueous solution
       examples  - NaCl , MgO , ZnS , CaF2

  2. COVALENT (NETWORK) SOLIDS
       particles - ATOMS
       force     - covalent bonds running through the whole crystal
       nature    - very hard, very high melting point
       conduct   - insulators (GRAPHITE is the exception)
       examples  - diamond , SiC , SiO2 (quartz)

  3. MOLECULAR SOLIDS
       particles - MOLECULES
       (a) non-polar : London dispersion forces   H2 , I2 , CO2
       (b) polar     : dipole-dipole forces       solid HCl , SO2
       (c) H-bonded  : hydrogen bonds             ICE (H2O)
       nature    - soft, low melting point, insulators

  4. METALLIC SOLIDS
       particles - positive kernels in a SEA OF FREE ELECTRONS
       force     - metallic bond
       nature    - malleable, ductile, lustrous
       conduct   - good conductors in the SOLID and molten state
       examples  - Cu , Ag , Fe , alloys
```

### Q B3
Derive the number of atoms present in simple cubic, body centred cubic and face
centred cubic unit cells.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SHARING RULE
        an atom at a CORNER is shared by 8 cells   ->  1/8
        an atom at an EDGE   is shared by 4 cells  ->  1/4
        an atom at a FACE    is shared by 2 cells  ->  1/2
        an atom at the BODY centre belongs to 1 cell ->  1

  SIMPLE CUBIC        atoms only at 8 corners
        Z  =  8 x 1/8  =  1

  BODY CENTRED CUBIC  8 corners + 1 body centre
        corners :  8 x 1/8  =  1
        body    :  1 x 1    =  1
        Z  =  1 + 1  =  2

  FACE CENTRED CUBIC  8 corners + 6 face centres
        corners :  8 x 1/8  =  1
        faces   :  6 x 1/2  =  3
        Z  =  1 + 3  =  4
```

### Q B4
Derive the packing efficiency of a body centred cubic unit cell.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In BCC the atoms touch along the BODY DIAGONAL.

     face diagonal  =  sqrt( a^2 + a^2 )  =  sqrt(2) a
     body diagonal  =  sqrt( 2a^2 + a^2 ) =  sqrt(3) a

  Along the body diagonal lie 3 atoms:  r + 2r + r  =  4 r

        sqrt(3) a  =  4 r        ->      a  =  4 r / sqrt(3)

  Number of atoms per cell,  Z  =  2

        volume of atoms  =  2 x (4/3) pi r^3  =  (8/3) pi r^3

        volume of cell   =  a^3  =  64 r^3 / ( 3 sqrt(3) )

                        (8/3) pi r^3
        P.E.  =  100 x ---------------------
                       64 r^3 / (3 sqrt 3)

                        8 pi      3 sqrt(3)          pi sqrt(3)
              =  100 x ------- x -----------  = 100 x -----------
                          3          64                    8

                        3.1416 x 1.732
              =  100 x ----------------  =  68.02 %
                              8

  PACKING EFFICIENCY OF BCC  =  68 %      (free space 32 %)
```

### Q B15
Explain Schottky and Frenkel defects with diagrams, and give the difference between
them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SCHOTTKY DEFECT - an EQUAL number of cations and anions are MISSING.

        +  -  +  -  +                  +  -  +  -  +
        -  +  -  +  -     ---->        -  +     +  -    <- anion missing
        +  -  +  -  +                  +     +  -  +    <- cation missing
        -  +  -  +  -                  -  +  -  +  -

  FRENKEL DEFECT - the smaller ion LEAVES its site and sits in an
                   INTERSTITIAL space.

        +  -  +  -  +                  +  -  +  -  +
        -  +  -  +  -     ---->        -  +  -  +  -
        +  -  +  -  +                  +  -     -  +    <- site vacated
        -  +  -  +  -                  -  + (+) +  -    <- ion in a gap

  +--------------------+------------------+-------------------+
  |  POINT             |  SCHOTTKY        |  FRENKEL          |
  +--------------------+------------------+-------------------+
  |  What happens      |  ion PAIR missing|  ion MOVES to an  |
  |                    |                  |  interstitial site|
```

### Q B16
Explain metal excess and metal deficiency defects with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  METAL EXCESS DEFECT - the metal is present in more than the
  stoichiometric amount. It arises in two ways.

  (a) ANION VACANCY
        NaCl is heated in sodium vapour.
             Na (vapour)  ->  Na+  +  e-
        The Na+ sits on the surface and the electron occupies a
        Cl- vacancy inside. That trapped electron is an F-CENTRE.
        Formula becomes  Na(1+x)Cl , and the crystal turns YELLOW.

  (b) EXTRA CATION IN AN INTERSTITIAL SITE
             ZnO  --(heat)-->  Zn2+ + (1/2)O2 + 2 e-
        Zn2+ enters an interstitial site and the electrons occupy
        neighbouring interstitial sites.  Formula  Zn(1+x)O .
        ZnO turns YELLOW on heating and white again on cooling.

  Both give n-TYPE semiconductors.

  METAL DEFICIENCY DEFECT - the metal is present in LESS than the
  stoichiometric amount.
        FeO is nearly always found as  Fe(0.95)O .
        For every 3 Fe2+ ions lost, 2 Fe3+ ions take their place:
             3 Fe2+   ->   2 Fe3+  +  one cation vacancy
        The holes make it a p-TYPE semiconductor.
```

### Q B17
Explain how a semiconductor is made n-type and p-type by doping.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Silicon and germanium belong to GROUP 14 and have FOUR valence
  electrons, all used in bonding.

  n-TYPE   -  dope with a GROUP 15 element (P, As, Sb).
              Four of its five electrons form bonds; the FIFTH is
              left FREE.

                    Si     Si     Si
                      \   /  \   /
                       Si -- P -- Si          * = free electron
                      /   \ *|*  \
                    Si     Si     Si

              Conduction is by FREE ELECTRONS (negative) -> n-type.

  p-TYPE   -  dope with a GROUP 13 element (B, Al, Ga).
              It has only THREE electrons, so one bonding position
              stays EMPTY - an electron HOLE.

                    Si     Si     Si
                      \   /  \   /
                       Si -- B -- Si          o = electron hole
                      /   \  o|   \
                    Si     Si     Si

              A neighbouring electron hops in and the hole moves the
              other way. Conduction is by POSITIVE holes -> p-type.
```

### Q B18
Explain the magnetic properties of solids with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. PARAMAGNETIC     - have UNPAIRED electrons, WEAKLY ATTRACTED by
                        a magnetic field, lose magnetism when the
                        field is removed.         Example: O2 , Cu2+

  2. DIAMAGNETIC      - all electrons PAIRED, WEAKLY REPELLED.
                                                  Example: NaCl , H2O

  3. FERROMAGNETIC    - all domain moments PARALLEL, STRONGLY
       ^ ^ ^ ^ ^        attracted, can be permanently magnetised.
                                                  Example: Fe , Co , Ni

  4. ANTIFERROMAGNETIC- moments OPPOSITE and EQUAL, so they cancel;
       ^ v ^ v ^        net moment ZERO.          Example: MnO

  5. FERRIMAGNETIC    - moments OPPOSITE but UNEQUAL, leaving a SMALL
       ^ ^ v ^ ^ v      net moment; weakly attracted.
                                                  Example: Fe3O4
```

### Q C3
What are point defects? Explain stoichiometric and non-stoichiometric defects in
detail with examples.

**ANSWER (mark split: 1 + 3 + 3 + 1):**

```
  POINT DEFECT - an irregularity or deviation from the ideal
  arrangement around a POINT or an ATOM in a crystal.     [1 mark]

  A crystal is perfect only at 0 K. Above that, thermal vibration
  displaces some particles, so every real crystal has defects.
  Defects INCREASE with temperature.

                        POINT DEFECTS
                             |
           +-----------------+------------------+
           |                 |                  |
      STOICHIOMETRIC   NON-STOICHIOMETRIC   IMPURITY


  (A) STOICHIOMETRIC DEFECTS  (the cation : anion ratio is unchanged)
                                                          [3 marks]

      1. SCHOTTKY DEFECT
         An equal number of cations AND anions are missing.

              +  -  +  -  +                +  -  +  -  +
              -  +  -  +  -    ---->       -  +     +  -
              +  -  +  -  +                +     +  -  +
              -  +  -  +  -                -  +  -  +  -

         Favoured by:  HIGH coordination number and a SMALL
                       difference in the sizes of the ions.
         Effect     :  DENSITY DECREASES.
         Examples   :  NaCl , KCl , KBr , CsCl , AgBr

      2. FRENKEL DEFECT (dislocation defect)
         The smaller ion leaves its site and occupies an
         interstitial site.

              +  -  +  -  +                +  -  +  -  +
              -  +  -  +  -    ---->       -  +  -  +  -
              +  -  +  -  +                +  -     -  +
              -  +  -  +  -                -  + (+) +  -

         Favoured by:  LOW coordination number and a LARGE
                       difference in the sizes of the ions.
         Effect     :  DENSITY UNCHANGED ; DIELECTRIC CONSTANT
                       INCREASES.
         Examples   :  ZnS , AgCl , AgBr , AgI


  (B) NON-STOICHIOMETRIC DEFECTS  (the ratio DOES change)  [3 marks]

      1. METAL EXCESS by ANION VACANCY
             NaCl heated in Na vapour:
                  Na  ->  Na+  +  e-
             The electron occupies a Cl- vacancy = F-CENTRE.
             The crystal turns YELLOW.
             KCl in K vapour -> VIOLET ; LiCl in Li vapour -> PINK.

                  Na+ Cl- Na+ Cl-
                  Cl- Na+  e-  Na+     <- F-centre
                  Na+ Cl- Na+ Cl-

      2. METAL EXCESS by EXTRA CATION in an interstitial site
             ZnO --(heat)--> Zn2+ + (1/2) O2 + 2 e-
             Zn2+ and the electrons occupy interstitial sites.
             ZnO turns YELLOW on heating.  Formula Zn(1+x)O.
             Both types give n-TYPE semiconductors.

      3. METAL DEFICIENCY
             FeO is really Fe(0.95)O.
                  3 Fe2+  ->  2 Fe3+  +  one cation vacancy
             Gives a p-TYPE semiconductor.


  (C) IMPURITY DEFECT                                     [1 mark]

      SrCl2 added to molten NaCl: one Sr2+ replaces two Na+ but
      occupies only one site, so ONE CATION VACANCY is created for
      every Sr2+ ion added.
```

```
  BAND THEORY                                              [3 marks]

  When a large number of atoms come close together their atomic

     VALENCE BAND    - the band containing the valence electrons

     ///conduction///      ///conduction///       ///conduction///
     ===============             |                       |
      OVERLAP or             SMALL GAP              LARGE GAP
      PARTLY FILLED          0.5 - 3 eV              > 3 eV
     ===============             |                       |
     \\\  valence \\\      \\\ valence \\\        \\\ valence \\\

     electrons move        a few electrons        no electron can
     freely                cross on heating       cross the gap

  |  INSULATOR      |  10^-20 to 10^-10 |  practically none         |
  +-----------------+-------------------+---------------------------+
```
