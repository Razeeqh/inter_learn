## Topic: Alcohols

### Q A1
What is the Lucas test? How is it used to distinguish primary, secondary and
tertiary alcohols?

**ANSWER:**
```
  LUCAS REAGENT  =  conc. HCl  +  anhydrous ZnCl2

  R-OH  +  HCl  --anhyd. ZnCl2-->  R-Cl  +  H2O

  R-Cl is insoluble in the reagent, so it shows up as CLOUDINESS (turbidity).

     TERTIARY   ->  turbidity IMMEDIATELY
     SECONDARY  ->  turbidity in about 5 minutes
     PRIMARY    ->  NO turbidity at room temperature
```

### Q A2
Write the equation for the hydroboration-oxidation of propene. Name the type
of product obtained.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CH3-CH=CH2   --(i) B2H6  (ii) H2O2 / OH- -->   CH3-CH2-CH2-OH

  The product is propan-1-ol, a PRIMARY alcohol.
  This is an ANTI-MARKOVNIKOV addition of water.
```

### Q A3
How is propan-2-ol prepared from propene? Which rule is followed?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CH3-CH=CH2   --H2O / dil. H2SO4-->   CH3-CH(OH)-CH3

  MARKOVNIKOV'S RULE is followed: the -OH goes to the carbon carrying the
  smaller number of hydrogen atoms.
```

### Q A4
Why is the Grignard reaction always carried out in dry ether?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A Grignard reagent reacts instantly with any compound containing an
  active hydrogen, including water:

      CH3-Mg-Br  +  H2O   -->   CH4  +  Mg(OH)Br

  If moisture is present the reagent is destroyed as methane before it can
  react with the carbonyl compound. Hence dry (anhydrous) ether is used.
```

## Topic: Phenols

### Q A8
Why is phenol more acidic than ethanol?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Phenol loses H+ to give the PHENOXIDE ion, in which the negative charge
  is delocalised over the benzene ring by RESONANCE. This makes the ion
  very stable.

  Ethanol gives the ETHOXIDE ion, in which the charge stays localised on
  oxygen, and the ethyl group further destabilises it by its
  electron-releasing (+I) effect.

  A more stable anion means easier ionisation, so PHENOL IS MORE ACIDIC.
```

### Q A14
Arrange in increasing order of acidity: phenol, p-nitrophenol, p-cresol.
Give the reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
  p-cresol   <   phenol   <   p-nitrophenol

  -CH3 in p-cresol RELEASES electrons (+I), destabilising the phenoxide
  ion, so acidity decreases.

  -NO2 in p-nitrophenol WITHDRAWS electrons (-I and -R), stabilising the
  phenoxide ion, so acidity increases.
```

## Topic: Ethers

### Q A15
What is Williamson's synthesis? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An alkyl halide is heated with sodium alkoxide to give an ether.

      R-X   +   R'-ONa    -->   R-O-R'   +   NaX

  Example:
      CH3-CH2-Br  +  CH3-ONa   -->   CH3-CH2-O-CH3   +   NaBr

  The alkyl halide used must be PRIMARY, otherwise elimination gives an
  alkene instead.
```

### Q A16
What happens when anisole is heated with HI?

**ANSWER:**
```
  C6H5-O-CH3   +   HI    -->    C6H5-OH   +   CH3-I

  PHENOL and methyl iodide are formed.
  Iodobenzene is NEVER formed, because the C-O bond attached to the ring
  has partial double-bond character and is too strong to break.
```

### Q A17
Why do ethers have lower boiling points than alcohols of comparable
molecular mass?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Alcohol molecules form INTERMOLECULAR HYDROGEN BONDS with one another,
  which need extra energy to break.

  An ether has no hydrogen attached to its oxygen, so ether molecules
  cannot hydrogen bond with each other. Only weak dipole-dipole forces
  act between them, so ethers boil at a lower temperature.
```

### Q A23
Why does benzaldehyde not respond to Fehling's test although it responds to
Tollens' test?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Fehling's solution is a WEAKER oxidising agent and works only on
  ALIPHATIC aldehydes.

  Benzaldehyde is an AROMATIC aldehyde. Its -CHO group is stabilised by
  resonance with the ring and is not oxidised by the mild Cu2+ complex,
  so no red precipitate of Cu2O is formed.

  Tollens' reagent is a stronger oxidant, so benzaldehyde does give the
  silver mirror.
```

### Q A28
Why are aldehydes more reactive than ketones towards nucleophilic addition?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. ELECTRONIC REASON - a ketone has TWO alkyl groups pushing electrons
     (+I effect) towards the carbonyl carbon, an aldehyde has only ONE.
     So the carbonyl carbon in a ketone is less positive and attracts the
     nucleophile less strongly.

  2. STERIC REASON - the two bulky alkyl groups in a ketone block the
     approach of the nucleophile. In an aldehyde one of them is only a
     small hydrogen atom.
```

### Q A32
Why is formic acid stronger than acetic acid?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In acetic acid the -CH3 group RELEASES electrons (+I effect) towards
  the -COO- group, increasing the negative charge on the carboxylate ion
  and destabilising it.

  Formic acid, HCOOH, has only a hydrogen atom there, with no
  electron-releasing effect, so its anion HCOO- is more stable.

  Hence HCOOH is the stronger acid.
```

---

### Q A33
Arrange in increasing order of acidity:
CH3-COOH , ClCH2-COOH , Cl2CH-COOH , Cl3C-COOH.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CH3-COOH  <  ClCH2-COOH  <  Cl2CH-COOH  <  Cl3C-COOH

  Each chlorine atom exerts an electron-withdrawing (-I) effect which
  pulls electron density away from the -COO- group and stabilises the
  carboxylate ion. More chlorines = greater stabilisation = stronger acid.
```

---

### Q A34
Why is a carboxylic acid a stronger acid than phenol?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In the CARBOXYLATE ion the negative charge is shared equally between
  TWO EQUIVALENT OXYGEN atoms by resonance. Oxygen is highly
  electronegative and holds the charge comfortably, so the ion is very
  stable.

  In the PHENOXIDE ion the charge is delocalised onto CARBON atoms of the
  ring, which are less electronegative and hold the charge poorly, and
  the structures are not equivalent.

  So the carboxylate ion is more stable, and the acid is stronger.
```

### Q B1
Explain the acidity of phenols. How is it affected by substituents on the
ring? Give examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. WHY PHENOL IS ACIDIC
     C6H5-OH  <-->  C6H5-O-  +  H+
     The phenoxide ion is stabilised by resonance - the negative charge is
     delocalised over the ortho and para carbons of the ring.
     There are FOUR resonating structures, so the ion is stable and the
     H+ leaves easily.

  2. COMPARISON WITH ALCOHOL
     The ethoxide ion has no resonance; the charge sits on one oxygen and
     is pushed further by the +I effect of the ethyl group.
     Therefore  phenol  >  water  >  alcohol  in acidity.

  3. ELECTRON-WITHDRAWING SUBSTITUENTS (-NO2, -CN, -CHO, -X)
     They pull electron density away from the O-, spreading the charge
     further and STABILISING the phenoxide ion.
     ->  ACIDITY INCREASES.
     Example: p-nitrophenol is more acidic than phenol; 2,4,6-trinitro-
     phenol (picric acid) is the most acidic of all.

  4. ELECTRON-RELEASING SUBSTITUENTS (-CH3, -OCH3, -NH2)
     They push electron density towards the O-, increasing the charge
     density and DESTABILISING the phenoxide ion.
     ->  ACIDITY DECREASES.
     Example: p-cresol is less acidic than phenol.

  ORDER:
     picric acid > p-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol > p-cresol
```

### Q B3
Explain Williamson's synthesis. Why must the alkyl halide be primary?

**ANSWER:**
```
  METHOD
     An alkyl halide is heated with a sodium alkoxide:
        R-X  +  R'-ONa   -->   R-O-R'  +  NaX

     Example:
        CH3-CH2-Br  +  CH3-ONa  -->  CH3-CH2-O-CH3  +  NaBr

     The reaction is an SN2 substitution: the alkoxide ion attacks the
     carbon bearing the halogen from the opposite side.

  WHY THE HALIDE MUST BE PRIMARY
     An alkoxide ion is not only a nucleophile, it is also a STRONG BASE.
     With a secondary or tertiary halide the crowded carbon cannot be
     attacked from behind, so the alkoxide instead removes a beta
     hydrogen and ELIMINATION occurs, giving an alkene.

        (CH3)3C-Br + CH3-ONa --> (CH3)2C=CH2 + CH3-OH + NaBr
                                 (alkene, NOT the ether)

  CORRECT CHOICE for tert-butyl methyl ether:
        CH3-I  +  (CH3)3C-ONa   -->   (CH3)3C-O-CH3
     i.e. put the halogen on the SMALLER, primary group.
```

### Q B4
Explain the cleavage of ethers by HX with suitable examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Ethers are broken by hot concentrated HI or HBr:

        R-O-R'  +  HX   -->   R-X  +  R'-OH

  RULE 1 - BOTH GROUPS PRIMARY:  the halide comes from the SMALLER group,
  because SN2 attack occurs at the less hindered carbon.

        CH3-O-CH2-CH3  +  HI  -->  CH3-I  +  CH3-CH2-OH

  RULE 2 - ONE GROUP TERTIARY:  the halide comes from the TERTIARY group,
  because the reaction goes by SN1 through a stable 3deg carbocation.

        (CH3)3C-O-CH3  +  HI  -->  (CH3)3C-I  +  CH3-OH

  RULE 3 - ONE GROUP ARYL (anisole):  the halide always comes from the
  ALKYL group, and PHENOL is the other product.

        C6H5-O-CH3  +  HI  -->  C6H5-OH  +  CH3-I

  Reason: the bond between oxygen and the ring carbon has partial double
  bond character due to resonance and is too strong to break.
  Iodobenzene is never formed.
```

### Q B6
How are primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols distinguished by
oxidation and by the Lucas test?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BY OXIDATION (K2Cr2O7 / H+):

     PRIMARY    CH3-CH2-OH  -->  CH3-CHO  -->  CH3-COOH
                gives an aldehyde first, then a CARBOXYLIC ACID with the
                SAME number of carbon atoms.

     SECONDARY  CH3-CH(OH)-CH3  -->  CH3-CO-CH3
                gives a KETONE with the same number of carbons, and
                oxidation stops there.

     TERTIARY   (CH3)3C-OH  -->  no reaction under mild conditions.
                Under drastic conditions the carbon chain BREAKS and a
                mixture of smaller acids and ketones is formed.

  BY THE LUCAS TEST (conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl2):

     TERTIARY   -> turbidity appears IMMEDIATELY
     SECONDARY  -> turbidity appears in about 5 minutes
     PRIMARY    -> NO turbidity at room temperature

     Reason: the reaction proceeds through a carbocation, and the
     stability order is 3deg > 2deg > 1deg.
```

### Q B7
Explain the mechanism of dehydration of ethanol to ethene.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Reaction:  CH3-CH2-OH  --conc. H2SO4, 443 K-->  CH2=CH2  +  H2O

  MECHANISM (E1, three steps):

  STEP 1  PROTONATION (fast)
          The oxygen of the -OH picks up a proton from the acid.

          CH3-CH2-OH  +  H+   -->   CH3-CH2-OH2(+)

  STEP 2  FORMATION OF THE CARBOCATION (slow, rate determining)
          Water is a good leaving group and departs.

          CH3-CH2-OH2(+)   -->   CH3-CH2(+)   +   H2O

  STEP 3  LOSS OF A PROTON (fast)
          A base removes a hydrogen from the beta carbon and the double
          bond forms.

          CH3-CH2(+)   -->   CH2=CH2   +   H+

  Because a carbocation is involved, the ease of dehydration follows
       3deg  >  2deg  >  1deg
```

### Q B8
Explain the mechanism of nucleophilic addition to a carbonyl compound.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The carbonyl group is polar:  the carbon is (delta+) and the oxygen is
  (delta-). The positive carbon attracts nucleophiles.

  STEP 1  (SLOW, rate determining)
          The nucleophile Nu(-) attacks the carbonyl carbon from above or
          below the plane. The pi electrons shift onto oxygen and the
          carbon changes from sp2 (planar) to sp3 (tetrahedral).

                 O                        O(-)
                 ||                       |
            R -- C -- H     ---->    R -- C -- H
                 ^                        |
                 |                        Nu
               Nu(-)

  STEP 2  (FAST)
          The alkoxide oxygen picks up a proton from the medium.

                 O(-)                     OH
                 |                        |
            R -- C -- H   + H+ ---->  R --C -- H
                 |                        |
                 Nu                       Nu

  RESULT: the C=O double bond has become a C-OH single bond and the
  nucleophile is attached to the carbon.

  Examples of Nu(-): CN- (gives cyanohydrin), HSO3- (bisulphite adduct),
  R-MgX (gives alcohols).
```

### Q B9
Explain the aldol condensation with its mechanism.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  Two molecules of an aldehyde or ketone having at least one ALPHA
  HYDROGEN combine in dilute alkali to give a beta-hydroxy carbonyl
  compound (an aldol), which loses water on heating.

     2 CH3-CHO --dil. NaOH--> CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CHO --heat--> CH3-CH=CH-CHO

  MECHANISM

  STEP 1  The base removes an alpha hydrogen to give a resonance
          stabilised CARBANION.

             CH3-CHO  +  OH-   -->   (-)CH2-CHO   +   H2O

  STEP 2  The carbanion attacks the carbonyl carbon of a second aldehyde
          molecule (nucleophilic addition).

             CH3-CHO  +  (-)CH2-CHO  -->  CH3-CH(O-)-CH2-CHO

### Q B10
Explain the Cannizzaro reaction with its mechanism.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  An aldehyde having NO alpha hydrogen, on treatment with concentrated
  alkali, undergoes DISPROPORTIONATION - one molecule is oxidised to the
  salt of an acid while another is reduced to an alcohol.

     2 C6H5-CHO --conc. NaOH--> C6H5-CH2-OH + C6H5-COONa

  MECHANISM

  STEP 1  The hydroxide ion adds to the carbonyl carbon.

             C6H5-CHO  +  OH-   -->   C6H5-CH(O-)-OH

  STEP 2  This intermediate transfers a HYDRIDE ION (H:-) to the carbonyl
          carbon of a second aldehyde molecule. (Key step.)

             C6H5-CH(O-)-OH + C6H5-CHO --> C6H5-COOH + C6H5-CH2-O(-)

  STEP 3  Proton exchange gives the final products.

             C6H5-COOH + C6H5-CH2-O(-) --> C6H5-COO(-) + C6H5-CH2-OH

  Other examples:  2 HCHO --conc. NaOH--> CH3-OH + HCOONa

  CROSSED CANNIZZARO: with a mixture, formaldehyde is always oxidised and
  the other aldehyde is reduced.
     HCHO + C6H5-CHO --conc. NaOH--> C6H5-CH2-OH + HCOONa
```

### Q B13
Explain the effect of substituents on the acidity of carboxylic acids with
examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A carboxylic acid ionises as
        R-COOH  <-->  R-COO-  +  H+
  and the more STABLE the carboxylate ion, the stronger the acid.

  1. ELECTRON-WITHDRAWING GROUPS (-I effect: -NO2, -CN, -F, -Cl, -Br)
     They pull electron density away from the -COO- group and disperse
     the negative charge, STABILISING the anion.

     The -I effect weakens rapidly with distance from -COOH.

        CH3-CH2-CHCl-COOH > CH3-CHCl-CH2-COOH > CH2Cl-CH2-CH2-COOH
             (alpha)             (beta)             (gamma)

  4. ELECTRON-RELEASING GROUPS (+I effect: alkyl groups)
     They push electron density into the -COO- group and DESTABILISE it.
     ->  ACIDITY DECREASES.

        HCOOH > CH3-COOH > CH3-CH2-COOH > (CH3)3C-COOH

  5. AROMATIC ACIDS
        p-nitrobenzoic acid > benzoic acid > p-methylbenzoic acid

### Q B15
Explain why aldehydes are more reactive than ketones towards nucleophilic
addition. Arrange the following in decreasing order of reactivity:
HCHO, CH3-CHO, CH3-CO-CH3, C6H5-CO-CH3.

**ANSWER:**
```
  REASON 1 - INDUCTIVE EFFECT
  An alkyl group is electron-releasing (+I). It pushes electron density
  towards the carbonyl carbon and reduces its positive charge.
  A ketone has TWO such groups, an aldehyde only ONE (the other position
  is occupied by a small hydrogen). So the carbonyl carbon of a ketone is
  less electrophilic and attracts nucleophiles less strongly.

  REASON 2 - STERIC HINDRANCE
  In a ketone, two bulky alkyl groups surround the carbonyl carbon and
  physically block the approach of the nucleophile. Also, the transition
  state becomes more crowded when the carbon changes from planar sp2 to
  tetrahedral sp3.

  REASON 3 - RESONANCE (for aromatic carbonyls)
  In C6H5-CO-CH3 the ring donates electron density into the carbonyl
  group by resonance, making the carbon even less positive.

  ORDER OF REACTIVITY:
     HCHO  >  CH3-CHO  >  CH3-CO-CH3  >  C6H5-CO-CH3
```

  alkali.

     2 HCHO --conc. NaOH--> CH3-OH + HCOONa
     2 C6H5-CHO --conc. NaOH--> C6H5-CH2-OH + C6H5-COONa

  MECHANISM
     Step 1: OH- adds to the carbonyl carbon.
     Step 2: the intermediate transfers a HYDRIDE ION to a second
             aldehyde molecule - one is oxidised, the other reduced.
     Step 3: proton exchange gives the alcohol and the carboxylate.


  (c) CLEMMENSEN AND WOLFF-KISHNER REDUCTIONS  (2 marks)

  Both convert C=O into CH2.

     CLEMMENSEN (acidic):
     CH3-CO-CH3 --Zn-Hg / conc. HCl--> CH3-CH2-CH3 + H2O

     WOLFF-KISHNER (basic):
     CH3-CO-CH3 + NH2NH2 --> CH3-C(=N-NH2)-CH3
     CH3-C(=N-NH2)-CH3 --KOH / ethylene glycol, 453-473 K-->
                                          CH3-CH2-CH3 + N2

  Use Clemmensen when the compound is stable to acid and Wolff-Kishner
  when it is stable to base.


  (d) TESTS  (1 mark)

     TOLLENS'  : CH3-CHO gives a SILVER MIRROR; ketones give nothing.
     FEHLING'S : aliphatic aldehydes give a RED ppt of Cu2O; ketones and
                 aromatic aldehydes give nothing.
     IODOFORM  : yellow CHI3 from methyl ketones and methyl carbinols.
     2,4-DNP   : orange ppt from ALL aldehydes and ketones - use it first
                 to prove a carbonyl group is present, then use Tollens'
                 to decide aldehyde or ketone.

### Q C6
Write notes on ethers: their preparation, physical properties and
chemical reactions, including electrophilic substitution in anisole.

**MODEL ANSWER:**
```
  PART A - PREPARATION (2 marks)

  1. WILLIAMSON SYNTHESIS
     R-X + R'-ONa --> R-O-R' + NaX
     CH3-CH2-Br + CH3-ONa --> CH3-CH2-O-CH3 + NaBr

     The alkyl halide must be PRIMARY. With a tertiary halide the
     alkoxide acts as a base and elimination gives an alkene instead:
     (CH3)3C-Br + CH3-ONa --> (CH3)2C=CH2 + CH3-OH + NaBr

  2. DEHYDRATION OF ALCOHOLS
     2 CH3-CH2-OH --conc. H2SO4, 413 K--> CH3-CH2-O-CH2-CH3 + H2O
     Suitable only for primary alcohols and symmetrical ethers.


  PART B - PHYSICAL PROPERTIES (2 marks)

  1. The C-O-C bond angle is about 111 degrees and the molecule is bent,
     so ethers have a small dipole moment.

  2. Ethers have MUCH LOWER boiling points than alcohols of similar mass,
     because there is no hydrogen attached to the oxygen and so no
     intermolecular hydrogen bonding between ether molecules.
     Example: ethanol boils at 351 K, dimethyl ether at 248 K.

  3. Ethers are appreciably soluble in water, because the ether oxygen
     can ACCEPT a hydrogen bond from water even though it cannot donate
     one.


  PART C - CHEMICAL REACTIONS (4 marks)

  1. CLEAVAGE BY HX
     R-O-R' + HX --> R-X + R'-OH

     (i)  Both groups primary - the halide comes from the SMALLER group:
          CH3-O-CH2-CH3 + HI --> CH3-I + CH3-CH2-OH
     (ii) One group tertiary - the halide comes from the TERTIARY group:
          (CH3)3C-O-CH3 + HI --> (CH3)3C-I + CH3-OH
     (iii)Aryl ether (anisole) - PHENOL is always formed:
          C6H5-O-CH3 + HI --> C6H5-OH + CH3-I
          Iodobenzene is never formed, because the C-O bond to the ring
          has partial double bond character.

  2. ELECTROPHILIC SUBSTITUTION IN ANISOLE
     The -OCH3 group releases electron density into the ring by
     resonance, so it is ACTIVATING and ORTHO/PARA directing. The para
     product usually predominates because the bulky -OCH3 group hinders
     the ortho positions.

     HALOGENATION:
        C6H5-O-CH3 --Br2 / CH3COOH--> p-bromoanisole (major)

     NITRATION:
        C6H5-O-CH3 --conc. HNO3 + conc. H2SO4-->
                                    o- and p-nitroanisole

     FRIEDEL-CRAFTS ALKYLATION:
        C6H5-O-CH3 --CH3Cl / anhyd. AlCl3--> o- and p-methylanisole

     FRIEDEL-CRAFTS ACYLATION:
        C6H5-O-CH3 --CH3-CO-Cl / anhyd. AlCl3-->
                                    o- and p-methoxyacetophenone
```
