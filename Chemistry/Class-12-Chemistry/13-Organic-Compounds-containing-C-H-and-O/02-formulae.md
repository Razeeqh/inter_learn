# Organic Compounds containing C, H and O — Reactions and Name-Reactions Sheet

In physics and physical chemistry you learn formulae.
In organic chemistry you learn **REACTIONS**. This is that sheet.

Copy it by hand into a notebook. Writing it once beats reading it ten times.

---

# 0. HOW TO READ THIS SHEET

```
   Every reaction is written in the same shape:

        STARTING COMPOUND   --REAGENT , CONDITION-->   PRODUCT

   The examiner gives you TWO of the three and asks for the third.
   So you must be able to read the line in ALL THREE directions:

        given compound + reagent   ->  what is the product?
        given compound + product   ->  what is the reagent?
        given reagent + product    ->  what did we start from?
```

# 1. PREPARATION OF ALCOHOLS

| STARTING MATERIAL | REAGENT / CONDITION | PRODUCT | NOTE |
|---|---|---|---|
| CH3-CH=CH2 | H2O / dil. H2SO4 | CH3-CH(OH)-CH3 | Markovnikov, 2deg |
| CH3-CH=CH2 | (i) B2H6 (ii) H2O2 / OH- | CH3-CH2-CH2-OH | anti-Markovnikov, 1deg |
| CH3-CHO | H2 / Ni or NaBH4 or LiAlH4 | CH3-CH2-OH | 1deg alcohol |
| CH3-CO-CH3 | H2 / Ni or NaBH4 or LiAlH4 | CH3-CH(OH)-CH3 | 2deg alcohol |
| CH3-COOH | (i) LiAlH4 (ii) H3O+ | CH3-CH2-OH | NaBH4 fails here |
| CH3-COOC2H5 | (i) LiAlH4 (ii) H3O+ | CH3-CH2-OH + C2H5-OH | ester reduction |
| HCHO | (i) CH3MgBr (ii) H3O+ | CH3-CH2-OH | Grignard -> 1deg |
| CH3-CHO | (i) CH3MgBr (ii) H3O+ | CH3-CH(OH)-CH3 | Grignard -> 2deg |
| CH3-CO-CH3 | (i) CH3MgBr (ii) H3O+ | (CH3)3C-OH | Grignard -> 3deg |
| CH3-CH2-Br | aq. NaOH or aq. KOH | CH3-CH2-OH | hydrolysis of halide |

```
   THE GRIGNARD RULE IN ONE BOX:

        HCHO       + RMgX  ->  1deg alcohol
        any other
        aldehyde   + RMgX  ->  2deg alcohol
        ketone     + RMgX  ->  3deg alcohol
        CO2        + RMgX  ->  CARBOXYLIC ACID

   Grignard reagent is made as:   R-X + Mg  --dry ether-->  R-Mg-X
   It is destroyed by water:      CH3MgBr + H2O  ->  CH4 + Mg(OH)Br
```

# 2. PREPARATION OF PHENOL

| STARTING MATERIAL | REAGENT / CONDITION | PRODUCT | NAME OF METHOD |
|---|---|---|---|
| C6H5-Cl | (i) NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm (ii) H+ | C6H5-OH | from haloarene (Dow) |
| C6H5-SO3Na | (i) NaOH fused, 573 K (ii) H+ | C6H5-OH | from benzenesulphonic acid |
| C6H5-N2+Cl- | H2O, warm, 283 K | C6H5-OH + N2 | from diazonium salt |
| C6H5-CH(CH3)2 | (i) O2 (air) (ii) dil. H2SO4 | C6H5-OH + CH3-CO-CH3 | CUMENE process |

```
   WRITE THESE FOUR EQUATIONS:

   C6H5-Cl        --(i) NaOH 623 K 300 atm  (ii) dil. HCl-->  C6H5-OH

   C6H6  --conc. H2SO4-->  C6H5-SO3H  --(i) NaOH fused (ii) H+-->  C6H5-OH

   C6H5-NH2 --NaNO2/HCl, 273-278 K--> C6H5-N2+Cl- --H2O, 283 K--> C6H5-OH

   C6H6 + CH3-CH=CH2 --H+--> C6H5-CH(CH3)2 --(i) O2 (ii) H+-->
                                            C6H5-OH  +  CH3-CO-CH3
```

**Remember: only the cumene process gives ACETONE as a by-product.**

## 3.1 What each class of alcohol gives

| ALCOHOL | OXIDATION (K2Cr2O7/H+) | DEHYDROGENATION (Cu, 573 K) | LUCAS TEST |
|---|---|---|---|
| PRIMARY  CH3-CH2-OH | CH3-CHO then CH3-COOH | CH3-CHO | no turbidity cold |
| SECONDARY CH3-CH(OH)-CH3 | CH3-CO-CH3 (ketone) | CH3-CO-CH3 | turbid in 5 min |
| TERTIARY (CH3)3C-OH | no reaction (mild); chain breaks (drastic) | alkene (CH3)2C=CH2 | turbid at once |

   CLEAVAGE RULE (learn as three lines):

      both primary        ->  halide comes from the SMALLER group

## 10.1 The equations, written out in full

```
  ---- 1. KOLBE ------------------------------------------------------
      C6H5-OH --NaOH--> C6H5-ONa
      C6H5-ONa --(i) CO2, 400 K, 4-7 atm (ii) H+--> C6H4(OH)-COOH
                                                    (salicylic acid)

  ---- 2. REIMER-TIEMANN ---------------------------------------------
      C6H5-OH --(i) CHCl3 + aq. NaOH, 340 K (ii) H3O+--> C6H4(OH)-CHO
                                                         (salicylaldehyde)
      Intermediate: dichlorocarbene  :CCl2

  ---- 3. WILLIAMSON --------------------------------------------------
      CH3-CH2-Br + CH3-ONa  -->  CH3-CH2-O-CH3 + NaBr
      RULE: the HALIDE must be primary or methyl.

  ---- 4. ROSENMUND ---------------------------------------------------
      CH3-CO-Cl --H2 / Pd-BaSO4 (S or quinoline)--> CH3-CHO + HCl

  ---- 5. STEPHEN -----------------------------------------------------
      CH3-CN --(i) SnCl2 / HCl (ii) H3O+--> CH3-CHO + NH3

  ---- 6. ETARD -------------------------------------------------------
      C6H5-CH3 --(i) CrO2Cl2 in CS2 (ii) H3O+--> C6H5-CHO

  ---- 7. GATTERMAN-KOCH ----------------------------------------------
      C6H6 + CO + HCl --anhyd. AlCl3 / CuCl--> C6H5-CHO

  ---- 8. CLEMMENSEN --------------------------------------------------
      CH3-CO-CH3 --Zn-Hg / conc. HCl--> CH3-CH2-CH3 + H2O

  ---- 9. WOLFF-KISHNER -----------------------------------------------
      CH3-CO-CH3 --NH2NH2--> CH3-C(=N-NH2)-CH3
      CH3-C(=N-NH2)-CH3 --KOH / ethylene glycol, 453-473 K-->
                                        CH3-CH2-CH3 + N2

  ---- 10. ALDOL ------------------------------------------------------
      2 CH3-CHO --dil. NaOH--> CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CHO
      CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CHO --heat, -H2O--> CH3-CH=CH-CHO

      2 CH3-CO-CH3 --dil. Ba(OH)2--> (CH3)2C(OH)-CH2-CO-CH3

  ---- 11. CROSS ALDOL ------------------------------------------------
      C6H5-CHO + CH3-CHO --dil. NaOH--> C6H5-CH=CH-CHO + H2O
                                        (cinnamaldehyde)

  ---- 12. CANNIZZARO -------------------------------------------------
      2 HCHO --conc. NaOH--> CH3-OH + HCOONa
      2 C6H5-CHO --conc. NaOH--> C6H5-CH2-OH + C6H5-COONa
      CROSSED: HCHO + C6H5-CHO --conc. NaOH--> C6H5-CH2-OH + HCOONa

  ---- 13. HVZ --------------------------------------------------------
      CH3-COOH --(i) Cl2 / red P (ii) H2O--> Cl-CH2-COOH + HCl
      Needs an ALPHA hydrogen, so HCOOH and C6H5-COOH do not react.

  ---- 14. DECARBOXYLATION --------------------------------------------
      CH3-COONa --NaOH + CaO, heat--> CH4 + Na2CO3
      C6H5-COONa --NaOH + CaO, heat--> C6H6 + Na2CO3

  ---- 15. CUMENE PROCESS ---------------------------------------------
      C6H6 + CH3-CH=CH2 --H+--> C6H5-CH(CH3)2
      C6H5-CH(CH3)2 --(i) O2 (ii) dil. H2SO4--> C6H5-OH + CH3-CO-CH3

  ---- 16. FRIEDEL-CRAFTS ACYLATION -----------------------------------
      C6H6 + CH3-CO-Cl --anhyd. AlCl3--> C6H5-CO-CH3 + HCl

  ---- 17. HYDROBORATION-OXIDATION ------------------------------------
      CH3-CH=CH2 --(i) B2H6 (ii) H2O2 / OH---> CH3-CH2-CH2-OH

  ---- 18. OZONOLYSIS -------------------------------------------------
      CH3-CH=CH-CH3 --(i) O3 (ii) Zn / H2O--> 2 CH3-CHO

  ---- 19. KOLBE ELECTROLYSIS -----------------------------------------
      2 CH3-COONa + 2 H2O --electrolysis--> CH3-CH3 + 2 CO2 + H2 + 2 NaOH

  ---- 20. ESTERIFICATION ---------------------------------------------
      CH3-COOH + C2H5-OH --conc. H2SO4, heat--> CH3-COO-C2H5 + H2O
```

# ===============================================================
# 12. ACIDITY ORDERS — with reasons
# ===============================================================

## 12.1 The master order

```
   R-COOH   >   H2CO3   >   C6H5-OH   >   H2O   >   R-OH   >   HC(triple)CH
```

| COMPOUND | ANION FORMED | WHY IT IS STABLE OR NOT |
|---|---|---|
| CH3-COOH | CH3-COO- | resonance over TWO equivalent oxygens — very stable |
| C6H5-OH | C6H5-O- | resonance into the ring, but charge goes onto CARBON — less stable |
| H2O | OH- | no resonance, but no +I group either |
| CH3-CH2-OH | CH3-CH2-O- | no resonance, and the alkyl group pushes charge in (+I) — least stable |

## 12.2 Substituent effect on PHENOL

| PHENOL | SUBSTITUENT EFFECT | ACIDITY |
|---|---|---|
| 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid) | three strong -I / -R groups | STRONGEST |
| p-nitrophenol | -NO2 withdraws by -I and -R | very high |
| m-nitrophenol | -NO2 withdraws by -I only (no resonance from meta) | high |
| p-chlorophenol | -Cl withdraws by -I | slightly higher than phenol |
| PHENOL | reference | reference |
| p-methylphenol (p-cresol) | -CH3 releases electrons (+I) | LOWER |
| p-methoxyphenol | -OCH3 releases by +R | LOWEST |

```
   ORDER TO WRITE:
   picric acid > p-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > p-chlorophenol
              > phenol > p-cresol > p-methoxyphenol
```

## 12.3 Substituent effect on CARBOXYLIC ACIDS

| ORDER | REASON |
|---|---|
| Cl3C-COOH > Cl2CH-COOH > ClCH2-COOH > CH3-COOH | more -I groups pull more charge away |
| F-CH2-COOH > Cl-CH2-COOH > Br-CH2-COOH > I-CH2-COOH | -I effect follows electronegativity |
| alpha-chloro > beta-chloro > gamma-chloro acid | the -I effect fades with distance |
| HCOOH > CH3-COOH > CH3-CH2-COOH > (CH3)3C-COOH | more alkyl (+I) = weaker acid |
| C6H5-COOH > CH3-COOH | the phenyl ring withdraws electrons |
| p-nitrobenzoic > benzoic > p-methylbenzoic | -NO2 withdraws, -CH3 releases |

# ===============================================================
# 13. REACTIVITY ORDERS — with reasons
# ===============================================================

| SITUATION | ORDER | REASON |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleophilic addition to C=O | HCHO > CH3-CHO > CH3-CH2-CHO > CH3-CO-CH3 > C6H5-CO-CH3 > C6H5-CO-C6H5 | more alkyl/aryl groups = more +I electron push and more steric crowding |
| Aldehyde vs ketone in general | aldehyde > ketone | one alkyl group instead of two |
| Alcohols with HX (and Lucas) | 3deg > 2deg > 1deg > CH3-OH | stability of the carbocation formed |
| Alcohols with Na metal | 1deg > 2deg > 3deg | +I of alkyl groups reduces the acidity of the O-H |
| Ease of dehydration | 3deg > 2deg > 1deg | stability of the carbocation formed |
| Reactivity of HX towards alcohol | HI > HBr > HCl | HI is the strongest acid and I- the best nucleophile |
| Esterification rate of alcohols | 1deg > 2deg > 3deg | steric hindrance |
| Electrophilic substitution on ring | phenol > anisole > benzene > benzoic acid | -OH and -OCH3 activate; -COOH deactivates |
| Boiling point | acid > alcohol > ketone > aldehyde > ether > alkane | strength of intermolecular hydrogen bonding |

```
   THE TWO ORDERS THAT LOOK OPPOSITE (do not panic):

      With HX / dehydration :   3deg > 2deg > 1deg     (carbocation stability)
      With Na metal         :   1deg > 2deg > 3deg     (acidity of O-H)

   They are different questions, so they have different answers.
```
