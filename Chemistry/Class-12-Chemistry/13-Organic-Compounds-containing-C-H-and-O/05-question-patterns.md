# Organic Compounds containing C, H and O — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The compounds change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — CLASSIFY THE ALCOHOL (1deg / 2deg / 3deg)          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A structure is given and you are asked whether it is primary,
            secondary or tertiary, or the question mentions Lucas.
  METHOD    Look at the carbon carrying the -OH.
            Count how many OTHER CARBONS are attached to it.
               0 or 1  ->  PRIMARY
               2       ->  SECONDARY
               3       ->  TERTIARY
  TRAP      Count CARBONS, not hydrogens. And C6H5-CH2-OH is a PRIMARY
            ALCOHOL, not a phenol, because the -OH is not on the ring.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — MARKOVNIKOV OR ANTI-MARKOVNIKOV                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   An alkene plus a reagent, asking for the alcohol formed.
  METHOD    Read the REAGENT, not the alkene:
               H2O / dil. H2SO4        ->  MARKOVNIKOV
                                           -OH on the carbon with FEWER H
                                           gives 2deg or 3deg alcohol
               (i) B2H6 (ii) H2O2/OH-  ->  ANTI-MARKOVNIKOV
                                           -OH on the carbon with MORE H
                                           gives 1deg alcohol
  EXAMPLE   CH3-CH=CH2 --H2O/H2SO4--> CH3-CH(OH)-CH3
            CH3-CH=CH2 --(i) B2H6 (ii) H2O2/OH---> CH3-CH2-CH2-OH
  TRAP      Writing the Markovnikov product when B2H6 is given.
            This is the single most common careless mistake in the chapter.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — GRIGNARD PRODUCT PREDICTION                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "R-MgX reacts with ... followed by hydrolysis. Product?"
  METHOD    Memorise the four lines:
               HCHO      + RMgX  ->  PRIMARY alcohol
               R-CHO     + RMgX  ->  SECONDARY alcohol
               R-CO-R'   + RMgX  ->  TERTIARY alcohol
               CO2       + RMgX  ->  CARBOXYLIC ACID
            Always write the reaction in TWO steps:
               (i) RMgX in dry ether     (ii) H3O+
  TRAP      Forgetting the "(ii) H3O+" step. The intermediate is a
            magnesium alkoxide, not the alcohol. Half a mark gone.
  NOTE      "Why dry ether?" is a separate 2-marker.
            Answer: CH3MgBr + H2O -> CH4 + Mg(OH)Br, the reagent is wasted.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — PREPARATION OF PHENOL (list the methods)            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is phenol prepared?" or a specific starting material is
            named (chlorobenzene, cumene, diazonium salt).
  METHOD    Write FOUR routes, each with the reagent AND the condition:
               1. C6H5-Cl --NaOH, 623 K, 300 atm, then H+--> C6H5-OH
               2. C6H5-SO3Na --NaOH fused 573 K, then H+--> C6H5-OH
               3. C6H5-N2+Cl- --H2O, 283 K--> C6H5-OH + N2
               4. cumene --(i) O2 (ii) dil. H2SO4--> C6H5-OH + acetone
  TRAP      Conditions carry marks. "NaOH" alone is not enough for route 1;
            you need the temperature and the pressure.
  NOTE      "Which method gives acetone as a by-product?" -> CUMENE.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WHY IS PHENOL MORE ACIDIC THAN ETHANOL?        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any acidity comparison between phenol and an alcohol.
  METHOD    Three sentences, in this order:
               1. Phenol gives the PHENOXIDE ion, in which the negative
                  charge is delocalised over the ring by RESONANCE ->
                  the ion is STABLE.
               2. Ethanol gives the ETHOXIDE ion, charge LOCALISED on
                  oxygen, no resonance -> less stable.
               3. The alkyl group's +I effect makes the ethoxide worse
                  still.
            Conclude: more stable anion = stronger acid.
  TRAP      Writing only "phenol has resonance" without saying that the
            RESONANCE STABILISES THE ANION. The examiner wants the link.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — EFFECT OF A SUBSTITUENT ON PHENOL ACIDITY      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange in increasing/decreasing order of acidity" with
            substituted phenols, or "explain the effect of -NO2".
  METHOD    Sort the substituents into two piles:
               ELECTRON WITHDRAWING (-NO2, -CN, -CHO, -X)
                    -> stabilise the phenoxide -> ACIDITY UP
               ELECTRON RELEASING (-CH3, -OCH3, -NH2)
                    -> destabilise the phenoxide -> ACIDITY DOWN
            Then write the order and the reason together.
  ORDER     picric acid > p-nitrophenol > m-nitrophenol > phenol
                        > p-cresol > p-methoxyphenol
  TRAP      ORTHO and PARA nitrophenols are MORE acidic than META,
            because only from o/p can -NO2 withdraw by RESONANCE too.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — THE LUCAS TEST                                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish 1deg, 2deg and 3deg alcohols" or the word
            "turbidity".
  METHOD    1. Name the reagent: conc. HCl + anhydrous ZnCl2.
            2. Write the reaction: R-OH + HCl --ZnCl2--> R-Cl + H2O
            3. Give the three observations:
                  3deg -> turbid IMMEDIATELY
                  2deg -> turbid in about 5 minutes
                  1deg -> NO turbidity in the cold
            4. Give the reason: carbocation stability 3deg > 2deg > 1deg.
  TRAP      Leaving out the anhydrous ZnCl2, or saying "primary reacts
            slowly" instead of "primary does not react in the cold".
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — PRODUCT OF DEHYDRATION (which temperature?)         2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Ethanol + conc. H2SO4 at ... K gives?"
  METHOD    Read the temperature and nothing else:
               443 K (170 C) , excess ACID     ->  ALKENE
               413 K (140 C) , excess ALCOHOL  ->  ETHER
  EXAMPLE   CH3-CH2-OH --conc.H2SO4, 443 K--> CH2=CH2
            2 CH3-CH2-OH --conc.H2SO4, 413 K--> CH3-CH2-O-CH2-CH3
  SHORTCUT  Higher temperature makes the SMALLER molecule.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — OXIDATION PRODUCT OF AN ALCOHOL                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any "[O]" or oxidising reagent acting on an alcohol.
  METHOD    Step 1: classify the alcohol (Pattern 1).
            Step 2: read the reagent.
                 1deg + K2Cr2O7/H+ or KMnO4  ->  CARBOXYLIC ACID
                 1deg + PCC or Cu 573 K      ->  ALDEHYDE (stops there)
                 2deg + any                  ->  KETONE
                 3deg                        ->  no reaction (mild);
                                                 chain breaks (drastic)
  TRAP      If the question asks for an ALDEHYDE you must choose PCC or
            Cu/573 K. Writing K2Cr2O7 overshoots to the acid.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — REACTIONS OF PHENOL (name the product)        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when phenol is treated with ...?"
  METHOD    Run down this list:
               dil. HNO3        ->  o- and p-nitrophenol
               conc. HNO3       ->  picric acid (2,4,6-trinitrophenol)
               Br2 in CS2 273 K ->  p-bromophenol (MONO)
               Br2 in WATER     ->  2,4,6-tribromophenol (WHITE ppt)
               NaOH + CO2 press ->  KOLBE, salicylic ACID
               CHCl3 + NaOH     ->  REIMER-TIEMANN, salicylALDEHYDE
               Zn dust, distil  ->  BENZENE
               Na2Cr2O7 / H+    ->  benzoquinone
               neutral FeCl3    ->  VIOLET colour
  TRAP      The SOLVENT decides mono versus tri bromination.
            CS2 = mono, water = tri. Read the solvent before answering.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — KOLBE versus REIMER-TIEMANN                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Either name is mentioned, or CO2 / CHCl3 appears with phenol.
  METHOD    KOLBE:            C6H5-ONa --(i) CO2, 400 K, 4-7 atm (ii) H+
                              --> salicylic ACID  (-COOH)
            REIMER-TIEMANN:   C6H5-OH --(i) CHCl3 + NaOH, 340 K (ii) H3O+
                              --> salicylALDEHYDE (-CHO)
                              intermediate: dichlorocarbene :CCl2
  SHORTCUT  K for Kolbe, K for "karboxylic acid".
            CHCl3 has an H in it -> gives the CHO.
  TRAP      Swapping the two products. This is the most common single
            error in Section A of this chapter.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — WILLIAMSON SYNTHESIS: PICK THE RIGHT PAIR     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How would you prepare ... ether?" or four halide/alkoxide
            combinations are given as options.
  METHOD    1. Split the target ether at the oxygen into two groups.
            2. Give the HALOGEN to the SMALLER (primary or methyl) group.
            3. Make the other group the sodium alkoxide.
            4. Write:  R-X + R'-ONa --> R-O-R' + NaX
  EXAMPLE   (CH3)3C-O-CH3  is made from  CH3-I + (CH3)3C-ONa
                           NOT from      (CH3)3C-Br + CH3-ONa
  TRAP      With a tertiary halide the alkoxide acts as a BASE and you get
            an ALKENE by elimination, not the ether. Say this if asked why.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — CLEAVAGE OF AN ETHER BY HX                    2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any ether plus HI or HBr.
  METHOD    Decide which side takes the halogen:
               both primary       ->  SMALLER group gets the halogen
               one tertiary       ->  TERTIARY group gets the halogen
               one aryl (anisole) ->  ALKYL group gets the halogen,
                                      and PHENOL is the other product
  EXAMPLES  CH3-O-CH2-CH3 + HI --> CH3-I + CH3-CH2-OH
            (CH3)3C-O-CH3 + HI --> (CH3)3C-I + CH3-OH
            C6H5-O-CH3 + HI    --> C6H5-OH + CH3-I
  TRAP      Writing iodobenzene from anisole. It never forms - the
            oxygen-to-ring bond has partial double bond character.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — PREPARATION OF AN ALDEHYDE OR KETONE          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is acetaldehyde / benzaldehyde / acetone prepared?"
  METHOD    Match the starting material to its method:
               acyl chloride  ->  ROSENMUND, H2 / Pd-BaSO4 poisoned
               nitrile        ->  STEPHEN, SnCl2 / HCl then H3O+
               ester          ->  DIBAL-H
               toluene        ->  ETARD, CrO2Cl2 / CS2
               benzene        ->  GATTERMAN-KOCH, CO + HCl / AlCl3, CuCl
               alkene         ->  OZONOLYSIS, O3 then Zn/H2O
               ethyne         ->  H2O / H2SO4, HgSO4
               1deg alcohol   ->  PCC or Cu 573 K
               2deg alcohol   ->  K2Cr2O7 / H+  (gives a KETONE)
  TRAP      Ethyne is the only alkyne giving an ALDEHYDE. Every other
            alkyne gives a KETONE.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — WHY ARE ALDEHYDES MORE REACTIVE THAN KETONES  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the relative reactivity ..." or an order is asked.
  METHOD    Give BOTH reasons - one alone loses half the marks.
               1. ELECTRONIC: a ketone has TWO electron-releasing alkyl
                  groups (+I), an aldehyde only ONE. So the ketone's
                  carbonyl carbon is less positive.
               2. STERIC: two bulky groups in a ketone block the attack
                  of the nucleophile.
            Then write the order:
               HCHO > CH3-CHO > CH3-CH2-CHO > CH3-CO-CH3 > C6H5-CO-CH3
  TRAP      For aromatic carbonyls add the third reason: the ring donates
            electron density by RESONANCE, reducing reactivity further.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — NUCLEOPHILIC ADDITION MECHANISM                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the mechanism of addition of HCN / NaHSO3 to a
            carbonyl compound."
  METHOD    1. State the polarity: C is (delta+), O is (delta-).
            2. STEP 1 (SLOW): Nu(-) attacks the carbonyl carbon; the pi
               electrons shift to oxygen; carbon goes sp2 -> sp3.
            3. STEP 2 (FAST): the alkoxide oxygen picks up H+.
            4. Draw both steps with the tetrahedral intermediate.
  TRAP      Not labelling which step is rate determining, and forgetting
            to mention the sp2 -> sp3 change. Both carry marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — REACTION WITH AMMONIA DERIVATIVES             2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   NH2-OH , NH2-NH2 , 2,4-DNP or semicarbazide is given.
  METHOD    Use the general pattern:  R2C=O + H2N-Z --> R2C=N-Z + H2O
               NH2-OH          ->  OXIME
               NH2-NH2         ->  HYDRAZONE
               C6H5-NH-NH2     ->  PHENYLHYDRAZONE
               2,4-DNP         ->  2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazone, ORANGE ppt
               NH2-NH-CO-NH2   ->  SEMICARBAZONE
  TRAP      Forgetting the water molecule on the product side. Also note
            the reaction needs pH about 3.5 - weakly acidic, not strongly.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — ALDOL OR CANNIZZARO? (decide first)           2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A carbonyl compound plus NaOH. Read the CONCENTRATION.
  METHOD    STEP 1: count the ALPHA hydrogens (on the carbon next to C=O).
            STEP 2:
               HAS alpha-H  +  dilute NaOH   ->  ALDOL
               NO alpha-H   +  conc.  NaOH   ->  CANNIZZARO
            STEP 3: write the equation.
               2 CH3-CHO --dil. NaOH--> CH3-CH(OH)-CH2-CHO
               2 C6H5-CHO --conc. NaOH--> C6H5-CH2-OH + C6H5-COONa
  NO-ALPHA-H LIST:  HCHO , C6H5-CHO , (CH3)3C-CHO
  TRAP      Answering before counting alpha hydrogens. Count first, always.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — ALDOL MECHANISM                                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the aldol condensation with mechanism."
  METHOD    Four steps, each with an equation:
               1. OH- removes an alpha H -> resonance stabilised CARBANION
               2. the carbanion attacks the C=O of a SECOND molecule
               3. the alkoxide takes H+ from water -> the ALDOL
               4. on heating, water is eliminated -> the alpha,beta
                  unsaturated carbonyl compound
  TRAP      Stopping at the aldol. The word "condensation" means the
            heating step is part of the answer.
  NOTE      Add CROSS ALDOL if there is room: it is useful only when one
            partner has no alpha-H, e.g. C6H5-CHO + CH3-CHO.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — CANNIZZARO MECHANISM                               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the Cannizzaro reaction with mechanism."
  METHOD    1. Define: aldehyde with NO alpha-H + conc. alkali
               -> DISPROPORTIONATION (one oxidised, one reduced).
            2. Step 1: OH- adds to the carbonyl carbon.
            3. Step 2: the intermediate transfers a HYDRIDE ION (H:-) to
               a second aldehyde molecule.   <-- the key step
            4. Step 3: proton exchange gives the alcohol + the carboxylate.
            5. Give the example with equation.
  TRAP      Saying a "proton" is transferred. It is a HYDRIDE ION, H:-,
            carrying two electrons. That word is worth a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — CLEMMENSEN OR WOLFF-KISHNER?                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Convert a ketone into an alkane" or either name is given.
  METHOD    Both turn C=O into CH2.
               CLEMMENSEN     Zn-Hg / conc. HCl        (ACIDIC)
               WOLFF-KISHNER  NH2NH2 then KOH /
                              ethylene glycol, 453-473 K (BASIC)
            Example: CH3-CO-CH3 --> CH3-CH2-CH3 by either route.
  TRAP      Mixing up acidic and basic conditions. Hook: CLemmensen has
            the CL of HCl in it, so it is the ACID one.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — WHICH COMPOUND GIVES WHICH TEST               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which of these responds to Tollens'/Fehling's/iodoform?"
  METHOD    Apply the four rules:
               TOLLENS  - ALL aldehydes (and HCOOH). No ketone.
               FEHLING  - ALIPHATIC aldehydes only (and HCOOH).
                          C6H5-CHO FAILS.
               IODOFORM - needs CH3-CO- or CH3-CH(OH)- in the molecule.
               2,4-DNP  - EVERY aldehyde and EVERY ketone.
  TRAP      Benzaldehyde: Tollens YES, Fehling NO. That single fact is
            asked every year in some form.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — "DISTINGUISH BETWEEN THE FOLLOWING PAIRS"     2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Two or three pairs of compounds are listed.
  METHOD    For EACH pair write THREE things:
               1. the REAGENT
               2. what compound A does
               3. what compound B does
            The standard answers:
               ethanol / phenol          ->  neutral FeCl3, violet
               phenol / benzoic acid     ->  NaHCO3, CO2 fizz
               aldehyde / ketone         ->  Tollens', silver mirror
               ethanal / benzaldehyde    ->  Fehling's, red ppt
               acetone / pentan-3-one    ->  iodoform, yellow ppt
               ethanol / methanol        ->  iodoform, yellow ppt
               1deg / 2deg / 3deg alcohol->  Lucas reagent, turbidity time
               alcohol / ether           ->  Na metal, H2 gas
               formic / acetic acid      ->  Tollens', mirror with HCOOH
  TRAP      Describing only the positive result. You must also say the
            other compound gives NO reaction, otherwise it is half a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — PREPARATION OF A CARBOXYLIC ACID              2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is acetic acid / benzoic acid prepared?"
  METHOD    Pick from the six standard routes:
               1deg alcohol   --KMnO4 / H+-->        R-COOH
               aldehyde       --K2Cr2O7 / H+-->      R-COOH
               alkylbenzene   --KMnO4/KOH, H+-->     C6H5-COOH
               nitrile        --H3O+, heat-->        R-COOH + NH3
               amide          --H3O+, heat-->        R-COOH + NH3
               RMgX + CO2     --then H3O+-->         R-COOH
  TRAP      Alkylbenzene oxidation ALWAYS gives benzoic acid, however long
            the side chain, PROVIDED a benzylic hydrogen exists.
            C6H5-C(CH3)3 has none, so it is NOT oxidised.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — ORDER OF ACID STRENGTH                        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange in order of acidity" with substituted acids.
  METHOD    Check the four factors in this order:
               1. Electron-WITHDRAWING groups raise acidity (-I).
                  Cl3C-COOH > Cl2CH-COOH > ClCH2-COOH > CH3-COOH
               2. More electronegative halogen = stronger acid.
                  F > Cl > Br > I
               3. Closer to -COOH = stronger effect.
                  alpha > beta > gamma
               4. Alkyl groups LOWER acidity (+I).
                  HCOOH > CH3-COOH > CH3-CH2-COOH > (CH3)3C-COOH
            Always give the REASON with the order.
  TRAP      HCOOH is stronger than CH3-COOH. Students reverse this
            constantly because formic acid "sounds weaker".
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — COMPARE ACIDITY ACROSS FAMILIES               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Acid, phenol, water and alcohol appear in the same list.
  METHOD    Write the order first, then one reason per species:
               R-COOH > H2CO3 > C6H5-OH > H2O > R-OH
               carboxylate - charge on TWO EQUIVALENT oxygens
               phenoxide   - charge delocalised onto ring CARBONS
               alkoxide    - no delocalisation, plus +I from the alkyl
            Add the experimental proof:
               acid + NaHCO3 -> CO2 evolved
               phenol + NaHCO3 -> no reaction
  TRAP      Not explaining WHY the carboxylate beats the phenoxide.
            The key phrase is "two equivalent oxygen atoms".
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — REACTIONS OF CARBOXYLIC ACIDS                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when acetic acid is treated with ...?"
  METHOD    Run down the list:
               NaHCO3     ->  salt + CO2 (BRISK FIZZ, the TEST)
               C2H5OH/H+  ->  ESTER
               PCl5/SOCl2 ->  ACID CHLORIDE
               P2O5, heat ->  ANHYDRIDE
               NH3, heat  ->  AMIDE
               LiAlH4     ->  1deg ALCOHOL
               soda lime  ->  DECARBOXYLATION, alkane with one less C
               Cl2/red P  ->  HVZ, alpha halo acid
  TRAP      NaBH4 does NOT reduce a carboxylic acid. Only LiAlH4 or B2H6.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — HVZ REACTION                                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Cl2 / red phosphorus" with an acid, or the name is given.
  METHOD    CH3-COOH --(i) Cl2 / red P (ii) H2O--> Cl-CH2-COOH + HCl
            Halogenation is at the ALPHA carbon, never at -COOH.
  TRAP      HCOOH and C6H5-COOH have NO alpha hydrogen, so they do NOT
            give this reaction. That is a favourite MCQ.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — RING SUBSTITUTION: WHERE DOES IT GO?          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A substituted benzene is nitrated or halogenated.
  METHOD    Classify the group already on the ring:
               ACTIVATING, ORTHO/PARA directing:  -OH , -OCH3 , -CH3 , -NH2
               DEACTIVATING, META directing:      -COOH , -CHO , -NO2
            Then place the new group accordingly.
  EXAMPLES  C6H5-OH  --HNO3-->  o- and p-nitrophenol
            C6H5-COOH --HNO3--> m-nitrobenzoic acid
            C6H5-O-CH3 --Br2--> p-bromoanisole (para major, ortho hindered)
  TRAP      Friedel-Crafts does NOT work on benzoic acid or nitrobenzene -
            the ring is deactivated and -COOH complexes with AlCl3.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — CONVERSION QUESTION (A into B)                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Convert ethanol into acetic acid" / "How will you obtain ...
            from ...?"
  METHOD    Walk along the ladder:
               alkene <-- ALCOHOL <--> ALDEHYDE <--> ACID
            Going RIGHT  = oxidise   ([O], K2Cr2O7/H+, PCC, Cu)
            Going LEFT   = reduce    (LiAlH4, NaBH4, H2/Ni)
            ADD a carbon = RMgX + CO2  ,  or  KCN then hydrolysis
            LOSE a carbon= decarboxylation (soda lime)
            Write each step as a separate arrow with its reagent.
  EXAMPLE   ethanol -> propanoic acid:
               CH3-CH2-OH --conc. HI--> CH3-CH2-I
               CH3-CH2-I --KCN--> CH3-CH2-CN
               CH3-CH2-CN --H3O+, heat--> CH3-CH2-COOH
  TRAP      Trying to do it in one step when the carbon count changes.
            Count the carbons in A and B FIRST.
```

```
  PATTERN 31 — HYDROGEN BONDING / BOILING POINT COMPARISON        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does ethanol boil higher than dimethyl ether?" or an
            order of boiling points is asked.
  METHOD    ORDER:  acid > alcohol > ketone > aldehyde > ether > alkane
            REASONS:
               acid    - DIMER held by TWO hydrogen bonds
               alcohol - intermolecular hydrogen bonds
               ketone/aldehyde - only dipole-dipole
               ether   - no H on the oxygen, so no H bonding at all
  TRAP      Ethers cannot H bond with EACH OTHER but CAN accept an H bond
            from water, so they are still somewhat water soluble.
            Both facts may be asked separately.
```

```
  PATTERN 32 — IDENTIFY THE UNKNOWN COMPOUND                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compound A of formula C3H6O gives a positive iodoform test
            but does not reduce Tollens' reagent. Identify A."
  METHOD    Read each clue as a filter:
               2,4-DNP positive         ->  it is an aldehyde OR ketone
               Tollens' positive        ->  it is an ALDEHYDE
               Tollens' negative + DNP+ ->  it is a KETONE
               iodoform positive        ->  it has CH3-CO- or CH3-CH(OH)-
               NaHCO3 fizz              ->  carboxylic acid
               FeCl3 violet             ->  phenol
               Na gives H2 but no fizz  ->  alcohol
            Then fit the molecular formula.
  WORKED    C3H6O , iodoform YES , Tollens NO  ->  a methyl ketone
            ->  CH3-CO-CH3  (acetone)
  TRAP      Use EVERY clue. A negative result is as informative as a
            positive one.
```
