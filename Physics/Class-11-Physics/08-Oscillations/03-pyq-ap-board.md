## Topic: Periodic motion and basic terms

### Q A1
Give two examples of periodic motion which are not oscillatory.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  The revolution of the Earth around the Sun.
  2.  The rotation of the blades of a fan (or of the Earth on its axis).

  Both repeat after equal intervals of time, but the body does not move
  TO AND FRO about a mean position, so they are not oscillatory.
```

---

### Q A2
Distinguish between periodic motion and oscillatory motion.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PERIODIC     -  any motion that repeats itself after equal intervals
                  of time.   Example: the Earth round the Sun.

  OSCILLATORY  -  periodic motion in which the body moves TO AND FRO
                  about a fixed mean position.
                  Example: a simple pendulum.

  Every oscillatory motion is periodic, but not the other way round.
```

---

### Q A3
Define the time period and the frequency of an oscillating body. Give their
units.

**ANSWER:**
```
  TIME PERIOD (T)  -  the time taken for ONE complete oscillation.
                      SI unit: second (s)

  FREQUENCY (n)    -  the number of complete oscillations made in
                      one second.        SI unit: hertz (Hz)

                      n = 1 / T
```

---

### Q A4
What is the total distance travelled by a particle of amplitude A in one
complete oscillation?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Mean -> right extreme  =  A
  right extreme -> mean  =  A
  mean -> left extreme   =  A
  left extreme -> mean   =  A
                            ----
  Total distance         =  4A

  The DISPLACEMENT in one complete oscillation is ZERO.
```

## Topic: SHM — definition and basic quantities

### Q A5
Define simple harmonic motion. Give two examples.

**ANSWER:** A body is in simple harmonic motion if its acceleration is
directly proportional to its displacement from the mean position and is always
directed **towards** the mean position.
```
      a  =  - omega^2 y

  EXAMPLES:  (i)  a simple pendulum swinging through a small angle
             (ii) a mass oscillating at the end of a spring
```

---

### Q A6
Write the equation for the displacement of a particle in SHM and name each
symbol.

**ANSWER:**
```
      y  =  A sin( omega t + phi )

      y      =  displacement from the mean position
      A      =  amplitude (maximum displacement)
      omega  =  angular frequency,  omega = 2 pi / T
      phi    =  phase constant (initial phase or epoch)
      (omega t + phi)  =  phase of the particle at time t
```

---

### Q A7
Define amplitude and phase of a particle in SHM.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AMPLITUDE  -  the maximum displacement of the particle on either side
                of the mean position. Unit: metre.

  PHASE      -  the quantity (omega t + phi) which completely specifies
                the position AND the direction of motion of the particle
                at any instant. Unit: radian.
```

---

### Q A8
What is meant by the epoch (phase constant) of a particle in SHM?

**ANSWER:** The **epoch** is the value of the phase at time t = 0, i.e. the
constant `phi` in `y = A sin(omega t + phi)`. It fixes the starting position of
the particle. It is also called the initial phase or the phase constant. Its
unit is the radian.

---

### Q A9
Write the differential equation of simple harmonic motion.

**ANSWER:**
```
        d2y
        ----   +   omega^2  y   =   0
        dt2

  Its solution is  y = A sin(omega t + phi).
```

---

### Q A10
The displacement of a particle is `y = 4 sin(2t + pi/3)` metre. Find its
amplitude and time period.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Compare with  y = A sin(omega t + phi)

      A      =  4 m
      omega  =  2 rad / s

           2 pi        2 x 3.14
      T  = ------  =  ----------  =  3.14 s
           omega          2
```

## Topic: Velocity, acceleration and phase

### Q A11
Where in its path is the velocity of a particle in SHM maximum, and where is its
acceleration maximum?

**ANSWER:**
```
  VELOCITY is MAXIMUM at the MEAN position       v_max = A omega
  ACCELERATION is MAXIMUM at the EXTREME positions   a_max = A omega^2

  At the mean position the acceleration is ZERO.
  At the extreme positions the velocity is ZERO.
```

---

### Q A12
What is the phase difference between displacement and acceleration in SHM?

**ANSWER:** **pi radian (180 degrees).** The acceleration is always in the
direction opposite to the displacement, which is why `a = -omega^2 y` carries a
minus sign.

---

### Q A13
What is the phase difference between the displacement and the velocity of a
particle in SHM?

**ANSWER:** **pi/2 radian (90 degrees)**, with the velocity **leading** the
displacement.
```
      y = A sin(wt)          v = A w cos(wt) = A w sin(wt + pi/2)
```

---

### Q A14
What is the shape of the graph drawn between acceleration and displacement for a
particle in SHM?

**ANSWER:** A **straight line passing through the origin with a negative
slope**. The slope equals `-omega^2`.

## Topic: Energy

### Q A15
Write the expression for the total energy of a particle in SHM. Does it change
during the motion?

**ANSWER:**
```
      E  =  (1/2) m omega^2 A^2   =   (1/2) k A^2

  NO — it does not change. Kinetic and potential energy keep converting
  into each other, but their SUM stays constant at every point.
```

---

### Q A16
The amplitude of a particle in SHM is doubled. What happens to its total energy?

**ANSWER:**
```
  E is proportional to A^2.

  A -> 2A   gives   E -> (2)^2 E  =  4 E

  The total energy becomes FOUR TIMES the original.
```

---

### Q A17
At what displacement is the kinetic energy of a particle in SHM equal to its
potential energy?

**ANSWER:**
```
   KE = PE

   (1/2) m w^2 (A^2 - y^2)  =  (1/2) m w^2 y^2

   A^2 - y^2  =  y^2    ->   A^2 = 2 y^2

              A
   y   =   -------- =  0.707 A
            sqrt(2)
```

## Topic: Simple pendulum

### Q A18
What is a seconds pendulum? What is its length?

**ANSWER:** A seconds pendulum is one whose **time period is exactly 2 seconds**
(so it ticks once every second).
```
           T^2 g       (2)^2 x 9.8
      l = -------  =  -------------  =  0.9927 m   (nearly 1 metre)
           4 pi^2         4 pi^2
```

---

### Q A19
Does the time period of a simple pendulum depend on the mass of the bob? Give
the reason.

**ANSWER:** **No.** In the derivation the restoring force is `-mg sin theta` and
the acceleration is `F/m = -g sin theta`, so the mass **cancels out**. The period
`T = 2 pi sqrt(l/g)` contains no m.

---

### Q A20
What happens to the time period of a simple pendulum when it is taken to the
Moon?

**ANSWER:**
```
  g on the Moon is about g/6, and T is inversely proportional to sqrt(g).

      T_moon  =  T_earth x sqrt(6)  =  2.45  T_earth

  The time period INCREASES about 2.45 times (the clock runs slow).
```

---

### Q A21
What happens to a simple pendulum kept in a freely falling lift?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In free fall the effective acceleration due to gravity is

        g_eff  =  g - g  =  0

  So   T = 2 pi sqrt( l / 0 )  =  INFINITY.

  There is no restoring force, so the pendulum DOES NOT OSCILLATE at all.
```

---

### Q A22
A pendulum clock is taken from the equator to the pole. Will it gain or lose
time?

**ANSWER:** g is **larger** at the pole, and T is inversely proportional to
sqrt(g), so **T decreases** — the clock ticks faster and **gains** time.

---

## Topic: Springs, damping and resonance

### Q A23
Define the force constant of a spring. Give its SI unit and dimensions.

**ANSWER:** The force constant `k` is the restoring force produced per unit
extension (or compression) of the spring.
```
      F = -k y      ->      k = F / y

      SI unit    :  newton per metre (N / m)
      Dimensions :  [ M T^-2 ]
```

---

### Q A24
Does the time period of a loaded spring depend on the acceleration due to
gravity? Justify.

**ANSWER:** **No.** `T = 2 pi sqrt(m/k)` contains only m and k; g does not
appear. Gravity only shifts the mean position downwards, it does not change the
period. So a spring-mass system has the same period on the Moon as on Earth.

---

### Q A25
What are damped oscillations?

**ANSWER:** Oscillations in which the **amplitude decreases steadily with time**
because a resistive force (air resistance, friction) continuously dissipates the
energy of the oscillator as heat and sound. The amplitude falls exponentially:
`A(t) = A e^(-bt/2m)`.

---

### Q A26
Define resonance. Give one example.

**ANSWER:** When the frequency of an applied periodic force becomes **equal to
the natural frequency** of the body, the amplitude of the forced oscillation
becomes **maximum**. This condition is called resonance.
```
  EXAMPLE:  tuning a radio — the receiver circuit is adjusted until its
            natural frequency matches the broadcast frequency of one
            station, which is then received loudly.
```

---

### Q A27
Why are soldiers ordered to break step while crossing a bridge?

**ANSWER:** Marching in step applies a **periodic force** to the bridge. If its
frequency happens to equal a natural frequency of the bridge, **resonance**
occurs, the amplitude of vibration builds up enormously and the bridge may
collapse. Breaking step destroys the periodicity of the force.

### Q B1
Derive expressions for the velocity and acceleration of a particle executing
SHM, and hence find their maximum values.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Let    y  =  A sin(omega t + phi)

  VELOCITY

        dy
   v = --- = A omega cos(omega t + phi)
        dt

   Using  cos = sqrt(1 - sin^2)  and  sin(wt + phi) = y / A :

                            y^2
   v  =  A omega sqrt( 1 - ----- )
                            A^2

   +-----------------------------------+
   |   v  =  omega sqrt( A^2 - y^2 )   |
   +-----------------------------------+

  ACCELERATION

        dv
   a = --- = - A omega^2 sin(omega t + phi)
        dt

   +-----------------------------+
   |   a  =  - omega^2  y        |
   +-----------------------------+

  MAXIMUM VALUES

   v is maximum when y = 0  (MEAN position)      v_max = A omega
   a is maximum when y = A  (EXTREME position)   a_max = A omega^2

   At the mean position a = 0 ; at the extreme position v = 0.
```

### Q B2
Show that the projection of uniform circular motion on a diameter is simple
harmonic motion.

**ANSWER:**
```
                            Y
                            |
                       .----+----.
                    .'    P |      `.
                  .'      /\|        `.
                 /       /  |          \
                |       /   |           |
                |    N /----+           |
                |     /theta|           |
                 \   /      |          /
                  `./       |        .'
                    `.      |      .'
                       `----+----'

  1.  A particle P moves on a circle of radius A with constant angular
      speed omega. This circle is called the REFERENCE CIRCLE.

  2.  In time t the radius turns through an angle (omega t + phi).

  3.  N is the foot of the perpendicular from P onto the Y-diameter.
      Its displacement from the centre is

            y  =  A sin( omega t + phi )

  4.  Differentiating twice:

            dy/dt   =  A omega cos(omega t + phi)

            d2y/dt2 =  - A omega^2 sin(omega t + phi)
                    =  - omega^2 y

  5.  The acceleration of N is proportional to its displacement and is
      directed towards the centre.

  HENCE the projection N executes SIMPLE HARMONIC MOTION, with the
  radius A as its amplitude and the angular speed omega as its
  angular frequency.
```

### Q B3
Derive expressions for the kinetic energy and the potential energy of a particle
in SHM.

**ANSWER:**
```
  KINETIC ENERGY

     KE  =  (1/2) m v^2 ,   and   v = omega sqrt(A^2 - y^2)

     +-------------------------------------------+
     |   KE  =  (1/2) m omega^2 ( A^2 - y^2 )    |
     +-------------------------------------------+

     Maximum at the mean position (y = 0):  KE = (1/2) m omega^2 A^2
     Zero at the extreme position (y = A).

  POTENTIAL ENERGY

     Restoring force  F = - m omega^2 y
     Work done against it for a further displacement dy is
           dW = ( m omega^2 y ) dy

     PE  =  INT ( m omega^2 y ) dy   from 0 to y

         =  m omega^2 [ y^2 / 2 ]

     +-------------------------------------------+
     |   PE  =  (1/2) m omega^2 y^2              |
     +-------------------------------------------+

     Zero at the mean position, maximum at the extreme position.
```

---

### Q B4
Show that the total energy of a particle in SHM is constant.

**ANSWER:**
```
   E  =  KE  +  PE

      =  (1/2) m omega^2 (A^2 - y^2)  +  (1/2) m omega^2 y^2

      =  (1/2) m omega^2 A^2  -  (1/2) m omega^2 y^2
                              +  (1/2) m omega^2 y^2

   The two y-terms cancel:

      +---------------------------------------+
      |    E  =  (1/2) m omega^2 A^2          |
      +---------------------------------------+

   E contains no y and no t, so it is the SAME at every point of the
   path and at every instant. The total energy of a particle in SHM is
   CONSTANT, and it is proportional to m, to A^2 and to omega^2.

   ENERGY GRAPH

      E |\                          /
        | \        TOTAL E         /       PE (upward parabola)
        |--\----------------------/-----   flat line
        |   \                    /
        |    \       KE        /           KE (downward parabola)
        |     \____________ _/
      0 +---+---------+---------+--- y
           -A         0        +A
```

### Q B5
Derive an expression for the time period of a mass attached to a spring
(a loaded spring).

**ANSWER:**
```
        wall
        |///|~~~~~~~~~~~[ m ]     equilibrium
        |///|~~~~~~~~~~~~~~[ m ]  displaced by y
                <--- F = -k y

  1.  Let a mass m attached to a spring of force constant k be displaced
      through a small distance y from its equilibrium position.

  2.  By Hooke's law the spring exerts a restoring force

            F  =  - k y

  3.  F is proportional to y and is directed towards the mean position,
      so the motion is SIMPLE HARMONIC.

  4.  Acceleration:

            F        k
      a  = ---  =  - - y
            m        m

  5.  Comparing with  a = - omega^2 y :

                       k
           omega = sqrt(-)
                       m

  6.  Therefore

           +----------------------------------+
           |                        m         |
           |    T  =  2 pi  sqrt( ----- )     |
           |                        k         |
           +----------------------------------+

  The same result holds for a VERTICAL spring, because the static
  extension e satisfies mg = ke and those two terms cancel from the
  net force. Since g does not appear, the period is unchanged on the
  Moon or in a freely falling lift.
```

Two springs of force constants k1 and k2 are connected (a) in series (b) in

**ANSWER:**

```
           F      F     F                1      1     1
          ---  = ---- + ----     ->     ---- = ---- + ----

                    k1 k2                            m (k1 + k2)
           k_s  =  --------      T  =  2 pi  sqrt( -------------- )
                   k1 + k2                             k1 k2

  (b) PARALLEL — the same extension in both, the forces add:

           k_p  =  k1 + k2

                                    m

  Parallel gives a LARGER k, hence a SHORTER period.
```

### Q B7
What are damped oscillations? Derive/write the equation of motion and describe
how the amplitude varies with time.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION: oscillations whose amplitude decreases with time because a
  resistive force continuously removes energy from the system.

  DAMPING FORCE:   F_d  =  - b v      (b = damping constant, unit kg/s)

  EQUATION OF MOTION:

       m a  =  - k x  -  b v

         d2x        dx
       m ---  +  b  --  +  k x  =  0
         dt2        dt

  SOLUTION:

       x  =  A e^( - b t / 2m )  cos( omega' t + phi )

                         k        b^2
       omega'  =  sqrt( ---  -  ------- )
                         m       4 m^2

  AMPLITUDE:    A(t) = A e^(-bt/2m)  —  it falls EXPONENTIALLY,
                not linearly.

  ENERGY:       E(t) = (1/2) k A^2 e^(-bt/m)  —  it falls twice as fast.

  GRAPH
     x |  .-.
       | /   \  .-.
       |/     \/   \ .-.
     0 +------------'---'--.--.----.---.----> t
       |        \   /  `-'  `-'  '--'
       |         `-'
       |   (the dashed envelope is A e^(-bt/2m))

  The period stays practically unchanged; only the peaks shrink.
```

### Q B8
Explain forced oscillations and resonance. Give two examples of resonance.

**ANSWER:**
```
  FREE OSCILLATION   -  a body displaced and released oscillates at its
                        own NATURAL frequency n0.

  FORCED OSCILLATION -  when an external periodic force of frequency n
                        is applied continuously, the body is made to
                        oscillate at the DRIVING frequency n, not at n0.
                        The amplitude stays constant but is usually
                        small.

  RESONANCE          -  when n becomes EQUAL to n0, the amplitude of the
                        forced oscillation becomes MAXIMUM. This is
                        resonance. The amplitude is then limited only by
                        the damping present.

  RESONANCE CURVE
     A |            /|\        <- small damping: tall sharp peak
       |           / | \
       |       .--'  |  `--.   <- larger damping: short broad peak
       |  ..--'      |      `--..
       +-------------+-------------> driving frequency
                     n0

  EXAMPLES
    1.  Soldiers break step on a bridge, so that the frequency of their
        marching does not match the natural frequency of the bridge.
    2.  Tuning a radio: the circuit's natural frequency is matched to
        the station's frequency, which is then received loudly.
    3.  The Tacoma Narrows bridge (USA, 1940) was destroyed when wind
        drove it at one of its natural frequencies.
```

### Q B13 (numerical)
A particle in SHM has a total energy of 0.04 J and an amplitude of 4 cm. Find


### Q B16 (numerical)
A particle in SHM has a velocity of 16 cm/s at a displacement of 3 cm, and 12
cm/s at a displacement of 4 cm. Find the amplitude and the period.

**ANSWER:**
```
   v^2  =  omega^2 ( A^2 - y^2 )

   (16)^2  =  w^2 ( A^2 - 9 )      ->    256 = w^2 (A^2 - 9)
   (12)^2  =  w^2 ( A^2 - 16 )     ->    144 = w^2 (A^2 - 16)

  Divide:

    256       A^2 - 9
   -----  =  ----------
    144       A^2 - 16

   256 ( A^2 - 16 )  =  144 ( A^2 - 9 )

   256 A^2 - 4096    =  144 A^2 - 1296

   112 A^2           =  2800

       A^2  =  25    ->    A  =  5 cm

  Substitute back:   256 = w^2 (25 - 9) = 16 w^2   ->  w^2 = 16, w = 4 rad/s

       T  =  2 pi / omega  =  6.28 / 4  =  1.57 s

  CHECK the second equation: w^2 (A^2 - 16) = 16 x (25 - 16) = 144  TICK
```

```
                        |    \
                        |     O   bob of mass m
                        |    /|\
                        |   / | \
                        |  /  |  \
                    mean  /   |   \
                  position    |    \
                 mg sin theta |   mg cos theta
                              |
                              v
                             mg


  PART 3 — DERIVATION                                        [4 marks]

  1.  A simple pendulum consists of a point mass (bob) suspended by a
      weightless inextensible string from a rigid support.

  2.  Let the bob be displaced through a small angle theta. Its
      displacement along the arc is  x = l theta.

  3.  The weight mg is resolved into two components:
          mg cos theta  -  along the string, balanced by the tension T
          mg sin theta  -  perpendicular to the string, this is the
                           RESTORING force.

  4.  Hence            F  =  - mg sin theta

      (the minus sign shows it is directed towards the mean position)

  5.  For SMALL angles (theta less than about 4 degrees, in radians)

                sin theta  =  theta         approximately

          so    F  =  - mg theta

  6.  From the geometry,  theta = x / l , therefore

                            m g
                    F  =  - ---  x
                             l

      F is proportional to -x, so THE MOTION IS SIMPLE HARMONIC.

  7.  Acceleration:

                    F         g
             a  =  ---  =  -  -  x
                    m         l

  8.  Comparing with  a = - omega^2 x :

                         g                        g
             omega^2 =  ---      ->  omega = sqrt(---)
                         l                        l

  9.  Time period:

                   2 pi
             T  = ------
                  omega

             +-------------------------------+
             |                     l         |
             |    T  =  2 pi  sqrt(---)      |
             |                     g         |
             +-------------------------------+

      and the frequency  n = (1 / 2 pi) sqrt(g / l).


  PART 4 — SECONDS PENDULUM                                  [1 mark]

  A seconds pendulum is one whose time period is exactly 2 seconds.

             T^2 g       4 x 9.8
       l  = -------  =  ---------  =  0.9927 m   (nearly 1 metre)
             4 pi^2       39.48

  ASSUMPTIONS: small amplitude; weightless inextensible string; point
  mass bob; no air resistance; rigid support; swing in a vertical plane.

  T does NOT depend on the mass of the bob or on the amplitude.
```

### Q C2
Derive expressions for the kinetic energy, the potential energy and the total
energy of a particle executing SHM. Show that the total energy is constant, and
draw a graph of the energies against displacement.

**ANSWER — model answer with the mark split:**

```
  SET-UP                                                     [1 mark]

  Let a particle of mass m execute SHM of amplitude A and angular
  frequency omega.

        y  =  A sin(omega t + phi)
        v  =  omega sqrt(A^2 - y^2)
        a  =  - omega^2 y
        restoring force  F = - m omega^2 y  =  - k y ,  k = m omega^2


  KINETIC ENERGY                                             [2 marks]

        KE  =  (1/2) m v^2

            =  (1/2) m [ omega sqrt(A^2 - y^2) ]^2

        +--------------------------------------------+
        |   KE  =  (1/2) m omega^2 ( A^2 - y^2 )     |
        +--------------------------------------------+

        In terms of time:
            KE  =  (1/2) m A^2 omega^2 cos^2(omega t + phi)

        Maximum at y = 0  :  KE = (1/2) m omega^2 A^2
        Zero at y = ±A.


  POTENTIAL ENERGY                                           [2 marks]

        To displace the particle a further distance dy against the
        restoring force, the work done is

            dW  =  ( m omega^2 y )  dy

        Total work done from 0 to y is stored as potential energy:

            PE  =  INT ( m omega^2 y ) dy    from 0 to y

                =  m omega^2 [ y^2 / 2 ]  from 0 to y

        +--------------------------------------------+
        |   PE  =  (1/2) m omega^2 y^2  =  (1/2)k y^2|
        +--------------------------------------------+

        In terms of time:
            PE  =  (1/2) m A^2 omega^2 sin^2(omega t + phi)

        Zero at y = 0 ; maximum at y = ±A.


  TOTAL ENERGY                                               [2 marks]

        E  =  KE + PE

           =  (1/2) m omega^2 (A^2 - y^2) + (1/2) m omega^2 y^2

           =  (1/2) m omega^2 A^2 - (1/2) m omega^2 y^2
                                  + (1/2) m omega^2 y^2

        +---------------------------------------------------+
        |   E  =  (1/2) m omega^2 A^2  =  2 pi^2 m n^2 A^2  |
        +---------------------------------------------------+

        E contains neither y nor t, therefore the TOTAL ENERGY IS
        CONSTANT throughout the motion. Energy is only exchanged
        between the kinetic and potential forms; none is lost.

        E is proportional to  m , to A^2 and to n^2.


  GRAPH                                                      [1 mark]

     ENERGY
        |
     E  |\                                       /|
        | \                                     / |   PE  (parabola up)
        |  \                                   /  |
        |---\---------------------------------/---|--- TOTAL E (flat)
        |    \                               /    |
        |     \            KE               /     |   KE  (parabola down)
        |      \___                    ___/       |
        |          ---____________----            |
      0 +----------+----------+----------+-------- y
                  -A          0         +A

     NOTE: KE and PE each complete TWO cycles in one full oscillation,
     so the frequency of the energy variation is 2n and its period T/2.
```

### Q C3
What is simple harmonic motion? Derive expressions for the displacement,
velocity and acceleration of a particle in SHM. Draw graphs of these three
quantities against time and state the phase relations between them.

**ANSWER — model answer:**

```
  DEFINITION                                                 [1 mark]

  Motion in which the acceleration is directly proportional to the
  displacement from the mean position and is always directed towards
  the mean position:      a  =  - omega^2 y


  DISPLACEMENT                                               [2 marks]

  The defining condition gives the differential equation

           d2y
           ----  +  omega^2 y  =  0
           dt2

  whose general solution is

           y  =  A sin( omega t + phi )

  where A is the amplitude, omega the angular frequency, phi the epoch
  and (omega t + phi) the phase.


  VELOCITY                                                   [2 marks]

           dy
      v = ---- = A omega cos( omega t + phi )
           dt

      Using cos(wt+phi) = sqrt(1 - y^2/A^2) :

           v  =  omega sqrt( A^2 - y^2 )

      v is MAXIMUM (= A omega) at the mean position, ZERO at the
      extreme positions.


  ACCELERATION                                               [2 marks]

           dv
      a = ---- = - A omega^2 sin( omega t + phi )  =  - omega^2 y
           dt

      a is ZERO at the mean position and MAXIMUM (= A omega^2) at the
      extreme positions.

      Since a is proportional to -y, the motion IS simple harmonic —
      which proves that y = A sin(wt + phi) represents SHM.


  GRAPHS AND PHASE RELATIONS                                 [1 mark]

     y  |   ,-.        ,-.
        |  /   \      /   \
      0 +-/-----\----/-----\----> t
        |/       \  /       \
        |         '-         '-

     v  |,-.        ,-.
        |    \      /  \
      0 +-----\----/----\-----> t
        |      '--'      '--

     a  |     ,-.        ,-.
        |    /   \      /   \
      0 +---/-----\----/-----\--> t
        |  /       '--'       '

     PHASE RELATIONS
        velocity     LEADS displacement by  pi/2  (90 degrees)
        acceleration LEADS velocity     by  pi/2  (90 degrees)
        acceleration is OPPOSITE to displacement, pi (180 degrees)
```

### Q C4
Derive an expression for the time period of a body oscillating at the end of a
spring, both horizontally and vertically. Discuss the effect of connecting two
springs in series and in parallel.

**ANSWER — detailed skeleton with the mark split:**

```
  PART 1 — HORIZONTAL SPRING                                 [3 marks]

     wall
     |///|~~~~~~~~~~~~[ m ]      equilibrium
     |///|~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~[ m ] displaced by y
              <--- F = -k y

     1.  Displace the block through a small distance y.
     2.  Hooke's law:  F = - k y  (restoring, towards the mean position)
     3.  F is proportional to -y  ->  the motion is SHM.
     4.  a = F/m = -(k/m) y
     5.  Comparing with a = -omega^2 y :  omega = sqrt(k/m)
     6.  T = 2 pi / omega

              +-------------------------------+
              |                     m         |
              |    T  =  2 pi  sqrt(---)      |
              |                     k         |
              +-------------------------------+


  PART 2 — VERTICAL SPRING                                   [3 marks]

     ///////////
         $
         $  spring, constant k
         $  natural length
     ----+-----------------------
         $   e  = static extension
        [m]     with  m g  =  k e        ...(1)
         |  y  = further displacement
        [m]

     Net upward force when pulled a further distance y:

         F  =  - [ k(e + y)  -  m g ]
            =  - [ k e  +  k y  -  m g ]

     Using (1),  k e = m g , so those two terms cancel:

         F  =  - k y             ->    SHM again

     Therefore the SAME formula holds:

              T  =  2 pi sqrt( m / k )

     and, substituting k = mg/e ,

              T  =  2 pi sqrt( e / g )

     IMPORTANT: g does NOT appear in T = 2 pi sqrt(m/k). Gravity only
     shifts the mean position downwards by e; it does not change the
     period. So a loaded spring has the same period on the Moon and
     even inside a freely falling lift, unlike a pendulum.


  PART 3 — SERIES AND PARALLEL                               [2 marks]

     SERIES (end to end)  -  the same force acts through both springs,
     and the extensions add:

              1        1       1                   k1 k2
            -----  =  ---- +  ----     ->   k_s = --------
             k_s       k1      k2                 k1 + k2

            k_s is smaller than either  ->  T INCREASES

     PARALLEL (side by side)  -  the same extension, the forces add:

              k_p  =  k1  +  k2

            k_p is larger than either   ->  T DECREASES

     CUTTING A SPRING: k is inversely proportional to the length, so
     cutting a spring into n equal parts gives each part k' = n k,
     which DECREASES the time period.
```

### Q C5
Explain free, damped and forced oscillations. What is resonance? Describe the
resonance curve and give examples of resonance in everyday life.

**ANSWER — detailed skeleton:**

```
  1. FREE OSCILLATIONS                                       [2 marks]

     A body displaced from equilibrium and released oscillates at its
     own NATURAL frequency n0, determined only by its own properties:

          simple pendulum:  n0 = (1/2 pi) sqrt(g / l)
          loaded spring  :  n0 = (1/2 pi) sqrt(k / m)

     In the ideal (frictionless) case the amplitude stays constant
     forever.


  2. DAMPED OSCILLATIONS                                     [2 marks]

     In practice air resistance and friction remove energy, so the
     amplitude decreases with time. The damping force is

          F_d  =  - b v

     and the equation of motion is

          m (d2x/dt2)  +  b (dx/dt)  +  k x  =  0

     with solution   x = A e^(-bt/2m) cos(omega' t + phi)

     The amplitude falls EXPONENTIALLY:  A(t) = A e^(-bt/2m)

        x |  .-.
          | /   \ .-.
        0 +------'---'--.--.---.--.---> t
          |     \  /  `-' `-'  '-'
          |      `'


  3. FORCED OSCILLATIONS                                     [2 marks]

     If an external periodic force F = F0 cos(omega_d t) is applied
     continuously, the body is forced to oscillate at the DRIVING
     frequency, not at its own natural frequency. The amplitude then
     stays constant, but it depends on how close omega_d is to omega_0.


  4. RESONANCE AND ITS CURVE                                 [2 marks]

     When the driving frequency EQUALS the natural frequency, the
     amplitude becomes MAXIMUM. This is RESONANCE.

        A |            /|\        <- small damping: tall, sharp peak
          |           / | \
          |       .--'  |  `--.   <- more damping: short, broad peak
          |  ..--'      |      `--..
          +-------------+-------------> driving frequency
                        n0

     Less damping -> taller and sharper peak.
     More damping -> shorter and broader peak.

     EXAMPLES
       (i)   Soldiers break step on a bridge so that their marching
             frequency does not match a natural frequency of the
             bridge.
       (ii)  Tuning a radio — the receiver circuit is adjusted so that
             its natural frequency equals the station's frequency.
       (iii) The Tacoma Narrows bridge (USA, 1940) collapsed when wind
             drove it at one of its natural frequencies.
       (iv)  A child on a swing pushed once per swing goes higher and
             higher.
       (v)   Buildings whose natural frequency matches earthquake waves
             suffer the greatest damage.
```
