# Kinetic Theory — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — STATE THE POSTULATES OF KINETIC THEORY              4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the postulates / assumptions of the kinetic theory
            of gases."
  METHOD    Write EIGHT numbered lines:
            1. very large number of identical elastic spheres
            2. continuous RANDOM motion, all directions, all speeds
            3. molecular SIZE negligible compared with the container
            4. NO intermolecular forces except during collisions
            5. all collisions PERFECTLY ELASTIC, collision time negligible
            6. straight-line motion between collisions, Newton's laws hold
            7. molecules distributed UNIFORMLY, gravity neglected
            8. pressure is caused by BOMBARDMENT on the walls
  TRAP      Writing only 3 or 4 postulates. The mark scheme usually gives
            half a mark per postulate - write them ALL.
  NOTE      Pure recall. Never lose this.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — DERIVE P = (1/3) rho c^2                         4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Derive the expression for the pressure exerted by an ideal
            gas" / "Give the kinetic interpretation of pressure."
  METHOD    1. Draw the CUBE of side L with a molecule striking one face.
            2. Momentum given to the wall per collision  =  2 m vx
            3. Time between two hits on the same face    =  2 L / vx
            4. Force by one molecule = 2m vx / (2L/vx) = m vx^2 / L
            5. Total force  F = (m/L) SUM(vx^2)
            6. P = F / L^2 = (m/V) SUM(vx^2)
            7. Randomness:  SUM(vx^2) = (1/3) SUM(v^2)
            8. Mean square speed:  SUM(v^2) = N c^2
            9. P = (1/3)(m N/V) c^2 = (1/3) rho c^2
  TRAP      Saying "average speed" instead of "MEAN SQUARE speed".
            Forgetting the FACTOR 2 in the momentum change.
            Forgetting to divide by the area L^2.
            Not drawing the diagram - it carries its own mark.
  NOTE      *** THE HIGHEST-FREQUENCY QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — KINETIC INTERPRETATION OF TEMPERATURE               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Show that the average kinetic energy of a molecule is
            proportional to the absolute temperature" /
            "What is the kinetic interpretation of temperature?"
  METHOD    1. Kinetic theory:  P V = (1/3) m N c^2
            2. Gas equation  :  P V = N k T
            3. Equate and cancel N:  (1/3) m c^2 = k T
            4. Multiply by 3/2:  (1/2) m c^2 = (3/2) k T
            5. State: average KE depends ONLY on absolute temperature.
            6. Per mole (multiply by NA): (1/2) M c^2 = (3/2) R T
  TRAP      Not writing the concluding SENTENCE. The algebra alone is
            not the full answer - the examiner wants the interpretation.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — EXPLAIN ABSOLUTE ZERO                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the significance of absolute zero?" /
            "Explain absolute zero on the basis of kinetic theory."
  METHOD    Put T = 0 into (1/2) m c^2 = (3/2) k T  ->  c = 0.
            State: molecular motion CEASES and the gas exerts ZERO
            pressure. 0 K = -273.15 degC is the lowest possible
            temperature.
  TRAP      Saying "the gas becomes solid". Say MOTION CEASES.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — DEDUCE BOYLE'S LAW / CHARLES' LAW                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Deduce Boyle's law from the kinetic theory."
  METHOD    1. P V = (1/3) m N c^2
            2. = (2/3) N x (1/2) m c^2 = (2/3) N x (3/2) k T
            3. so  P V = N k T
            4. BOYLE:   T fixed -> N k T constant -> P V = constant
               CHARLES: P fixed -> V = (Nk/P) T   -> V ~ T
            5. Add the physical explanation in one sentence.
  TRAP      Starting from P V = n R T. That is the EXPERIMENTAL law -
            you are being asked to DERIVE it from the theory, so you
            must start from P = (1/3) rho c^2.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — DEDUCE AVOGADRO / DALTON / GRAHAM                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Deduce Avogadro's law / Dalton's law / Graham's law from
            the kinetic theory."
  METHOD    AVOGADRO  same P, V, T -> N1 m1 c1^2 = N2 m2 c2^2 ,
                      same T -> m1 c1^2 = m2 c2^2 , divide -> N1 = N2
            DALTON    no intermolecular forces, so each gas acts alone
                      P = P1 + P2 + P3 + ...
            GRAHAM    r ~ c_rms = sqrt(3P/rho) ~ 1/sqrt(rho) ~ 1/sqrt(M)
                      r1/r2 = sqrt(M2/M1)
  TRAP      For Avogadro, forgetting the SECOND equation (equal KE at
            equal T). Without it you cannot cancel.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — CALCULATE c_rms FROM T AND M                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the rms speed of oxygen molecules at 27 degC."
  METHOD    1. T to KELVIN (add 273).
            2. M to kg/mol (divide g/mol by 1000).
            3. c = sqrt( 3 R T / M ), R = 8.314
  TRAP      Leaving M in g/mol. That makes the answer sqrt(1000) = 31.6
            times too big. Sanity check: ordinary gases give a few
            hundred m/s.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — CALCULATE c_rms FROM P AND rho                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The density of a gas at a pressure of ... is ... Find the
            rms speed."
  METHOD    c = sqrt( 3 P / rho ). One line. Nothing else needed.
  TRAP      Converting the pressure: 1 atm = 1.013 x 10^5 Pa,
            76 cm of Hg = 1 atm. Do this conversion FIRST.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — FIND THE TEMPERATURE THAT CHANGES c_rms             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "At what temperature will the rms speed be double / triple
            its value at 27 degC?"
  METHOD    c ~ sqrt(T)   ->   T2 / T1 = ( c2 / c1 )^2
            Double the speed  ->  T x 4
            Triple the speed  ->  T x 9
  TRAP      Doubling the CELSIUS temperature. Convert to kelvin FIRST,
            multiply, then convert back if the answer is wanted in degC.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — COMPARE TWO GASES AT THE SAME TEMPERATURE          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare the rms speeds of hydrogen and oxygen at the same
            temperature."
  METHOD               c1              M2
                      ----  =   sqrt  ----      (FLIP, then square-root)
                       c2              M1
  TRAP      Getting the ratio upside down. Remember: the LIGHTER gas is
            always the FASTER one. Check your answer against that.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — EQUAL SPEEDS AT DIFFERENT TEMPERATURES             4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "At what temperature will the rms speed of oxygen equal that
            of hydrogen at 27 degC?"
  METHOD    Equal c_rms means equal  T / M :

                 T1        T2
                ----  =  -----          then solve for the unknown T.
                 M1        M2
  TRAP      Using masses in kg here is unnecessary - since it is a
            RATIO, g/mol works fine. But be consistent on both sides.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — THE THREE SPEEDS AND THEIR RATIO                   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define most probable / average / rms speed" or
            "Give the ratio of the three speeds."
  METHOD    c_rms = sqrt(3RT/M)     v_av = sqrt(8RT/pi M)
            v_mp  = sqrt(2RT/M)
            RATIO  v_mp : v_av : c_rms = sqrt2 : sqrt(8/pi) : sqrt3
                                       = 1 : 1.128 : 1.224
            ORDER  v_mp < v_av < c_rms
  TRAP      Mixing up the 2, 8/pi and 3. Memory hook: the numbers
            2, 8, 3 go with MOST PROBABLE, AVERAGE, RMS -
            and only the AVERAGE one has pi in it.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — RMS FROM A LIST OF SPEEDS                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Five molecules have speeds 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 m/s. Find the
            average and the rms speed."
  METHOD    v_av  = (sum of speeds) / N
            c^2   = (sum of the SQUARES) / N ,   c_rms = sqrt(c^2)
  TRAP      Squaring the average instead of averaging the squares.
            Sanity check: c_rms must come out LARGER than v_av.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — THE MAXWELL-BOLTZMANN DISTRIBUTION                 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw and explain the Maxwell distribution of molecular
            speeds" / "How does the curve change with temperature?"
  METHOD    1. Draw dN/dv against v: starts at zero, peaks at v_mp,
               long tail on the right, NOT symmetric.
            2. Mark v_mp, v_av and c_rms on it, in that order.
            3. Say the AREA = total number of molecules N.
            4. On heating: peak moves RIGHT, becomes LOWER and BROADER,
               more molecules have high speeds, AREA UNCHANGED.
  TRAP      Drawing a symmetric bell curve. It is skewed, with a long
            right tail. Also, students often forget to say the area is
            unchanged - that is a whole mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — DEFINE DEGREES OF FREEDOM AND COUNT THEM           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are degrees of freedom? Give them for a monatomic /
            diatomic / polyatomic gas."
  METHOD    Definition: number of independent ways a molecule can move
            and store energy.
                Monatomic   f = 3   (3 translational)
                Diatomic    f = 5   (3 trans + 2 rotational)
                Diatomic with vibration  f = 7
                Polyatomic  f = 6   (3 trans + 3 rotational)
  TRAP      Giving a diatomic molecule 3 rotational degrees of freedom.
            Rotation ABOUT the bond axis has a negligible moment of
            inertia, so a diatomic gets only 2.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — STATE AND APPLY THE LAW OF EQUIPARTITION           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the law of equipartition of energy and use it to find
            the internal energy of ..."
  METHOD    1. Statement: in thermal equilibrium the energy is shared
               equally, (1/2) k T per degree of freedom per molecule.
            2. Mention the exception: a VIBRATIONAL mode gets k T,
               because it has both a KE term and a PE term.
            3. U = (f/2) n R T
  TRAP      Forgetting the vibration exception - it is often the whole
            point of the question.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — DERIVE Cv, Cp AND gamma                            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Using the law of equipartition, obtain Cv, Cp and gamma
            for monatomic, diatomic and polyatomic gases."
  METHOD    1. U = (f/2) R T per mole
            2. Cv = dU/dT = (f/2) R
            3. Cp = Cv + R = (f/2 + 1) R
            4. gamma = Cp/Cv = 1 + 2/f
            5. Write the TABLE:
                  mono  f=3   3/2 R , 5/2 R , 5/3 = 1.67
                  di    f=5   5/2 R , 7/2 R , 7/5 = 1.40
                  di+v  f=7   7/2 R , 9/2 R , 9/7 = 1.29
                  poly  f=6     3 R ,   4 R , 4/3 = 1.33
  TRAP      Writing Cp = Cv - R. It is ALWAYS Cp = Cv + R,
            because the gas must also do work when it expands.
  NOTE      *** SECOND MOST FREQUENT QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — IDENTIFY THE GAS FROM gamma                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The ratio of specific heats of a gas is 1.4. Find its
            degrees of freedom / name the gas."
  METHOD                       2
                     f  =  ----------
                            gamma - 1
            Then read off:  f=3 mono, f=5 diatomic, f=6 polyatomic,
                            f=7 vibrating diatomic.
  TRAP      Using gamma - 1 in the numerator. Write the formula down
            first, then substitute.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — HEAT SUPPLIED AT CONSTANT V OR CONSTANT P          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How much heat is needed to raise n moles of oxygen from
            ... to ... at constant volume / constant pressure?"
  METHOD    1. Identify the gas type -> f -> Cv and Cp.
            2. Constant volume:    Q = n Cv delta T
               Constant pressure:  Q = n Cp delta T
            3. Work done at constant P:  W = Q(p) - Q(v) = n R delta T
  TRAP      delta T is the same in kelvin and celsius (a DIFFERENCE),
            so you do NOT need to convert it - but the individual
            temperatures in any other part of the question DO need it.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — SPECIFIC HEATS OF A MIXTURE                        4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "n1 moles of gas A are mixed with n2 moles of gas B. Find
            Cv, Cp and gamma of the mixture."
  METHOD                n1 Cv1  +  n2 Cv2
            Cv(mix) = ----------------------
                           n1  +  n2

            Cp(mix) = Cv(mix) + R
            gamma   = Cp(mix) / Cv(mix)
  TRAP      *** NEVER AVERAGE gamma DIRECTLY. ***
            (1.67 + 1.40)/2 is a WRONG answer that is always offered
            as an option. Average Cv, then add R, then divide.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — SPECIFIC HEAT OF SOLIDS AND OF WATER               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the specific heat of solids / of water using the
            law of equipartition."
  METHOD    SOLID  each atom has 3 vibrational modes, each of energy kT
                   -> U = 3 R T  ->  C = 3 R = 25 J/mol/K
                   (LAW OF DULONG AND PETIT; Cp is nearly Cv for solids)
                   Fails at low T and for diamond / boron / beryllium.
            WATER  3 atoms per molecule x 3 k T each  =  9 k T
                   -> U = 9 R T  ->  C = 9 R = 75 J/mol/K
                   ( = 4200 J/kg/K, the familiar value)
  TRAP      Using (1/2) kT per vibrational mode. A vibration is worth
            a FULL kT.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — DEFINE MEAN FREE PATH / STATE ITS FORMULA          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define mean free path" / "Write the expression for it."
  METHOD    Definition: the average distance travelled by a molecule
            between two successive collisions.

                             1                 k T
            lambda  =  ---------------  =  ----------------
                        sqrt2 pi d^2 n     sqrt2 pi d^2 P
  TRAP      Writing d instead of d^2, or leaving out the sqrt2.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — WHAT DOES THE MEAN FREE PATH DEPEND ON?            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "On what factors does the mean free path depend?" /
            "How does lambda change if the pressure is doubled?"
  METHOD    lambda ~ 1/n , 1/d^2 , 1/rho
            at constant T:  lambda ~ 1 / P
            at constant P:  lambda ~ T
            at constant V:  INDEPENDENT of T
  TRAP      *** ALWAYS CHECK WHAT IS BEING HELD CONSTANT. ***
            "Raise the temperature - does lambda increase?" has two
            different answers depending on whether P or V is fixed.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — MEAN FREE PATH NUMERICAL                           4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Values of d, P and T (or of d and n) are given.
  METHOD    1. Compute k T.
            2. Compute sqrt2 x pi = 4.443.
            3. Compute d^2.
            4. lambda = kT / (4.443 x d^2 x P)
            5. Collision frequency Z = v_av / lambda if asked.
  TRAP      Sanity check: at ordinary pressure lambda should come out
            around 10^-7 m and Z around 10^9 per second. If you get
            10^-2 or 10^-15, you slipped a power of ten.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — BROWNIAN MOTION                                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is Brownian motion? What causes it? What does it
            prove?"
  METHOD    1. Definition: continuous, random, zig-zag motion of tiny
               particles suspended in a fluid.
            2. Cause: unbalanced bombardment by the fluid molecules.
            3. Significance: direct visible evidence for the molecular
               nature of matter and for continuous random motion.
            4. Faster when: T higher, particle smaller/lighter, fluid
               less viscous and less dense.
  TRAP      Saying it is caused by "convection currents" or "air".
            It is caused by MOLECULAR BOMBARDMENT.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — AVERAGE KE NUMERICALS                              2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the average kinetic energy of a molecule / of a mole /
            of x grams of a gas at ... degC."
  METHOD    per MOLECULE   (3/2) k T
            per MOLE       (3/2) R T
            of n moles     (3/2) n R T   with n = mass / molar mass
  TRAP      Reading "molecule" as "mole". The two answers differ by a
            factor of 6 x 10^23. Underline the word in the question.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — "DOES IT DEPEND ON ...?" CONCEPT CHECK             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The average kinetic energy of a molecule depends on ..." /
            "A gas is compressed isothermally. What happens to c_rms?"
  METHOD    Memorise this box:
              average KE       depends ONLY on T
              c_rms            depends ONLY on T and M
              pressure         depends on n/V and T
              mean free path   depends on n and d (not on speed)
  TRAP      Assuming that compressing a gas speeds up its molecules.
            At constant temperature it does NOT.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — IDEAL GAS EQUATION NUMERICALS                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   Any two of P, V, n, T given and the third asked; or
            "find the number of molecules in a bulb of volume ...".
  METHOD    P V = n R T   or   P V = N k T
            Convert:  litre -> 10^-3 m^3 ,  cm^3 -> 10^-6 m^3 ,
                      atm -> 1.013 x 10^5 Pa ,  mm of Hg -> h rho g
  TRAP      Volume in litres and pressure in atmospheres with
            R = 8.314. Either convert everything to SI, or use
            R = 0.0821 litre-atm/mol/K consistently. Do not mix.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — GRAHAM'S LAW / DIFFUSION                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Compare the rates of diffusion of ... and ..." /
            "Gas A diffuses 4 times faster than gas B. Find the ratio
            of their molar masses."
  METHOD                r1             M2               rho2
                       ----  =  sqrt  ----   =   sqrt  ------
                        r2             M1               rho1
  TRAP      Going backwards from rates to masses: the mass ratio is
            the SQUARE of the inverse speed ratio, so a factor of 4 in
            rate is a factor of 16 in molar mass.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — RELATION BETWEEN R, k AND NA                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the relation between the universal gas constant, the
            Boltzmann constant and the Avogadro number."
  METHOD    R = NA x k    ,   k = R / NA
            R is per MOLE (8.314 J/mol/K)
            k is per MOLECULE (1.38 x 10^-23 J/K)
            Verify: 6.022 x 10^23 x 1.38 x 10^-23 = 8.31
  TRAP      Writing k = R NA. Since k is a much SMALLER number,
            it must be R DIVIDED by the huge Avogadro number.
```

# GUARANTEED-MARKS PLAN FOR THIS CHAPTER

```
  STEP 1 - THE 2-MARK BANK  (learn these ten short answers word for word)

     1.  Definition of mean free path + its formula          [P22]
     2.  Factors affecting the mean free path                [P23]
     3.  Statement of the law of equipartition               [P16]
     4.  Degrees of freedom: 3 / 5 / 6                       [P15]
     5.  Kinetic interpretation of temperature               [P3]
     6.  Meaning of absolute zero                            [P4]
     7.  Definition of c_rms + c = sqrt(3RT/M)               [P7]
     8.  Ratio 1 : 1.128 : 1.224                             [P12]
     9.  R = NA k                                            [P30]
    10.  Brownian motion: what, why, significance            [P25]

     Any two of these appearing in Section A = 4 easy marks.


  STEP 2 - THE 4-MARK BANK  (be able to WRITE these on blank paper)

     A.  The postulates (all eight)                          [P1]
     B.  *** THE DERIVATION OF P = (1/3) rho c^2 ***         [P2]
     C.  Cv, Cp and gamma table with the derivation          [P17]
     D.  Mean free path derivation + factors                 [P9, P22]

     Practise B and C by hand THREE TIMES each. Nothing else in this
     chapter comes close in value per hour spent.


  STEP 3 - THE NUMERICAL DRILL  (fifteen minutes, every other day)

     Pick any gas, any temperature, and compute:
        c_rms  ,  v_av  ,  v_mp  ,  average KE per molecule ,
        internal energy of 1 mole ,  Cv , Cp , gamma
     Check:  v_mp < v_av < c_rms  and  c_rms is a few hundred m/s.

     If those two checks pass, your arithmetic is right.


  STEP 4 - THE FIVE MISTAKES THAT COST THE MOST MARKS

     1.  Temperature not converted to KELVIN.
     2.  Molar mass left in g/mol instead of kg/mol.
     3.  Averaging gamma for a mixture instead of averaging Cv.
     4.  Confusing "per molecule" (k) with "per mole" (R).
     5.  Saying "average speed" in the pressure derivation instead of
         "MEAN SQUARE speed".


  REALISTIC TARGET

     Section A  ->  2 questions x 2 marks   =  4
     Section B  ->  1 question  x 4 marks   =  4
                                              ---
                                               8 marks out of 60

     For a weak student that is a whole grade, from ONE evening of work.
```
