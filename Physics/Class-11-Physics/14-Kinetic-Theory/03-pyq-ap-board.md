## Topic: Molecular nature of matter and the ideal gas

### Q A1
State Avogadro's hypothesis. What is the Avogadro number?

**ANSWER:**
```
  AVOGADRO'S HYPOTHESIS:
     Equal volumes of all gases, at the same temperature and pressure,
     contain equal numbers of molecules.

  AVOGADRO NUMBER:
     NA  =  6.022 x 10^23 per mole
     = the number of molecules present in one mole of any substance.
```

### Q A3
Under what conditions does a real gas behave like an ideal gas? Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  At LOW PRESSURE and HIGH TEMPERATURE (i.e. at low density).

  Reason: the molecules are then very far apart, so
     (i)  their own volume is negligible compared with the container, and
     (ii) the intermolecular forces are negligible.
  These are exactly the two assumptions of the kinetic theory.
```

### Q A7
What is the kinetic interpretation of temperature?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The absolute temperature of a gas is a measure of the AVERAGE KINETIC
  ENERGY of its molecules:

        1               3
       --- m c^2   =   --- k T
        2               2

  The average KE depends ONLY on the absolute temperature - not on the

```

### Q A8
What is the significance of absolute zero on the basis of kinetic theory?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Putting T = 0 in  (1/2) m c^2 = (3/2) k T  gives  c = 0.

  So absolute zero (0 K = -273.15 degC) is the temperature at which
  the random molecular motion of a gas COMPLETELY CEASES and the gas
  exerts ZERO PRESSURE. It is the lowest attainable temperature.
```

## Topic: Degrees of freedom and equipartition

### Q A17
Define degrees of freedom. Give the degrees of freedom of a monatomic, a
diatomic and a polyatomic gas molecule.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEGREES OF FREEDOM = the number of independent ways in which a molecule
  can move and store energy (= number of independent coordinates needed
  to describe its motion completely).

     Monatomic  (He, Ar)      ->  f = 3      (3 translational)
     Diatomic   (O2, N2)      ->  f = 5      (3 trans + 2 rotational)
     Polyatomic (H2O, CH4)    ->  f = 6      (3 trans + 3 rotational)

  A vibrating diatomic molecule has f = 7.
```

### Q A18
State the law of equipartition of energy.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In thermal equilibrium at temperature T, the total energy of a system
  is distributed EQUALLY among all its degrees of freedom, and the
  energy associated with EACH degree of freedom is

        (1/2) k T   per molecule       [ or (1/2) R T per mole ]

  Each VIBRATIONAL MODE gets k T, because it has both a kinetic and a
  potential energy term.
```

---

### Q A19
Why does a vibrational mode contribute k T and not (1/2) k T?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Vibrational energy  =  (1/2) m (dy/dt)^2   +   (1/2) K y^2
                          ^ kinetic term         ^ potential term

  There are TWO squared terms, and each squared term gets (1/2) k T.

        2  x  (1/2) k T   =   k T
```

### Q A20
Why is Cp greater than Cv? Write the relation between them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  At constant VOLUME all the heat supplied raises the internal energy.

  so extra heat is needed for the same temperature rise.

  Therefore  Cp > Cv , and

        Cp  -  Cv  =  R          (MAYER'S RELATION, per mole)
```

## Topic: Mean free path and Brownian motion

### Q A24
Define mean free path and write its expression.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MEAN FREE PATH is the AVERAGE distance travelled by a molecule
  BETWEEN TWO SUCCESSIVE COLLISIONS.

                     1                      k T
  lambda   =  -----------------   =   -----------------
               sqrt2 pi d^2 n          sqrt2 pi d^2 P

  d = molecular diameter,  n = number of molecules per unit volume
```

---

### Q A25
On what factors does the mean free path depend?

**ANSWER:**
```
  lambda  ~  1 / n        (number density)
  lambda  ~  1 / d^2      (molecular diameter)
  lambda  ~  1 / P        (at constant temperature)
  lambda  ~  T            (at constant pressure)
  lambda  ~  1 / rho      (density)

  It is INDEPENDENT of the speed of the molecules, and therefore
  independent of temperature at constant VOLUME.
```

---

### Q A26
What is Brownian motion? What causes it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BROWNIAN MOTION is the continuous, random, zig-zag motion of small
  particles (such as pollen grains or smoke particles) suspended in a
  fluid.

  CAUSE: unbalanced bombardment of the particle by the rapidly moving
  molecules of the fluid.

  It is direct visible EVIDENCE for the molecular nature of matter and
  for continuous random molecular motion.
```

---

### Q A27
Give two factors that make Brownian motion more vigorous.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  A HIGHER temperature (fluid molecules move faster).
  2.  A SMALLER, lighter suspended particle.

  (Also: lower viscosity and lower density of the fluid.)
```

### Q B1  *** VERY FREQUENT ***
State the postulates of the kinetic theory of gases.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  A gas consists of a very LARGE NUMBER of identical molecules,
      which behave as tiny hard perfectly elastic spheres.

  2.  The molecules are in CONTINUOUS RANDOM MOTION in all directions
      with all possible speeds.

  3.  The SIZE of a molecule is NEGLIGIBLE compared with the volume of
      the container; they are treated as point masses.

  4.  There are NO FORCES of attraction or repulsion between molecules
      except during collisions, so a molecule has no potential energy -
      all its energy is kinetic.

  5.  All collisions (molecule-molecule and molecule-wall) are
      PERFECTLY ELASTIC: momentum and kinetic energy are conserved.
      The time of a collision is negligible compared with the time
      between collisions.

  6.  Between collisions a molecule moves in a STRAIGHT LINE with
      constant velocity, obeying Newton's laws.

  7.  The molecules are distributed UNIFORMLY in the container and the
      effect of GRAVITY on them is neglected.

  8.  The PRESSURE of the gas is due to the continuous bombardment of
      the molecules on the walls of the container.
```

### Q B2  *** THE MOST ASKED QUESTION IN THE CHAPTER ***
Derive an expression for the pressure of an ideal gas on the basis of the kinetic
theory. (Derive P = (1/3) rho c^2)

**ANSWER:**
```
  Consider a cubical vessel of side L containing N molecules of an ideal
  gas, each of mass m.  Volume  V = L^3.

                     y
                     ^
             +-------|---------------+
            /|       |              /|
           / |       |             / |
          +--+-------|------------+  |
          |  |       |            |  |
          |  |       +------------|--+-------> x
          |  |      /             |  /
          |  +-----/--------------|-/
          | /     /               |/
          +------z----------------+
        FACE ABCD  (area L^2, perpendicular to the x-axis)


  Take one molecule with velocity components vx, vy, vz.

  STEP 1  It strikes face ABCD elastically, so vx becomes -vx.

             Momentum before  =  + m vx
             Momentum after   =  - m vx
             Momentum given to the wall  =  2 m vx

  STEP 2  It travels to the opposite face and back, a distance 2L,
          before hitting this face again:

             time between two hits    =    2 L / vx

  STEP 3  Force due to this ONE molecule (Newton's second law):

                    2 m vx           m vx^2
             F1 = ----------   =   ---------
                   2 L / vx            L

  STEP 4  Total force on the face from all N molecules:

                    m
             F  =  --- ( vx1^2 + vx2^2 + ... + vxN^2 )
                    L

  STEP 5  Pressure = Force / Area = F / L^2 :

                     m                        m
             P  =  ----- SUM( vx^2 )   =    ----- SUM( vx^2 )
                    L^3                       V

  STEP 6  The motion is completely RANDOM, so no direction is special:

             SUM(vx^2)  =  SUM(vy^2)  =  SUM(vz^2)

             and since  v^2 = vx^2 + vy^2 + vz^2 ,

                                     1
             SUM(vx^2)   =          --- SUM( v^2 )
                                     3

  STEP 7  Define the MEAN SQUARE SPEED

                      SUM(v^2)
             c^2  =  ----------      ->     SUM(v^2)  =  N c^2
                          N

  STEP 8  Substituting:

                     1    m N
             P  =   --- x ----- c^2
                     3      V

          and since  m N / V = rho (density of the gas):

             +----------------------------+
             |            1               |
             |     P  =  --- rho c^2      |
             |            3               |
             +----------------------------+
```

### Q B3
On the basis of kinetic theory, show that the average kinetic energy of a gas
molecule is directly proportional to the absolute temperature. Hence explain
absolute zero.

**ANSWER:**
```
  From kinetic theory:         P V  =  (1/3) m N c^2       ... (i)

  From the gas equation:       P V  =  N k T               ... (ii)

  Equating (i) and (ii):

         1
        --- m N c^2   =   N k T
         3

         1
        --- m c^2   =   k T
         3

  Multiplying by 3/2:

         1               3
        --- m c^2   =   --- k T
         2               2

  +--------------------------------------------------------------+
  |   AVERAGE KINETIC ENERGY PER MOLECULE  =  (3/2) k T          |
  |   which is directly proportional to the ABSOLUTE temperature |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------+

  Multiplying by NA (using m NA = M and k NA = R):

         1               3
        --- M c^2   =   --- R T        =  average KE per MOLE
         2               2

  ABSOLUTE ZERO:
     Putting T = 0 gives c = 0. So at 0 K the random motion of the
     molecules ceases entirely and the gas exerts no pressure. This is
     the lowest possible temperature.
```

### Q B4
Deduce Boyle's law and Charles' law from the kinetic theory of gases.

**ANSWER:**
```
  From kinetic theory:

        P V   =   (1/3) m N c^2

              =   (2/3) N x [ (1/2) m c^2 ]

              =   (2/3) N x (3/2) k T

        P V   =   N k T                          ... master equation


  BOYLE'S LAW
     For a given mass of gas, N is fixed. At CONSTANT TEMPERATURE T,
     the right-hand side N k T is a constant.

           =>   P V  =  constant        =>       P  ~  1/V

     Physically: halve the volume and the molecules strike the walls
     twice as often, so the pressure doubles.


  CHARLES' LAW
     At CONSTANT PRESSURE, for a fixed mass of gas,

                  N k
           V  =  ----- x T      with N, k and P constant
                   P

           =>    V  ~  T

     Physically: heating makes the molecules faster; to keep the
     pressure the same the gas must expand.
```

### Q B5
Deduce Avogadro's law, Dalton's law and Graham's law from the kinetic theory.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AVOGADRO'S LAW
     Two gases at the same P, V and T:

        P V = (1/3) N1 m1 c1^2      and      P V = (1/3) N2 m2 c2^2

        =>   N1 m1 c1^2  =  N2 m2 c2^2                  ... (i)

     Same temperature means equal average kinetic energies:

             (1/2) m1 c1^2  =  (1/2) m2 c2^2
        =>         m1 c1^2  =  m2 c2^2                  ... (ii)

     Dividing (i) by (ii):        N1  =  N2      QED


  DALTON'S LAW OF PARTIAL PRESSURES
     There are no intermolecular forces, so each gas in a mixture
     exerts its pressure independently of the others:

              1                       1
        P = ----- m1 N1 c1^2   +   ----- m2 N2 c2^2  +  ...
             3 V                     3 V

        P = P1 + P2 + P3 + ...       QED


  GRAHAM'S LAW OF DIFFUSION
     The rate of diffusion r is proportional to the molecular speed:

                          ________
                         / 3 P                    1
        r  ~  c_rms  =  / ------      ->   r  ~  ---------
                      \/   rho                   sqrt(rho)

              r1            rho2               M2
             ----  =  sqrt -------   =   sqrt -----      QED
              r2            rho1               M1
```

### Q B6
Explain the law of equipartition of energy and use it to find the internal energy
of one mole of a monatomic, a diatomic and a polyatomic gas.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW OF EQUIPARTITION OF ENERGY
     In thermal equilibrium at temperature T, the total energy of a
     system is shared EQUALLY among all its degrees of freedom, each
     degree of freedom carrying (1/2) k T per molecule, i.e.
     (1/2) R T per mole.
     Every VIBRATIONAL MODE carries k T, because it has both a kinetic
     and a potential energy term.

  Energy per mole  U  =  (f / 2) R T


  MONATOMIC   f = 3  (3 translational)

        U  =  (3/2) R T


  DIATOMIC    f = 5  (3 translational + 2 rotational)

        U  =  (5/2) R T

        (with vibration, f = 7 and U = (7/2) R T)


  POLYATOMIC  f = 6  (3 translational + 3 rotational)

        U  =  3 R T

  NOTE: the TRANSLATIONAL part is (3/2) R T for EVERY gas. The extra
  energy of a diatomic or polyatomic gas is stored in rotation.
```

### Q B8
Explain the specific heat capacity of solids and of water on the basis of the law
of equipartition of energy.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SOLIDS
     An atom in a solid can neither travel nor rotate. It can only
     VIBRATE about its mean position, and it can do so along x, y and z
     - that is 3 vibrational modes.

     Each vibrational mode contributes k T:

           energy per atom  =  3 k T

           energy per mole  U  =  3 R T

                        dU
           C  =  ------  =  3 R  =  24.9  =  about 25 J mol^-1 K^-1
                        dT

     This is the LAW OF DULONG AND PETIT. For a solid the expansion is
     negligible, so Cp is almost the same as Cv.
     It FAILS at low temperatures and for diamond, boron and beryllium.


  WATER
     Treat water as a solid whose "atoms" are those of the molecule.
     Each water molecule H2O contains 3 atoms, and each atom
     contributes 3 k T:

           energy per molecule  =  3 x 3 k T  =  9 k T

           energy per mole      U  =  9 R T

           C  =  9 R  =  74.8 J mol^-1 K^-1

     The measured value is 75.2 J mol^-1 K^-1 - excellent agreement.
     Dividing by the molar mass 0.018 kg gives about 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1,
     the familiar specific heat of water.
```

### Q B9
Explain the term mean free path. Derive an expression for it and state the
factors on which it depends.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MEAN FREE PATH is the average distance travelled by a molecule
  between two successive collisions.

                       *
                      / \                    * = collision
      o              /   \
       \            /     \      /\
        \          /       \    /  \
         \        /         \  /    \      /\
          \      /           \/      \    /  \
           \    /             *       \  /    \
            \  /                        \/      \
             \/                          *       o
              *


  DERIVATION (outline)
     A molecule of diameter d moving with speed v sweeps out an
     imaginary cylinder of radius d.

           <-------------  v t  ------------->
        +-------------------------------------+
        |    o       o        o       o       |
      ( |        o        o       o           | )   radius d
        |   o        o        o        o      |
        +-------------------------------------+

     It collides with every molecule whose centre lies inside this
     cylinder.

        Volume swept in time t     =  pi d^2 v t
        Number of collisions       =  n pi d^2 v t     (n = number density)

                     distance travelled          v t              1
        lambda  =  ----------------------  =  -------------  =  ---------
                   number of collisions       n pi d^2 v t      n pi d^2

     Since the other molecules are also moving, Maxwell's more careful
     treatment using the RELATIVE speed introduces a factor sqrt(2):

        +----------------------------------------------------+
        |                     1                  k T         |
        |   lambda  =  ---------------  =  ---------------   |
        |               sqrt2 pi d^2 n      sqrt2 pi d^2 P   |
        +----------------------------------------------------+


  FACTORS
     lambda ~ 1 / n        more crowded  ->  shorter path
     lambda ~ 1 / d^2      bigger molecules  ->  shorter path
     lambda ~ 1 / P        at constant temperature
     lambda ~ T            at constant pressure
     lambda ~ 1 / rho

     It does NOT depend on the molecular speed, and therefore is
     INDEPENDENT of temperature at constant volume.
```

   4.  The AREA under the curve is the total number of molecules N and

   5.  v_mp  <  v_av  <  c_rms , in the ratio 1 : 1.128 : 1.224

   1.  The peak moves to the RIGHT (v_mp increases as sqrt T).
   2.  The peak becomes LOWER and the curve BROADER / flatter.
   3.  The fraction of molecules with high speeds INCREASES.
   4.  The total area (i.e. N) is UNCHANGED.

       Light gas (H2)  ->  peak far right, broad
       Heavy gas (O2)  ->  peak to the left, tall and narrow

### Q C1  *** THE MOST LIKELY LONG ANSWER ***
State the postulates of the kinetic theory of gases. Derive the expression for
the pressure exerted by an ideal gas and hence deduce the kinetic interpretation
of temperature.

**MARK SPLIT**
```
  Postulates (any 6 of the 8, one mark for every two)  ...  3 marks
  Diagram of the cube with a molecule striking a wall  ...  1 mark
  Derivation up to P = (1/3) rho c^2                   ...  3 marks
  Kinetic interpretation of temperature                ...  1 mark
```

**MODEL ANSWER**
```
  PART 1 - POSTULATES
     (write the 8 postulates exactly as in Q B1 above)


  PART 2 - PRESSURE OF AN IDEAL GAS

     Consider a cube of side L containing N molecules each of mass m.
     V = L^3.

                     y
                     ^
             +-------|---------------+
            /|       |              /|
           / |       |             / |
          +--+-------|------------+  |
          |  |       |            |  |
          |  |       +------------|--+-------> x
          |  |      /             |  /
          |  +-----/--------------|-/
          | /     /               |/
          +------z----------------+
             FACE ABCD, area L^2, perpendicular to x


      BEFORE                        AFTER
        vx --------->      |          <--------- vx      |
        o============>     | WALL     <============o     | WALL
        momentum + m vx    |          momentum - m vx    |

     Momentum transferred to the wall per collision   =  2 m vx
     Time between two hits on the same face           =  2 L / vx

                          2 m vx        m vx^2
     Force by 1 molecule = --------  =  --------
                          2L / vx          L

                            m
     Total force        F = --- SUM( vx^2 )
                            L

                            F         m
     Pressure           P = ----  =  ----- SUM( vx^2 )
                           L^2        L^3

     Randomness of motion gives   SUM(vx^2) = SUM(vy^2) = SUM(vz^2)
     and  v^2 = vx^2 + vy^2 + vz^2 , so  SUM(vx^2) = (1/3) SUM(v^2)

     With the mean square speed  c^2 = SUM(v^2) / N :

                 1    m N               1
           P  = --- x ----- c^2   =    --- rho c^2
                 3      V               3


  PART 3 - KINETIC INTERPRETATION OF TEMPERATURE

     From above:            P V  =  (1/3) m N c^2
     From the gas law:      P V  =  N k T

           (1/3) m N c^2   =   N k T

           (1/2) m c^2     =   (3/2) k T

     The average kinetic energy of a molecule is (3/2) k T. It is
     directly proportional to the ABSOLUTE TEMPERATURE and is
     independent of the pressure, the volume and the nature of the gas.
     Temperature is therefore a measure of the average kinetic energy
     of molecular motion.

     At T = 0 K, c = 0: molecular motion ceases. This is absolute zero.
```

### Q C2
State the law of equipartition of energy. Explain degrees of freedom for
monatomic, diatomic and polyatomic gases, and derive Cv, Cp and gamma for each.
Extend the argument to the specific heat capacity of solids and of water.

**MARK SPLIT**
```
  Law of equipartition, with the vibration exception   ...  2 marks
  Degrees of freedom of the three types (with reasons) ...  2 marks
  Derivation of Cv, Cp, gamma and the table            ...  3 marks
  Solids (3R) and water (9R)                           ...  1 mark
```

**MODEL ANSWER**
```
  LAW OF EQUIPARTITION
     In thermal equilibrium at temperature T, the total energy of a
     system is distributed equally among all its degrees of freedom,
     each degree of freedom carrying (1/2) k T per molecule.
     A vibrational mode carries k T because its energy
     (1/2)m(dy/dt)^2 + (1/2)K y^2 has TWO squared terms.


  DEGREES OF FREEDOM

     Monatomic (He, Ar)
        A single atom can only translate along x, y and z.
        f = 3.

     Diatomic (O2, N2)
        Translation along x, y, z gives 3.
        Rotation about the two axes PERPENDICULAR to the bond gives 2.
        (Rotation about the bond axis has negligible moment of inertia.)
        f = 5 at ordinary temperature; f = 7 if the bond vibrates.

     Polyatomic (H2O, CH4, NH3)
        3 translational + 3 rotational (all three axes now have a real
        moment of inertia)  =  6, plus 2 per vibrational mode.
        f = 6 unless vibration is mentioned.


  SPECIFIC HEATS

        U   =  (f/2) R T   per mole

        Cv  =  dU/dT   =  (f/2) R

        Cp  =  Cv + R  =  (f/2 + 1) R                 (Mayer)

        gamma = Cp/Cv  =  1 + 2/f

     +-----------------+---+-----------+-----------+---------------+
     |  GAS            | f |    Cv     |    Cp     |    gamma      |
     +-----------------+---+-----------+-----------+---------------+
     |  Monatomic      | 3 |  (3/2) R  |  (5/2) R  |  5/3 = 1.67   |
     |  Diatomic       | 5 |  (5/2) R  |  (7/2) R  |  7/5 = 1.40   |
     |  Diatomic (vib) | 7 |  (7/2) R  |  (9/2) R  |  9/7 = 1.29   |
     |  Polyatomic     | 6 |    3 R    |    4 R    |  4/3 = 1.33   |
     +-----------------+---+-----------+-----------+---------------+


  SOLIDS
     Each atom of a solid only vibrates, and it can vibrate along
     x, y and z: 3 vibrational modes, each of energy k T.
        U = 3 R T  ->  C = 3 R = about 25 J mol^-1 K^-1
     This is the law of Dulong and Petit. It fails at low temperature.

  WATER
     Water is treated as a solid of 3-atom molecules:
        U = 3 x 3 R T = 9 R T  ->  C = 9 R = 74.8 J mol^-1 K^-1
     The measured value is 75.2 J mol^-1 K^-1.
```

### Q C4
What is the mean free path of a gas molecule? Derive its expression and discuss
the factors affecting it. What is Brownian motion, and how does it support the
kinetic theory?

**MARK SPLIT**
```

  Brownian motion, its cause and its significance      ...  2 marks
```

**MODEL ANSWER**
```
  (Definition, diagram, derivation and factors exactly as in Q B9.)

  BROWNIAN MOTION
     In 1827 Robert Brown observed pollen grains suspended in water
     under a microscope. They moved in a continuous, random, zig-zag
     way that never stopped.

              . . .
            .       \
          .          \  .
         /            \/ \
        /                  \
       .          .         \
        \        / \        /
         \      /   \      .
          \    .     \    /
           \  /       \  /
            \/         \/
             .          .

     CAUSE: the suspended particle is bombarded on all sides by the
     molecules of the fluid. At any instant the number of impacts on
     one side differs slightly from the number on the other, so there
     is a net unbalanced force that pushes the particle. The direction
     of the imbalance changes constantly, giving a random path.

     SIGNIFICANCE: it is DIRECT VISIBLE EVIDENCE that
        (i)  matter is made of molecules, and
        (ii) those molecules are in continuous random motion,
     which are the first two postulates of the kinetic theory.

     It becomes more vigorous at higher temperature, for smaller and
     lighter particles, and in fluids of lower viscosity and density.
```
