## THE 60-SECOND VERSION

Every solid is a crystal of atoms held together by **springs** (the interatomic forces).
Pull the solid and you stretch the springs; let go and the springs pull it back.
That "pull it back" ability is **ELASTICITY**.

```
  1.  You apply a force.  The solid fights back      ->  STRESS  =  F / A
      with an internal restoring force per area.         unit: pascal

  2.  The solid changes shape a little.              ->  STRAIN  =  change
      Measure that change as a fraction.                            --------
                                                                    original
                                                          NO UNIT

  3.  For small loads the two are proportional.      ->  HOOKE'S LAW
                                                         stress = E x strain

  4.  E has three names depending on WHAT you did:   ->  Y   (stretch a wire)
                                                         B   (squeeze from all sides)
                                                         eta (slide the top face)

  5.  Push too hard and the solid never comes        ->  the STRESS-STRAIN CURVE
      back. It has gone PLASTIC, then it breaks.         and its five labelled points
```

That is the entire chapter. Everything else is a numerical dressed up in a story.

## THE ONE-LINE SUMMARY OF EVERY SECTION

| Section | The one line that matters |
|---|---|
| Interatomic forces | Atoms sit at `r0` where the force is zero; stretch them and they pull back |
| Elasticity | The property of regaining the original shape when the load is removed |
| Stress | Restoring force per unit area, `F/A`, unit pascal, dimensions `[M L^-1 T^-2]` |
| Strain | Change divided by original value — a pure number with **no units** |
| Hooke's law | Within the elastic limit, stress is directly proportional to strain |
| Young's modulus | `Y = F L / (A dL)` — for stretching a wire |
| Bulk modulus | `B = -P / (dV/V)` — for squeezing from all sides |
| Rigidity modulus | `eta = F / (A theta)` — for sliding one face over another |
| Poisson's ratio | Lateral strain / longitudinal strain, dimensionless, 0 to 0.5 |
| Elastic energy | `U = (1/2) stress x strain x volume` = area under the graph |
| Applications | I-girder (depth beats width), thick stranded crane rope, 10 km mountain |
