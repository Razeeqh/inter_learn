# TOPIC 1 — WHAT IS PHYSICS?

## The plain-English version

Physics is the study of the **basic laws of nature** and how those laws show
up in everything around you.

The word comes from a Sanskrit-related Greek root meaning "nature".

The whole subject rests on one bet that has paid off for four hundred years:

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  A small number of simple laws, written in the language of        |
  |  mathematics, can explain an enormous variety of things that      |
  |  look completely unrelated on the surface.                        |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## An analogy you already understand

Think of a cricket match. There are thousands of different-looking events —
a cover drive, a yorker, a catch at slip, a ball going for six. But there are
only about 40 rules in the rule book. Every event you see is those same few
rules playing out in different combinations.

Physics claims the universe is like that. A stone falling, the Moon going
round the Earth, the tides in the ocean, and a satellite in orbit look like
four different things. Newton showed they are **one** law wearing four
costumes.

## Two ways physicists work

```
  UNIFICATION                         REDUCTIONISM
  -----------                         ------------
  Explain many different              Explain the properties of a
  phenomena with ONE law.             BIG system by studying the
                                      properties of its small parts.

  Example: Newton's law of            Example: explaining the pressure
  gravitation explains the            and temperature of a gas from the
  falling apple, the orbiting         motion of its individual
  Moon and the ocean tides.           molecules (kinetic theory).

  Example: Maxwell's equations        Example: explaining the properties
  explain electricity, magnetism      of a solid from the arrangement
  and light all at once.              of its atoms.
```

> **TRAP:** Unification and reductionism are the two words examiners like.
> Unification = "many phenomena, one law". Reductionism = "big thing
> explained by its small parts". Do not swap them.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** What is physics? What are the two principal thrusts in physics?

**ANSWER (write exactly this):**
```
  Physics is the study of the basic laws of nature and their
  manifestation in different natural phenomena.

  The two principal thrusts in physics are:
     1. UNIFICATION  - explaining many different phenomena
                       using a single law or a small set of laws.
     2. REDUCTIONISM - explaining the properties of a large,
                       complex system in terms of the properties
                       of its simpler constituent parts.
```

# TOPIC 2 — SCOPE AND EXCITEMENT OF PHYSICS

## The two domains

Physics splits into two big territories, decided purely by **size**.

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |   MACROSCOPIC DOMAIN                MICROSCOPIC DOMAIN             |
  |   ------------------                ------------------             |
  |   Things you can see, or            Atoms, nuclei, electrons,      |
  |   at least see with an              protons, neutrons and          |
  |   ordinary microscope.              elementary particles.          |
  |                                                                    |
  |   Laboratory-sized objects,         Everything smaller than        |
  |   planets, stars, galaxies.         about 10^-10 m.                |
  |                                                                    |
  |   Handled by CLASSICAL PHYSICS:     Handled by QUANTUM MECHANICS.  |
  |     - Mechanics                                                    |
  |     - Thermodynamics                Classical physics FAILS here.  |
  |     - Electrodynamics                                              |
  |     - Optics                                                       |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+

  (Some books add a third, in-between territory called the MESOSCOPIC
   domain: clusters of a few tens or hundreds of atoms, which behave
   partly classically and partly quantum-mechanically.)
```

## Why the excitement? Because of the RANGE

The astonishing thing about physics is not any one law. It is that the same
handful of laws work across a range of sizes so large that the human brain
cannot really picture it.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** What are the macroscopic and microscopic domains of physics?

**ANSWER (write exactly this):**
```
  MACROSCOPIC DOMAIN - deals with phenomena at the laboratory,
  terrestrial and astronomical scales: ordinary objects, planets,
  stars and galaxies. It is covered by classical physics -
  mechanics, thermodynamics, electrodynamics and optics.

  MICROSCOPIC DOMAIN - deals with phenomena at the atomic, molecular
  and nuclear scale, and with elementary particles. It is covered by
  quantum mechanics, because classical physics fails at this scale.
```

# TOPIC 3 — PHYSICS, TECHNOLOGY AND SOCIETY

## The two-way street

Students usually think the arrow goes only one way: scientists discover
something, then engineers build a gadget. That is only half of it.

```
                    +--------------------------------+
                    |                                |
                    v                                |
        +-------------------+            +-------------------+
        |     PHYSICS       |            |    TECHNOLOGY     |
        |  (basic laws and  | ---------> |  (useful devices  |
        |   principles)     |            |   and machines)   |
        +-------------------+            +-------------------+
                    ^                                |
                    |                                |
                    +--------------------------------+

  DOWNWARD ARROW  - a physics discovery makes a new technology possible.
                    Faraday's induction  ->  the electric generator.

  UPWARD ARROW    - a practical technology problem forces new physics.
                    People were building steam engines and wanted them
                    more efficient. Trying to answer "how efficient CAN
                    an engine be?" CREATED the subject of thermodynamics.
                    The engine came FIRST. The theory came SECOND.
```

That second arrow is the part examiners like to hear about, so mention the
steam engine example if you are asked for a two-way relationship.

## The table that actually gets asked

```
  +----------------------------+------------------------------------------+
  |  TECHNOLOGY                |  SCIENTIFIC PRINCIPLE BEHIND IT          |
  +----------------------------+------------------------------------------+
  |  Steam engine              |  Laws of thermodynamics                  |
  |  Nuclear reactor           |  Controlled nuclear fission              |
  |  Nuclear / atom bomb       |  Uncontrolled nuclear fission; E = mc^2  |
  |  Radio and television      |  Generation, propagation and detection   |
  |                            |  of electromagnetic waves                |
  |  Computers                 |  Semiconductor physics and digital logic |
  |  Transistor                |  Semiconductor p-n junction behaviour    |
  |  Laser                     |  Light Amplification by Stimulated       |
  |                            |  Emission of Radiation                   |

  |                            |  induction                               |
```

**Learn the first six rows.** If a 2-mark question comes from this chapter at

## The society part

Technology does not stay in the laboratory. It reshapes ordinary life:

```
  Electricity        ->  ended the working day being fixed by sunlight
  Steam engine       ->  the Industrial Revolution, railways, cities
  Semiconductors     ->  computers, mobile phones, the internet
  Nuclear physics    ->  cheap power, and also nuclear weapons
  Medical physics    ->  X-rays, ultrasound, MRI, radiotherapy
```

> **TRAP:** If a question asks about "physics and society", say something
> balanced. Physics gave us both the nuclear reactor and the atom bomb.
> The knowledge is neutral; the choice of how to use it is a social one.
> A one-line comment like that reads as a mature answer.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** Give two examples where a technology arose out of a scientific
principle, and one example where the technology came first.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE FIRST, TECHNOLOGY AFTER:
     1. Faraday's laws of electromagnetic induction  ->  electric generator
     2. The photoelectric effect  ->  photocell and digital camera sensor

  TECHNOLOGY FIRST, PRINCIPLE AFTER:
     The steam engine was already in use before the laws of
     thermodynamics were formulated. The attempt to improve
     engine efficiency led to the science of thermodynamics.
```

# TOPIC 4 — THE SCIENTIFIC METHOD

## The cycle

Physics is not "someone had a clever idea". It follows a repeatable loop.

```
       +---------------------------------------------------------+
       |                                                         |
       v                                                         |
  1. OBSERVATION                                                 |
     Watch something carefully. Measure it.                      |
       |                                                         |
       v                                                         |
  2. HYPOTHESIS                                                  |
     Make an intelligent guess that could explain it.            |
       |                                                         |
       v                                                         |
  3. CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT                                       |
     Change ONE thing at a time and see what happens.            |
       |                                                         |
       v                                                         |
  4. MATHEMATICAL MODEL / THEORY                                 |
     Write the guess as an equation or a law.                    |
       |                                                         |
       v                                                         |
  5. PREDICTION                                                  |
     Use the theory to predict something NOT yet observed.       |
       |                                                         |
       v                                                         |
  6. VERIFICATION                                                |
     Test the prediction.                                        |
       |                                                         |
       +--- prediction FAILS -> modify or discard the theory ----+
       |
       v
     prediction SUCCEEDS -> the theory survives (for now)
```

The last point matters. A theory in physics is never "proved forever". It is
only "not yet contradicted". Newton's mechanics survived for 200 years and
then failed at very high speeds and very small sizes, and had to be replaced
by relativity and quantum mechanics in those regions.

## Approximation and idealisation

This is the part beginners find dishonest, and it is actually the heart of
the subject.

Physics never describes the real object. It describes a **simplified
imaginary version** of it, chosen so the mathematics becomes possible while
the answer stays close enough to be useful.

```
  +--------------------------+---------------------------------------------+
  |  IDEALISATION            |  WHAT IS BEING IGNORED                      |
  +--------------------------+---------------------------------------------+
  |  Point mass              |  the size and shape of the object           |
  |  Rigid body              |  the object bending or squashing            |
  |  Frictionless surface    |  friction and air resistance                |
  |  Ideal gas               |  the size of molecules and forces between   |
  |                          |  them                                       |
  |  Massless, inextensible  |  the weight of the string and its stretch   |
  |  string                  |                                             |
  |  Simple pendulum         |  the mass of the thread, air drag, and      |
  |                          |  large angles                               |
  |  Perfectly black body    |  any reflection at all from the surface     |
  +--------------------------+---------------------------------------------+
```

The Earth is a lumpy, spinning, atmosphere-covered ball with mountains on it.
To calculate its orbit round the Sun you treat it as a **dot**. And the answer
is right to many decimal places. That is idealisation working.

> **TRAP:** Do not write that approximation is a "weakness" of physics.
> It is a deliberate technique. The skill is knowing WHICH detail can be
> thrown away for the question being asked. Ignoring air resistance is fine
> for a cricket ball over 20 m, and completely wrong for a parachute.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** What is the role of approximation in physics? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Real systems are too complicated to describe exactly, so physics
  replaces them by simplified idealised models that keep only the
  features relevant to the problem. This makes the mathematics
  solvable while the result remains accurate enough to be useful.

  Example: while calculating the orbit of the Earth around the Sun,
  the Earth is treated as a POINT MASS. Its radius, shape, rotation
  and atmosphere are ignored, and the orbit still comes out correct.
```

# TOPIC 5 — THE FOUR FUNDAMENTAL FORCES IN NATURE

**This is the single most important topic in the chapter. If you skip
everything else, do not skip this.**

Every push or pull you have ever seen — friction, tension, muscle force, a
magnet, a nuclear explosion, the Moon's orbit — is one of exactly **four**
forces underneath.

## Force 1 — GRAVITATIONAL FORCE

```
  WHAT IT IS   The mutual attraction between any two objects that
               have mass.
  RANGE        INFINITE. It never switches off, only gets weaker
               with distance (as 1/r^2).
  STRENGTH     10^-39 relative to the strong force. THE WEAKEST OF ALL.
  ALWAYS       ATTRACTIVE. There is no repulsive gravity, and no
               "negative mass" to cancel it.
  MEDIATED BY  the graviton (predicted by theory, never detected).
  ACTS ON      every object in the universe, big or small.
  YOU SEE IT   things falling, your weight, the Moon's orbit, tides,
               the shape of galaxies.
```

Here is the puzzle a good student should notice:

```
  Gravity is the WEAKEST force by a factor of 10^39.
  So why does it dominate the entire universe?

  BECAUSE:
     - it has INFINITE range, and
     - it is ALWAYS ATTRACTIVE, so it never cancels out.

  Electric force is 10^37 times stronger, but ordinary matter contains
  equal amounts of + and - charge, so the electric forces cancel almost
  perfectly on a large scale. Gravity never cancels. Pile up enough mass
  and gravity wins, even though it is feeble per particle.
```

## Force 2 — ELECTROMAGNETIC FORCE

```
  WHAT IT IS   The force between electric charges. Electricity and
               magnetism are two faces of one force.
  RANGE        INFINITE (also falls off as 1/r^2).
  STRENGTH     10^-2 relative to the strong force. Roughly 10^37
               TIMES STRONGER THAN GRAVITY.
  CAN BE       BOTH attractive and repulsive (unlike gravity).
               Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.
  MEDIATED BY  the photon (massless, which is why the range is infinite).
  ACTS ON      charged particles.
  YOU SEE IT   Everything in ordinary daily life EXCEPT weight:
               friction, tension in a rope, the normal reaction from a
               table, the hardness of a solid, chemical bonds, light,
               all of chemistry, all of biology.
```

> **TRAP:** Students write "friction is a separate force". It is not.
> Friction, tension, normal reaction, viscous drag, elastic forces and
> chemical bonds are ALL electromagnetic at the atomic level. Only four
> forces exist. This is a favourite one-line question.

## Force 3 — STRONG NUCLEAR FORCE

```
  WHAT IT IS   The force that binds protons and neutrons together
               inside the nucleus.
  RANGE        VERY SHORT, about 10^-15 m (one fermi) - roughly the
               size of a nucleus. Beyond that it drops to zero
               almost immediately.
  STRENGTH     1. THE STRONGEST FORCE IN NATURE.
               About 100 times stronger than the electromagnetic force.
  CHARGE       It does NOT care about charge. It is equally strong
  INDEPENDENT  between proton-proton, proton-neutron and
               neutron-neutron.
  MEDIATED BY  gluons (in the modern quark picture);
               pi-mesons / pions in the older Yukawa picture.
  ACTS ON      nucleons (protons and neutrons) and other heavy
               particles. It does NOT act on electrons.
  YOU SEE IT   the fact that nuclei do not blow apart, nuclear energy,
               the energy of the Sun.
```

Why this force must exist:

```
  A nucleus contains several protons packed into 10^-14 m.
  All of them are POSITIVE. They repel each other electrically,
  and at that distance the repulsion is enormous.

  Yet the nucleus is stable.

  Therefore SOMETHING stronger than the electric repulsion must be
  holding them together over that tiny distance.
  That something is the STRONG NUCLEAR FORCE.
```

## Force 4 — WEAK NUCLEAR FORCE

```
  WHAT IT IS   The force responsible for radioactive beta decay, in
               which a neutron turns into a proton, emitting an
               electron and an antineutrino.
  RANGE        THE SHORTEST OF ALL, about 10^-16 m - even smaller
               than the nucleus itself.
  STRENGTH     10^-13 relative to the strong force.
               Stronger than gravity, weaker than everything else.
  MEDIATED BY  the W+, W- and Z bosons (these are heavy, which is
               exactly why the range is so tiny).
  ACTS ON      leptons - electrons, neutrinos, and quarks during decay.
  YOU SEE IT   beta radioactivity, neutrino interactions, and the
               hydrogen-burning reactions that power the Sun.
```

## THE COMPARISON TABLE — memorise this one

```
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+------------------+
  |  FORCE         |  RELATIVE  |    RANGE     |  MEDIATING  |  WHERE IT ACTS   |
  |                |  STRENGTH  |              |  PARTICLE   |                  |
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+------------------+
  | Gravitational  |   10^-39   |   INFINITE   |  graviton   | all objects that |
  |                |  (weakest) |              | (predicted, | have mass        |
  |                |            |              |  not found) |                  |
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+------------------+
  | Weak nuclear   |   10^-13   |  ~10^-16 m   |  W and Z    | electrons,       |
  |                |            | (shortest of |  bosons     | neutrinos,       |
  |                |            |     all)     |             | beta decay       |
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+------------------+
  | Electromagnetic|   10^-2    |   INFINITE   |  photon     | charged          |
  |                |            |              |             | particles        |
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+------------------+
  | Strong nuclear |     1      |  ~10^-15 m   |  gluon      | protons and      |
  |                | (strongest)| (size of the |  (pion in   | neutrons inside  |
  |                |            |   nucleus)   | older text) | the nucleus      |
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+------------------+
```

Two facts that carry almost all the marks:

```
  ORDER OF STRENGTH (weakest to strongest):
       GRAVITATIONAL  <  WEAK  <  ELECTROMAGNETIC  <  STRONG

  ONLY TWO FORCES HAVE INFINITE RANGE:
       gravitational and electromagnetic.
       (Both are carried by MASSLESS particles - that is why.)
```

> **TRAP:** The commonest mistake is putting the weak force at the bottom
> because of its name. **Gravity is the weakest, not the weak force.**
> The weak nuclear force is about 10^26 times stronger than gravity.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** What are the four fundamental forces in nature? Arrange them in
increasing order of strength.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The four fundamental forces in nature are:
     1. Gravitational force
     2. Weak nuclear force
     3. Electromagnetic force
     4. Strong nuclear force

  In INCREASING order of strength:

     Gravitational  <  Weak nuclear  <  Electromagnetic  <  Strong nuclear
        10^-39           10^-13             10^-2               1
```

# TOPIC 6 — UNIFICATION OF FORCES

Physics keeps discovering that two forces which looked different are really
the same force seen in two situations. This is the unification story, and it
is the biggest running theme in the subject.

## The history, in order

```
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  YEAR  |  WHO                     |  WHAT WAS UNIFIED                  |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  1687  |  Isaac Newton            |  Terrestrial gravity and celestial |
  |        |                          |  motion. The falling apple and the |
  |        |                          |  orbiting Moon obey ONE law.       |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  1820  |  Hans Christian Oersted  |  Showed an electric current        |
  |  1831  |  Michael Faraday         |  deflects a compass, and a moving  |
  |        |                          |  magnet drives a current.          |
  |        |                          |  Electricity and magnetism are     |
  |        |                          |  inseparable.                      |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  1873  |  James Clerk Maxwell     |  ELECTRICITY + MAGNETISM + OPTICS. |
  |        |                          |  Light is an electromagnetic wave. |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  1970s |  Sheldon Glashow,        |  ELECTROWEAK THEORY.               |
  |        |  Abdus Salam,            |  The electromagnetic force and the |
  |        |  Steven Weinberg         |  weak nuclear force are two faces  |
  |        |  (Nobel Prize 1979)      |  of ONE electroweak force.         |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  1983  |  Carlo Rubbia and        |  Discovered the W and Z bosons,    |
  |  1984  |  Simon van der Meer      |  experimentally CONFIRMING the     |
  |        |  (Nobel Prize 1984)      |  electroweak theory.               |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  ????  |  nobody yet              |  GRAND UNIFIED THEORY (GUT):       |
  |        |                          |  electroweak + strong nuclear.     |
  |        |                          |  ATTEMPTED, NOT ACHIEVED.          |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  ????  |  nobody yet              |  "THEORY OF EVERYTHING":           |
  |        |                          |  all four, including gravity.      |
  |        |                          |  STILL AN OPEN PROBLEM.            |
  +--------+--------------------------+------------------------------------+
```

## The picture

```
   GRAVITATIONAL ------------------------------------------------+
                                                                 |
   WEAK NUCLEAR ------+                                          |
                      |                                          |
                      +---> ELECTROWEAK ------+                  +--> ???
                      |     (DONE, 1970s)     |                  |  "Theory of
   ELECTROMAGNETIC ---+                       +---> GUT ---------+   Everything"
                                              |    (NOT DONE)         (NOT DONE)
   STRONG NUCLEAR ----------------------------+
```

> **TRAP:** Only the **electroweak** unification has actually been achieved
> and experimentally verified. GUT and the "theory of everything" are goals,
> not results. Do not write that all four forces have been unified.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** What is electroweak theory? Who proposed it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Electroweak theory shows that the ELECTROMAGNETIC force and the
  WEAK NUCLEAR force are not two separate forces, but two different
  manifestations of a single underlying force called the
  ELECTROWEAK force.

  It was proposed by Sheldon Glashow, Abdus Salam and Steven Weinberg,
  who shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1979. It was confirmed
  experimentally by the discovery of the W and Z bosons in 1983.
```

# TOPIC 7 — CONSERVATION LAWS

## What a conservation law says

A conservation law says:

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |   For an ISOLATED system, a certain quantity has the SAME value   |
  |   before and after ANY process, no matter how complicated the     |
  |   process is.                                                     |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

"Isolated" means nothing enters or leaves — no external force, no external
torque, no external energy or charge crossing the boundary.

## Why conservation laws are so powerful

This is worth understanding, because it is the reason the topic is in the
syllabus at all.

```
  A collision between two cars is hideously complicated. Metal bends,
  paint scratches, glass shatters, heat is produced, sound is made.
  You could not possibly write down the force at every instant.

  BUT:  total momentum before  =  total momentum after.

  You get the answer WITHOUT knowing any of the messy details.
```

So conservation laws let you jump straight from the "before" picture to the
"after" picture and skip everything in between. They also hold in regions
where the detailed laws are unknown, which is why physicists trust them more
than they trust any particular force law.

## The conservation laws you must know

### 1. Conservation of ENERGY

```
  Energy can neither be created nor destroyed. It only changes form.
  The TOTAL energy of an isolated system stays constant.

  Example: a stone dropped from a height.
     At the top     -  all potential energy, no kinetic energy
     Halfway down   -  half potential, half kinetic
     Just before it -  all kinetic energy, no potential energy
     hits the ground
     After impact   -  converted into heat and sound

  Total stays the same at every stage.

  CONDITION: only for a system with no external energy transfer.
             (If friction acts, mechanical energy is not conserved,
              but TOTAL energy including heat still is.)
```

### 2. Conservation of LINEAR MOMENTUM

```
  If the net EXTERNAL FORCE on a system is zero, its total linear
  momentum stays constant.

     m1 u1 + m2 u2   =   m1 v1 + m2 v2

  It follows directly from Newton's third law.

  Example: firing a gun. Before firing, total momentum = 0.
  After firing, the bullet goes forward and the gun recoils
  backward, and the two momenta cancel exactly.

  Example: rocket propulsion. Gas is thrown backward,
  so the rocket moves forward.
```

### 3. Conservation of ANGULAR MOMENTUM

```
  If the net EXTERNAL TORQUE on a system is zero, its total angular
  momentum stays constant.

     L  =  I omega  =  constant

  Example: a spinning ice skater. When she pulls her arms in,
  her moment of inertia I decreases, so her angular speed omega
  must increase to keep I x omega constant. She spins faster.

  Example: a planet moves faster when it is nearer the Sun.
  (This is exactly Kepler's second law.)
```

### 4. Conservation of CHARGE

```
  The total electric charge of an isolated system is constant.
  Charge is never created or destroyed - only transferred.

  Example: rub a glass rod with silk. The rod becomes positive
  and the silk becomes equally negative. Total charge is still zero.

  Example: in beta decay, a neutron (charge 0) becomes a
  proton (+e) plus an electron (-e) plus an antineutrino (0).
     Charge before = 0.    Charge after = +e - e + 0 = 0.  Balanced.
```

### 5. Conservation of MASS-ENERGY

```
  In ordinary chemistry, mass alone is conserved.
  In NUCLEAR reactions, mass alone is NOT conserved - some mass
  disappears. It has been converted into energy, according to

        E  =  m c^2

  where c = 3 x 10^8 m/s is the speed of light.

  So the correct universal statement is that MASS AND ENERGY TOGETHER
  are conserved, not mass by itself.

  Example: in nuclear fission of uranium, the products weigh slightly
  less than the original nucleus. That "missing mass" (the mass defect)
  appears as the enormous energy released.
```

### 6. Conservation of PARITY

```
  PARITY means mirror symmetry. Conservation of parity says:
  if a physical process can happen, then its MIRROR IMAGE is also
  a possible physical process. Nature has no preference for
  left over right.

  IMPORTANT: parity is conserved in gravitational, electromagnetic
  and strong interactions -

  BUT PARITY IS VIOLATED IN WEAK INTERACTIONS.

  This was predicted by T.D. Lee and C.N. Yang, and demonstrated
  experimentally by C.S. Wu in 1957 using the beta decay of cobalt-60.
  It was one of the biggest shocks in 20th-century physics.
```

> **TRAP:** Parity is the one on this list that is **not** always conserved.
> If a question asks "which conservation law is violated, and where?", the
> answer is parity, in weak interactions. Free marks if you remember it.

## SYMMETRY AND CONSERVATION — stated simply

This is the deep idea behind the whole topic, and it is beautiful. Every
conservation law comes from a **symmetry** of nature — some way in which the
laws of physics refuse to care about a change.

```
  +----------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  SYMMETRY OF NATURE              |  CONSERVATION LAW IT GIVES     |
  |  ("the laws do not care about")  |                                |
  +----------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  WHERE you are                   |  Conservation of               |
  |  (space is homogeneous - the     |  LINEAR MOMENTUM               |
  |  laws are the same in Vijayawada |                                |
  |  and on Mars)                    |                                |
  +----------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  WHICH DIRECTION you face        |  Conservation of               |
  |  (space is isotropic - the laws  |  ANGULAR MOMENTUM              |
  |  are the same north, south, up   |                                |
  |  or down)                        |                                |
  +----------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  WHEN you do the experiment      |  Conservation of               |
  |  (time is homogeneous - the laws |  ENERGY                        |
  |  are the same today and in a     |                                |
  |  thousand years)                 |                                |
  +----------------------------------+--------------------------------+
  |  MIRROR REFLECTION               |  Conservation of PARITY        |
  |  (left-right symmetry)           |  (violated by the weak force)  |
  +----------------------------------+--------------------------------+
```

Say it in one sentence: **every symmetry of nature gives rise to a
conservation law.** That single sentence is enough for any question the
board can ask you on this.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** State any two conservation laws and give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. CONSERVATION OF LINEAR MOMENTUM
     If no external force acts on a system, its total linear
     momentum remains constant.
     Example: when a gun is fired, the forward momentum of the

     Example: for a stone falling freely, the loss in potential
     energy equals the gain in kinetic energy at every point.
```

# TOPIC 9 — THE NATURE OF PHYSICAL LAWS

What makes something a "law of physics"? These are the features:

```
  1.  UNIVERSAL
      The same law works here, on the Moon, and in a distant galaxy.
      Newton's gravitation governs a falling apple and a binary star.

  2.  WRITTEN IN MATHEMATICS
      A physical law is a quantitative statement, not a vague one.
      "Heavy things fall" is not a law. F = m a is.

  3.  SIMPLE, AND FEW IN NUMBER
      An enormous variety of phenomena come from a very small
      number of laws. This simplicity is not obvious - it is a
      discovery, and it is why the subject works at all.

  4.  BASED ON EXPERIMENT, AND FALSIFIABLE
      Every law must be testable. One reliable experiment that
      contradicts a law is enough to kill it.

  5.  VALID ONLY IN A DOMAIN
      Every law has a region where it applies. Newton's mechanics is
      superb for ordinary speeds and ordinary sizes, and WRONG near
      the speed of light (need relativity) and at atomic sizes
      (need quantum mechanics).

  6.  CONSERVATION LAWS ARE THE MOST RELIABLE
      Force laws change as physics advances. Conservation of energy,
      momentum, angular momentum and charge have survived every
      revolution in physics so far.

  7.  THEY ARISE FROM SYMMETRIES
      As shown in Topic 7, the conservation laws are consequences of
      the symmetries of space and time.
```

> **TRAP:** Do not write that Newton's laws were "proved wrong" and thrown
> away. The correct statement is that they were shown to be a **special
> case**, valid in a limited domain, of a more general theory. Relativity
> reduces to Newtonian mechanics when speeds are much less than c.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — model 2-mark answer

**Question:** Write any two characteristics of physical laws.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. Physical laws are UNIVERSAL - the same law applies everywhere
     in the universe and at all times. For example, the law of
     gravitation governs both a falling stone on Earth and the
     motion of distant stars.

  2. Physical laws have a LIMITED DOMAIN OF VALIDITY - each law
     works within a certain range. Newton's laws of motion hold
     for speeds much less than the speed of light, and are
     replaced by relativity at very high speeds.
```
