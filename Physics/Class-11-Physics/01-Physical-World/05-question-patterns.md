# Physical World — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The wording changes every year. The shape
does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — NAME THE FOUR FUNDAMENTAL FORCES                    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the fundamental forces in nature?"
            "Name the four basic forces."
  METHOD    Write all four, in this order:
              1. Gravitational
              2. Electromagnetic
              3. Strong nuclear
              4. Weak nuclear
  TRAP      Writing only three. There are FOUR. Count them before you
            move on.
  NOTE      *** THE SINGLE MOST LIKELY QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — ARRANGE THE FORCES BY STRENGTH                      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Arrange the four fundamental forces in the increasing /
            decreasing order of their strength."
  METHOD    Increasing:
              gravitational < weak < electromagnetic < strong
                 10^-39      10^-13      10^-2           1
            Write the numbers underneath. They cost nothing and they
            prove you know it.
  TRAP      Assuming the "weak" force is the weakest because of its name.
            GRAVITY IS THE WEAKEST.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — STRONGEST / WEAKEST FORCE                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which is the weakest force in nature?"
            "Which is the strongest force in nature?"
  METHOD    Weakest    -> GRAVITATIONAL   (10^-39)
            Strongest  -> STRONG NUCLEAR  (1)
            Add the relative strength in brackets for the second mark.
  TRAP      Answering with only the name and no number, when the
            question says "with its relative strength".
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — RANGE OF THE FORCES                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which forces have infinite range?"
            "Which force has the shortest range?"
  METHOD    INFINITE range  -> gravitational and electromagnetic ONLY
            SHORT range     -> strong nuclear  ~ 10^-15 m (nuclear size)
                            -> weak nuclear    ~ 10^-16 m (shortest)
  BONUS     Add the reason: the mediating particles of the two
            infinite-range forces (graviton, photon) are MASSLESS.
            That one line often converts a half mark into a full one.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — NAME THE MEDIATING PARTICLE                         2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Name the particle that mediates each fundamental force."
            "Which force do W and Z bosons mediate?"
  METHOD    Gravitational    ->  graviton (predicted, not yet detected)
            Electromagnetic  ->  photon
            Strong nuclear   ->  gluon  (pi-meson / pion in older books)
            Weak nuclear     ->  W and Z bosons
  TRAP      Mixing up gluon and graviton. GLUON = GLUES the nucleus =
            STRONG. GRAVITON = GRAVITY.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — WHICH FORCE IS RESPONSIBLE FOR ...?                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Which force is responsible for beta decay / for holding the
            nucleus together / for friction / for the tides?"
  METHOD    Match it against this short list:
              nucleus holds together, nuclear energy   -> STRONG
              beta decay, neutrinos                    -> WEAK
              friction, tension, normal force,         -> ELECTROMAGNETIC
                elasticity, chemical bonds, light
              weight, tides, orbits, galaxies          -> GRAVITATIONAL
  TRAP      Calling friction a separate force. Every contact force in
            daily life is ELECTROMAGNETIC underneath.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — WHY DOES GRAVITY DOMINATE DESPITE BEING WEAKEST?    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Gravity is the weakest force, yet it governs planetary
            motion. Explain."
  METHOD    Give exactly two reasons:
              1. Gravity has INFINITE RANGE.
              2. Gravity is ALWAYS ATTRACTIVE, so it never cancels.
            Then the contrast: the electromagnetic force is about 10^37
            times stronger, but ordinary matter has equal + and -
            charge, so electric forces cancel out over large distances.
  TRAP      Only writing "because it has infinite range". The
            never-cancels point is the more important half.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — CHARGE INDEPENDENCE OF THE STRONG FORCE             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is meant by charge independence of the nuclear force?"
  METHOD    It means the strong nuclear force is EQUAL in strength for
            all three pairs:
                proton-proton = proton-neutron = neutron-neutron
            It does not depend on electric charge at all.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — UNIFICATION: WHO UNIFIED WHAT                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is electroweak theory?"  /  "Who unified electricity,
            magnetism and optics?"  /  "Name the unifications in physics."
  METHOD    Newton                     -> terrestrial + celestial gravity
            Oersted, Faraday           -> electricity and magnetism linked
            Maxwell                    -> electricity + magnetism + optics;
                                          light is an EM wave
            Glashow, Salam, Weinberg   -> ELECTROWEAK (Nobel 1979)
            NOT YET DONE               -> GUT, and a theory including
                                          gravity
  TRAP      Claiming all four forces have been unified. Only the
            ELECTROWEAK unification is finished and verified.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — STATE A CONSERVATION LAW WITH AN EXAMPLE           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State any two conservation laws."
            "State the law of conservation of linear / angular momentum."
  METHOD    Three parts, every time:
              1. THE CONDITION  ("if the net external force is zero ...")
              2. THE STATEMENT  ("the total linear momentum stays constant")
              3. ONE EXAMPLE    (gun recoil / ice skater / falling stone)
  TRAP      Leaving out the CONDITION. "Momentum is always conserved" is
            wrong; it is conserved only when no external force acts.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — IS MASS ALWAYS CONSERVED?                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Is mass conserved in a nuclear reaction?"
            "State the law of conservation of mass-energy."
  METHOD    1. Say NO for mass alone in nuclear reactions.
            2. Give the reason: some mass converts into energy through
                     E = m c^2
            3. State the correct universal law: MASS AND ENERGY TOGETHER
               are conserved.
            4. Example: the mass defect in nuclear fission.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — PARITY AND ITS VIOLATION                           2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is conservation of parity?"
            "Which conservation law is not universally valid?"
  METHOD    1. Parity = mirror symmetry. If a process occurs in nature,
               its mirror image is also a possible process.
            2. It holds for gravitational, electromagnetic and strong
               interactions.
            3. IT IS VIOLATED IN WEAK INTERACTIONS (seen in the beta
               decay of cobalt-60).
  TRAP      Saying parity is always conserved. The violation IS the
            examinable point.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — SYMMETRY GIVES A CONSERVATION LAW                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How is symmetry related to conservation laws?"
  METHOD    One sentence, then three lines:
              "Every symmetry of nature gives rise to a conservation law."
                 space is HOMOGENEOUS -> LINEAR MOMENTUM conserved
                 space is ISOTROPIC   -> ANGULAR MOMENTUM conserved
                 time is HOMOGENEOUS  -> ENERGY conserved
  TRAP      Swapping the first two. HOMOGENEOUS = same at every PLACE
            (position -> linear). ISOTROPIC = same in every DIRECTION
            (direction -> angular).
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — PHYSICIST, CONTRIBUTION AND COUNTRY                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the contribution of C.V. Raman?"
            "Name four Indian physicists and their contributions."
            "Who discovered the neutron?"
  METHOD    Always write THREE things: NAME + CONTRIBUTION + COUNTRY.
            The six Indians are the most likely:
              C.V. Raman        - Raman effect, Nobel 1930
              S. Chandrasekhar  - Chandrasekhar limit, Nobel 1983
              S.N. Bose         - quantum statistics, the boson
              M.N. Saha         - thermal ionisation
              J.C. Bose         - ultra-short radio waves
              H.J. Bhabha       - cascade process of cosmic radiation
  TRAP      Writing only the name. The contribution carries the mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — TECHNOLOGY AND ITS UNDERLYING PRINCIPLE            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give the scientific principle behind: steam engine /
            nuclear reactor / laser / photocell / computer."
  METHOD    Steam engine     -> laws of thermodynamics
            Nuclear reactor  -> controlled nuclear fission
            Laser            -> stimulated emission of radiation
            Photocell,       -> photoelectric effect
              digital camera
            Computer         -> semiconductor physics, digital logic
            Generator        -> electromagnetic induction
            Rocket           -> conservation of linear momentum
            Optical fibre    -> total internal reflection
  BONUS     If asked for the TWO-WAY relationship, add the reverse
            example: the steam engine existed BEFORE thermodynamics,
            and trying to improve it CREATED thermodynamics.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — MACROSCOPIC VS MICROSCOPIC DOMAIN                  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the macroscopic and microscopic domains
            of physics?"
  METHOD    MACROSCOPIC - laboratory, terrestrial and astronomical
                          scales; handled by CLASSICAL PHYSICS
                          (mechanics, thermodynamics, electrodynamics,
                          optics).
            MICROSCOPIC - atoms, molecules, nuclei and elementary
                          particles; handled by QUANTUM MECHANICS,
                          because classical physics fails there.
  TRAP      Forgetting to name the THEORY used in each domain. That is
            where the second mark sits.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — APPROXIMATION AND IDEALISATION                     2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is the role of approximation in physics?"
  METHOD    1. Real systems are too complex to treat exactly.
            2. Physics uses a simplified MODEL that keeps only the
               relevant features, so the mathematics becomes solvable.
            3. Give one example: the Earth treated as a POINT MASS
               when computing its orbit; frictionless surface;
               ideal gas; massless string.
  TRAP      Calling approximation a "defect" of physics. It is a
            deliberate and necessary technique.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — NATURE OF PHYSICAL LAWS                            2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write any two characteristics of physical laws."
  METHOD    Pick any two from:
              universal      - the same everywhere and at all times
              mathematical   - quantitative, not vague
              few and simple - a small number explain a huge variety
              falsifiable    - testable, and killed by one contrary
                               experiment
              limited domain - Newton's laws fail near the speed of
                               light and at atomic sizes
            Give ONE example with whichever two you choose.
  TRAP      Writing that Newton's laws were "proved wrong". Correct
            wording: they are a SPECIAL CASE of a more general theory,
            valid within a limited domain.
```

*(Patterns 15 to 18 are numbered beyond the "fourteen" headline because the
last four are lower-frequency variants. The core pool you must know is
Patterns 1 to 14.)*
