# PATTERN 1 — Rank the four forces

### Q1
The correct order of the four fundamental forces in **increasing** order of
strength is

(a) gravitational < weak < electromagnetic < strong
&nbsp; (b) weak < gravitational < electromagnetic < strong
&nbsp; (c) gravitational < electromagnetic < weak < strong
&nbsp; (d) strong < electromagnetic < weak < gravitational

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  10^-39   <   10^-13   <   10^-2   <   1
  gravity      weak         EM         strong
```
> **SHORTCUT:** The trap is always option (b) — people assume the "weak"
> force must be the weakest because of its name. **Gravity is the weakest.**

---

### Q2
The weakest fundamental force in nature is

(a) strong nuclear &nbsp; (b) electromagnetic &nbsp; (c) weak nuclear
&nbsp; (d) gravitational

**ANSWER: (d)** Gravitational, with relative strength 10^-39.

---

### Q3
The strongest fundamental force in nature is

(a) gravitational &nbsp; (b) strong nuclear &nbsp; (c) electromagnetic
&nbsp; (d) weak nuclear

**ANSWER: (b)** Strong nuclear force, taken as relative strength 1.

---

### Q4
The ratio of the electrostatic force to the gravitational force between two
protons is of the order of

(a) 10^-39 &nbsp; (b) 10^2 &nbsp; (c) 10^36 &nbsp; (d) 10^55

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  The electromagnetic force is about 10^-2 on the same scale on
  which gravity is 10^-39.

      10^-2 / 10^-39  =  10^37

  For the proton-proton pair specifically the standard quoted figure
  is of the order 10^36. Accept 10^36 - 10^37; it is the ORDER that
  is being tested, not the digit.
```

# PATTERN 2 — Range and mediating particle

### Q5
Which of the following forces has infinite range?

(a) strong nuclear &nbsp; (b) weak nuclear &nbsp; (c) electromagnetic
&nbsp; (d) both strong and weak nuclear

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Infinite range:  GRAVITATIONAL and ELECTROMAGNETIC only.
  Reason: their mediating particles (graviton, photon) are MASSLESS.
```

---

### Q6
The force with the **shortest** range is

(a) gravitational &nbsp; (b) electromagnetic &nbsp; (c) strong nuclear
&nbsp; (d) weak nuclear

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
  weak nuclear  ~ 10^-16 m     <-- shortest
  strong nuclear ~ 10^-15 m
  gravitational and electromagnetic: infinite
```

---

### Q7
The mediating particle of the electromagnetic force is

(a) graviton &nbsp; (b) photon &nbsp; (c) gluon &nbsp; (d) W boson

**ANSWER: (b)** Photon.

---

### Q8
The W and Z bosons mediate the

(a) strong nuclear force &nbsp; (b) weak nuclear force
&nbsp; (c) gravitational force &nbsp; (d) electromagnetic force

**ANSWER: (b)** Weak nuclear force. Their large mass is exactly why the
weak force has such a tiny range.

---

### Q9
The particle that is predicted to mediate the gravitational force but has
never been detected is the

(a) gluon &nbsp; (b) neutrino &nbsp; (c) graviton &nbsp; (d) meson

**ANSWER: (c)** Graviton.

---

### Q10
According to the older Yukawa picture, the strong nuclear force between
nucleons is mediated by

(a) electrons &nbsp; (b) photons &nbsp; (c) pi-mesons (pions)
&nbsp; (d) neutrinos

**ANSWER: (c)** Pi-mesons. In the modern quark picture the mediator is
the **gluon**. Both are accepted answers depending on which the paper uses.

# PATTERN 3 — Which force is responsible for this?

### Q11
Friction between two surfaces is fundamentally

(a) a separate fifth force &nbsp; (b) gravitational
&nbsp; (c) electromagnetic &nbsp; (d) weak nuclear

**ANSWER: (c)**
> **SHORTCUT:** Any contact force in daily life — friction, tension,
> normal reaction, elasticity, viscosity, chemical bonding — is
> **electromagnetic**. There are only four forces, and contact forces are
> not one of them.

---

### Q12
Beta decay of a nucleus is governed by the

(a) strong nuclear force &nbsp; (b) weak nuclear force
&nbsp; (c) electromagnetic force &nbsp; (d) gravitational force

**ANSWER: (b)** Weak nuclear force.

---

### Q13
The stability of the nucleus, in spite of the mutual repulsion of protons,
is due to the

(a) gravitational force &nbsp; (b) weak nuclear force
&nbsp; (c) strong nuclear force &nbsp; (d) magnetic force

**ANSWER: (c)** Strong nuclear force, which is about 100 times stronger
than the electrostatic repulsion at nuclear separations.

---

### Q14
The strong nuclear force is charge independent. This means it is the same
between

(a) only p-p pairs &nbsp; (b) only n-n pairs &nbsp; (c) p-p, p-n and n-n pairs
&nbsp; (d) electrons and protons

**ANSWER: (c)** p-p, p-n and n-n pairs equally.

# PATTERN 4 — Unification, conservation and history

### Q15
The electroweak theory unified

(a) the strong and weak nuclear forces
&nbsp; (b) gravitational and electromagnetic forces
&nbsp; (c) electromagnetic and weak nuclear forces
&nbsp; (d) all four fundamental forces

**ANSWER: (c)** Electromagnetic + weak nuclear, by Glashow, Salam and
Weinberg (Nobel Prize 1979).

---

### Q16
Which unification has **not** yet been achieved?

(a) electricity and magnetism &nbsp; (b) terrestrial and celestial gravity
&nbsp; (c) electromagnetic and weak &nbsp; (d) gravity with the other three

**ANSWER: (d)** A theory including gravity — the "theory of everything" —
remains unsolved. So does the grand unified theory joining the strong force.

---

### Q17
Conservation of linear momentum is a direct consequence of

(a) Newton's first law &nbsp; (b) Newton's second law
&nbsp; (c) Newton's third law &nbsp; (d) the law of gravitation

**ANSWER: (c)** Newton's third law.

---

### Q18
Which conservation law is **violated** in weak interactions?

(a) energy &nbsp; (b) charge &nbsp; (c) parity &nbsp; (d) angular momentum

**ANSWER: (c)** Parity. It holds for gravitational, electromagnetic and
strong interactions, but not for the weak interaction.

---

### Q19
Conservation of energy is a consequence of the fact that the laws of physics
are unchanged under

(a) translation in space &nbsp; (b) rotation in space
&nbsp; (c) translation in time &nbsp; (d) mirror reflection

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  homogeneity of SPACE  ->  linear momentum
  isotropy of SPACE     ->  angular momentum
  homogeneity of TIME   ->  ENERGY            <-- this one
  mirror symmetry       ->  parity
```

---

### Q20
C.V. Raman was awarded the Nobel Prize for

(a) the photoelectric effect &nbsp; (b) inelastic scattering of light by
molecules &nbsp; (c) the discovery of the neutron &nbsp; (d) cosmic rays

**ANSWER: (b)** The Raman effect — inelastic scattering of light by
molecules. Nobel Prize in Physics, 1930.
