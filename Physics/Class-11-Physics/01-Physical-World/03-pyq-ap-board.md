## Topic: Fundamental forces — THE MOST ASKED AREA

### Q A1
What are the four fundamental forces in nature?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  Gravitational force
  2.  Electromagnetic force
  3.  Strong nuclear force
  4.  Weak nuclear force
```

---

### Q A2
Arrange the four fundamental forces in the increasing order of their strength.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Gravitational  <  Weak nuclear  <  Electromagnetic  <  Strong nuclear

      10^-39         10^-13             10^-2               1
```

---

### Q A3
Which is the weakest force in nature? Which is the strongest?

**ANSWER:**
```
  WEAKEST    -  the GRAVITATIONAL force  (relative strength 10^-39)
  STRONGEST  -  the STRONG NUCLEAR force (relative strength 1)
```

---

### Q A4
Which of the fundamental forces have infinite range?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The GRAVITATIONAL force and the ELECTROMAGNETIC force.

  Both are carried by MASSLESS particles (the graviton and the
  photon), which is the reason their range is infinite.

  The two nuclear forces are short-range:
     strong nuclear  ~ 10^-15 m
     weak nuclear    ~ 10^-16 m
```

---

### Q A5
Name the particle that mediates each of the four fundamental forces.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Gravitational     ->  graviton (predicted by theory, not yet detected)
  Electromagnetic   ->  photon
  Strong nuclear    ->  gluon  (pi-meson / pion in the older picture)
  Weak nuclear      ->  W and Z bosons
```

---

### Q A6
Gravity is the weakest of the four forces, yet it controls the motion of
planets and galaxies. Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because gravity has TWO advantages that the other forces lack:

     1. It has INFINITE RANGE.
     2. It is ALWAYS ATTRACTIVE - it never cancels out.

  The electromagnetic force is about 10^37 times stronger, but ordinary
  matter contains equal amounts of positive and negative charge, so
  electric forces cancel almost exactly over large distances.
  Gravity never cancels, so for large masses it dominates.
```

---

### Q A7
To which fundamental force do friction, tension and the normal reaction
belong?

**ANSWER:**
```
  All of them are ELECTROMAGNETIC in origin.

  Friction, tension in a string, the normal reaction of a surface,
  elastic forces, viscous drag and chemical bonds all arise from the
  electromagnetic interaction between the atoms and electrons of the
  bodies in contact. They are NOT separate fundamental forces.
```

---

### Q A8
What is meant by saying that the strong nuclear force is charge independent?

**ANSWER:**
```
  It means the strong nuclear force acts with the SAME strength
  between any pair of nucleons, regardless of their charge:

         proton - proton   =   proton - neutron   =   neutron - neutron

  The force does not depend on electric charge at all.
```

---

### Q A9
Why must a force stronger than the electric force exist inside the nucleus?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A nucleus contains several protons, all positively charged, packed
  into a space of about 10^-14 m. At that separation the electric
  repulsion between them is enormous, and it should blow the nucleus
  apart instantly.

  Since nuclei are in fact stable, a stronger attractive force must
  be acting over that short distance. This is the STRONG NUCLEAR FORCE.
```

---

### Q A10
Which fundamental force is responsible for beta decay?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The WEAK NUCLEAR FORCE.

  In beta decay a neutron converts into a proton, emitting an
  electron and an antineutrino. Its range is about 10^-16 m,
  the shortest of all four forces.
```

## Topic: Unification

### Q A11
What is the electroweak theory? Who proposed it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The electroweak theory shows that the ELECTROMAGNETIC force and the
  WEAK NUCLEAR force are not two different forces, but two
  manifestations of a single force called the ELECTROWEAK force.

  Proposed by SHELDON GLASHOW, ABDUS SALAM and STEVEN WEINBERG,
  who shared the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1979.
  Confirmed experimentally by the discovery of the W and Z bosons.
```

---

### Q A12
Name the unifications achieved in physics, with the physicist responsible.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NEWTON    -  terrestrial gravity and celestial motion are the same law
  OERSTED
  & FARADAY -  electricity and magnetism are inseparable
  MAXWELL   -  electricity + magnetism + optics; light is an EM wave
  GLASHOW,
  SALAM,    -  electromagnetic + weak nuclear = ELECTROWEAK force
  WEINBERG

  NOT YET ACHIEVED: a grand unified theory including the strong force,
  and a "theory of everything" including gravity.
```

---

### Q A13
What did Maxwell unify?

**ANSWER:**
```
  James Clerk Maxwell unified ELECTRICITY, MAGNETISM and OPTICS
  into a single electromagnetic theory, and showed that
  LIGHT IS AN ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVE.
```

## Topic: Conservation laws

### Q A14
State any two conservation laws in physics.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. CONSERVATION OF ENERGY - the total energy of an isolated system
     remains constant; energy only changes from one form to another.

  2. CONSERVATION OF LINEAR MOMENTUM - if no external force acts on a
     system, its total linear momentum remains constant.
```

---

### Q A15
State the law of conservation of linear momentum and give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW: If the net external force acting on a system is zero, the total
  linear momentum of the system remains constant.

        m1 u1 + m2 u2   =   m1 v1 + m2 v2

  EXAMPLE: When a gun is fired, the total momentum before firing is
  zero. After firing, the forward momentum of the bullet is exactly
  equal and opposite to the backward recoil momentum of the gun,
  so the total is still zero.
```

---

### Q A16
State the law of conservation of angular momentum and give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW: If the net external torque on a system is zero, its total
  angular momentum remains constant.

        L  =  I omega  =  constant

  EXAMPLE: A spinning ice skater pulls her arms close to her body.
  Her moment of inertia I decreases, so her angular speed omega
  increases to keep the product constant, and she spins faster.
```

---

### Q A17
Why are conservation laws so useful in physics?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. They let us relate the initial and final states of a system
     WITHOUT knowing the complicated details of what happened in
     between (for example, in a collision).

  2. They are more general and more reliable than any particular
     force law. They hold in regions of physics where the detailed
     interaction is not yet known, and they have survived every
     revolution in physics so far.
```

---

### Q A18
Is mass always conserved? Justify your answer.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NO. Mass alone is conserved in ordinary chemical and mechanical
  processes, but NOT in nuclear reactions.

  In nuclear fission and fusion, part of the mass disappears and
  reappears as energy according to

        E  =  m c^2

  So the correct universal statement is the conservation of
  MASS-ENERGY, not the conservation of mass by itself.
```

---

### Q A19
What is conservation of parity? Is it always valid?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Conservation of parity means MIRROR SYMMETRY: if a process can
  occur in nature, its mirror image is also a possible process.
  Nature shows no preference for left over right.

  It is NOT always valid. Parity is conserved in gravitational,
  electromagnetic and strong interactions, but it is VIOLATED in
  WEAK INTERACTIONS - demonstrated in the beta decay of cobalt-60.
```

---

### Q A20
How is a symmetry of nature related to a conservation law? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Every symmetry of nature gives rise to a conservation law.

     Space is HOMOGENEOUS (laws are the same at every point)
                  ->  conservation of LINEAR MOMENTUM
     Space is ISOTROPIC (laws are the same in every direction)
                  ->  conservation of ANGULAR MOMENTUM
     Time is HOMOGENEOUS (laws are the same at all instants)
                  ->  conservation of ENERGY
```

## Topic: Scope of physics, method, and physicists

### Q A21
What are the macroscopic and microscopic domains of physics?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MACROSCOPIC DOMAIN - phenomena at laboratory, terrestrial and
  astronomical scales. Covered by CLASSICAL PHYSICS: mechanics,
  thermodynamics, electrodynamics and optics.

  MICROSCOPIC DOMAIN - phenomena at atomic, molecular and nuclear
  scales, and elementary particles. Covered by QUANTUM MECHANICS,
  because classical physics fails at this scale.
```

Name any four Indian physicists and their contributions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  C.V. RAMAN          -  Raman effect: inelastic scattering of light
                         by molecules. Nobel Prize 1930.
  S. CHANDRASEKHAR    -  Chandrasekhar limit; structure and evolution
                         of stars. Nobel Prize 1983.
  S.N. BOSE           -  Quantum statistics; the "boson" is named
                         after him.
  M.N. SAHA           -  Theory of thermal ionisation, which explains
                         the spectra of stars.

  (also acceptable: J.C. BOSE - ultra-short radio waves;
                    H.J. BHABHA - cascade process of cosmic radiation)
```

---

### Q A23
Give any four technologies and the scientific principle behind each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Steam engine        ->  laws of thermodynamics
  Nuclear reactor     ->  controlled nuclear fission
  Laser               ->  stimulated emission of radiation
  Photocell / digital ->  photoelectric effect
  camera sensor

  (also acceptable: computer -> semiconductor physics and digital logic;
                    electric generator -> electromagnetic induction;
                    rocket -> conservation of linear momentum;
                    optical fibre -> total internal reflection)
```

---

### Q A24
What is the role of approximation in physics? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Real systems are too complicated to describe exactly, so physics
  replaces them with simplified idealised models that keep only the
  features that matter for the problem. This makes the mathematics
  solvable while the answer stays accurate enough to be useful.

  EXAMPLE: while calculating the orbit of the Earth around the Sun,
  the Earth is treated as a POINT MASS - its size, shape, rotation
  and atmosphere are all ignored - and the orbit still comes out
  correct.
```

Compare the four fundamental forces in nature with respect to their relative
strength, range, mediating particle and the region in which each acts.

**ANSWER:**
```
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+---------------+
  |  FORCE         |  RELATIVE  |    RANGE     |  MEDIATOR   |  ACTS ON      |
  |                |  STRENGTH  |              |             |               |
  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+---------------+
  | Gravitational  |   10^-39   |   Infinite   |  graviton   | all masses    |
  | Weak nuclear   |   10^-13   |  ~10^-16 m   |  W, Z bosons| electrons,    |

  +----------------+------------+--------------+-------------+---------------+

  ADDITIONAL POINTS worth writing:
   - Order of strength: gravitational < weak < electromagnetic < strong.
   - Gravity is always attractive; the electromagnetic force can be

   - Friction, tension and chemical bonding are all electromagnetic;
     they are not separate fundamental forces.
```

### Q B2 (revision-grade)
Explain the conservation laws of energy, linear momentum, angular momentum
and charge, with one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. ENERGY
     The total energy of an isolated system is constant; energy only
     changes form.
     Example: a freely falling stone converts potential energy into
     kinetic energy, and the sum stays the same at every point.

  2. LINEAR MOMENTUM  (condition: net external force = 0)
     m1 u1 + m2 u2 = m1 v1 + m2 v2
     Example: recoil of a gun; forward momentum of the bullet equals
     backward momentum of the gun.

  3. ANGULAR MOMENTUM  (condition: net external torque = 0)
     L = I omega = constant
     Example: a spinning skater pulling her arms in spins faster,
     because I decreases so omega must increase.

  4. CHARGE
     The total charge of an isolated system is constant; charge is only
     transferred, never created or destroyed.
     Example: rubbing glass with silk gives the glass +q and the silk
     -q; the total remains zero.
```

### Q B3 (revision-grade)
Explain the relationship between physics and technology, with examples of
the influence in both directions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The relationship is TWO-WAY.

  A. PHYSICS -> TECHNOLOGY
     A discovered principle makes a new device possible.
        Faraday's electromagnetic induction  ->  electric generator
        Photoelectric effect                 ->  photocell, digital camera
        Controlled nuclear fission           ->  nuclear reactor
        Stimulated emission                  ->  laser
        Semiconductor physics                ->  transistor, computer
        Total internal reflection            ->  optical fibre

  B. TECHNOLOGY -> PHYSICS
     A practical problem forces the creation of new physics.
        The STEAM ENGINE was already in use before anyone understood
        heat. The attempt to make engines more efficient led directly
        to the LAWS OF THERMODYNAMICS. The machine came first, the
        science came second.

        Similarly, the need for better vacuum tubes and detectors
        drove work that produced modern electronics.

  C. EFFECT ON SOCIETY
     Technology born of physics reshapes ordinary life - electricity,
     railways, computers, medical imaging. It can also be destructive,
     as with nuclear weapons. The physics itself is neutral; the choice
     of how it is used is a social decision.
```

### Q B4 (revision-grade)
Describe the scientific method, and explain the role of idealisation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE SCIENTIFIC METHOD - the repeating cycle:

     1. OBSERVATION           - watch and measure carefully
     2. HYPOTHESIS            - a testable guess to explain it
     3. CONTROLLED EXPERIMENT - vary one factor at a time
     4. THEORY / MODEL        - express it mathematically as a law
     5. PREDICTION            - use the law to predict something new
     6. VERIFICATION          - test the prediction

     If the prediction fails, the theory is modified or discarded.
     A theory is never "proved forever"; it has only "not yet failed".

  IDEALISATION:
     Physics never describes the real object. It describes a
     simplified model chosen so that the mathematics is possible
     while the answer stays accurate enough.

     Examples: point mass, rigid body, frictionless surface,
     ideal gas, massless inextensible string, perfectly black body.

     The skill lies in knowing WHICH detail can be discarded.
     Ignoring air resistance is fine for a cricket ball over 20 m,
     and completely wrong for a parachute.
```

---

### Q B5 (revision-grade)
Write the nature and general characteristics of physical laws.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. UNIVERSAL - the same law applies everywhere in the universe and
     at all times. Newton's gravitation governs a falling apple and
     a distant binary star equally.

  2. MATHEMATICAL - a physical law is a quantitative statement, not a
     vague one. "Heavy things fall" is not a law; F = m a is.

  3. FEW AND SIMPLE - an enormous variety of phenomena follow from a
     very small number of laws.

  4. EXPERIMENTAL AND FALSIFIABLE - every law must be testable, and one
     reliable contradicting experiment is enough to overturn it.

  5. LIMITED DOMAIN OF VALIDITY - Newtonian mechanics works for ordinary
     speeds and sizes, and is replaced by relativity near the speed of
     light and by quantum mechanics at atomic sizes.

  6. CONSERVATION LAWS ARE THE MOST DURABLE - force laws are revised as
     physics advances, but conservation of energy, momentum, angular
     momentum and charge have survived every revision.

  7. THEY REFLECT SYMMETRIES - the conservation laws follow from the
     symmetries of space and time.
```

### Q B6 (revision-grade)
Explain the unification of forces in physics, with the historical steps.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Unification means showing that forces which appeared different are
  really different aspects of ONE force. This is the central theme of
  the history of physics.

     NEWTON (1687)      - the force that makes an apple fall and the
                          force that holds the Moon in orbit are the
                          SAME force. Terrestrial + celestial gravity.

     OERSTED (1820)     - an electric current deflects a magnetic needle.
     FARADAY (1831)     - a changing magnetic field produces a current.
                          Electricity and magnetism are inseparable.

     MAXWELL (1873)     - unified electricity, magnetism and optics.
                          Light itself is an electromagnetic wave.

     GLASHOW, SALAM,    - ELECTROWEAK THEORY. The weak nuclear force
     WEINBERG (Nobel      and the electromagnetic force are two
     1979)                manifestations of one electroweak force.
                          Confirmed by the discovery of the W and Z
                          bosons (Rubbia and van der Meer, Nobel 1984).

     STILL UNSOLVED     - a GRAND UNIFIED THEORY joining the strong
                          force to the electroweak force, and a
                          "theory of everything" that also includes
                          gravity.

  Picture:

     WEAK ------+
                +--> ELECTROWEAK --+
     EM --------+   (ACHIEVED)     +--> GUT --+
                                   |  (not    +--> "Theory of
     STRONG ------------------------+  done)  |     Everything"
                                             |     (not done)
     GRAVITY ---------------------------------+
```
