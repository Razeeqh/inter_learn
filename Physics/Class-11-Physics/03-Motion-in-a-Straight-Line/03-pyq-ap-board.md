## Topic: Distance, displacement and basic definitions

### Q A1
Distinguish between distance and displacement.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DISTANCE      -  the total length of the actual path travelled.
                   SCALAR.  Always positive.

  DISPLACEMENT  -  the shortest distance from the initial to the final
                   position, along with the direction.
                   VECTOR.  Can be positive, negative or zero.

  Always:   distance  >=  | displacement |
```

---

### Q A2
Can the displacement of a body be zero when the distance travelled is not
zero? Give an example.

**ANSWER:** **Yes.**
```
  A body that returns to its starting point has ZERO displacement,
  but the distance it covered is not zero.

  Example: a man walks 5 m east and then 5 m west.
           Distance = 10 m ,  Displacement = 0
```

---

### Q A3
Can the speed of a body be negative? Can its velocity be negative?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SPEED     -  NEVER negative. It is a scalar and is defined as
               path length divided by time, both of which are positive.

  VELOCITY  -  CAN be negative. The negative sign simply means the body
               is moving in the direction chosen as negative.
```

---

### Q A4
Define average velocity and average speed. Write their SI units.

**ANSWER:**
```
                             total displacement
   AVERAGE VELOCITY   =   -------------------------      SI unit: m/s
                             total time taken

                             total path length
   AVERAGE SPEED      =   -------------------------      SI unit: m/s
                             total time taken
```

---

### Q A5
Under what condition are the magnitudes of average velocity and average speed
equal?

**ANSWER:** When the body moves **along a straight line in one direction
only**, without reversing. Then the path length equals the magnitude of the
displacement, so the two averages are equal.

---

### Q A6
What is a frame of reference? Why is it needed?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A FRAME OF REFERENCE is a coordinate system (x, y, z) attached to a
  body, together with a clock to measure time.

  It is needed because rest and motion are RELATIVE. Without stating
  the frame, the statement "the body is moving" has no meaning.
```

---

### Q A7
When can a body be treated as a point object? Give one example.

**ANSWER:** When the **size of the body is very small compared with the
distance it travels**.
Example: the Earth revolving around the Sun, or a car travelling from
Vijayawada to Visakhapatnam.

## Topic: Acceleration and its sign

### Q A8
Can a body have zero velocity and still be accelerating? Give an example.

**ANSWER:** **Yes.**
```
  A ball thrown vertically upward has v = 0 at its HIGHEST POINT,
  but its acceleration there is still  g = 9.8 m/s^2  downwards.

  If the acceleration were also zero at that point, the ball would
  remain hanging in the air.
```

---

### Q A9
Can a body have constant speed but variable velocity? Can it have constant
velocity but variable speed?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Constant SPEED with variable VELOCITY   ->  YES.
       Example: a body moving in a circle at a steady rate.
       The direction changes, so the velocity changes.

  Constant VELOCITY with variable SPEED   ->  NO.
       Constant velocity means both magnitude AND direction are fixed,
       so the speed must also be fixed.
```

---

### Q A10
What does the slope of a position-time graph represent? What does the slope of
a velocity-time graph represent?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Slope of position-time (x-t) graph   =   VELOCITY

  Slope of velocity-time (v-t) graph   =   ACCELERATION
```

---

### Q A11
What does the area under a velocity-time graph represent?

**ANSWER:** The **displacement** of the body during that time interval.
(The area under an acceleration-time graph gives the **change in velocity**.)

---

### Q A12
Give an example of a body having zero acceleration but non-zero velocity.

**ANSWER:** A car moving along a straight road at a **constant 60 km/h**.
Its velocity is not zero, but since the velocity does not change, its
acceleration is zero.

### Q B1
Derive the equations of motion `v = u + at` and `s = ut + (1/2)at^2` using a
velocity-time graph.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A body has initial velocity u and moves with uniform acceleration a
  for a time t, reaching a final velocity v.

   v |
     |                                C
   v |. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . ./|
     |                            ./  |
     |                         ./     |
     |                      ./        |
   u |B . . . . . . . . ./            |
     |                  |             |
     |                  |             |
   0 +A-----------------+-------------D--- t
     0                                t

     OA = u ,  CD = v ,  OD = t ,  CB = v - u


  PART 1 :  v  =  u  +  a t

     STEP 1   The slope of the line BC is the acceleration.

     STEP 2            CB        v - u
                a =  ------  =  -------
                       BD          t

     STEP 3   a t  =  v - u

     RESULT   v  =  u  +  a t


  PART 2 :  s  =  u t  +  (1/2) a t^2

     STEP 1   Displacement s = AREA under the graph
                             = area of rectangle ABDO
                               + area of triangle BCD

     STEP 2   Area of rectangle  =  OA x OD  =  u t

     STEP 3   Area of triangle   =  (1/2) x BD x CB
                                 =  (1/2) x t x (v - u)
                                 =  (1/2) x t x (a t)
                                 =  (1/2) a t^2

     RESULT   s  =  u t  +  (1/2) a t^2
```

### Q B3
Derive the expression for the distance travelled by a body in the nth second
of its motion.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1   Distance covered in n seconds:

              s(n)     =   u n      +   (1/2) a n^2

  STEP 2   Distance covered in (n - 1) seconds:

              s(n-1)   =   u(n - 1) +   (1/2) a (n - 1)^2

  STEP 3   Distance in the nth second is the difference:

              s(nth)  =  s(n)  -  s(n-1)

                      =  u [ n - (n-1) ]  +  (1/2) a [ n^2 - (n-1)^2 ]

  STEP 4   Simplify the bracket:

              n^2 - (n - 1)^2  =  n^2 - ( n^2 - 2n + 1 )  =  2n - 1

  RESULT
                                   a
              s(nth)   =   u   +  ----- ( 2 n  -  1 )
                                   2
```

### Q B4
Show that for a body thrown vertically upward, the time of ascent equals the
time of descent. Also find the maximum height and the total time of flight.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Take the UPWARD direction as positive, so a = -g.
  Initial velocity = u.

  STEP 1   MAXIMUM HEIGHT
           At the top,  v = 0. Use  v^2 - u^2 = 2 a s :

               0  -  u^2   =   2 ( -g ) H

                              u^2
                     H   =   -------
                              2 g

  STEP 2   TIME OF ASCENT
           Use  v = u + at   with  v = 0 , a = -g :

               0  =  u  -  g t(up)          ->     t(up)  =  u / g

  STEP 3   TIME OF DESCENT

           starting from rest:

               H  =  (1/2) g t(down)^2

                                  +--------+           +-------------+

                                  +--------+           +-------------+

                        =   u / g

  STEP 4   Therefore      t(up)  =  t(down)  =  u / g      ... PROVED

  STEP 5   TOTAL TIME OF FLIGHT

                              2 u
               T  =  2 t(up) = -----
                                g
```

### Q B10
A stone is dropped from the top of a tower 100 m high. At the same instant
another stone is thrown vertically upward from the ground with a velocity of
25 m/s. When and where do they meet? (g = 10 m/s^2)

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1   Let them meet after time t, at a height x above the ground.

  STEP 2   STONE 1 (dropped from the top):
              distance fallen  =  (1/2)(10) t^2  =  5 t^2

  STEP 3   STONE 2 (thrown up from the ground):
              height risen     =  25 t  -  5 t^2

  STEP 4   The two distances together make up the full 100 m:

              5 t^2  +  ( 25 t  -  5 t^2 )   =   100

                                    25 t     =   100

                                        t    =   4 s

  STEP 5   Height above the ground where they meet:

              x  =  25(4)  -  5(16)  =  100 - 80  =  20 m

  ANSWER   They meet after 4 s, at a height of 20 m above the ground.

  SHORTCUT:  The relative acceleration of the two stones is ZERO
             (both accelerate at g downwards), so
                  time  =  separation / relative velocity  =  100 / 25  =  4 s
```

### Q B12
Explain the terms reaction time and stopping distance. A car moving at 72 km/h
is stopped with a retardation of 5 m/s^2. If the driver's reaction time is
0.2 s, find the total stopping distance.

**ANSWER:**
```
  REACTION TIME
      the time interval between the moment the driver SEES the danger
      and the moment he actually PRESSES the brake. During this time
      the vehicle keeps moving at its original speed.

  STOPPING DISTANCE
      the total distance travelled from the moment the danger is seen
      until the vehicle comes to rest.
      = reaction distance  +  braking distance


  NUMERICAL

  STEP 1   72 km/h  =  72 x 5/18  =  20 m/s

  STEP 2   Reaction distance  =  v x t  =  20 x 0.2  =  4 m

  STEP 3   Braking distance:   v^2 - u^2 = 2 a s
              0 - 400  =  2(-5) s
              s  =  40 m

  STEP 4   Total stopping distance  =  4 + 40  =  44 m

  ANSWER   44 m
```

### Q C1
(a) Derive the three equations of motion for a uniformly accelerated body
using the velocity-time graph.
(b) A body starting from rest travels with uniform acceleration and covers
100 m in the first 5 s. Find its acceleration and the distance covered in the
next 5 s.

**ANSWER (a):**
```
  SET UP THE GRAPH

     v |
       |                                C
     v |. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . ./|
       |                            ./  |
       |                         ./     |
       |                      ./        |
     u |B . . . . . . . . ./            |
       |                  |             |
       |                  |             |
     0 +A-----------------+-------------D--- t
       0                                t

     OA = u  = initial velocity
     CD = v  = final velocity
     OD = t  = time interval
     CB = CD - BD = v - u


  EQUATION 1 :  v  =  u  +  a t
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
     The acceleration is the slope of the straight line BC.

                     CB          v - u
         a   =     ------   =   --------
                     BD             t

         a t  =  v - u

         v    =  u  +  a t                                    ... (1)


  EQUATION 2 :  s  =  u t  +  (1/2) a t^2
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
     The displacement is the AREA under the v-t graph, i.e. the
     area of the figure OABCD.

         s   =   area of rectangle ABDO   +   area of triangle BCD

         area of rectangle  =  OA x OD    =  u t

         area of triangle   =  (1/2) x BD x CB
                            =  (1/2) x t  x ( v - u )
                            =  (1/2) x t  x ( a t )     [ using (1) ]
                            =  (1/2) a t^2

         s   =   u t  +  (1/2) a t^2                          ... (2)


  EQUATION 3 :  v^2 - u^2  =  2 a s
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
     The same area, treated as a TRAPEZIUM with parallel sides
     OA and CD and height OD:

                      1
         s    =     ----- ( OA + CD ) x OD
                      2

                      1
              =     ----- ( u + v ) t
                      2

     From (1),        t  =  ( v - u ) / a . Substitute:

                      1                 ( v - u )
         s    =     ----- ( u + v ) x -------------
                      2                     a

                    ( v + u )( v - u )        v^2  -  u^2
              =    --------------------  =  ---------------
                            2 a                   2 a

         2 a s  =  v^2  -  u^2

         v^2  -  u^2   =   2 a s                              ... (3)


  NOTE:  All three are valid ONLY when the acceleration is constant.
```

**ANSWER (b):**
```
  STEP 1   First 5 seconds:  u = 0 , t = 5 s , s = 100 m

              s  =  u t  +  (1/2) a t^2

            100  =  0  +  (1/2) a (25)

            100  =  12.5 a

              a  =  8 m/s^2

  STEP 2   Distance in the FIRST 10 seconds:

              s(10)  =  0  +  (1/2)(8)(100)   =   400 m

  STEP 3   Distance in the NEXT 5 seconds (from t = 5 to t = 10):

              =  s(10)  -  s(5)   =   400  -  100   =   300 m

  ANSWER   a = 8 m/s^2 ,  distance in the next 5 s = 300 m

  CHECK:   For a body starting from rest, distances in successive
           equal intervals are in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5 ...
           Here 100 : 300  =  1 : 3.   Correct.
```

### Q C2
(a) Derive the equations of motion by the calculus method.
(b) Show that the distance travelled in the nth second is
`s(nth) = u + (a/2)(2n - 1)`, and use it to find the distance travelled by a
freely falling body in the 3rd second. (g = 9.8 m/s^2)

**ANSWER (a):**
```
  EQUATION 1
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1   By definition of acceleration,      a  =  dv / dt

  STEP 2   Rearrange:                          dv  =  a dt

  STEP 3   Integrate. When t = 0 the velocity is u; when the time
           is t the velocity is v:

               integral(u to v) dv   =   a  x  integral(0 to t) dt

               v  -  u   =   a ( t - 0 )

  RESULT       v   =   u  +  a t


  EQUATION 2
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1   By definition of velocity,          v  =  dx / dt

  STEP 2   So                                  dx  =  v dt  =  (u + a t) dt

  STEP 3   Integrate. When t = 0 the position is x0; at time t it is x:

               x  -  x0   =   u t   +   (1/2) a t^2

  STEP 4   And  x - x0  is the displacement s.

  RESULT       s   =   u t  +  (1/2) a t^2


  EQUATION 3
  -----------------------------------------------------------------
  STEP 1   Write acceleration in terms of position using the chain rule:

                     dv        dv     dx            dv
               a  = ----   =  ---- x ----    =  v  ----
                     dt        dx     dt            dx

  STEP 2   Rearrange:              v dv   =   a dx

  STEP 3   Integrate: velocity from u to v, position from 0 to s:

                  v^2      u^2
                 -----  - -----   =   a s
                   2        2

  STEP 4   Multiply throughout by 2:

  RESULT       v^2  -  u^2   =   2 a s
```

**ANSWER (b):**
```
  DERIVATION

  STEP 1   Distance in n seconds:       s(n)   = u n + (1/2) a n^2
  STEP 2   Distance in (n-1) seconds:   s(n-1) = u(n-1) + (1/2) a (n-1)^2

  STEP 3   s(nth) = s(n) - s(n-1)

                 = u [ n - (n-1) ] + (1/2) a [ n^2 - (n-1)^2 ]

  STEP 4   n^2 - (n-1)^2  =  n^2 - n^2 + 2n - 1  =  2n - 1

  RESULT                          a
           s(nth)  =   u   +   ------- ( 2 n - 1 )
                                  2


  NUMERICAL — freely falling body, 3rd second

  STEP 1   u = 0 ,  a = g = 9.8 m/s^2 ,  n = 3

  STEP 2                 9.8
           h(3rd) = 0 + ------ ( 2 x 3  -  1 )
                          2

                  = 4.9 x 5

                  = 24.5 m

  ANSWER   24.5 m
```

### Q C3

(b) A ball is thrown vertically upward from the top of a tower 40 m high with

(ii) the time taken to reach the ground (iii) the velocity with which it

**ANSWER (a):**
```
  THE SLOPE OF AN x-t GRAPH IS THE VELOCITY

     x |                  Q
       |                 /|
       |               /  |
    x2 |. . . . . . ./ . .|
       |           /|     |
    x1 |. . . . ./  |     |
       |   P   /    |     |
       |     /      |     |
     0 +----+-------+-----+-------- t
            t1            t2

              change in position       x2 - x1
     slope = ---------------------  =  ---------  =  velocity
                change in time          t2 - t1

     If the graph is a curve, the slope of the TANGENT at a point
     gives the INSTANTANEOUS velocity at that instant.


  THE AREA UNDER A v-t GRAPH IS THE DISPLACEMENT

     v |
       |          _______________
    v0 |         |               |
       |         |     AREA      |
       |         |  = v0 x time  |
       |         |               |
     0 +---------+---------------+------- t
                t1              t2

     Reason: in a small interval dt the body covers  v dt.
     Adding all such strips from t1 to t2 gives the total
     displacement, which is exactly the area under the curve.

     Area ABOVE the time axis  ->  positive displacement
     Area BELOW the time axis  ->  negative displacement
```

### Q C4
(a) Define relative velocity. Derive the expression for the relative velocity
of one body with respect to another in one dimension, and discuss the cases of
motion in the same and in opposite directions.
(b) Two trains 100 m and 150 m long move in the same direction with speeds
72 km/h and 54 km/h. Find the time taken by the faster train to overtake the
slower one.

**ANSWER (a):**
```
  DEFINITION

     The RELATIVE VELOCITY of a body A with respect to a body B is the
     velocity with which A appears to move when observed from B.


  DERIVATION

  STEP 1   Let A and B move along the same straight line. Measured
           from the ground, let their positions at time t be

                x(A)  =  x(A0)  +  v(A) t
                x(B)  =  x(B0)  +  v(B) t

  STEP 2   The position of A as seen from B is the difference:

                x(A) - x(B)  =  [ x(A0) - x(B0) ]  +  [ v(A) - v(B) ] t

  STEP 3   Comparing with the standard form
           (position = initial position + velocity x time),
           the velocity of A relative to B is the coefficient of t:

                +------------------------------------+
                |     v(AB)   =   v(A)   -   v(B)    |
                +------------------------------------+

           and similarly    v(BA)  =  v(B) - v(A)  =  - v(AB)


  CASE 1 — SAME DIRECTION

      A ---> vA                B ---> vB
      o------------------------o--------------

      v(AB)  =  vA  -  vB          ->   relative speed = DIFFERENCE

      If vA = vB , the relative velocity is ZERO and each appears
      to be at rest relative to the other.


  CASE 2 — OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS

      A ---> vA                      vB <--- B
      o------------------------      --------o

      Taking the right as positive, v(B) = -vB , so

      v(AB)  =  vA  -  ( -vB )  =  vA  +  vB

                                   ->   relative speed = SUM
```

**ANSWER (b):**
```
  STEP 1   Convert:
              72 km/h  =  72 x 5/18  =  20 m/s
              54 km/h  =  54 x 5/18  =  15 m/s

  STEP 2   Same direction  ->  relative velocity

              v(rel)  =  20  -  15  =  5 m/s

  STEP 3   To completely overtake, the faster train must cover the
           sum of the two lengths:

              distance  =  100  +  150  =  250 m

  STEP 4                250
              t   =   -------   =   50 s
                         5

  ANSWER   50 seconds
```
