## Topic: Pressure

### Q A1
Define thrust and pressure. Give their SI units.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THRUST   -  the total NORMAL force exerted by a fluid on a surface.
              SI unit :  newton (N).   It is a VECTOR.

  PRESSURE -  the thrust acting per unit area.

                     F
              P  =  ---        SI unit :  N/m^2  =  pascal (Pa)
                     A
              It is a SCALAR.
```

---

### Q A2
Is pressure a scalar or a vector? Justify.

**ANSWER:** Pressure is a **SCALAR**.
```
  Although it is defined as force / area, the force exerted by a fluid at
  rest at a point is the SAME in every direction. Pressure therefore has
  no unique direction associated with it, so it is a scalar.
```

---

### Q A3
What is the difference between gauge pressure and absolute pressure?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ABSOLUTE PRESSURE  =  the total, real pressure at a point
                     =  P0  +  h rho g

  GAUGE PRESSURE     =  the EXCESS over atmospheric pressure
                     =  P  -  P0  =  h rho g

  A pressure gauge on a tyre or a cylinder reads the GAUGE pressure.
  Absolute = Atmospheric + Gauge.
```

---

### Q A4
State the hydrostatic paradox.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The pressure at the bottom of a vessel depends ONLY on the vertical
  height of the liquid column, NOT on the shape of the vessel or on
  the amount of liquid it holds.

  So three vessels of very different shapes, filled to the same height,
  all show the SAME pressure at the base — which seems paradoxical but
  follows directly from  P = P0 + h rho g.
```

---

### Q A5
Why does the barometer use mercury and not water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  For water :  h  =  P0 / (rho g)  =  1.013 x 10^5 / (1000 x 9.8)
                  =  10.34 m

  A water barometer would need a glass tube over 10 metres tall,
  which is impractical.

  Mercury is 13.6 times denser, so it needs only 0.76 m.
  Mercury also has a very low vapour pressure and does not wet glass.
```

## Topic: Pascal's law

### Q A6
State Pascal's law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  When a change of pressure is applied to an ENCLOSED fluid at rest,
  it is transmitted UNDIMINISHED to every point of the fluid and to
  the walls of the containing vessel.

  Equivalent form: the pressure at a point in a fluid at rest is the
  same in ALL directions.
```

---

### Q A7
Name two applications of Pascal's law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  HYDRAULIC LIFT  (used to lift cars in a service station)
  2.  HYDRAULIC BRAKES in motor vehicles

  Also acceptable: hydraulic press, hydraulic jack, dentist's chair.
```

---

### Q A8
In a hydraulic lift, does one get more work out than one puts in? Explain.

**ANSWER:** **No.**
```
  The force is multiplied by  a2/a1 , but the distance moved is
  divided by the same factor, because  a1 d1 = a2 d2.

        F1 d1  =  F2 d2      ->      Work in  =  Work out

  Energy is conserved. Only the FORCE is magnified, never the energy.
```

## Topic: Archimedes' principle

### Q A9
State Archimedes' principle.

**ANSWER:**
```
  When a body is wholly or partly immersed in a fluid at rest, it
  experiences an upward force (buoyant force) equal to the WEIGHT
  OF THE FLUID DISPLACED by it.

        Fb  =  V_displaced  x  rho_fluid  x  g
```

---

### Q A10
State the law of floatation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A body floats in a liquid when the weight of the liquid displaced
  by the immersed part of the body is EQUAL to the weight of the body.

  Condition:  rho_body  <  rho_liquid
```

### Q A11
Why does a piece of iron sink in water but an iron ship float?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A solid iron piece has density 7800 kg/m^3 > 1000, so it sinks.

  A ship is HOLLOW. Its AVERAGE density (iron + the large volume of
  air inside) is LESS than that of water, so it floats. The volume
  of water it displaces weighs more than the ship.
```

## Topic: Flow and continuity

### Q A12
What is streamline flow? What is a streamline?

**ANSWER:**
```
  STREAMLINE FLOW - flow in which every particle passing a given point
  follows exactly the same path with the same velocity as the particle
  that went before it.

  STREAMLINE - the path followed by a fluid particle. The tangent at
  any point on it gives the direction of the velocity there.
```

---

### Q A13
Why can two streamlines never cross each other?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The tangent to a streamline gives the direction of the fluid velocity.
  If two streamlines crossed, the particle at the crossing point would
  have TWO different velocity directions at the same instant, which is
  impossible for steady flow.
```

---

### Q A14
State the equation of continuity. Which conservation law does it express?

**ANSWER:**
```
        a1 v1   =   a2 v2   =   constant

  It expresses the CONSERVATION OF MASS for a flowing incompressible
  fluid: whatever mass enters per second must leave per second.
```

---

### Q A15
Why does the stream of water from a tap get narrower as it falls?

**ANSWER:**
```
  As the water falls, gravity makes it SPEED UP.

  By the equation of continuity  a v = constant , if v increases the
  cross-section area a must DECREASE. So the stream gets thinner.
```

## Topic: Bernoulli

### Q A16
State Bernoulli's theorem.

**ANSWER:**
```
  For the streamline flow of an ideal fluid, the sum of the pressure
  energy, the kinetic energy and the potential energy PER UNIT VOLUME
  remains constant at every point.

               1
      P   +    - rho v^2   +   rho g h   =   constant
               2

  It is a statement of the CONSERVATION OF ENERGY for a flowing fluid.
```

---

### Q A17
State the assumptions made in deriving Bernoulli's equation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  The fluid is non-viscous (ideal).
  2.  The fluid is incompressible (rho is constant).
  3.  The flow is streamline and steady.
  4.  The flow is irrotational (no eddies).
```

---

### Q A18
State Torricelli's law. Write the expression for the speed of efflux.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The speed with which a liquid escapes from a small hole in a tank is
  equal to the speed a body would acquire falling freely through a
  height equal to the depth of the hole below the free surface.

        v   =   sqrt( 2 g h )
```

---

### Q A19
Why does a fast-moving train pull a person standing near the platform
edge towards it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The train drags the air between itself and the person, so that air
  moves FAST. By Bernoulli's principle, fast-moving air has LOW pressure.

  The still air behind the person is at the normal, HIGHER atmospheric
  pressure, and it PUSHES the person towards the train.
```

---

### Q A20
Why is the roof of a tin shed blown off (upward) during a cyclone?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Fast wind blowing over the roof reduces the pressure ABOVE it
  (Bernoulli). The air inside the shed is still, so it is at the higher
  atmospheric pressure and pushes UP on the roof.

  The net upward force lifts the roof off. That is why the roof is blown
  UPWARD and not pressed down.
```

## Topic: Viscosity

### Q A21
Define coefficient of viscosity. Give its SI unit and dimensions.

**ANSWER:**
```
                    F
      eta   =   -------------
                 A ( dv/dx )

  eta is the viscous force per unit area of contact between two layers
  having unit velocity gradient between them.

  SI unit      :  N s / m^2  =  Pa.s   ( = poiseuille )
  CGS unit     :  poise  ;  1 Pa.s = 10 poise
  Dimensions   :  [ M L^-1 T^-1 ]
```

---

### Q A22
How does the viscosity of a liquid and of a gas change with temperature?

**ANSWER:**
```
  LIQUIDS :  viscosity DECREASES as temperature increases,
             because the cohesive forces between molecules weaken.

  GASES   :  viscosity INCREASES as temperature increases,
             because faster molecules transfer more momentum between
             layers.  ( eta is proportional to sqrt(T) )

  They behave in OPPOSITE ways.
```

---

### Q A23
State Stokes' law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The viscous drag on a small sphere moving through a viscous fluid
  in streamline flow is

        F   =   6  pi  eta  r  v

  where eta = coefficient of viscosity, r = radius of the sphere,
  v = its velocity.
```

**ANSWER:**
```
  TERMINAL VELOCITY is the CONSTANT maximum velocity acquired by a body
  falling through a viscous fluid, reached when the viscous drag plus
  the upthrust exactly balance the weight, so the net force is zero.

              2  r^2 ( rho  -  sigma )  g
      v_t  =  ----------------------------
                       9  eta
```

## Topic: Surface tension

### Q A26
Define surface tension. Give its SI unit and dimensions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Surface tension is the force acting per unit length on an imaginary
  line drawn tangentially on the free surface of a liquid, acting
  perpendicular to that line and tangential to the surface.

        T  =  F / l

  SI unit    :  N / m
  Dimensions :  [ M T^-2 ]
```

---

### Q A27
Define surface energy. How is it related to surface tension?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SURFACE ENERGY is the extra potential energy per unit area of the
  free surface of a liquid.

  Surface energy per unit area  =  SURFACE TENSION (numerically equal)

        W  =  T x ( increase in area )

  The units J/m^2 and N/m are identical, since  N/m = N.m/m^2 = J/m^2.
```

---

### Q A28
Why are small liquid drops spherical in shape?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Surface tension makes the free surface of a liquid contract to the
  SMALLEST POSSIBLE AREA.

  For a given volume, the SPHERE has the least surface area.

  Hence a small drop, in which gravity is negligible compared with
  surface tension, takes a spherical shape.
```

---

### Q A29
Write the excess pressure inside a liquid drop, a soap bubble and an
air bubble in a liquid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Liquid drop            (1 surface)   ->   P  =  2 T / r
  Air bubble in a liquid (1 surface)   ->   P  =  2 T / r
  Soap bubble in air     (2 surfaces)  ->   P  =  4 T / r
```

---

### Q A30
How does temperature affect surface tension?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Surface tension DECREASES as temperature increases, because heating
  increases the average separation of the molecules and weakens the
  cohesive forces.

  At the CRITICAL TEMPERATURE the surface tension becomes ZERO.
```

---

### Q A31
Why does hot soapy water clean clothes better than cold plain water?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Both heating and adding soap REDUCE the surface tension of water.

  Low surface tension lets the water spread out, WET the fabric and
  penetrate into the fine spaces between the fibres, so it can carry
  the dirt away.
```

```
  CAPILLARITY is the rise or fall of a liquid inside a fine (capillary)

  Examples:
```

Why does mercury FALL in a capillary tube while water rises?

### Q B1
Derive an expression for the pressure at a depth h below the free surface
of a liquid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Consider an imaginary cylinder of liquid of area A and height h,
  with its top at the free surface and its bottom at depth h.

   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~   free surface, pressure P0
              +---------+
              |         |   |
        h     | LIQUID  |   | weight  m g
              |CYLINDER |   v
              +---------+
                   ^
                   |   P A  (up)

  Forces on the cylinder, in equilibrium:

     upward         =   downward
      P A           =   P0 A   +   m g

  Mass of the cylinder,  m  =  (A h) rho

      P A   =   P0 A   +   A h rho g

  Dividing by A :

  +---------------------------------------+
  |        P   =   P0   +   h rho g       |
  +---------------------------------------+

  Gauge pressure  =  P - P0  =  h rho g.
  The pressure depends only on the DEPTH, not on the shape of the vessel.
```

### Q B2
Explain the principle and working of a hydraulic lift with a diagram.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE : Pascal's law.

        F1
        |
        V
     +-----+                             +---------------+
     | a1  |                             |      a2       |
     +--|--+                             +-------|-------+
        |                                        ^  F2
     ===|========================================|=====
     #  |         ENCLOSED  LIQUID (oil)         |    #
     #  +----------------------------------------+    #
     ###################################################

  WORKING
  1.  A small force F1 on the small piston of area a1 produces a
      pressure  P = F1 / a1.
  2.  By Pascal's law this pressure is transmitted undiminished
      throughout the liquid.
  3.  It acts on the large piston of area a2, giving

              F2  =  P a2  =  F1 ( a2 / a1 )

  4.  Since a2 >> a1 , F2 is very much larger than F1, so a car can
      be lifted with a small effort.

  Mechanical advantage  =  F2 / F1  =  a2 / a1

  NOTE: a1 d1 = a2 d2 , so F1 d1 = F2 d2 — energy is conserved.
```

### Q B3
Explain the working of hydraulic brakes.

**ANSWER:**
```
     PEDAL
       |
       V
    +------+  MASTER CYLINDER (small area a1)
    |  a1  |
    +---|--+
    ====|=================================
    #   |          BRAKE  FLUID           #
    ####|##################|###############
        |                  |
     +--v--+            +--v--+
     | a2  |            | a2  |   WHEEL CYLINDERS (large area a2)
     +--|--+            +--|--+
        V                  V
     BRAKE SHOE         BRAKE SHOE  ->  pressed against the drum/disc

  1.  A light push on the pedal creates a pressure  P = F1 / a1
      in the master cylinder.
  2.  Pascal's law transmits this pressure equally through the brake
      fluid to every wheel cylinder.
  3.  At each wheel the force becomes  F2 = P a2 , which is large
      because a2 >> a1. The shoes press on the drum and the wheel stops.
  4.  Because the pressure is the SAME at all four wheels, all four
      are braked EQUALLY, so the vehicle stops without skidding
      or swerving. This uniform braking is the main advantage.
```

### Q B4
State and explain Archimedes' principle. Derive the expression for the
fraction of a floating body that is submerged.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ARCHIMEDES' PRINCIPLE
  A body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid at rest experiences an
  upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

        Fb  =  V_displaced  rho_fluid  g

  WHY : the pressure on the lower face is greater than on the upper
  face (because it is deeper), so there is a net upward force.

  FRACTION SUBMERGED
  For a floating body, weight = buoyant force:

        V  rho_body  g   =   V_sub  rho_fluid  g

  +----------------------------------------+
  |     V_sub          rho_body            |
  |    -------   =   ------------          |
  |       V           rho_fluid            |
  +----------------------------------------+

  Example: wood of density 600 in water (1000)
           ->  V_sub / V  =  0.6  ->  60 % submerged, 40 % above.
```

### Q B5
Derive the equation of continuity and explain its physical meaning.

**ANSWER:**
```
        a1                              a2
    +---------+                    +---------+
    |   v1    |-------------------->|   v2   |
    +---------+                    +---------+
     <-v1 dt->                       <-v2 dt->

  In a small time dt:

     mass entering at section 1  =  rho a1 v1 dt
     mass leaving  at section 2  =  rho a2 v2 dt

  The fluid is incompressible and no fluid leaves through the walls,
  so by CONSERVATION OF MASS these are equal:

        rho a1 v1 dt  =  rho a2 v2 dt

  +-------------------------------------------+
  |       a1 v1   =   a2 v2   =   constant    |
  +-------------------------------------------+

  PHYSICAL MEANING
   -  a v is the volume flow rate, and it is the same everywhere.
   -  v is INVERSELY proportional to a : a narrow section forces the
      fluid to move faster, a wide section slows it down.
   -  Where the streamlines are crowded together, the speed is large.
   -  It is the law of CONSERVATION OF MASS for a flowing fluid.
```

### Q B7
State Torricelli's law and derive the expression for the speed of efflux.

**ANSWER:**
```
       ______________________
      |     ~~~~~~~~~~~~~    |   <-- 1 : free surface, area A, v1 ~ 0
      |          |           |
      |          | h         |
      |          v           |
      |                ()====|====>  v      <-- 2 : hole, area a << A
      |______________________|

  Apply Bernoulli between the free surface (1) and the hole (2).
  Both are open to the atmosphere, so  P1 = P2 = P0.
  Since a << A , continuity gives  v1 ~ 0.
  Take the hole as the reference level : h1 = h , h2 = 0.

             1                                1
      P0  +  - rho (0)^2  +  rho g h   =  P0 + - rho v^2  +  0
             2                                2

                       rho g h   =   (1/2) rho v^2

  +-------------------------------+
  |      v   =   sqrt( 2 g h )    |
  +-------------------------------+

  STATEMENT : the speed of efflux is the same as the speed a body
  would gain falling freely through the height h.

  Horizontal range of the jet :   x  =  2 sqrt( h ( H - h ) ) ,
  which is a maximum when h = H/2.
```

### Q B8
Explain the dynamic lift on an aeroplane wing and the Magnus effect.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AEROPLANE WING (aerofoil)

     ------->  ------>  ------>     FAST air  ->  LOW pressure
       __________________
     /                    \____
    /___________________________\
     ---->      ---->            SLOW air  ->  HIGH pressure
                  ^
                  |  LIFT

  1.  The wing is curved on top, flat below, and slightly tilted.
  2.  Air over the top is crowded and so moves FASTER.
  3.  By Bernoulli, faster air has LOWER pressure.
  4.  The higher pressure below gives a net UPWARD force = LIFT.

              1
      Lift =  - rho ( v_top^2  -  v_bottom^2 )  x  A
              2

  MAGNUS EFFECT (spinning ball)

  1.  A spinning ball drags the air layer next to its surface around
      with it.
  2.  On one side the dragged air ADDS to the oncoming air -> faster
      -> LOWER pressure.
  3.  On the other side they OPPOSE -> slower -> HIGHER pressure.
  4.  The pressure difference gives a sideways (or upward) force that
      makes the ball SWERVE. This is the Magnus effect, and the force
      is called dynamic lift.

  Seen in: a swinging cricket ball, a curling football free kick,
  a tennis topspin that dips.
```

### Q B9
Explain the working of an atomiser (sprayer) and a Bunsen burner using
Bernoulli's principle.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ATOMISER

                    squeeze
                       |
     +=================V=========+
     ||  fast air ---->          ||====>  fine spray
     +========|==================+
              |  low pressure here
            | |
        ____|_|____
       |  LIQUID   |   <-- atmospheric pressure pushes the liquid up
       |___________|

  1.  Squeezing the bulb sends a fast jet of air across the top of
      the vertical tube.
  2.  Fast air means LOW pressure there (Bernoulli).
  3.  The atmospheric pressure on the liquid surface in the bottle is
      now greater, so it PUSHES the liquid up the tube.
  4.  At the top the liquid meets the fast air stream and is broken
      up into a fine spray.

  BUNSEN BURNER
  Gas rushes out of a fine jet at the bottom of the burner tube.
  The fast gas lowers the pressure near the air holes, so atmospheric
  pressure DRIVES AIR IN through the holes. The air mixes with the gas
  and gives complete combustion — the hot, blue, non-luminous flame.

  Same principle: filter pump, paint sprayer, car carburettor.
```

### Q B11
State Stokes' law and derive the expression for the terminal velocity of
a sphere falling through a viscous fluid. Draw the velocity-time graph.

### Q B12
Explain the molecular theory of surface tension using the idea of the
sphere of influence.

**ANSWER:**
```
                       AIR
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
            .- - .            .- - .
          /       \         /       \
         |    A    |       |    B    |
          \  \|/  /         \  \|/  /
            `-|- '            `-|- '
              V                  V
        net force DOWN      net force DOWN (smaller)

              .- - .
            /       \
         <-|<-  C  ->|->      net force ZERO
            \       /
              `- -'

  SPHERE OF INFLUENCE : a sphere of radius about 10^-9 m drawn round a
  molecule, inside which its attraction on other molecules matters.

  1.  For a molecule C deep inside the liquid, the sphere of influence
      is entirely filled with liquid. It is pulled equally in all
      directions, so the NET FORCE IS ZERO.

  2.  For a molecule A at the surface, the upper half of its sphere of
      influence is occupied by air (which attracts negligibly) and the
      lower half by liquid. So there is a NET INWARD (downward) PULL.

  3.  Every surface molecule is therefore dragged inward. To bring a
      molecule to the surface, work must be done against this pull,
      so surface molecules have EXTRA POTENTIAL ENERGY.

  4.  A system always tries to minimise its potential energy, so the
      surface contracts to the SMALLEST POSSIBLE AREA and behaves like
      a stretched elastic membrane.

  THAT contracting tendency of the free surface is SURFACE TENSION.
```

```
      W2  =  - P2 a2 ( v2 dt )      (negative — opposes the motion)

      a1 v1 dt   =   a2 v2 dt   =   V
```

```
                                1
```

```
                                2
                          +  rho V g ( h2 - h1 )

  |          1                                1                    |
```

### Q C2  *** revision-grade combined answer ***
Define surface tension and surface energy and show that they are equal.
Explain surface tension on the basis of molecular theory. Derive the
expression for the excess pressure inside a liquid drop and a soap bubble.

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  Definition of surface tension, units, dimensions   1 mark
  Molecular theory + sphere of influence diagram     2 marks
  Surface energy = surface tension (derivation)      2 marks
  Excess pressure in a drop (derivation)             1.5 marks
```

**(1) DEFINITION**
```
  Surface tension is the force per unit length acting on an imaginary
  line drawn tangentially on the free surface of a liquid, at right
  angles to that line.

        T  =  F / l     ;   unit N/m ;   dimensions [ M T^-2 ]
```

**(2) MOLECULAR THEORY**
```
                       AIR
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~
            .- - .            .- - .
          /       \         /       \
         |    A    |       |    B    |     A on the surface
          \  \|/  /         \  \|/  /      B just below it
            `-|- '            `-|- '
              V                  V

              .- - .
            /       \
         <-|<-  C  ->|->     C deep inside : NET FORCE ZERO
            \       /
              `- -'

  - The SPHERE OF INFLUENCE of a molecule is the small sphere
    (radius ~ 10^-9 m) within which its attraction is appreciable.
  - Molecule C, deep inside, has its sphere entirely in liquid and is
    pulled equally in all directions -> no net force.
  - Molecule A, on the surface, has air above (negligible attraction)
    and liquid below -> a NET INWARD PULL.
  - Work must be done to bring a molecule to the surface, so surface
    molecules carry extra potential energy.
  - The surface therefore contracts to the smallest possible area and
    behaves like a stretched membrane. That is surface tension.
```

**(3) SURFACE ENERGY = SURFACE TENSION**
```
        +===============================+
        |         SOAP  FILM            ||  <-- wire of length l
        +===============================+  --> F , moved dx

  A film has TWO surfaces, so   F  =  T ( 2 l ).

     Work    W   =  F dx    =  T ( 2 l ) dx
     New area dA =  2 l dx

     W / dA   =   T

  So the surface energy per unit area equals the surface tension,
  and  W = T x dA.  ( N/m = J/m^2 )
```

**(4) EXCESS PRESSURE INSIDE A LIQUID DROP**
```
  Let the radius grow by dr under the excess pressure P.

     Work done by P            =   P ( 4 pi r^2 ) dr
     Increase in area          =   8 pi r dr
     Increase in surface energy =  T ( 8 pi r dr )

     P ( 4 pi r^2 ) dr   =   T ( 8 pi r dr )

  +----------------------+
  |    P  =  2 T / r     |
  +----------------------+
```

**(5) EXCESS PRESSURE INSIDE A SOAP BUBBLE**
```
  A soap bubble has an INNER and an OUTER surface, so the increase in
  surface energy is doubled:

     P ( 4 pi r^2 ) dr   =   2 T ( 8 pi r dr )

  +----------------------+
  |    P  =  4 T / r     |
  +----------------------+

  An air bubble inside a liquid has only one surface, so P = 2T/r.

  In every case P is INVERSELY proportional to r: a smaller bubble has
  a HIGHER pressure inside it. That is why, when a small and a large
  soap bubble are joined by a tube, air flows from the SMALL bubble
  into the LARGE one.
```

### Q C4  *** revision-grade combined answer ***
State Pascal's law and explain the working of a hydraulic lift and
hydraulic brakes. State Archimedes' principle and the law of floatation,
and obtain the fraction of a floating body that lies submerged.

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  Pascal's law statement                             1 mark
  Hydraulic lift : diagram + theory                  2 marks
  Hydraulic brake : diagram + working                2 marks
  Archimedes' principle + law of floatation          1.5 marks
  Fraction submerged derivation                      1.5 marks
  ---------------------------------------------------------
```

**(1) PASCAL'S LAW**
```
  A change in pressure applied to an enclosed fluid at rest is
  transmitted UNDIMINISHED to every point of the fluid and to the walls
  of the container.
```

**(2) HYDRAULIC LIFT**
```
      F1
      |
      V
   +-----+                             +---------------+
   | a1  |                             |      a2       |
   +--|--+                             +-------|-------+
   ===|=========================================|=====  ^ F2
   #  |          ENCLOSED  OIL                  |    #
   ##################################################

     Pressure produced :   P  =  F1 / a1
     Transmitted undiminished, so on the large piston

            F2  =  P a2  =  F1 ( a2 / a1 )      with a2 >> a1

     Mechanical advantage = a2/a1.
     Since a1 d1 = a2 d2 , F1 d1 = F2 d2 — energy is conserved.
```

**(3) HYDRAULIC BRAKES**
```
   PEDAL -> MASTER CYLINDER (a1) -> BRAKE FLUID -> WHEEL CYLINDERS (a2)

   A light push produces P = F1/a1 in the master cylinder. Pascal's law
   carries it to all four wheel cylinders, where the force becomes
   F2 = P a2 , large enough to press the brake shoes against the drums.
   Because the SAME pressure reaches every wheel, all four wheels are
   braked EQUALLY and the vehicle stops without skidding.
```

**(4) ARCHIMEDES' PRINCIPLE**
```
  A body wholly or partly immersed in a fluid at rest experiences an
  upward force equal to the weight of the fluid displaced.

        Fb  =  V_displaced x rho_fluid x g

  Apparent weight = real weight - Fb ;  loss of weight = Fb.
```

**(5) LAW OF FLOATATION AND THE FRACTION SUBMERGED**
```
  A body floats when the weight of the liquid displaced equals its own
  weight, which requires  rho_body < rho_liquid.

        V rho_body g   =   V_sub rho_liquid g

  +-------------------------------------------+
  |      V_sub          rho_body              |
  |     -------   =   -------------           |
  |        V           rho_liquid             |
  +-------------------------------------------+

  Example : ice (917) in sea water (1030)  ->  0.89 , so 89 % is
  submerged and only 11 % shows above the surface.
```

### Q C5  *** revision-grade combined answer ***
Derive the expression for the pressure at a depth inside a liquid.
Explain the construction and working of a mercury barometer and of an
open-tube manometer. Distinguish between gauge and absolute pressure.

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
  ---------------------------------------------------------
  Derivation of P = P0 + h rho g (with diagram)      3 marks
  Pressure is the same at the same level             1 mark
  Mercury barometer : diagram + theory               2 marks
  Manometer : diagram + theory                       1.5 marks
  Gauge vs absolute pressure                         0.5 mark
  ---------------------------------------------------------
```

**(1) PRESSURE AT A DEPTH**
```
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~  free surface, pressure P0
              |  P0 A
              V
          +---------+
     h    | LIQUID  |   mass m = A h rho
          |CYLINDER |   weight m g downward
          +---------+
              ^ P A

  Equilibrium :   P A  =  P0 A  +  A h rho g

  +------------------------------+
  |     P  =  P0  +  h rho g     |
  +------------------------------+
```

**(2) SAME LEVEL, SAME PRESSURE**
```
  Take a thin HORIZONTAL cylinder of liquid of area A and length L.
  Its weight is perpendicular to the axis, so along the axis

        P1 A  =  P2 A     ->     P1 = P2

  Hence all points at the same horizontal level in the same connected
  liquid are at the same pressure. This is why the pressure at the base
  depends only on the HEIGHT, not on the shape of the vessel
  (the hydrostatic paradox).
```

**(3) MERCURY BAROMETER**
```
                 ___
                |   |  VACUUM ( P = 0 )
                |   |
                | H |
                | g |   h = 76 cm
        P0      |   |     P0
        |       |   |      |
        V       |   |      V
   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~|   |~~~~~~~~~~~~~
   |     A      |___|     B      |    trough of mercury
   |____________________________|

  A tube about a metre long is filled with mercury and inverted in a
  trough. The mercury falls until a column of height h remains, leaving
  a vacuum (the Torricellian vacuum) above.

  A and B are at the same level in the same liquid, so

        pressure at A  =  pressure at B

        0  +  h rho g  =  P0

  +--------------------------+
  |      P0  =  h rho g      |
  +--------------------------+

  Check :  0.76 x 13600 x 9.8  =  1.013 x 10^5 Pa.

  Mercury is used because water would need a column of 10.34 m.
  The reading does not depend on the tube's diameter or its tilt —
  only the VERTICAL height matters.
```

**(4) OPEN-TUBE MANOMETER**
```
        GAS
     +-------+                          open to the air ( P0 )
     |   P   |                                 |
     +---|---+                                 V
         |       _______________________
         |      |                       |    ---
         |      |                       |     | h
         |______|                       |     v
      A  |_______________________________|   ---
                           B

     Pressure at A (gas side)   =  P
     Pressure at B (open side)  =  P0 + h rho g
     A and B are at the same level, so

  +---------------------------------------+
  |     P  =  P0  +  h rho g              |
  |     gauge pressure  =  h rho g        |
  +---------------------------------------+

  If the liquid stands higher on the GAS side instead,
  P = P0 - h rho g (the gas is below atmospheric pressure).
```

**(5) GAUGE vs ABSOLUTE**
```
        ABSOLUTE  =  ATMOSPHERIC  +  GAUGE

        Gauge pressure    =  P - P0  =  h rho g
        Absolute pressure =  P0 + h rho g

  A tyre gauge reading 2 atm means the absolute pressure inside is 3 atm.
```
