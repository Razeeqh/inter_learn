# PATTERN 1 — Pressure at a depth, barometer, U-tube

### Q1
The gauge pressure at a depth of 10 m in a lake is (g = 10 m/s², rho = 1000)

(a) 10^4 Pa &nbsp; (b) 10^5 Pa &nbsp; (c) 10^6 Pa &nbsp; (d) 2 x 10^5 Pa

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   P_gauge  =  h rho g  =  10 x 1000 x 10  =  1 x 10^5  Pa
```
> **SHORTCUT:** every 10 m of water is roughly 1 atmosphere.

---

### Q2
In a U-tube, a water column of height 13.6 cm balances a mercury column of
height (rho_Hg = 13.6 g/cm³)

(a) 1 cm &nbsp; (b) 13.6 cm &nbsp; (c) 136 cm &nbsp; (d) 0.1 cm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Same pressure at the common level:

       h1 rho1  =  h2 rho2

       13.6 x 1  =  h2 x 13.6      ->     h2 = 1 cm
```

---

### Q3
The pressure at the bottom of a vessel containing a liquid depends on

(a) the shape of the vessel
(b) the total volume of the liquid
(c) the vertical height of the liquid column
(d) the area of the base

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   P = P0 + h rho g  contains only h , rho and g.
   The HYDROSTATIC PARADOX: shape and volume are irrelevant.
```

### Q4
A mercury barometer reads 76 cm. If the tube is tilted so that it makes 60°
with the vertical, the length of the mercury column in the tube becomes

(a) 76 cm &nbsp; (b) 38 cm &nbsp; (c) 152 cm &nbsp; (d) 66 cm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   The VERTICAL height must stay 76 cm.

        vertical height  =  L cos(60)

        76  =  L x 0.5      ->      L  =  152 cm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** tilting NEVER changes the vertical height. It only lengthens
> the column along the tube.

### Q5
A closed vessel is completely filled with water and moved with a constant
acceleration. The pressure at a point in the water then depends on

(a) depth only &nbsp; (b) the horizontal position only
(c) both the depth and the horizontal position &nbsp; (d) neither

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   In an accelerating vessel the free surface tilts, so the pressure varies
   along the horizontal direction too (an effective gravity that is tilted).
```

# PATTERN 2 — Pascal's law and the hydraulic machine

### Q6
The pistons of a hydraulic press have radii in the ratio 1 : 10. A force of
100 N on the small piston produces a force on the large piston of

(a) 1000 N &nbsp; (b) 10^4 N &nbsp; (c) 10 N &nbsp; (d) 100 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   F2 / F1  =  a2 / a1  =  ( r2 / r1 )^2  =  10^2  =  100

   F2  =  100 x 100  =  10^4  N
```
> **SHORTCUT:** when RADII are given, always SQUARE the ratio. The commonest
> mistake in this pattern is answering 1000 N.

### Q7
In a hydraulic lift, the small piston moves down 30 cm while the large piston
rises 3 cm. The ratio of the areas a2 : a1 is

(a) 1 : 10 &nbsp; (b) 10 : 1 &nbsp; (c) 1 : 100 &nbsp; (d) 100 : 1

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   a1 d1  =  a2 d2

   a2 / a1  =  d1 / d2  =  30 / 3  =  10
```

### Q8
Hydraulic brakes work on the principle of

(a) Archimedes' principle &nbsp; (b) Bernoulli's theorem

**ANSWER: (c)**

   Pressure applied at the master cylinder is transmitted undiminished

# PATTERN 3 — Archimedes' principle and floatation

### Q9
A body floats in water with one third of its volume above the surface.
Its density is

(a) 333 kg/m³ &nbsp; (b) 500 kg/m³ &nbsp; (c) 667 kg/m³ &nbsp; (d) 750 kg/m³

**ANSWER: (c)**

   rho_body  =  (2/3) x 1000  =  666.7  ~  667  kg/m^3
```

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
                W_air                 W
   RD  =  -------------------  =  ---------  =  2
           W_air  -  W_water        W/2
```

### Q11
A piece of ice floats in a beaker of water. When the ice melts completely,
the level of the water

(a) rises &nbsp; (b) falls &nbsp; (c) stays the same &nbsp; (d) first rises then falls

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Floating ice displaces water equal to its OWN WEIGHT.
   When it melts it becomes exactly that same weight of water,
   occupying exactly the volume it was already displacing.
   -> the level is UNCHANGED.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** if the ice contains a piece of **cork or wood**, the level still
> stays the same. If it contains a piece of **stone or iron**, the level **FALLS**.

---

### Q12
A block floats in water with 40 % of its volume above the surface. In a liquid
of density 1200 kg/m³ the fraction of its volume submerged will be

(a) 0.4 &nbsp; (b) 0.5 &nbsp; (c) 0.6 &nbsp; (d) 0.75

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   In water:  submerged fraction = 0.6   ->   rho_body = 600 kg/m^3

   In the new liquid:

        V_sub / V  =  600 / 1200  =  0.5
```

---

### Q13
A man is sitting in a boat floating on a pond. He drops a heavy stone from the
boat into the water. The level of the water in the pond

(a) rises &nbsp; (b) falls &nbsp; (c) stays the same &nbsp; (d) cannot be decided

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   In the boat, the stone displaced water equal to its WEIGHT (a lot).
   In the water, it displaces only its own VOLUME (less, since the stone
   is denser than water).
   -> less water is displaced  ->  the level FALLS.
```

# PATTERN 4 — Equation of continuity

### Q14
Water flows in a pipe of radius r with speed v. If the radius becomes r/2,
the new speed is

(a) v/2 &nbsp; (b) 2v &nbsp; (c) 4v &nbsp; (d) v/4

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   a v = constant , and a is proportional to r^2

        v2 / v1  =  ( r1 / r2 )^2  =  2^2  =  4
```
> **SHORTCUT:** speed goes as **1/r²**, never as 1/r.

---

### Q15
A pipe of cross-section 10 cm² carrying water at 5 m/s branches into two
identical pipes of cross-section 5 cm² each. The speed in each branch is

(a) 2.5 m/s &nbsp; (b) 5 m/s &nbsp; (c) 10 m/s &nbsp; (d) 20 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   10 x 5  =  5 v  +  5 v  =  10 v

   v  =  5  m/s
```
> **SHORTCUT:** total area in = total area out here (5 + 5 = 10), so nothing
> changes. Do not be tricked into halving or doubling.

### Q16
The equation of continuity is a consequence of the conservation of

(a) energy &nbsp; (b) momentum &nbsp; (c) mass &nbsp; (d) angular momentum

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Continuity  ->  MASS.
   Bernoulli   ->  ENERGY.
   Do not confuse the two — this exact pair is asked every year.

### Q18
A tank is filled with water to a height of 20 m. The speed of efflux from a
small hole at the bottom is (g = 10 m/s²)

(a) 10 m/s &nbsp; (b) 20 m/s &nbsp; (c) 40 m/s &nbsp; (d) 400 m/s

### Q19
A hole is made in the side of a tank of liquid depth H. The horizontal range
of the jet is maximum when the hole is at a depth of

(a) H/4 &nbsp; (b) H/2 &nbsp; (c) 3H/4 &nbsp; (d) H

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   x  =  2 sqrt( h ( H - h ) )

   The product h(H - h) is maximum when h = H/2 ,
   and then  x_max  =  2 sqrt( H^2/4 )  =  H.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** maximum range = H, achieved at the MID-DEPTH. Two holes
> equidistant from the mid-point give the SAME range.

Water flows through a horizontal pipe. At a wide section its speed is 2 m/s

(a) 3000 Pa &nbsp; (b) 6000 Pa &nbsp; (c) 9000 Pa &nbsp; (d) 12000 Pa

**ANSWER: (b)**

       =  500 x 12  =  6000  Pa
```

A large open tank has a small hole at a depth h. If the tank is now pressurised

### Q29
The terminal velocity of a sphere falling through a viscous liquid is
proportional to

(a) r &nbsp; (b) r² &nbsp; (c) 1/r &nbsp; (d) 1/r²

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
              2 r^2 ( rho - sigma ) g
      v_t  =  ------------------------      ->     v_t  proportional to r^2
                       9 eta
```

---

### Q30
Eight identical rain drops, each falling with terminal velocity v, coalesce
into a single drop. Its terminal velocity is

(a) 2v &nbsp; (b) 4v &nbsp; (c) 8v &nbsp; (d) 16v

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Volume :  8 x (4/3) pi r^3  =  (4/3) pi R^3   ->   R = 2 r

   v_t proportional to (radius)^2   ->   V  =  ( 2 )^2 v  =  4 v
```
> **SHORTCUT:** for **n** drops merging, the new terminal velocity is
> `n^(2/3) x v`.  n = 8 -> 4v ; n = 27 -> 9v ; n = 64 -> 16v ; n = 1000 -> 100v.

---

### Q31
27 identical drops of mercury, each with terminal velocity v, combine into one
drop. Its terminal velocity is

(a) 3v &nbsp; (b) 9v &nbsp; (c) 27v &nbsp; (d) 81v

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   n^(2/3)  =  27^(2/3)  =  ( 3^3 )^(2/3)  =  3^2  =  9      ->  9 v
```

---

### Q32
When a body falls with its terminal velocity through a viscous fluid, its
acceleration is

(a) g &nbsp; (b) g/2 &nbsp; (c) zero &nbsp; (d) 2g

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   At the terminal velocity, weight = upthrust + viscous drag,
   so the NET FORCE is zero and the acceleration is zero.
```

---

### Q33
An air bubble rises through water. As it rises (assume the radius is constant),
its velocity

(a) keeps increasing &nbsp; (b) keeps decreasing
(c) first increases then becomes constant &nbsp; (d) is constant throughout

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Same as a falling ball, but upside down. The bubble accelerates upward
   until the drag balances the net upward force (upthrust - weight),
   then it moves with a constant terminal velocity.

   Formally, rho_air < rho_water makes v_t negative -> upward motion.
```

### Q40
The work done in blowing a soap bubble of radius r from a soap solution of
surface tension T is

(a) 4 pi r² T &nbsp; (b) 8 pi r² T &nbsp; (c) 2 pi r² T &nbsp; (d) 16 pi r² T

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   A soap bubble has TWO surfaces:

        W  =  T x 2 x ( 4 pi r^2 )  =  8 pi r^2 T
```
> **SHORTCUT:** SOAP BUBBLE -> always a factor of **2** (two surfaces).
> A single liquid DROP -> only `4 pi r² T`.

### Q41
A liquid drop of radius R breaks into 8 identical droplets. The work done is

(a) 4 pi R² T &nbsp; (b) 8 pi R² T &nbsp; (c) 2 pi R² T &nbsp; (d) 16 pi R² T

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   W  =  4 pi R^2 T ( n^(1/3)  -  1 )
      =  4 pi R^2 T ( 8^(1/3)  -  1 )
      =  4 pi R^2 T ( 2 - 1 )
      =  4 pi R^2 T
```
> **SHORTCUT:** splitting a drop ABSORBS energy, so the droplets **cool**.
> Droplets merging RELEASES energy, so the big drop **warms up**.

### Q47
Two soap bubbles of unequal radii are connected by a tube. Then

(a) the small one grows and the big one shrinks
(b) the big one grows and the small one shrinks
(c) both stay the same
(d) both shrink

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Excess pressure = 4T/r , so the SMALLER bubble has the HIGHER pressure.
   Air therefore flows from the small bubble to the big one:
   the SMALL bubble shrinks and the BIG bubble grows.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** "the rich get richer." Everyone guesses the opposite.

### Q48
Two soap bubbles of radii 5 cm and 3 cm are joined by a tube. The radius of
curvature of the common interface is

(a) 2 cm &nbsp; (b) 4 cm &nbsp; (c) 7.5 cm &nbsp; (d) 8 cm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
      1        1        1          4T     4T     4T
     ---  =   ---  -   ---     (from  --- = --- - --- )
      r        r2       r1            r     r2    r1

      1        1       1        5  -  3        2
     ---  =   ---  -  ---  =  -----------  =  ----
      r        3       5           15          15

      r  =  15 / 2  =  7.5  cm

   General formula :   r  =  r1 r2 / ( r1 - r2 )  with r1 > r2.
```

### Q53
Water rises to a height h in a capillary of radius r, and the mass of the
water in the tube is m. In a tube of radius 2r the mass of water raised is

(a) m/2 &nbsp; (b) m &nbsp; (c) 2m &nbsp; (d) 4m

```

> This is the single most-missed capillarity question in JEE.

A capillary tube is taken inside an orbiting satellite. The liquid

(a) does not rise at all
(b) rises to the full length of the tube and stays there
(c) overflows continuously
(d) falls

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   In free fall the effective g is ZERO, so h -> infinity.
   The liquid rises to the top of the tube and STOPS. It cannot overflow —
   that would create energy from nothing. The meniscus simply flattens.
```

### Q56
If the length of the capillary tube above the liquid surface is LESS than the
calculated rise h, then

(a) the liquid overflows like a fountain
(b) the liquid rises to the top and stops, the meniscus radius increasing
(c) the liquid does not rise at all
(d) the tube breaks

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   h R = constant. If the available height is smaller, the radius of
   curvature R of the meniscus INCREASES (the meniscus becomes flatter)
   so that the balance is still satisfied. No fountain — that would be
   a perpetual motion machine.
```

### Q57
The angle of contact for mercury and glass is about

(a) 0 degrees &nbsp; (b) 8 degrees &nbsp; (c) 90 degrees &nbsp; (d) 140 degrees

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   Water - glass    :   ~ 8 deg    (acute, concave, RISES)
   Mercury - glass  :   ~ 140 deg  (obtuse, convex, is DEPRESSED)
   Water - silver   :   90 deg     (flat surface, no rise)
```

### Q58
If the angle of contact is obtuse, then

(a) the liquid wets the solid and rises &nbsp; (b) the liquid is depressed
(c) the meniscus is concave &nbsp; (d) surface tension is zero

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   theta obtuse  ->  cos(theta) negative  ->  h negative  ->  DEPRESSION.
   The meniscus is convex and the liquid does not wet the solid.
```

### Q60
Surface tension of water is 0.072 N/m. The capillary rise in a tube of radius
0.1 mm (theta = 0, g = 10) is about

(a) 7.2 cm &nbsp; (b) 14.4 cm &nbsp; (c) 3.6 cm &nbsp; (d) 1.44 cm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
            2 T cos(theta)         2 x 0.072 x 1          0.144
   h  =  ------------------  =  --------------------  =  --------
              r rho g            10^-4 x 1000 x 10           1

      =  0.144 m  =  14.4 cm
```
