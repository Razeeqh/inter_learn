# Laws of Motion — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — STATE A LAW OF MOTION                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Newton's first / second / third law of motion."
  METHOD    Give the statement word-for-word, then ONE extra line:
              1st law -> also called the LAW OF INERTIA; it defines force.
              2nd law -> F = dp/dt = ma ; it gives the MEASURE of force.
              3rd law -> add "they act on TWO DIFFERENT BODIES."
  TRAP      Writing only F = ma for the second law. The examiner wants
            the sentence about the RATE OF CHANGE OF MOMENTUM.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — INERTIA AND ITS TYPES                               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is inertia?" / "Name the types of inertia with examples."
  METHOD    Define inertia, say MASS is its measure, then list:
              INERTIA OF REST      - dust off a beaten carpet
              INERTIA OF MOTION    - falling forward when a bus brakes
              INERTIA OF DIRECTION - mud flying off a wheel
  TRAP      Bus STARTS -> fall BACKWARD (rest).
            Bus STOPS  -> fall FORWARD  (motion).
            Students reverse these constantly.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — ARISTOTLE'S FALLACY                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is Aristotle's fallacy?"
  METHOD    1. State the WRONG belief: a force is needed to keep a body
               moving.
            2. Say why it is wrong: the body stops only because of the
               hidden force of FRICTION.
            3. Mention Galileo's inclined-plane experiment.
  TRAP      Only stating the correction. You must state the fallacy itself.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — DEFINE MOMENTUM / IMPULSE, UNIT AND DIMENSIONS      2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define linear momentum / impulse. Give its SI unit and
            dimensional formula."
  METHOD    p = m v  ;  J = F t = mv - mu
            Both:  unit  N s  (or kg m/s) ,  dimensions [ M L T^-1 ]
            Both are VECTORS.
  NOTE      Free marks. Never lose these.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — DERIVE F = ma FROM THE SECOND LAW                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Newton's second law and hence derive F = ma."
  METHOD    1. F is proportional to dp/dt
            2. F = k d(mv)/dt = k m dv/dt = k m a
            3. In SI units, k = 1 by the DEFINITION of the newton
            4. F = m a
            5. Add: F = 0 gives a = 0, so the FIRST law is contained
               inside the second.
  TRAP      Forgetting to say that k = 1 because of how the newton
            is defined.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — IMPULSE-MOMENTUM THEOREM + REAL-LIFE REASON      2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does a cricketer pull his hands back?" /
            "Why do we jump onto sand?" / "Why do cars have airbags?"
  METHOD    ONE line does all of them:

                            change in momentum
              Force   =   ----------------------
                                  time

            Same top, bigger bottom -> smaller force.
            Then apply that line to the situation named.
  TRAP      Saying "to reduce the momentum". The momentum change is
            FIXED. What changes is the TIME.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — IMPULSE / AVERAGE FORCE NUMERICAL                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A ball of mass ... hits a wall at ... and rebounds at ...
            Find the impulse and the average force."
  METHOD    1. Fix a POSITIVE direction and write u and v with SIGNS.
            2. J = m v - m u
            3. F = J / t
  TRAP      REBOUND means the sign flips, so the magnitudes ADD:
            |J| = m(v + u), NOT m(v - u).
            Also convert grams to kilograms first.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — AREA UNDER AN F-t GRAPH                             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What does the area under a force-time graph represent?" or
            a graph is given and impulse is asked.
  METHOD    Area = IMPULSE = change in momentum.
            Break the graph into triangles and rectangles and add
            the areas. Areas BELOW the time axis are NEGATIVE.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — THIRD LAW: WHY FORCES DO NOT CANCEL                 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "If action and reaction are equal and opposite, how does a
            horse pull a cart?" / "Explain why they never cancel."
  METHOD    1. Draw the two bodies as separate boxes with one arrow each.
            2. Say the two forces act on DIFFERENT BODIES.
            3. Analyse the CART alone: forward pull minus friction.
            4. Analyse the HORSE alone: backward pull plus the forward
               reaction of the GROUND on its hooves.
  TRAP      Forgetting the ground's reaction on the horse. That is the
            force that actually moves the system.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — CONSERVATION OF MOMENTUM: DERIVE IT                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State and prove the law of conservation of linear momentum."
  METHOD    1. Two bodies collide; contact time t.
            2. THIRD law:   F_12 = - F_21
            3. SECOND law on each: F = (mv - mu)/t
            4. Substitute, cancel t, rearrange.
            5. m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2
  TRAP      Skipping the third-law step. That step IS the proof.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — RECOIL OF A GUN                                 2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A gun of mass M fires a bullet of mass m at speed v ..."
  METHOD    1. Total momentum before firing = 0.
            2. M V + m v = 0
            3. V = - m v / M ; the minus sign means BACKWARD.
            4. Add: since M >> m, V is small; holding the gun to the
               shoulder increases M further and reduces the recoil.
  TRAP      Converting the bullet mass from grams to kilograms.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — ROCKET PROPULSION                               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain the principle of rocket propulsion."
  METHOD    1. Gases are thrown DOWNWARD at high speed (action).
            2. The gases push the rocket UPWARD (reaction, third law).
            3. Momentum conservation:  M V = m v
            4. Thrust = u (dm/dt) where u is the exhaust speed.
            5. As fuel burns, M decreases so V increases.
  NOTE      Mention that a rocket works in vacuum, since it needs
            nothing external to push against.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — LAMI'S THEOREM                                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Lami's theorem" / a body hangs from two strings at
            given angles, find the tensions.
  METHOD    1. Draw the three concurrent forces from one point.
            2. Mark the angle BETWEEN THE OTHER TWO for each force.
            3.       F1              F2              F3
                 -----------  =  -----------  =  -----------
                  sin(alpha)      sin(beta)      sin(gamma)
            4. Solve for the unknown tensions.
  TRAP      Using the angle NEXT TO the force instead of the angle
            BETWEEN THE OTHER TWO. Also, the three angles add to 360 deg.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — DRAW A FREE BODY DIAGRAM                        2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the free body diagram of ..." (any system)
  METHOD    1. Isolate ONE body, draw it as a box.
            2. Weight mg  -> straight DOWN, always.
            3. Normal N   -> PERPENDICULAR to the surface.
            4. Tension T  -> along the string, PULLING the body.
            5. Friction f -> along the surface, OPPOSING sliding.
            6. Mark the direction of the acceleration separately.
  TRAP      Drawing a "forward force" on a body just because it is
            moving. Motion is not a force. Every arrow must have an
            object that causes it.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — TWO CONNECTED BLOCKS ON A SURFACE               4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Two blocks joined by a string are pulled by a force F ..."
  METHOD    1. FBD of each block.
            2. F - T = m1 a   and   T = m2 a
            3. ADD to kill T:      a = F / (m1 + m2)
            4. Substitute back:    T = m2 F / (m1 + m2)
            5. If the surface is rough, subtract mu(m1 + m2)g from F.
  TRAP      T is NOT F, and NOT F/2 unless the masses are equal.
            The rule is:  T = (the mass BEHIND the string) x a.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — ATWOOD MACHINE (PULLEY)                            8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Two masses connected by a string over a frictionless pulley..."
            *** THE MOST FREQUENT 8-MARK QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
  METHOD    1. State the assumptions: light inextensible string, light
               frictionless pulley, so ONE tension and EQUAL accelerations.
            2. FBD of m1 (down):    m1 g - T = m1 a
            3. FBD of m2 (up):      T - m2 g = m2 a
            4. ADD:                 a = (m1 - m2) g / (m1 + m2)
            5. Substitute back:     T = 2 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2)
            6. Force on the pulley = 2T = 4 m1 m2 g / (m1 + m2)
            7. Check the special cases m1 = m2 and m2 = 0.
  TRAP      Using two different tensions, or forgetting that the heavier
            mass goes DOWN. ALWAYS CHECK  m2 g < T < m1 g.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — BODY IN A LIFT / APPARENT WEIGHT                4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A man of mass m stands in a lift ... find his apparent
            weight" / "when does he feel weightless?"
  METHOD    Apparent weight = the NORMAL REACTION N.
            1. At rest or constant velocity :   N = m g
            2. Accelerating UP              :   N = m ( g + a )
            3. Accelerating DOWN            :   N = m ( g - a )
            4. Free fall (a = g)            :   N = 0 , WEIGHTLESS
            Draw the lift box with N up and mg down for each case.
  TRAP      Judging by the VELOCITY. A lift going DOWN but SLOWING DOWN
            has an UPWARD acceleration, so the man feels HEAVIER.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — SMOOTH INCLINED PLANE                           4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A body slides down a smooth incline of angle theta ..."
  METHOD    1. Draw the FBD with TILTED axes.
            2. Resolve mg into mg sin(theta) ALONG and mg cos(theta)
               PERPENDICULAR.
            3. Perpendicular:  N = mg cos(theta)
            4. Along:          a = g sin(theta)
            5. Velocity at the bottom:  v = sqrt(2 g L sin th) = sqrt(2gh)
  TRAP      Swapping sin and cos. Remember: at theta = 90 the incline is
            vertical and a must equal g, and only  g sin(90) = g works.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — ROUGH INCLINED PLANE                            4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A body slides down a rough incline with coefficient mu ..."
  METHOD    1. N = mg cos(theta) , so f = mu mg cos(theta)
            2. SLIDING DOWN, friction acts UP:
                    a = g ( sin(theta) - mu cos(theta) )
            3. MOVING UP, friction acts DOWN:
                    a = g ( sin(theta) + mu cos(theta) )   (retardation)
            4. Minimum force to push it up at constant speed:
                    F = mg ( sin(theta) + mu cos(theta) )
  TRAP      Using the wrong SIGN in front of mu cos(theta). Ask yourself
            which way the body slides; friction goes the other way.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — ANGLE OF REPOSE = ANGLE OF FRICTION                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define the angle of repose and show that it equals the
            angle of friction."
  METHOD    1. Angle of friction:  tan(theta_f) = f_L / N = mu
            2. Angle of repose: on the verge of sliding,
                    mg sin(theta_r) = mu mg cos(theta_r)
                    so  tan(theta_r) = mu
            3. Therefore theta_f = theta_r.
            Draw BOTH diagrams.
  TRAP      Giving only the formulae without the two diagrams. The
            diagrams carry marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — TYPES AND LAWS OF FRICTION                      4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the laws of friction" / "Distinguish between static,
            limiting and kinetic friction."
  METHOD    1. STATIC   f_s <= mu_s N , SELF-ADJUSTING
            2. LIMITING f_L = mu_s N , the maximum
            3. KINETIC  f_k = mu_k N , constant, and f_k < f_L
            4. Draw the friction-versus-applied-force GRAPH.
            5. List the laws, including "independent of the AREA".
  TRAP      Writing that friction increases with the area of contact.
            It does NOT. This is asked almost every year.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — ADVANTAGES / DISADVANTAGES / REDUCING FRICTION     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is friction called a necessary evil?" / "How can
            friction be reduced?"
  METHOD    ADVANTAGES     - walking, braking, gripping, nails and knots,
                             belt drives
            DISADVANTAGES  - wear and tear, heat, wasted energy, more fuel
            REDUCING IT    - polishing, lubrication, ball bearings,
                             streamlining, air cushion
  NOTE      Pure memory. Four lines each earns the full 4 marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — FRICTION NUMERICAL ON A FLAT SURFACE            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The least force needed to move a block is ..." /
            "Find the retardation / stopping distance."
  METHOD    1. N = mg  (or mg -/+ F sin(theta) if the force is slanted)
            2. Least force to start motion = f_L = mu N
            3. Net force = F - mu N , so a = (F - mu N)/m
            4. Retardation with no push:      a = mu g
            5. Stopping distance:             s = v^2 / (2 mu g)
  TRAP      Remember that a and s do NOT contain the mass.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — WHY ROLLING FRICTION IS SMALLEST                   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why is rolling friction less than sliding friction?" /
            "Why are ball bearings used?"
  METHOD    In rolling, the surfaces do not slide over each other; the
            contact is momentary and almost at a point, so far fewer
            surface irregularities have to be broken.
            Ball bearings convert sliding friction into rolling friction.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — CENTRIPETAL FORCE: DEFINE AND GIVE EXAMPLES        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define centripetal force." / "What provides the centripetal
            force in ... ?"
  METHOD    F = m v^2 / r = m r omega^2 , directed towards the CENTRE.
            It does NO WORK, being perpendicular to the velocity.
            Stone on a string   -> TENSION
            Car on a level road -> FRICTION
            Car on a banked road-> component of the NORMAL REACTION
            Planet round the Sun-> GRAVITATION
  TRAP      Calling centrifugal force a real force. It is a PSEUDO force.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — MAXIMUM SPEED ON A LEVEL ROAD                      4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the maximum speed at which a car can take a turn on a
            level road."
  METHOD    1. FBD: N up, mg down, friction f towards the centre.
            2. N = mg
            3. m v^2 / r = f <= mu N = mu m g
            4. v_max = sqrt( mu g r )
            5. Note that it is INDEPENDENT of the mass, and that a wet
               road lowers mu and hence v_max.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — BANKING OF ROADS                                4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is banking of roads? Derive the angle of banking."
  METHOD    1. Draw the tilted road with N perpendicular to it.
            2. Resolve N into N cos(theta) up and N sin(theta) inward.
            3. Vertical  : N cos(theta) = mg
            4. Horizontal: N sin(theta) = m v^2 / r
            5. Divide    : tan(theta) = v^2 / ( r g )
            6. Optimum speed v = sqrt( r g tan(theta) )
            7. Note: independent of mass; no friction needed at that
               speed; for railways the outer rail is raised
               (superelevation).
  TRAP      Resolving mg instead of N. On a banked road it is the NORMAL
            REACTION that gets resolved.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — BENDING OF A CYCLIST                            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Why does a cyclist lean inwards while turning? Derive the
            angle of bending."
  METHOD    1. If upright, all forces are vertical and nothing supplies
               the centripetal force.
            2. Leaning makes the ground reaction R slant.
            3. R cos(theta) = mg ,  R sin(theta) = m v^2 / r
            4. tan(theta) = v^2 / ( r g )     (theta from the VERTICAL)
            5. Safety condition: tan(theta) <= mu
  NOTE      Same formula as banking. Learn once, use twice.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — EQUILIBRIUM OF CONCURRENT FORCES                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are concurrent forces? State the condition for
            equilibrium." / A body hangs by two strings.
  METHOD    1. Concurrent = all lines of action meet at one point.
            2. Condition: sum of F_x = 0 AND sum of F_y = 0.
            3. Resolve every force horizontally and vertically.
            4. With exactly three forces, use Lami's theorem instead.
  TRAP      Equilibrium does NOT mean at rest. Constant velocity is also
            equilibrium.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — MASS versus WEIGHT                                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between mass and weight."
  METHOD    Mass   - matter content, SCALAR, kg, same everywhere,
                     measure of inertia, beam balance.
            Weight - force of gravity, VECTOR, newton, changes with g,
                     W = mg, zero in free fall, spring balance.
```

# GUARANTEED-MARKS PLAN FOR THIS CHAPTER

Learn only these six things and you score about **12 out of 60** in Physics I:

```
  1.  The three laws, word-for-word, plus the three types of inertia.

  2.  Impulse:  F = (change in momentum) / time
      and the one-line explanation it gives for the cricketer, the sand
      and the airbag.

  3.  The LIFT table:   N = m(g + a) up ,  N = m(g - a) down ,  0 in free fall.

  4.  The ATWOOD results:

              ( m1 - m2 ) g                 2 m1 m2 g
        a  =  --------------      T  =   ---------------
                m1  +  m2                   m1  +  m2

  5.  FRICTION: the three types, the graph, mu = tan(theta_repose),
      and the advantages / disadvantages / reducing lists.

  6.  CIRCULAR MOTION:

        v_max( level )  =  sqrt( mu g r )

        tan( theta )    =  v^2 / ( r g )      for banking AND the cyclist
```

Total study time needed: about **two days**.

If you can name the pattern, you already know the first three steps of the answer.
**That is the entire point of this file.**
