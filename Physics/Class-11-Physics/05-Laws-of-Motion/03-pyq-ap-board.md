## Topic: The three laws and inertia

### Q A1
State Newton's first law of motion. What does it define?

**ANSWER:**
```
  "Every body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a
   straight line unless it is compelled by an external force to change
   that state."

  It DEFINES force (that which changes the state of motion) and
  introduces INERTIA. It is also called the LAW OF INERTIA.
```

---

### Q A2
What is inertia? What is the measure of inertia?

**ANSWER:** Inertia is the inability of a body to change its own state of rest
or of uniform motion by itself. **MASS is the measure of inertia** — the greater
the mass, the greater the inertia.

---

### Q A3
Name the three types of inertia and give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. INERTIA OF REST       -  dust flies off when a carpet is beaten.
  2. INERTIA OF MOTION     -  a passenger falls forward when a bus
                              suddenly stops.
  3. INERTIA OF DIRECTION  -  mud flies off tangentially from a moving
                              wheel.
```

---

### Q A4
Why does a passenger fall backward when a bus suddenly starts moving?

**ANSWER:** His feet move forward with the floor of the bus, but the upper part
of his body remains at rest because of the **inertia of rest**. So the body is
left behind and he falls backward.

---

### Q A5
State Newton's second law of motion.

**ANSWER:** The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to
the applied external force and takes place in the direction of the force.
```
             dp
     F  =   ----   =   m a         (for constant mass)
             dt
```

---

### Q A6
Define one newton.

**ANSWER:** One newton is the force which produces an acceleration of
**1 m/s^2** in a body of mass **1 kg**.
```
   1 N  =  1 kg m / s^2  =  10^5 dyne
```

---

### Q A7
State Newton's third law of motion. On how many bodies do the action and
reaction act?

**ANSWER:** To every action there is always an equal and opposite reaction.
They act on **TWO DIFFERENT BODIES**, which is why they never cancel each other.

---

### Q A8
Are the weight of a book and the normal reaction from the table an
action-reaction pair? Why?

**ANSWER:** **No.** Both act on the **same body** (the book), so they are not an
action-reaction pair. The true reaction to the book's weight is the gravitational
pull the book exerts on the **Earth**.

---

## Topic: Momentum and impulse

### Q A9
Define linear momentum. Give its SI unit and dimensional formula.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Linear momentum  =  mass  x  velocity        p  =  m v

  It is a VECTOR, along the direction of the velocity.

  SI unit      :  kg m / s   (or N s)
  Dimensions   :  [ M L T^-1 ]
```

---

### Q A10
Define impulse. Give its unit and dimensions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Impulse  =  force  x  time of action  =  change in momentum

           J  =  F t  =  m v  -  m u

  SI unit     :  N s
  Dimensions  :  [ M L T^-1 ]      (same as momentum)
```

---

### Q A11
What does the area under a force-time graph represent?

**ANSWER:** It represents the **IMPULSE** of the force, which is equal to the
**change in momentum** of the body.

---

### Q A12
Why does a cricketer pull his hands backwards while catching a ball?

**ANSWER:** Pulling the hands back **increases the time** over which the ball's
momentum is destroyed. Since
```
                  change in momentum
    Force  =  --------------------------
                        time
```
a longer time means a **smaller force** on the hands, so they are not hurt.

---

### Q A13
Why do we jump onto sand or a mattress rather than onto concrete?

**ANSWER:** Sand yields, so the stopping time is longer. For the same change in
momentum, a longer time gives a **smaller force** on the legs.

---

## Topic: Conservation of momentum

### Q A14
State the law of conservation of linear momentum.

**ANSWER:** When the **net external force** on a system is **zero**, the total
linear momentum of the system remains **constant**.
```
     m1 u1  +  m2 u2   =   m1 v1  +  m2 v2
```

---

### Q A15
Why does a gun recoil when a bullet is fired?

**ANSWER:** Before firing the total momentum is zero. No external force acts, so
it must stay zero. The bullet gains forward momentum `mv`, so the gun must gain
an equal backward momentum `MV`, giving `V = - mv/M`. This backward motion is the
**recoil**.

---

### Q A16
Explain the principle of rocket propulsion in one line.

**ANSWER:** The rocket throws hot gases **downward** at high speed (action); by
Newton's third law the gases push the rocket **upward** (reaction). Total
momentum is conserved: `MV = mv`.

---

## Topic: Equilibrium and Lami's theorem

### Q A17
State Lami's theorem.

**ANSWER:** If three concurrent forces keep a body in equilibrium, each force is
proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two.
```
         F1              F2              F3
     -----------  =  -----------  =  -----------
      sin(alpha)      sin(beta)      sin(gamma)
```

---

### Q A18
What are concurrent forces? What is the condition for their equilibrium?

**ANSWER:** Forces whose lines of action all pass through a single point.
They are in equilibrium when their **vector sum is zero**, i.e.
`sum of F_x = 0` and `sum of F_y = 0`.

---

## Topic: Friction

### Q A19
Define the coefficient of friction. What is its unit?

**ANSWER:**
```
                friction force          f
      mu  =  --------------------  =  -----
               normal reaction          N
```
It is a **ratio of two forces**, so it is **dimensionless and has NO UNIT**.

---

### Q A20
Distinguish between static, limiting and kinetic friction.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATIC    -  acts before motion begins; SELF-ADJUSTING;  f_s <= mu_s N
  LIMITING  -  the maximum static friction, just before sliding; f_L = mu_s N
  KINETIC   -  acts while the body slides;  f_k = mu_k N ;  always f_k < f_L
```

---

### Q A21
Define the angle of friction.

**ANSWER:** The angle between the **normal reaction** and the **resultant** of
the normal reaction and the limiting friction.
```
      tan( theta_f )   =   mu
```

---

### Q A22
Define the angle of repose. How is it related to the coefficient of friction?

**ANSWER:** The **minimum angle** of an inclined plane at which a body placed on
it just begins to slide down on its own.
```
      mu  =  tan( theta_r )        and       theta_r  =  theta_f
```

---

### Q A23
Why is rolling friction less than sliding friction?

**ANSWER:** In rolling, the surfaces do not slide over each other. The contact is
momentary and almost at a point, so far fewer surface irregularities have to be
broken. Hence rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction.

---

### Q A24
Why is friction called a "necessary evil"?

**ANSWER:** It is **necessary** because walking, driving, braking and holding
objects are impossible without it. It is an **evil** because it causes wear and
tear and wastes energy as heat.

---

### Q A25
Mention any two methods of reducing friction.

**ANSWER:** (i) **Lubrication** with oil or grease. (ii) Using **ball bearings**,
which convert sliding friction into rolling friction.
(Also acceptable: polishing, streamlining, air cushions.)

---

## Topic: Circular motion

### Q A26
Define centripetal force. Give one example.

**ANSWER:** The force that acts on a body moving in a circle, directed **towards
the centre**, which keeps it on the circular path.
```
              m v^2
      F  =   -------
                r
```
Example: the tension in a string when a stone is whirled in a circle.

---

### Q A27
Why is centripetal force said to do no work?

**ANSWER:** Because it is always **perpendicular** to the velocity (and hence to
the displacement). It changes only the **direction** of the velocity, never its
magnitude, so no work is done.

---

### Q A28
Why are roads banked at curves?

**ANSWER:** Banking tilts the road so that a component of the **normal reaction**
supplies the centripetal force. The vehicle can then take the turn safely
without depending on friction, which may fail on a wet road.

---

### Q A29
Why does a cyclist bend inwards while taking a turn?

**ANSWER:** Leaning inward makes the reaction of the ground slant, so its
horizontal component provides the necessary centripetal force.
```
      tan( theta )   =   v^2 / ( r g )
```

---

### Q A30
Is centrifugal force a real force?

**ANSWER:** **No.** It is a **pseudo (fictitious) force** experienced only by an
observer in a rotating (non-inertial) frame. In an inertial frame only the
inward centripetal force exists.

### Q B1
State and explain Newton's second law of motion. Hence derive F = ma.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT
  The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to
  the applied force, and takes place in the direction of the force.

  DERIVATION

                     dp
      F   prop.     ----
                     dt

                    dp          d(mv)                dv
      F   =   k    ----  =  k  -------   =   k m   ------   =  k m a
                    dt           dt                  dt

      (mass m is constant)

  In SI units the newton is DEFINED so that k = 1.

                    +---------------+
                    |    F = m a    |
                    +---------------+

  MEANING
  1.  Force and acceleration are in the same direction.
  2.  If F = 0 then a = 0, which is the FIRST law. So the first law is
      contained in the second.
  3.  A force is needed to produce ACCELERATION, not to maintain velocity.
```

### Q B2
Define impulse. State and explain the impulse-momentum theorem with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  IMPULSE  =  the product of a force and the time for which it acts.

              J  =  F t

  IMPULSE-MOMENTUM THEOREM

      The impulse of a force equals the change in momentum it produces.

              F t   =   m v   -   m u

  PROOF
                        v - u                       m v  -  m u
      F = m a  =  m  x  -------      so     F  =  ---------------
                          t                              t

      Therefore    F t  =  m v  -  m u

  For a varying force, the impulse is the AREA under the F-t graph:

     F ^
       |        ____
       |      /      \
       |    /  AREA   \
       |  /            \
       +---------------------> t

  APPLICATIONS
  1.  A cricketer pulls his hands back  -> longer time -> smaller force.
  2.  We jump onto sand, not concrete   -> longer time -> smaller force.
  3.  Cars have crumple zones/airbags   -> longer time -> smaller force.
  4.  A karate blow is very brief       -> shorter time -> HUGE force.
```

### Q B3  ***ASKED VERY OFTEN***
State the law of conservation of linear momentum and derive it from Newton's
third law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT
  If the net external force acting on a system is zero, the total linear
  momentum of the system remains constant.

  DERIVATION

  Two bodies of masses m1 and m2 move along the same line with velocities
  u1 and u2 (u1 > u2). They collide and afterwards move with v1 and v2.

     BEFORE                 DURING                   AFTER

  +----+   +----+       +----+ +----+           +----+     +----+
  | m1 |->u1| m2|->u2   | m1 |>| m2 |           | m1 |->v1 | m2 |->v2
  +----+   +----+       +----+ +----+           +----+     +----+
                        F12 -> <- F21

  STEP 1   By the THIRD law, the forces during contact are equal and
           opposite:
                          F_12  =  -  F_21

  STEP 2   By the SECOND law, applied to each body for contact time t:

                   m1 v1  -  m1 u1                m2 v2  -  m2 u2
        F_21  =  -------------------  ,  F_12 = -------------------
                          t                              t

  STEP 3   Substitute in F_12 = - F_21 :

              m2 v2 - m2 u2              m1 v1 - m1 u1
             ---------------  =   -    -----------------
                    t                          t

  STEP 4   Cancel t and rearrange:

              m2 v2  -  m2 u2   =   -  m1 v1  +  m1 u1

              +--------------------------------------+
              |   m1 u1 + m2 u2  =  m1 v1 + m2 v2    |
              +--------------------------------------+

           TOTAL MOMENTUM BEFORE  =  TOTAL MOMENTUM AFTER

  APPLICATIONS
  Recoil of a gun, rocket propulsion, explosion of a bomb, a man jumping
  from a boat.
```

### Q B4
Explain the recoil of a gun. A gun of mass 4 kg fires a 20 g bullet at 200 m/s.
Find the recoil velocity.

**ANSWER:**
```
        BEFORE FIRING                  AFTER FIRING

   +-------------------+          +-------------------+
   |      GUN  M       |          |    GUN  M         |
   |   +------------+  |          | <--V                 --> v
   |   |  bullet m  |  |          |                    (o) bullet m
   +-------------------+          +-------------------+

        total p = 0                 M V  +  m v  =  0

  EXPLANATION
  Before firing, gun and bullet are at rest, so the total momentum is zero.
  The explosion is an INTERNAL force, so the total momentum must stay zero.
  The bullet takes forward momentum m v, so the gun must take an equal
  backward momentum M V.

                                 m v
              M V + m v = 0  ->  V  =  -  -----
                                          M

  NUMERICAL
     M = 4 kg ,  m = 20 g = 0.02 kg ,  v = 200 m/s

               0.02  x  200          4
     V  =  -  ---------------  =  - ---  =  - 1 m/s
                    4                4

     The gun recoils at 1 m/s, opposite to the bullet.

  NOTE:  a gun is held tightly against the shoulder so that M effectively
         becomes the mass of gun + man, which makes V very small.
```

### Q B5  ***ASKED VERY OFTEN***
A body of mass m is placed in a lift. Find its apparent weight when the lift
is (a) at rest (b) moving up with acceleration a (c) moving down with
acceleration a (d) in free fall.

**ANSWER:**
```
  APPARENT WEIGHT = the normal reaction N from the floor = the reading on
  a weighing machine.

                 +---------------------+
                 |        LIFT         |
                 |         N ^         |
                 |      +---------+    |
                 |      |    m    |    |
                 |      +---------+    |
                 |           v  mg     |
                 +---------------------+


  (a) AT REST (or constant velocity),  a = 0

          N  -  mg  =  0        ->      N  =  m g

      Apparent weight = true weight.


  (b) ACCELERATING UPWARD with a

      The net force must point UP:

          N  -  mg  =  m a      ->      N  =  m ( g + a )

      The man feels HEAVIER.


  (c) ACCELERATING DOWNWARD with a

      The net force must point DOWN:

          mg  -  N  =  m a      ->      N  =  m ( g - a )

      The man feels LIGHTER.


  (d) FREE FALL  (cable snaps, a = g)

          N  =  m ( g - g )  =  0

      Apparent weight is ZERO -> WEIGHTLESSNESS.
      The true weight is unchanged; only the reaction has vanished.

  IMPORTANT:  what matters is the direction of the ACCELERATION,
              not the direction of the velocity.
```

### Q B6  ***ASKED VERY OFTEN***
State the laws of friction. Mention the advantages and disadvantages of
friction and the methods of reducing it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAWS OF STATIC / LIMITING FRICTION
  1.  Friction opposes the relative motion or the tendency of motion.
  2.  It acts tangentially along the surfaces in contact.
  3.  Limiting friction is proportional to the normal reaction:  f_L = mu_s N
  4.  It is INDEPENDENT of the AREA of contact.
  5.  It DEPENDS on the nature and roughness of the surfaces.

  LAWS OF KINETIC FRICTION
  1.  f_k = mu_k N
  2.  It is independent of the speed of the body.
  3.  It is independent of the area of contact.
  4.  It is always LESS than limiting friction:  mu_k < mu_s

  ADVANTAGES
  1.  Walking is possible.
  2.  Vehicles can move and can be braked.
  3.  Nails, screws and knots hold.
  4.  We can grip and hold objects.
  5.  Belt drives work.

  DISADVANTAGES
  1.  Wear and tear of machine parts and tyres.
  2.  Energy is wasted as heat, lowering efficiency.
  3.  Machines overheat.
  4.  Extra fuel is consumed.

  METHODS OF REDUCING FRICTION
  1.  Polishing the surfaces.
  2.  Lubricating with oil or grease.
  3.  Using ball bearings or roller bearings (rolling instead of sliding).
  4.  Streamlining vehicles, aircraft and ships.
  5.  Using an air cushion, as in a hovercraft.
```

### Q B7
Show that the angle of friction is equal to the angle of repose.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANGLE OF FRICTION (theta_f)

  The angle between the normal reaction and the resultant of the normal
  reaction and the limiting friction.

                    R
                     \     N
                      \    ^
                       \   |
                theta_f \  |
                         \ |
        <----------------- +
                 f_L

                              f_L        mu N
        tan( theta_f )  =   -------  =  ------  =  mu       .....(1)
                               N           N


  ANGLE OF REPOSE (theta_r)

  The minimum angle of an incline at which a body just begins to slide down.

                            N
                            ^
                      +-----+-----+
                     /|     m     |
                    /_+-----------+
                   / ^      |
                  /  f_L    v mg cos(theta_r)
                 /   and mg sin(theta_r) down the slope
                /  theta_r
               /_________________

        Perpendicular to the plane :   N  =  mg cos(theta_r)
        Along the plane, on the verge:  mg sin(theta_r)  =  f_L  =  mu N

        So   mg sin(theta_r)  =  mu  x  mg cos(theta_r)

             sin(theta_r)
            --------------  =  mu       ->    tan(theta_r) = mu   .....(2)
             cos(theta_r)


  From (1) and (2):

             tan( theta_f )   =   tan( theta_r )   =   mu

             +-------------------------------+
             |    theta_f    =    theta_r    |
             +-------------------------------+

  The angle of friction equals the angle of repose.
```

### Q B8
Derive the expression for the acceleration of a body sliding down a rough
inclined plane.

**ANSWER:**
```
                                  N
                                  ^
                            +-----+-----+
                           /|     m     |
                          /_+-----------+
                         / ^      |
                        /  f      |
                       /          v mg cos(theta)
                      /   and mg sin(theta) DOWN the slope
                     /  theta
                    /_________________________

  The body slides DOWN, so friction acts UP the slope.

  PERPENDICULAR to the plane (no motion):

          N   =   m g cos(theta)

  Therefore

          f   =   mu N   =   mu  m g cos(theta)

  ALONG the plane (the body accelerates):

          m g sin(theta)   -   f    =   m a

          m g sin(theta)  -  mu m g cos(theta)   =   m a

          +-------------------------------------------------+
          |   a  =  g ( sin(theta)  -  mu cos(theta) )      |
          +-------------------------------------------------+

  SPECIAL CASES
     mu = 0                    ->  a = g sin(theta)          (smooth)
     mu = tan(theta)           ->  a = 0  (verge of sliding, angle of repose)
     mu > tan(theta)           ->  the body does not slide at all
     Moving UP the slope       ->  a = g ( sin(theta) + mu cos(theta) )
```

### Q B9  ***ASKED VERY OFTEN***
What is banking of roads? Derive the expression for the angle of banking.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BANKING
  The outer edge of a curved road is raised above the inner edge. The angle
  the road surface makes with the horizontal is the ANGLE OF BANKING.
  Its purpose is to let a component of the NORMAL REACTION supply the
  centripetal force, so the turn is safe even without friction.

                                     N
                                      \
                        N cos(theta)   \
                              ^         \
                              |          \
                        +-----+-----------\
                        |       CAR        \
                        +--O-----------O----\
                       /       |             \
                      /        v  mg          \
                     /  theta                  \
                    /_____________________________\

              N sin(theta)  points horizontally, towards the centre

  VERTICAL DIRECTION (no vertical acceleration):

          N cos(theta)  =  m g                             .....(1)

  HORIZONTAL DIRECTION (this is the centripetal force):

                            m v^2
          N sin(theta)  =  --------                        .....(2)
                              r

  DIVIDING (2) by (1):

           N sin(theta)          m v^2  /  r
          --------------   =   ----------------
           N cos(theta)              m g

               +-------------------------------------+
               |                       v^2           |
               |    tan( theta )  =  ---------       |
               |                       r g           |
               +-------------------------------------+

  OPTIMUM SPEED:      v  =  sqrt( r g tan(theta) )

  CONCLUSIONS
  1.  The banking angle does NOT depend on the mass of the vehicle.
  2.  At this speed no friction at all is needed.
  3.  For railways the outer rail is raised; this is called SUPERELEVATION.
```

### Q B10
Derive the maximum speed at which a car can take a turn on a level (unbanked)
road.

**ANSWER:**
```
                              N
                              ^
                     +------------------+
                     |       CAR        |
                     +--O----------O----+
             f <--------+----------+
                        |
                        v  mg

  VERTICAL:      N  =  m g

  The only horizontal force available is FRICTION, and it must provide the
  centripetal force:

                m v^2
          f  =  -------
                   r

  But friction cannot exceed its limiting value:

          f   <=   mu N   =   mu m g

  So
                m v^2
               -------    <=    mu m g
                   r

                  v^2    <=    mu g r

          +---------------------------------------+
          |     v_max  =  sqrt(  mu  g  r  )      |
          +---------------------------------------+

  NOTES
  1.  Independent of the MASS of the vehicle.
  2.  On a wet or oily road mu drops, so v_max drops and the car skids.
  3.  This is why sharp turns must be taken slowly, and why roads are
      banked at fast curves.
```

Two blocks of masses m1 and m2 lie on a smooth horizontal surface, connected
by a light string. A force F is applied to m1. Find the acceleration and the
tension.

**ANSWER:**
```
                     T          T
              +-----+   ______   +-----+
              | m2  |==========  | m1  | <--- F
              +-----+            +-----+
             ///////////////////////////////

  FREE BODY DIAGRAMS

     BLOCK m1                            BLOCK m2

        N1 ^                                N2 ^
           |                                   |
      +---------+                         +---------+

     F  -  T  =  m1 a   ...(1)          T  =  m2 a   ...(2)

           F   =   ( m1  +  m2 ) a

                         m1 + m2

```

### Q B13
A 0.4 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s normally and rebounds at 8 m/s. If the
contact lasts 0.02 s, find the impulse and the average force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Take the direction TOWARDS the wall as POSITIVE.

     u  =  + 10 m/s          v  =  - 8 m/s   (it REBOUNDS)

  IMPULSE

     J  =  m v  -  m u  =  0.4 (-8)  -  0.4 (+10)

        =  - 3.2  -  4.0   =   - 7.2 N s

     Magnitude = 7.2 N s, directed AWAY from the wall.

  AVERAGE FORCE

            J        7.2
     F  =  ---  =  ------  =  360 N
            t       0.02

  NOTE: because the ball REBOUNDS, the two momenta ADD in magnitude.
        Using 0.4(10 - 8) = 0.8 would be WRONG.
```

### Q B14
Explain why a horse pulling a cart moves forward, even though the cart pulls
the horse back with an equal force.

**ANSWER:**
```
    +---------+                  +-----------+
    |  HORSE  |  --- F -------->  |   CART   |
    |         |  <------- F ---   |          |
    +---------+                  +-----------+

  1.  The two forces are equal and opposite, but they act on TWO DIFFERENT
      BODIES, so they do NOT cancel.

  2.  To see whether the CART moves, look only at the forces on the CART:
      the forward pull F from the horse, minus the friction on the cart.
      If F is greater than that friction, the cart accelerates forward.

  3.  To see whether the HORSE moves, look at the forces on the HORSE:
      the backward pull F from the cart, and the forward reaction of the
      GROUND on the horse's hooves. The horse pushes the ground backward,
      and the ground pushes the horse forward.

  4.  The system moves forward when the ground's forward reaction exceeds
      the total backward friction.

  CONCLUSION: motion is possible because action and reaction act on
  different bodies, and because the GROUND supplies an extra force.
```

### Q C1  ***THE MOST ASKED 8-MARKER IN THIS CHAPTER***
Two bodies of masses m1 and m2 (m1 > m2) are connected by a light
inextensible string passing over a light frictionless pulley. Derive
expressions for the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.
Also find the force on the pulley.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SET-UP

                       _____
                      /     \
                     |   O   |   light, frictionless pulley
                      \_____/
                       |   |
                       |   |
                    +--+   +--+
                    |m1|   |m2|
                    +--+   +--+
                      |      |
                      v      v
                    m1 g   m2 g

  ASSUMPTIONS
  1.  The string is light (massless) and inextensible.
  2.  The pulley is light and frictionless.
  3.  Therefore the TENSION is the SAME throughout the string, and both
      bodies have accelerations of the SAME MAGNITUDE a.

  Since m1 > m2 :   m1 moves DOWN and m2 moves UP.


  FREE BODY DIAGRAM OF m1  (moving DOWN)

              T
              ^
              |
          +-------+
          |  m1   |   |
          +-------+   | a
              |       v
              v  m1 g

          m1 g   -   T   =   m1 a            .....(1)


  FREE BODY DIAGRAM OF m2  (moving UP)

              T
              ^
              |
          +-------+   ^
          |  m2   |   | a
          +-------+   |
              |
              v  m2 g

          T   -   m2 g   =   m2 a            .....(2)


  STEP 1 — FIND THE ACCELERATION

  Add (1) and (2). The tension T cancels:

       m1 g  -  T  +  T  -  m2 g   =   m1 a  +  m2 a

       ( m1  -  m2 ) g   =   ( m1  +  m2 ) a

            +-------------------------------------+
            |                ( m1  -  m2 ) g      |
            |      a   =   -------------------    |
            |                  m1  +  m2          |
            +-------------------------------------+


  STEP 2 — FIND THE TENSION

  From (2):     T  =  m2 ( g  +  a )

                                 +-                 -+
                                 |   ( m1 - m2 ) g   |
       T  =  m2 g   +   m2   x   |  ---------------- |
                                 |     m1  +  m2     |
                                 +-                 -+

              m2 g ( m1 + m2 )   +   m2 g ( m1 - m2 )
         =  -------------------------------------------
                          m1  +  m2

              m1 m2 g + m2^2 g + m1 m2 g - m2^2 g
         =  --------------------------------------
                          m1  +  m2

            +-------------------------------------+
            |                  2 m1 m2 g          |
            |      T   =   ------------------     |
            |                  m1  +  m2          |
            +-------------------------------------+


  STEP 3 — FORCE ON THE PULLEY

  Both sides of the string pull DOWN on the pulley with tension T:

                                          4 m1 m2 g
       Force on the pulley  =   2 T   =   -----------
                                           m1  +  m2


  DISCUSSION / SPECIAL CASES

  1.  If m1 = m2 :   a = 0 and T = m1 g. The system stays in equilibrium.

  2.  If m2 = 0 :    a = g and T = 0. Free fall, as expected.

  3.  Always      m2 g   <   T   <   m1 g.
      Use this to CHECK your numerical answer.

  4.  The force on the pulley (2T) is LESS than (m1 + m2) g, because the
      system is accelerating.


  NUMERICAL CHECK:   m1 = 5 kg , m2 = 3 kg , g = 10 m/s^2

         (5 - 3)(10)                  2 (5)(3)(10)
   a  =  ------------ = 2.5 m/s^2 ,  T = ------------ = 37.5 N
            5 + 3                            5 + 3

   Check: 30 N < 37.5 N < 50 N.  CORRECT.
```

### Q C2
Explain the concept of a free body diagram. Using it, discuss the motion of
a body placed on a smooth inclined plane and on a rough inclined plane.
Derive the acceleration in each case and define the angle of repose.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PART A — FREE BODY DIAGRAM

  A free body diagram is a sketch of ONE chosen body alone, showing EVERY
  force that acts on it, with everything else in the problem removed.

  RULES FOR DRAWING FORCES
    Weight  mg  -  always straight DOWN.
    Normal  N   -  always PERPENDICULAR to the surface, pushing away.
    Tension T   -  along the string, always PULLING the body.
    Friction f  -  along the surface, OPPOSING relative sliding.

  THE METHOD
    1. Isolate one body.
    2. Draw all forces on it.
    3. Choose x along the acceleration, y perpendicular to it.
    4. Resolve slanted forces.
    5. Along the motion:            sum of F = m a
       Perpendicular to the motion: sum of F = 0
    6. Solve.


  PART B — SMOOTH INCLINED PLANE

                                  N
                                  ^
                            +-----+-----+
                           /|     m     |
                          / +-----------+
                         /       |      \
                        /        |       \  mg sin(theta)
                       /         v        v
                      /    mg cos(theta)
                     /  theta
                    /_________________________

  Resolve the weight mg into
       mg sin(theta)   ALONG the slope (down)
       mg cos(theta)   PERPENDICULAR to the slope

  PERPENDICULAR (no motion):        N  =  m g cos(theta)

  ALONG the slope:                  m g sin(theta)  =  m a

            +---------------------------------+
            |     a   =   g  sin( theta )     |
            +---------------------------------+

  The acceleration is INDEPENDENT of the mass.
     theta = 0   ->  a = 0    (flat ground)
     theta = 90  ->  a = g    (free fall)

  Velocity at the bottom of a slope of length L:

         v  =  sqrt( 2 g L sin(theta) )  =  sqrt( 2 g h )


  PART C — ROUGH INCLINED PLANE, BODY SLIDING DOWN

                                  N
                                  ^
                            +-----+-----+
                           /|     m     |
                          /_+-----------+
                         / ^      |
                        /  f      v mg cos(theta)
                       /   and mg sin(theta) down the slope
                      /  theta
                     /_________________________

  Friction acts UP the slope, opposing the downward sliding.

  PERPENDICULAR:        N  =  m g cos(theta)
  So                    f  =  mu N  =  mu m g cos(theta)

  ALONG the slope:      m g sin(theta)  -  mu m g cos(theta)  =  m a

            +-----------------------------------------------+
            |   a  =  g ( sin(theta)  -  mu cos(theta) )    |
            +-----------------------------------------------+

  If the body is pushed UP the slope, friction reverses and

            a  =  g ( sin(theta)  +  mu cos(theta) )     (retardation)


  PART D — ANGLE OF REPOSE

  The MINIMUM angle of the incline at which the body JUST begins to slide
  down on its own.

  At that angle the acceleration is zero, so

         g ( sin(theta_r)  -  mu cos(theta_r) )  =  0

         sin(theta_r)  =  mu  cos(theta_r)

            +-----------------------------+
            |    mu  =  tan( theta_r )    |
            +-----------------------------+

  Since the angle of friction also satisfies tan(theta_f) = mu,

            theta_f   =   theta_r

  If theta < theta_r  ->  the body stays at rest and the friction is
                          STATIC, equal to mg sin(theta), NOT mu N.
```

### Q C3
Define friction. Describe the different types of friction, draw the graph
between applied force and friction, state the laws of friction, and explain
the angle of friction and the angle of repose.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
  Friction is the force that opposes the relative motion, or the tendency of
  relative motion, between two surfaces in contact. It acts TANGENTIALLY
  along the surfaces.

  CAUSE
  No surface is perfectly smooth. Microscopic hills and valleys interlock
  and form tiny cold welds. Sliding means breaking these joints.

     ___/\____/\_____/\___/\____    body
        \/    \/     \/   \/
        /\    /\     /\   /\
     ___\/____\/_____\/___\/____    ground


  TYPES OF FRICTION

  1.  STATIC FRICTION  f_s
      Acts when the body has not yet started moving. It is SELF-ADJUSTING:
      it grows to exactly match the applied force.
                            f_s  <=  mu_s N

  2.  LIMITING FRICTION  f_L
      The maximum value of static friction, reached just before the body
      begins to slide.
                            f_L  =  mu_s N

  3.  KINETIC (DYNAMIC) FRICTION  f_k
      Acts once the body is sliding. It is nearly constant and does not
      depend on speed.
                            f_k  =  mu_k N        and    f_k  <  f_L

      Kinetic friction has two sub-types:
        (a) SLIDING friction  - one surface slides over another.
        (b) ROLLING friction  - a body rolls; much smaller, because
            contact is momentary and almost at a point.


  GRAPH BETWEEN APPLIED FORCE AND FRICTION

   Friction
   force f
      ^
      |                f_L
      |                 *
      |               / |
      |             /   |
      |           /     +-----------------------  f_k  (constant)
      |         /       |
      |       /         |
      |     /  f_s = F  |     body SLIDES
      |   /  (45 degree |
      | /     line)     |
      +-----------------+-------------------------> applied force F
      0     STATIC      |     KINETIC

  Until the body moves, friction rises along a 45-degree line because it
  equals the applied force. At f_L the body starts to move and the friction
  DROPS suddenly to the constant kinetic value f_k.


  LAWS OF FRICTION

  Static / limiting friction:
    1.  It opposes the relative motion or its tendency.
    2.  It acts tangentially to the surfaces.
    3.  f_L  =  mu_s N   -- proportional to the normal reaction.
    4.  It is INDEPENDENT of the AREA of contact.
    5.  It DEPENDS on the nature and roughness of the surfaces.

  Kinetic friction:
    1.  f_k  =  mu_k N
    2.  Independent of the SPEED.
    3.  Independent of the AREA of contact.
    4.  Always less than limiting friction:  mu_k < mu_s


  COEFFICIENT OF FRICTION

                     f
           mu  =   -----      DIMENSIONLESS, NO UNIT
                     N


  ANGLE OF FRICTION (theta_f)

  The angle between the normal reaction and the resultant of the normal
  reaction and the limiting friction.

                    R
                     \     N
                      \    ^
                       \   |
                theta_f \  |
                         \ |
        <----------------- +
                 f_L

                             f_L
        tan( theta_f )  =  -------  =  mu
                              N


  ANGLE OF REPOSE (theta_r)

  The minimum angle of an inclined plane at which a body just begins to
  slide down.

        mg sin(theta_r)  =  mu N  =  mu mg cos(theta_r)

        tan( theta_r )  =  mu

  THEREFORE      theta_f  =  theta_r


  ADVANTAGES: walking, driving, braking, gripping, nails and knots hold,
              belt drives work.
  DISADVANTAGES: wear and tear, energy wasted as heat, overheating,
              extra fuel needed.
  REDUCING FRICTION: polishing, lubrication, ball bearings, streamlining,
              air cushion.

  Friction is called a NECESSARY EVIL for exactly these reasons.
```

### Q C4
What is centripetal force? Derive the maximum safe speed of a vehicle on
(a) a level circular road and (b) a banked circular road. Explain why a
cyclist bends inwards while turning.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CENTRIPETAL FORCE

  A body moving in a circle at constant speed still has a changing VELOCITY,
  because the DIRECTION changes. So it is accelerating, and a force is
  needed. That force points towards the CENTRE and is called the
  CENTRIPETAL FORCE.

                   m v^2
           F  =   --------   =   m r omega^2
                      r

  It does NO WORK, because it is always perpendicular to the velocity.
  It is not a new kind of force; a real force always does the job
  (tension, friction, gravity, or a normal reaction).


  (a) VEHICLE ON A LEVEL CIRCULAR ROAD

                              N
                              ^
                     +------------------+
                     |       CAR        |
                     +--O----------O----+
             f <--------+----------+
                        |
                        v  mg

  VERTICAL:       N  =  m g

  The only horizontal force is FRICTION, which must supply the
  centripetal force:

                m v^2
          f  =  -------            with        f  <=  mu N  =  mu m g
                   r

                m v^2
               -------    <=    mu m g
                   r

                  v^2    <=    mu g r

          +---------------------------------------+
          |     v_max   =   sqrt( mu  g  r )      |
          +---------------------------------------+

  Independent of MASS. If the road is wet, mu falls and so does v_max.
  This is why unbanked turns are dangerous at speed.


  (b) VEHICLE ON A BANKED ROAD (frictionless case)

                                     N
                                      \
                        N cos(theta)   \
                              ^         \
                              |          \
                        +-----+-----------\
                        |       CAR        \
                        +--O-----------O----\
                       /       |             \
                      /        v  mg          \
                     /  theta                  \
                    /_____________________________\

  The normal reaction N is perpendicular to the TILTED road, so it has
  a horizontal component pointing towards the centre.

  VERTICAL:         N cos(theta)  =  m g                    .....(1)

                                     m v^2
  HORIZONTAL:       N sin(theta)  =  -------                .....(2)
                                        r

  Divide (2) by (1):

               +---------------------------------+
               |                    v^2          |
               |   tan( theta )  =  -------      |
               |                     r g         |
               +---------------------------------+

  OPTIMUM SPEED       v  =  sqrt( r g tan(theta) )

  At this exact speed NO friction is needed.
  The angle does NOT depend on the mass of the vehicle.

  WITH friction also acting, the maximum speed becomes

                          +-                        -+
                          |  mu  +  tan(theta)       |
       v_max = sqrt   rg  | ----------------------   |
                          |  1  -  mu tan(theta)     |
                          +-                        -+

  Putting theta = 0 gives back  v_max = sqrt( mu g r ).


  (c) BENDING OF A CYCLIST

                              R
                                \
                     R cos(th)   \
                          ^       \  o
                          |        \/|\
                          |        /\ \
              <-----------+---------(  )
              R sin(th)   |
                          v  mg
              //////////////////////////
                        theta = lean from the vertical

  If the cyclist stayed upright, the only forces would be vertical, and
  there would be nothing to supply the centripetal force. So he LEANS IN.

  VERTICAL:         R cos(theta)  =  m g

                                     m v^2
  HORIZONTAL:       R sin(theta)  =  -------
                                        r

  Dividing:
                    +---------------------------+
                    |                  v^2      |
                    |  tan( theta ) = -------   |
                    |                  r g      |
                    +---------------------------+

  The greater the speed, or the sharper the turn, the more he must lean.
  For safety we also need tan(theta) <= mu, i.e. v <= sqrt(mu g r).
```

### Q C5
State Newton's three laws of motion. Show that Newton's second law is the
real law of motion and that the first and third laws follow from it.
Explain the law of conservation of momentum as a consequence.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE THREE LAWS

  FIRST LAW
  Every body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion in a
  straight line unless compelled by an external force to change that state.

  SECOND LAW
  The rate of change of momentum of a body is directly proportional to the
  applied force and takes place in the direction of the force.

               dp
      F  =    ----   =  m a          (for constant mass)
               dt

  THIRD LAW
  To every action there is an equal and opposite reaction, and the two act
  on TWO DIFFERENT BODIES.


  PART A — THE FIRST LAW FOLLOWS FROM THE SECOND

      Put  F = 0  in the second law:

               dp
              ----  =  0        ->      p  =  constant
               dt

              m v  =  constant  ->      v  =  constant

      So with no force, the body stays at rest or keeps moving with the
      same velocity in a straight line. That IS the first law.


  PART B — THE THIRD LAW FOLLOWS FROM THE SECOND

      Consider two bodies A and B interacting, with NO external force on
      the pair. Then for the SYSTEM

               d ( p_A  +  p_B )
              --------------------   =   F_external   =   0
                      dt

               d p_A         d p_B
              -------   =  -  -------
                dt             dt

                F_A     =   -   F_B

      The force on A due to B is equal and opposite to the force on B due
      to A. That IS the third law.


  PART C — CONSERVATION OF MOMENTUM

      From Part B, for an isolated system

               d ( p_A  +  p_B )
              --------------------   =   0
                      dt

               p_A  +  p_B   =   CONSTANT

               m1 u1  +  m2 u2   =   m1 v1  +  m2 v2

      STATEMENT: when the net external force on a system is zero, the
      total linear momentum of the system remains constant.


  PART D — WHY THE SECOND LAW IS THE REAL LAW

  1.  It gives a QUANTITATIVE measure of force; the other two are
      qualitative.
  2.  The first law is the special case F = 0.
  3.  The third law follows from applying the second law to a system.
  4.  It works even when the mass changes (rockets), through F = dp/dt.


  PART E — APPLICATIONS OF MOMENTUM CONSERVATION

  1.  RECOIL OF A GUN

         M V  +  m v  =  0     ->     V  =  -  m v / M

      The gun moves back; because M >> m, V is small.

  2.  ROCKET PROPULSION

         Gases of mass m are thrown down at speed v, so the rocket of
         mass M rises with  M V = m v.  As fuel burns, M falls and V
         rises. This is the only way to move in empty space.

  3.  EXPLOSION OF A STATIONARY BOMB

         Total momentum before = 0, so the vector sum of all fragment
         momenta after the explosion is also zero. Two equal fragments
         fly apart with equal speeds in opposite directions.

  4.  A MAN JUMPING FROM A BOAT

         The man moves forward, so the boat moves backward, keeping the
         total momentum zero.
```
