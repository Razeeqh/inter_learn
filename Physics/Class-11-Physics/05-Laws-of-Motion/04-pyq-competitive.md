# PATTERN 1 — Straight F = ma

### Q1
A force of 12 N acts on a body of mass 3 kg initially at rest. Its velocity
after 4 s is

(a) 8 m/s &nbsp; (b) 12 m/s &nbsp; (c) 16 m/s &nbsp; (d) 48 m/s

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
        F      12
   a = --- =  ----  =  4 m/s^2

        m       3

   v  =  u + a t  =  0  +  4 x 4  =  16 m/s
```

---

### Q2
A body of mass 2 kg moving at 10 m/s is stopped in 5 s. The retarding force is

(a) 2 N &nbsp; (b) 4 N &nbsp; (c) 8 N &nbsp; (d) 20 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
        m ( v - u )       2 ( 0 - 10 )
   F = -------------  =  --------------  =  - 4 N
             t                  5

   Magnitude = 4 N, opposite to the motion.
```

---

### Q3
Two forces of 6 N and 8 N act at right angles on a 5 kg body. Its acceleration is

(a) 1 m/s^2 &nbsp; (b) 2 m/s^2 &nbsp; (c) 2.8 m/s^2 &nbsp; (d) 14 m/s^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Resultant  =  sqrt( 6^2 + 8^2 )  =  sqrt( 36 + 64 )  =  10 N

              10
   a  =    -------  =  2 m/s^2
               5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Forces ADD AS VECTORS, never as plain numbers.
> 6 and 8 at right angles always give 10.

# PATTERN 2 — Momentum and impulse

### Q4
A 0.5 kg ball strikes a wall normally at 10 m/s and rebounds at 10 m/s. The
change in momentum is

(a) 0 &nbsp; (b) 5 kg m/s &nbsp; (c) 10 kg m/s &nbsp; (d) 20 kg m/s

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Take towards the wall as positive.

   Change  =  m v  -  m u  =  0.5 (-10)  -  0.5 (+10)

           =  - 5  -  5  =  - 10 kg m/s

   Magnitude = 10 kg m/s
```
> **SHORTCUT:** For a body that REBOUNDS with the same speed,
> the change in momentum is `2 m v`. Never zero.

---

### Q5
A cricket ball of mass 150 g moving at 20 m/s is caught in 0.1 s. The average
force on the hands is

(a) 3 N &nbsp; (b) 30 N &nbsp; (c) 300 N &nbsp; (d) 3000 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
        m ( v - u )      0.15 ( 0 - 20 )        - 3
   F = -------------  = -----------------  =  ------  =  - 30 N
             t                  0.1             0.1
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Convert grams to kg FIRST. 150 g = 0.15 kg.

---

### Q6
The area under a force-time graph gives

(a) work &nbsp; (b) power &nbsp; (c) impulse &nbsp; (d) acceleration

**ANSWER: (c)** — impulse, which equals the change in momentum.

---

### Q7
Two bodies of masses 1 kg and 4 kg have the same kinetic energy. The ratio of
their momenta is

(a) 1 : 2 &nbsp; (b) 1 : 4 &nbsp; (c) 2 : 1 &nbsp; (d) 4 : 1

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   p  =  sqrt( 2 m KE )      so with the same KE,   p  is prop. to sqrt(m)

   p1 : p2  =  sqrt(1) : sqrt(4)  =  1 : 2
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Same KE -> `p` goes as `sqrt(m)`.
> Same `p` -> KE goes as `1/m`.

---

### Q8
A machine gun fires 10 bullets per second, each of mass 20 g, with a speed of
500 m/s. The force needed to hold the gun is

(a) 50 N &nbsp; (b) 100 N &nbsp; (c) 200 N &nbsp; (d) 1000 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Momentum given to bullets per second

   = n m v  =  10  x  0.02  x  500  =  100 kg m/s per second

   F  =  100 N
```
> **SHORTCUT:** "n bullets per second" -> `F = n m v`.

# PATTERN 3 — Conservation of momentum

### Q9
A gun of mass 10 kg fires a bullet of mass 20 g with a velocity of 500 m/s.
The recoil velocity of the gun is

(a) 0.5 m/s &nbsp; (b) 1 m/s &nbsp; (c) 5 m/s &nbsp; (d) 10 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
             m v         0.02 x 500          10
   V  =  -  -----  =  -  -----------  =  -  ----  =  - 1 m/s
               M             10              10
```

---

### Q10
A shell at rest explodes into two fragments of masses 2 kg and 3 kg. If the
2 kg piece moves at 15 m/s, the speed of the 3 kg piece is

(a) 5 m/s &nbsp; (b) 10 m/s &nbsp; (c) 15 m/s &nbsp; (d) 22.5 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Total momentum before  =  0

   2 (15)  +  3 v  =  0

              30
   v  =  -  ------  =  - 10 m/s      (opposite direction)
               3
```

---

### Q11
A 60 kg man jumps out of a 240 kg boat at rest with a horizontal velocity of
2 m/s. The velocity of the boat is

(a) 0.5 m/s &nbsp; (b) 1 m/s &nbsp; (c) 2 m/s &nbsp; (d) 8 m/s

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   60 (2)  +  240 V  =  0

             120
   V  =  -  ------  =  - 0.5 m/s     (backward)
             240
```

---

### Q12
Two bodies of masses 3 kg and 2 kg moving towards each other at 4 m/s and
6 m/s collide and stick together. Their common velocity is

(a) 0 &nbsp; (b) 1 m/s &nbsp; (c) 2 m/s &nbsp; (d) 5 m/s

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Take rightward as positive.

   3 (+4)  +  2 (-6)  =  ( 3 + 2 ) v

   12  -  12  =  5 v

   v  =  0        The pair comes to a complete stop.
```

# PATTERN 4 — Body in a lift

### Q13
A man of mass 50 kg stands in a lift accelerating upward at 2 m/s^2. His
apparent weight is

(a) 400 N &nbsp; (b) 500 N &nbsp; (c) 600 N &nbsp; (d) 900 N

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   N  =  m ( g + a )  =  50 ( 10 + 2 )  =  50 x 12  =  600 N
```

---

### Q14
A lift is descending with an acceleration of 3 m/s^2. A 40 kg person inside
feels an apparent weight of

(a) 120 N &nbsp; (b) 280 N &nbsp; (c) 400 N &nbsp; (d) 520 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   N  =  m ( g - a )  =  40 ( 10 - 3 )  =  40 x 7  =  280 N
```

---

### Q15
A spring balance in a freely falling lift reads

(a) mg &nbsp; (b) 2mg &nbsp; (c) mg/2 &nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   Free fall means a = g , so    N  =  m ( g - g )  =  0

   This is WEIGHTLESSNESS. The true weight is unchanged.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Lift questions: `N = m(g + a)` if the ACCELERATION is up,
> `N = m(g - a)` if the ACCELERATION is down. Ignore the velocity completely.

---

### Q16
A lift moving DOWNWARD is slowing down at 2 m/s^2. A 60 kg man inside feels

(a) lighter &nbsp; (b) heavier &nbsp; (c) normal &nbsp; (d) weightless

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   Moving down and SLOWING DOWN means the acceleration points UP.

   N  =  m ( g + a )  =  60 x 12  =  720 N   >   600 N

   He feels HEAVIER.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** This trap appears every year. Decide by the ACCELERATION,
> not by the direction of travel.

# PATTERN 5 — Connected blocks and pulleys

### Q17
Blocks of 4 kg and 6 kg on a smooth floor are joined by a string. A force of
20 N pulls the 6 kg block. The tension in the string is

(a) 4 N &nbsp; (b) 8 N &nbsp; (c) 12 N &nbsp; (d) 20 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
              20
   a  =   ---------  =  2 m/s^2
           4  +  6

   T  =  (mass on the FAR side) x a  =  4 x 2  =  8 N
```
> **SHORTCUT:** `T = (mass BEHIND the string) x a`. Never F, never F/2.

---

### Q18
Two masses of 8 kg and 2 kg hang over a smooth pulley. The acceleration of the
system is

(a) 2 m/s^2 &nbsp; (b) 4 m/s^2 &nbsp; (c) 6 m/s^2 &nbsp; (d) 8 m/s^2

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
         ( m1 - m2 ) g       ( 8 - 2 ) 10        60
   a  =  --------------  =  --------------  =  ------  =  6 m/s^2
            m1 + m2             8 + 2            10
```

---

### Q19
For the same system (8 kg and 2 kg over a pulley), the tension is

(a) 16 N &nbsp; (b) 32 N &nbsp; (c) 60 N &nbsp; (d) 80 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
         2 m1 m2 g        2 (8)(2)(10)        320
   T  =  ----------  =  ---------------  =  -------  =  32 N
          m1 + m2            8 + 2             10

   CHECK:  m2 g = 20 N  <  32 N  <  80 N = m1 g.  CORRECT.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Always check that `m2 g < T < m1 g`. If not, you slipped.

---

### Q20
Two equal masses hang over a frictionless pulley. The tension in the string is

(a) zero &nbsp; (b) mg/2 &nbsp; (c) mg &nbsp; (d) 2mg

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   m1 = m2 = m   ->   a = 0 , and each side simply hangs:  T = m g
```

---

### Q21
A 5 kg block on a smooth table is connected over a pulley at the table edge to
a hanging 5 kg block. The acceleration of the system is

(a) 2.5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (b) 5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (c) 7.5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (d) 10 m/s^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
              +-----+       ___
              | 5kg |======(   )
              +-----+       | |
           //////////     +--+--+
                          | 5kg |
                          +-----+
                             v  50 N

   Only the HANGING weight drives the system, but BOTH masses must be
   accelerated:

           m2 g          5 x 10          50
   a  =  ---------  =  -----------  =  ------  =  5 m/s^2
          m1 + m2         5 + 5          10
```

# PATTERN 6 — Friction on a horizontal surface

### Q22
A block of mass 10 kg rests on a floor with mu = 0.5. The minimum horizontal
force needed to move it is

(a) 5 N &nbsp; (b) 20 N &nbsp; (c) 50 N &nbsp; (d) 100 N

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   N  =  m g  =  10 x 10  =  100 N

   f_L  =  mu N  =  0.5 x 100  =  50 N
```

---

### Q23
A body slides on a rough horizontal surface with mu = 0.2. Its retardation is

(a) 0.2 m/s^2 &nbsp; (b) 2 m/s^2 &nbsp; (c) 5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (d) 10 m/s^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
        mu m g
   a =  --------  =  mu g  =  0.2 x 10  =  2 m/s^2
           m
```
> **SHORTCUT:** On a rough flat floor with no applied force,
> retardation `= mu g`, INDEPENDENT of mass.

---

### Q24
A car moving at 20 m/s brakes and stops on a road with mu = 0.5. The stopping
distance is

(a) 20 m &nbsp; (b) 40 m &nbsp; (c) 60 m &nbsp; (d) 80 m

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
         v^2            400            400
   s = --------  =  ------------  =  -------  =  40 m
        2 mu g       2(0.5)(10)         10
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Stopping distance goes as `v^2`. Double the speed,
> FOUR times the distance. This is why speed kills.

---

### Q25
The coefficient of friction has the dimensions

(a) [M L T^-2] &nbsp; (b) [M L^0 T^0] &nbsp; (c) dimensionless &nbsp; (d) [L T^-1]

**ANSWER: (c)** — it is a ratio of two forces, so it has no dimensions and no
unit.

### Q26
A block is pulled by a force F acting at an angle theta ABOVE the horizontal on
a rough floor. The normal reaction is

(a) mg &nbsp; (b) mg + F sin(theta) &nbsp; (c) mg - F sin(theta) &nbsp; (d) mg cos(theta)

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   The upward component F sin(theta) partly lifts the block,
   so the floor pushes back LESS:

        N  =  mg  -  F sin(theta)

   Friction  =  mu ( mg  -  F sin(theta) )  ->  it is REDUCED.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Pull UP at an angle -> N decreases -> easier.
> Push DOWN at an angle -> N increases -> harder. This is why we PULL a
> lawnmower and PUSH a wheelbarrow badly.

# PATTERN 7 — Inclined plane

### Q27
A block slides down a smooth incline of 30 deg. Its acceleration is

(a) 2.5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (b) 5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (c) 8.66 m/s^2 &nbsp; (d) 10 m/s^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   a  =  g sin(theta)  =  10 x sin(30)  =  10 x 0.5  =  5 m/s^2
```

---

### Q28
A body just begins to slide down an incline when the angle is 30 deg. The
coefficient of friction is

(a) 0.5 &nbsp; (b) 0.577 &nbsp; (c) 0.866 &nbsp; (d) 1.0

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   At the angle of repose:     mu  =  tan(theta_r)  =  tan(30)  =  0.577
```
> **SHORTCUT:** "Just begins to slide down" ALWAYS means `mu = tan(theta)`.

---

### Q29
A block slides down a rough incline of 45 deg with mu = 0.5. Its acceleration is

(a) 2.5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (b) 3.53 m/s^2 &nbsp; (c) 5 m/s^2 &nbsp; (d) 7.07 m/s^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   a  =  g ( sin 45  -  mu cos 45 )

      =  10 ( 0.707  -  0.5 x 0.707 )

      =  10 x 0.707 ( 1 - 0.5 )

      =  7.07 x 0.5   =   3.53 m/s^2
```

---

### Q30
The normal reaction on a body of mass m on an incline of angle theta is

(a) mg &nbsp; (b) mg sin(theta) &nbsp; (c) mg cos(theta) &nbsp; (d) mg tan(theta)

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   N  =  mg cos(theta)      ALWAYS on an incline.

   On an incline the normal reaction is LESS than the weight, which is why
   friction is weaker on a slope.
```

---

### Q31
A body slides from rest down a smooth incline of height h. Its speed at the
bottom is

(a) sqrt(g h) &nbsp; (b) sqrt(2 g h) &nbsp; (c) 2 g h &nbsp; (d) g h

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   v^2  =  2 ( g sin theta ) L    and    h  =  L sin(theta)

   v  =  sqrt( 2 g h )

   INDEPENDENT of the angle and of the mass. It depends only on the HEIGHT.
```

# PATTERN 8 — Circular motion

### Q32
The maximum speed at which a car can turn on a level road of radius 40 m with
mu = 0.4 is

(a) 8 m/s &nbsp; (b) 12.6 m/s &nbsp; (c) 16 m/s &nbsp; (d) 160 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   v_max  =  sqrt( mu g r )  =  sqrt( 0.4 x 10 x 40 )  =  sqrt(160)

          =  12.6 m/s
```

---

### Q33
A road of radius 100 m is banked for a speed of 20 m/s. The angle of banking is

(a) arctan(0.2) &nbsp; (b) arctan(0.4) &nbsp; (c) arctan(2) &nbsp; (d) arctan(4)

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
                 v^2         400
   tan(theta) = -------  =  -------  =  0.4
                  r g       100 x 10

   theta  =  arctan( 0.4 )  =  about 21.8 degrees
```

---

### Q34
The angle of banking of a road depends on

(a) the mass of the vehicle &nbsp; (b) speed and radius only
(c) mass and speed &nbsp; (d) mass, speed and radius

**ANSWER: (b)** — `tan(theta) = v^2 / (r g)` contains **no mass**.

### Q35
A stone of mass 0.5 kg is whirled in a horizontal circle of radius 2 m at
4 m/s. The tension in the string is

(a) 1 N &nbsp; (b) 2 N &nbsp; (c) 4 N &nbsp; (d) 8 N

**ANSWER: (c)**
```

   T = -------  =  -----------  =  ------  =  4 N

```

### Q36
A cyclist turning a corner of radius 20 m at 10 m/s must lean at an angle whose
tangent is

(a) 0.2 &nbsp; (b) 0.5 &nbsp; (c) 2.0 &nbsp; (d) 5.0

**ANSWER: (b)**

> **SHORTCUT:** Banked road and leaning cyclist use the SAME formula.

### Q37
Centripetal force does no work because

(a) it is small &nbsp; (b) it acts towards the centre
(c) it is perpendicular to the displacement &nbsp; (d) speed is constant

**ANSWER: (c)** — work needs a component of force along the displacement,

# THE FIVE ANSWERS THAT CONTAIN NO MASS

If your answer has an `m` in it and it is one of these, you have gone wrong.

```
  +-------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
  |  Acceleration on an incline   |  a  =  g sin(theta)               |
  |  Retardation from friction    |  a  =  mu g                       |
  |  Stopping distance            |  s  =  v^2 / (2 mu g)             |
  |  Max speed on a level turn    |  v  =  sqrt( mu g r )             |
  |  Banking angle / cyclist lean |  tan(theta)  =  v^2 / ( r g )     |
  +-------------------------------+-----------------------------------+
```
