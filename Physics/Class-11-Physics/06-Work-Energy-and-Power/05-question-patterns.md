# Work, Energy and Power — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — DEFINE WORK / STATE ITS UNIT AND DIMENSIONS         2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define work." / "Give the SI unit and dimensional formula
            of work."
  METHOD    1. Work is done when a force displaces a body.
            2. W = F s cos theta , where theta is the angle between the
               FORCE and the DISPLACEMENT.
            3. SI unit: joule. Dimensions: [ M L^2 T^-2 ]. Scalar.
  TRAP      Do not forget to say WHAT theta is. Half the mark sits there.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — POSITIVE, NEGATIVE AND ZERO WORK                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Give one example each of positive, negative and zero work."
            "When is the work done by a force zero?"
  METHOD    Everything follows from cos theta.
              theta < 90    ->  POSITIVE  (falling body, engine force)
              theta = 90    ->  ZERO      (coolie, centripetal force)
              theta > 90    ->  NEGATIVE  (friction, braking, gravity on
                                           a body thrown up)
            For zero work also mention F = 0 and s = 0.
  TRAP      Never write "no force acts" for the coolie. A force DOES act;
            it is simply perpendicular to the motion.
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — NUMERICAL: WORK BY A CONSTANT FORCE                 2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   A force, a distance and an angle are given.
  METHOD    1. Write W = F s cos theta.
            2. Substitute the value of cos theta
               (cos 0 = 1, cos 30 = 0.866, cos 60 = 0.5, cos 90 = 0,
                cos 180 = -1).
            3. Multiply and write the unit J.
  TRAP      theta is the angle between FORCE and DISPLACEMENT, not the
            angle of the slope.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — WORK FROM VECTORS (DOT PRODUCT)                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   F and s are given with i, j, k.
  METHOD    1. W = Fx sx + Fy sy + Fz sz.
            2. Multiply component by matching component and ADD.
            3. If the angle is asked, use cos theta = W / (|F| |s|).
  TRAP      Do NOT multiply i by j. Only like components multiply.
            Watch the minus signs on the components.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — WORK DONE BY A VARIABLE FORCE (AREA)                4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain how to find the work done by a variable force."
            A force-displacement GRAPH is printed in the paper.
  METHOD    1. Divide the displacement into small steps ds.
            2. Over a small step F is nearly constant, so dW = F ds.
            3. Add them all:  W = INTEGRAL F ds from s1 to s2.
            4. Conclude: WORK = AREA UNDER THE F - s GRAPH.
            5. If a graph is given, split it into rectangles and
               triangles and add the areas.
  TRAP      Area BELOW the axis is NEGATIVE work - subtract it.
            The area under a v - t graph is DISPLACEMENT, not work.
            Check the axis labels.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — INTEGRATE A GIVEN F(x)                            2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A force F = 3x^2 acts on a body. Find the work from
            x = 0 to x = 2."
  METHOD    1. W = INTEGRAL F dx between the given limits.
            2. Integrate the polynomial term by term.
            3. Substitute the upper limit, then the lower limit,
               and subtract.
  TRAP      Apply the limits in the order (upper) minus (lower).
            If F is LINEAR in x, you can skip the calculus and just
            use the triangle area.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — DEFINE KINETIC ENERGY / DERIVE (1/2) m v^2          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define kinetic energy and write its expression."
  METHOD    1. Energy possessed by a body BECAUSE OF ITS MOTION.
            2. K = (1/2) m v^2.
            3. If a derivation is asked: W = F s , F = m a ,
               v^2 = 0 + 2 a s , so W = (1/2) m v^2.
  TRAP      K is never negative.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — KINETIC ENERGY AND MOMENTUM RELATIONS               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "If the momentum is doubled, what happens to KE?"
            "Two bodies have the same momentum..."
  METHOD    Use  K = p^2 / 2m   and   p = sqrt(2 m K).
              Same p  ->  the LIGHTER body has MORE K.
              Same K  ->  the HEAVIER body has MORE p.
              p doubled  ->  K becomes 4 times.
              K becomes 9 times  ->  v becomes 3 times.
  TRAP      "Increased BY 300 percent" means the new value is FOUR times,
            not three times. Read the word BY.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — STATE AND PROVE THE WORK-ENERGY THEOREM           4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State and prove the work-energy theorem."
  METHOD    STATEMENT: net work done = change in kinetic energy,
                       W(net) = Kf - Ki.
            CONSTANT-FORCE PROOF (5 lines):
              1. v^2 = u^2 + 2 a s
              2. a s = (v^2 - u^2) / 2
              3. multiply by m
              4. m a = F , so the left side is W
              5. W = (1/2) m v^2 - (1/2) m u^2 = Kf - Ki
            VARIABLE-FORCE PROOF:
              dW = F ds = m (dv/dt) ds = m v dv , then integrate
              from u to v.
  NOTE      *** THE HIGHEST-FREQUENCY DERIVATION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
  TRAP      It is the NET work, of ALL forces. Final minus initial.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — WORK-ENERGY NUMERICAL (STOPPING FORCE/DISTANCE)    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A bullet stops after penetrating 10 cm ..."
            "A car of mass 1000 kg is brought to rest in 50 m ..."
  METHOD    1. Convert grams to kg and cm to m.
            2. Ki = (1/2) m u^2 , Kf = 0 (or (1/2) m v^2).
            3. W(net) = Kf - Ki  (this comes out NEGATIVE).
            4. The resisting force does W = - F s , so solve for F.
            SHORTCUT:   F = m u^2 / ( 2 s )
  TRAP      Forgetting the minus sign on the resisting work.
            Forgetting to convert cm to m.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — DEFINE POTENTIAL ENERGY / DERIVE mgh               2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define potential energy with examples."
  METHOD    1. Energy due to POSITION or CONFIGURATION.
            2. Gravitational: U = m g h , derived as the work done in
               lifting the body slowly through h against its weight.
            3. Examples: water behind a dam, a stretched bow,
               a wound spring.
  TRAP      h is measured from a chosen zero level. Say which level
            you have chosen.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — HOOKE'S LAW AND THE SPRING FORCE                   2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State Hooke's law." / "Why is there a minus sign in F = -kx?"
  METHOD    1. F = - k x.
            2. The minus sign shows the force is OPPOSITE to the
               displacement - it RESTORES the spring to its natural
               length.
            3. k has unit N/m and dimensions [ M T^-2 ].
  TRAP      x is measured from the NATURAL length, not from the support.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — DERIVE THE SPRING POTENTIAL ENERGY                 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Derive the expression for the energy stored in a
            stretched spring."
  METHOD    1. The applied force is F = + k x , which is NOT constant.
            2. So use the area under the F - x graph.
            3. The graph is a straight line through the origin, so the
               area is a triangle:  (1/2) x (x) x (k x).
            4. U = (1/2) k x^2 .
            5. Mention that integration gives the same result.
            DRAW THE TRIANGLE GRAPH - it carries marks.
  TRAP      Do not write U = (1/2) k x. The square is essential.
            The energy is the same for a stretch x and a compression x.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — SPRING NUMERICALS                                2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Energy stored when a spring is compressed by 5 cm."
            "Extra work to stretch it from x to 2x."
            "Maximum compression when a block hits a spring."
  METHOD    Energy stored : U = (1/2) k x^2 (convert cm to m FIRST).
            Extra work    : U2 - U1 .
            Block hitting : (1/2) m v^2 = (1/2) k x^2 , solve for x.
            Ratios        : energies at x, 2x, 3x are 1 : 4 : 9.
  TRAP      A spring cut in half has DOUBLE the spring constant.
            Springs in parallel: k adds. In series: 1/k adds.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — CONSERVATIVE vs NON-CONSERVATIVE FORCES          2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between conservative and non-conservative
            forces." / "What are the tests for a conservative force?"
  METHOD    THREE TESTS
              1. Work is INDEPENDENT of the path.
              2. Work in a CLOSED LOOP is ZERO.
              3. A potential energy can be defined,  F = - dU/dx .
            Examples: gravity, spring, electrostatic (conservative);
                      friction, air drag, viscosity (non-conservative).
            Explain WHY friction fails: going and returning both give
            - f d , so the round trip gives - 2 f d , not zero.
  TRAP      "Non-conservative" does NOT mean energy is destroyed.
            Only MECHANICAL energy is lost; total energy is conserved.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — CONSERVATION OF MECHANICAL ENERGY: FREE FALL       8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State and prove the law of conservation of mechanical
            energy in the case of a freely falling body."
  METHOD    1. State the law:  K + U = constant, when only conservative
               forces act.
            2. DRAW the diagram: point A at height h, point B after
               falling x, point C at the ground.
            3. AT A:  KE = 0 , PE = m g h , TOTAL = m g h
            4. AT B:  v1^2 = 2 g x , so KE = m g x ,
                      PE = m g (h - x) , TOTAL = m g h
            5. AT C:  v2^2 = 2 g h , so KE = m g h ,
                      PE = 0 , TOTAL = m g h
            6. Write the three-row TABLE and conclude.
            7. Draw the energy graph (PE falling, KE rising, sum flat).
  NOTE      *** THE SINGLE MOST-ASKED 8-MARK QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
  TRAP      You MUST state that air resistance is neglected.
            KE = PE happens exactly at height h/2.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — ENERGY-CONSERVATION NUMERICALS                   2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A body is dropped from 20 m. Find its speed at ..."
            "A pendulum released from horizontal ..."
            "A block slides down a smooth incline ..."
  METHOD    1. Choose a zero level (usually the lowest point).
            2. Write  Ki + Ui = Kf + Uf .
            3. Cancel m - it always cancels.
            4. Solve for v.
            STANDARD RESULTS
              dropped through h            :  v = sqrt( 2 g h )
              pendulum from horizontal     :  v = sqrt( 2 g L )
              smooth incline of height h   :  v = sqrt( 2 g h )
  TRAP      Only valid if the surface is SMOOTH and air resistance is
            ignored. If the word "rough" appears, use
            Ki + Ui = Kf + Uf + (friction loss).
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — ENERGY LOST TO FRICTION                            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A block slides down a ROUGH incline and reaches the bottom
            at 6 m/s. Find the energy lost."
  METHOD    1. Energy at the start  = m g h .
            2. Energy at the end    = (1/2) m v^2 .
            3. Loss = start - end.
            4. If the friction force is asked, divide the loss by the
               distance travelled.
  TRAP      The word "rough" is the whole clue. Do not apply plain
            energy conservation.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — VERTICAL CIRCLE: MINIMUM SPEEDS                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A stone is whirled in a vertical circle. Find the minimum
            speed at the highest / lowest point."
            "The body just completes the circle."
  METHOD    1. DRAW the circle with T and mg marked at the top and
               the bottom.
            2. AT THE TOP: T + mg = m v^2 / L . Put T = 0 (the string
               just goes slack) to get  v(top) = sqrt( g L ).
            3. Use energy conservation over the height 2L:
                 (1/2) v(bot)^2 = (1/2) v(top)^2 + g (2L)
               giving  v(bot) = sqrt( 5 g L ).
            4. AT THE BOTTOM: T - mg = m v^2 / L , giving T = 6 m g.
  TRAP      A STRING can only pull, so T cannot be negative - that is
            why T = 0 gives the minimum. A rigid ROD can push, so with
            a rod the minimum top speed is zero.
            T(bottom) - T(top) = 6 m g holds at ANY speed.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — DEFINE POWER / DERIVE P = F . v                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define power and distinguish average from instantaneous
            power." / "Show that P = F . v ."
  METHOD    1. Power = rate of doing work. Scalar. Unit watt.
            2. Average P = W / t ; instantaneous P = dW / dt .
            3. Proof: dW = F . ds , divide by dt , and ds/dt = v ,
               so P = F . v .
            4. Add: 1 hp = 746 W , dimensions [ M L^2 T^-3 ].
  TRAP      The kilowatt-hour is a unit of ENERGY, not of power.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — POWER NUMERICALS                                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A pump lifts 3000 litres per minute to 10 m ..."
            "A machine gun fires 60 bullets per minute ..."
            "A car moves at constant 20 m/s against 600 N ..."
  METHOD    PUMP / LIFT :  P = m g h / t . Convert "per minute" to
                           "per second" FIRST.
            MACHINE GUN :  P = (bullets per second) x (1/2) m v^2 .
            VEHICLE at constant speed : engine force = resistance,
                           so P = F v .
            VEHICLE accelerating : P = ( F(resist) + m a ) v .
            Convert to horsepower by dividing by 746 if asked.
  TRAP      1 litre of water = 1 kg. "Per minute" is not "per second".
            The mass of the car is usually a DISTRACTOR in constant-speed
            questions.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — ELASTIC vs INELASTIC COLLISIONS                  2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between elastic and inelastic collisions."
  METHOD    Draw the comparison table:
              momentum conserved in BOTH
              kinetic energy conserved ONLY in elastic
              e = 1 for elastic, e < 1 for inelastic, e = 0 for
              perfectly inelastic
              examples: atomic/molecular (elastic), clay on the floor
              (inelastic)
  TRAP      Do not write "energy is not conserved" for an inelastic
            collision. TOTAL energy is conserved; only KINETIC energy
            is lost.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — COEFFICIENT OF RESTITUTION                       2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define the coefficient of restitution."
            "A ball dropped from 5 m rebounds to 1.8 m. Find e."
  METHOD    1.  e = (relative velocity of separation)
                    / (relative velocity of approach)
                  = ( v2 - v1 ) / ( u1 - u2 )
            2.  For a ball on the floor:  e = sqrt( h1 / h ) .
            3.  Fraction of KE retained = e^2 .
            4.  After n bounces:  h(n) = h e^(2n) .
  TRAP      e has NO units and NO dimensions. State that.
            e can never exceed 1.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — 1-D ELASTIC COLLISION: FULL DERIVATION             8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Derive the expressions for the final velocities in a
            one-dimensional elastic collision and discuss the special
            cases."
  METHOD    1. DRAW before-and-after diagrams.
            2. Momentum:  m1(u1 - v1) = m2(v2 - u2)          ... (1)
            3. Kinetic energy: m1(u1^2 - v1^2) = m2(v2^2 - u2^2) ... (2)
            4. DIVIDE (2) by (1) to get  u1 - u2 = v2 - v1 .
               State it in words: approach speed = separation speed.
            5. Substitute back and solve for v1 and v2. BOX both.
            6. Discuss the three special cases (see Pattern 25).
  NOTE      *** THE SECOND MOST-ASKED 8-MARK QUESTION ***
  TRAP      Step 4 (dividing one equation by the other) is the key step.
            Practise it until it is automatic. Do not try to solve
            the quadratic directly.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — THE THREE SPECIAL CASES OF ELASTIC COLLISION     4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What happens when a light body strikes a heavy body at
            rest?" (or any of the three cases)
  METHOD    Always take the target at rest, u2 = 0.
            CASE 1  m1 = m2      :  v1 = 0 , v2 = u1
                                    THEY EXCHANGE VELOCITIES.
                                    Example: carrom, billiards.
            CASE 2  m1 >> m2     :  v1 = u1 , v2 = 2 u1
                                    The heavy body ploughs on; the light
                                    one flies off at twice the speed.
                                    Example: a truck hitting a football.
            CASE 3  m1 << m2     :  v1 = - u1 , v2 = 0
                                    The light body bounces straight back.
                                    Example: a ball bouncing off a wall.
  TRAP      Give the numerical result AND an everyday example for each
            case. Examiners award a mark for the example.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — PERFECTLY INELASTIC COLLISION AND KE LOSS        4 or 8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The bodies stick together after impact."
            "A bullet embeds itself in a block."
            "Show that kinetic energy is lost."
  METHOD    1. Momentum conservation:
                 v = ( m1 u1 + m2 u2 ) / ( m1 + m2 )
            2. Ki = (1/2) m1 u1^2 + (1/2) m2 u2^2
            3. Kf = (1/2) ( m1 + m2 ) v^2
            4. Loss = Ki - Kf
                    = [ m1 m2 / 2(m1 + m2) ] ( u1 - u2 )^2
            5. Note the loss is always POSITIVE (a square times
               positive masses), and is the MAXIMUM possible loss.
  TRAP      Do not try to conserve kinetic energy here. Only momentum.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — EXPLOSION / RECOIL                               2 or 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A body at rest explodes into two pieces ..."
            "A gun of mass M fires a bullet of mass m ..."
  METHOD    1. Total momentum BEFORE = 0.
            2. So  m1 v1  =  m2 v2  in opposite directions.
            3. For kinetic energies use K = p^2 / 2m ; since the momenta
               are equal, the LIGHTER piece carries MORE energy.
  TRAP      An explosion is the reverse of a perfectly inelastic
            collision. Kinetic energy INCREASES here (chemical energy
            is released), but momentum is still conserved.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — TWO-DIMENSIONAL COLLISIONS                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A body collides obliquely with a stationary body ..."
            Angles theta1 and theta2 are mentioned.
  METHOD    1. Resolve into x and y directions.
            2. ALONG X:  m1 u1 = m1 v1 cos theta1 + m2 v2 cos theta2
            3. ALONG Y:     0  = m1 v1 sin theta1 - m2 v2 sin theta2
            4. If ELASTIC, add the kinetic-energy equation.
            5. Special result: EQUAL masses, elastic, one at rest
               -> the two separate at 90 degrees.
  TRAP      You cannot add speeds directly. Conserve momentum
            SEPARATELY in each direction.
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — MASS-ENERGY EQUIVALENCE                          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write Einstein's mass-energy relation."
            "Find the energy equivalent of 1 g of matter."
  METHOD    1.  E = m c^2 , with c = 3 x 10^8 m/s.
            2.  Substitute the mass in KILOGRAMS.
            3.  1 g gives 9 x 10^13 J ; 1 kg gives 9 x 10^16 J.
            4.  Mention fission, fusion and the Sun as applications.
  TRAP      Convert grams to kilograms before substituting, and
            remember to SQUARE c.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — THE ENERGY GRAPH / IDENTIFY THE FORCE FROM U(x)   objective
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "A potential energy curve U(x) is shown. Where is the force
            zero / where is the equilibrium?"
  METHOD    1.  F = - dU / dx , that is, minus the SLOPE.
            2.  F = 0 wherever the slope is zero (a maximum or minimum
                of U).
            3.  A MINIMUM of U is STABLE equilibrium.
                A MAXIMUM of U is UNSTABLE equilibrium.
                A FLAT region is NEUTRAL equilibrium.
  TRAP      The force is minus the slope, not the value of U itself.
```
