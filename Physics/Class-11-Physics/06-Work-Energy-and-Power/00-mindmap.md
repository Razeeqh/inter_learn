## THE 60-SECOND VERSION

```
  1.  WORK is done only when a force MOVES something along its own direction.
          W = F s cos theta

  2.  ENERGY is the capacity to do work. Two kinds here:
          moving  -> kinetic     K = (1/2) m v^2
          stored  -> potential   U = m g h   or   U = (1/2) k x^2

  3.  WORK-ENERGY THEOREM: the net work done EQUALS the change in kinetic
      energy.        W(net) = Kf - Ki

  4.  If only conservative forces act, KE + PE never changes.
      This one line answers most 8-mark questions.

  5.  POWER is how FAST work is done.     P = W / t = F . v

  6.  In EVERY collision momentum is conserved.
      Kinetic energy is conserved ONLY in an ELASTIC collision.
```

That is the whole chapter. Everything else is detail hanging off these six lines.

The single most useful line on that list:

```
                    W(net)  =  Kf  -  Ki

  If the question gives you a FORCE and a DISTANCE and asks for SPEED,
  or gives you SPEEDS and asks for a FORCE or a DISTANCE,
  use the work-energy theorem. It is almost always faster than
  the equations of motion.
```

## IF YOU ONLY HAVE 2 DAYS

```
  DAY 1  (about 3 hours)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------
  Hour 1   W = F s cos theta.
           Do 10 tiny numericals with different theta values.
           Learn the three standard "zero work" examples by heart.

  Hour 2   Kinetic energy, and the WORK-ENERGY THEOREM.
           Write the constant-force derivation 3 times from memory.
           Learn K = p^2 / 2m .

  Hour 3   Potential energy  m g h , spring  (1/2) k x^2 , F = -k x .
           Conservative vs non-conservative table.


  DAY 2  (about 3 hours)
  ---------------------------------------------------------------
  Hour 4   CONSERVATION OF MECHANICAL ENERGY, free-fall proof.
           Write the top / middle / ground table until it is automatic.
           This alone is an 8-mark answer.

  Hour 5   Power: P = W/t , P = F v , watt, 1 hp = 746 W.
           Do 4 numericals (pump, lift, motor, car).
           Vertical circle: memorise the two speeds and T(b) - T(t) = 6mg.

  Hour 6   Collisions. Learn:
             - momentum always conserved
             - KE conserved only if elastic
             - e = (v2 - v1) / (u1 - u2)
             - the three special cases
             - perfectly inelastic KE loss formula
```
