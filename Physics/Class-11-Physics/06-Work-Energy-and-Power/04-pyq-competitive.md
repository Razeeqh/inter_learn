# PATTERN 1 — Work done by a constant force

### Q1
A force of 10 N acts on a body and moves it 5 m at 60 degrees to the force.
The work done is

(a) 25 J &nbsp; (b) 50 J &nbsp; (c) 43.3 J &nbsp; (d) 0 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  W = F s cos theta
    = 10 x 5 x cos 60
    = 10 x 5 x 0.5
    = 25 J
```

---

### Q2
A coolie carries a load of 20 kg on his head and walks 50 m on a level road.
The work done by him on the load is

(a) 1000 J &nbsp; (b) 10000 J &nbsp; (c) 500 J &nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
  The force on the load is UPWARD (the normal reaction of his head).
  The displacement is HORIZONTAL.

  theta = 90 ,  cos 90 = 0  ,  W = 0
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Any question with "carries on his head" or "walks on level
> ground" is a zero-work question. Answer it in three seconds.

---

### Q3
A body moves under a force F = 2 i + 3 j - k newton through a displacement
s = i + j + k metre. The work done is

(a) 4 J &nbsp; (b) 5 J &nbsp; (c) 6 J &nbsp; (d) 0 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  W = Fx sx + Fy sy + Fz sz
    = (2)(1) + (3)(1) + (-1)(1)
    = 2 + 3 - 1
    = 4 J
```

---

### Q4
A block of mass 2 kg is pulled 5 m up a smooth incline of 30 degrees. The work
done against gravity is

(a) 25 J &nbsp; (b) 50 J &nbsp; (c) 100 J &nbsp; (d) 87 J

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Vertical rise  h = 5 sin 30 = 5 x 0.5 = 2.5 m

  W = m g h = 2 x 10 x 2.5 = 50 J
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Work against gravity is ALWAYS m g x (vertical rise), whatever
> the path. Never use the slope length directly.

# PATTERN 2 — Work by a variable force / area under a graph

### Q5
A force varies with displacement as shown. Find the work done from x = 0 to
x = 6 m.

```
  F (N)
    ^
 10 |     +---------+
    |     |         |
    |     |         |
    |     |         |
    +-----+---------+---------> x (m)
    0     2         6
```

(a) 20 J &nbsp; (b) 40 J &nbsp; (c) 60 J &nbsp; (d) 30 J

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  The area is a rectangle from x = 2 to x = 6, height 10 N.

  W = 10 x (6 - 2) = 10 x 4 = 40 J
```

---

### Q6
A force F = 5 x newton acts on a body. The work done in moving it from
x = 0 to x = 4 m is

(a) 20 J &nbsp; (b) 40 J &nbsp; (c) 80 J &nbsp; (d) 10 J

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
             4                     +-        -+ 4
  W = INTEGRAL 5 x dx          =   |  5x^2/2  |     =  5(16)/2  =  40 J
            0                      +-        -+ 0

  Or, faster, by the AREA of the triangle:
        base = 4 , height = 5(4) = 20
        W = (1/2)(4)(20) = 40 J
```
> **SHORTCUT:** For F = c x, the graph is a triangle, so
> W = (1/2) c x^2. For a spring, c = k, giving the familiar (1/2) k x^2.

---

### Q7
A force F = (3 x^2 - 2 x) N acts on a particle. The work done from x = 0 to
x = 2 m is

(a) 4 J &nbsp; (b) 8 J &nbsp; (c) 6 J &nbsp; (d) 2 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
             2                         +-              -+ 2
  W = INTEGRAL ( 3x^2 - 2x ) dx    =   |   x^3  -  x^2  |
            0                          +-              -+ 0

    = ( 8 - 4 )  -  ( 0 )
    = 4 J
```

# PATTERN 3 — Kinetic energy and momentum

### Q8
If the kinetic energy of a body is increased by 300 percent, its momentum
increases by

(a) 100 percent &nbsp; (b) 150 percent &nbsp; (c) 200 percent &nbsp; (d) 300 percent

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  K increased by 300 percent  ->  new K = 4 x old K

  p = sqrt( 2 m K )  , so  p goes as sqrt(K)

  new p = sqrt(4) x old p = 2 x old p

  That is an INCREASE of 100 percent.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** "Increased BY 300 percent" means the new value is 4 times, not
> 3 times. Read the word BY very carefully — it costs marks every year.

---

### Q9
Two bodies of masses m and 4m have equal kinetic energy. The ratio of their
momenta is

(a) 1 : 2 &nbsp; (b) 1 : 4 &nbsp; (c) 2 : 1 &nbsp; (d) 4 : 1

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  p = sqrt( 2 m K ) , and K is the same for both.

  So  p is proportional to sqrt(m).

        p1 : p2  =  sqrt(m) : sqrt(4m)  =  1 : 2
```

---

### Q10
Two bodies have equal momentum. Their masses are in the ratio 1 : 4. The ratio
of their kinetic energies is

(a) 1 : 4 &nbsp; (b) 4 : 1 &nbsp; (c) 1 : 2 &nbsp; (d) 2 : 1

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  K = p^2 / 2m , and p is the same for both.

  So  K is inversely proportional to m.

        K1 : K2  =  1/1 : 1/4  =  4 : 1

  The LIGHTER body has MORE kinetic energy.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Same p -> lighter has more K. Same K -> heavier has more p.
> Memorise these two lines; they cover most of this pattern.

---

### Q11
The kinetic energy of a body becomes 9 times its original value. Its speed
becomes

(a) 3 times &nbsp; (b) 9 times &nbsp; (c) 81 times &nbsp; (d) 1.5 times

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  K goes as v^2 , so  v goes as sqrt(K).

  v(new) = sqrt(9) x v = 3 v
```

# PATTERN 4 — Work-energy theorem numericals

### Q12
A bullet of mass 20 g moving at 200 m/s stops after penetrating 10 cm into a
block. The average resisting force is

(a) 2000 N &nbsp; (b) 4000 N &nbsp; (c) 6000 N &nbsp; (d) 8000 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  m = 0.02 kg , u = 200 m/s , s = 0.1 m

  Ki = (1/2)(0.02)(200)^2 = 0.01 x 40000 = 400 J

  W = -F s = 0 - 400

  F = 400 / 0.1 = 4000 N
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Use   F = m u^2 / (2 s)   directly. It works for every
> "bullet stops after penetrating s" question.

---

### Q13
A car moving at 20 m/s stops in 40 m. If its speed had been 40 m/s, the
stopping distance (same braking force) would be

(a) 80 m &nbsp; (b) 120 m &nbsp; (c) 160 m &nbsp; (d) 200 m

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  With the same force,  s  is proportional to  u^2 .

  Speed doubled  ->  distance becomes 4 times.

  s = 4 x 40 = 160 m
```

---

### Q14
A body of mass 2 kg is dropped from a height of 5 m. Its kinetic energy just
before hitting the ground is

(a) 50 J &nbsp; (b) 100 J &nbsp; (c) 10 J &nbsp; (d) 200 J

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  All the PE becomes KE:

  K = m g h = 2 x 10 x 5 = 100 J
```

---

### Q15
A block of mass 5 kg slides down a rough incline of height 4 m. It reaches the
bottom with a speed of 6 m/s. The energy lost to friction is

(a) 110 J &nbsp; (b) 90 J &nbsp; (c) 200 J &nbsp; (d) 10 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  Energy at the top   =  m g h  =  5 x 10 x 4  =  200 J
  Energy at the bottom = (1/2) m v^2 = 0.5 x 5 x 36 = 90 J

  Lost to friction = 200 - 90 = 110 J
```
> **SHORTCUT:** The word "rough" tells you mechanical energy is NOT conserved.
> The missing energy is exactly the friction loss.

# PATTERN 5 — Springs

### Q16
A spring of force constant 500 N/m is compressed by 10 cm. The energy stored is

(a) 2.5 J &nbsp; (b) 5 J &nbsp; (c) 25 J &nbsp; (d) 50 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  x = 0.1 m

  U = (1/2) k x^2 = 0.5 x 500 x (0.1)^2 = 250 x 0.01 = 2.5 J
```

---

### Q17
The work done in stretching a spring from x to 2x, given that stretching it
from 0 to x needs W, is

(a) W &nbsp; (b) 2W &nbsp; (c) 3W &nbsp; (d) 4W

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Energy at x   = (1/2) k x^2       =  W
  Energy at 2x  = (1/2) k (2x)^2    =  4 W

  Extra work needed  =  4W - W  =  3 W
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Spring energy goes as x^2, so the energies at
> x, 2x, 3x are in the ratio 1 : 4 : 9. The *extra* work in each stage is
> then 1 : 3 : 5.

---

### Q18
A spring of spring constant k is cut into two equal halves. The spring constant
of each half is

(a) k / 2 &nbsp; (b) k &nbsp; (c) 2 k &nbsp; (d) 4 k

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  k is INVERSELY proportional to the length of the spring.

  Halving the length DOUBLES k.       k' = 2 k

  A shorter spring is stiffer.
```

---

### Q19
A block of mass 2 kg moving at 4 m/s compresses a spring of constant 800 N/m.
The maximum compression is

(a) 0.1 m &nbsp; (b) 0.2 m &nbsp; (c) 0.4 m &nbsp; (d) 0.05 m

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  At maximum compression the block is momentarily at rest,
  so ALL its kinetic energy is stored in the spring:

        (1/2) m v^2  =  (1/2) k x^2

        (1/2)(2)(16) = (1/2)(800) x^2

              16     =  400 x^2

              x^2    =  0.04

              x      =  0.2 m
```

# PATTERN 6 — Conservation of mechanical energy

### Q20
A body is dropped from a height h. At what height is its kinetic energy equal
to its potential energy?

(a) h / 4 &nbsp; (b) h / 2 &nbsp; (c) 3h / 4 &nbsp; (d) h / 3

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Total energy = m g h everywhere.

  If KE = PE, then each is half of the total:

        PE  =  m g y  =  (1/2) m g h

              y  =  h / 2
```
> **SHORTCUT:** KE = PE always happens at exactly HALF the drop height.
> Memorise it — this exact question appears constantly.

---

### Q21
A pendulum bob of mass m is released from a horizontal position. Its speed at
the lowest point (string length L) is

(a) sqrt(g L) &nbsp; (b) sqrt(2 g L) &nbsp; (c) sqrt(3 g L) &nbsp; (d) sqrt(5 g L)

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Released from horizontal, the bob falls a height L.

        (1/2) m v^2  =  m g L

              v      =  sqrt( 2 g L )
```

---

### Q22
A body slides down a smooth incline of height h. Its speed at the bottom
depends on

(a) the mass only &nbsp; (b) the angle only &nbsp; (c) the height only &nbsp; (d) both mass and angle

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
        (1/2) m v^2  =  m g h    ->    v = sqrt( 2 g h )

  The mass cancels. The angle never entered. Only h matters.
```

# PATTERN 7 — The vertical circle

### Q23
A stone tied to a string of length L is whirled in a vertical circle. The
minimum speed at the highest point is

(a) sqrt(g L) &nbsp; (b) sqrt(2 g L) &nbsp; (c) sqrt(3 g L) &nbsp; (d) sqrt(5 g L)

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  At the top, the string just goes slack (T = 0), so gravity alone
  supplies the centripetal force:

        m g  =  m v^2 / L      ->      v = sqrt( g L )
```

---

### Q24
For a body just completing a vertical circle of radius r, the difference in
tension between the lowest and highest points is

(a) 2 m g &nbsp; (b) 4 m g &nbsp; (c) 6 m g &nbsp; (d) 8 m g

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
        T(bottom) - T(top)  =  6 m g

  This holds for ANY speed, not just the minimum one.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** 6 m g is a fixed number to memorise. It never changes with
> speed or with the radius.

---

### Q25
A body of mass 0.2 kg is whirled in a vertical circle of radius 0.5 m. The
minimum speed at the lowest point is

(a) 3 m/s &nbsp; (b) 5 m/s &nbsp; (c) 7 m/s &nbsp; (d) 10 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  v(bottom) = sqrt( 5 g L )
            = sqrt( 5 x 10 x 0.5 )
            = sqrt( 25 )
            = 5 m/s
```

# PATTERN 8 — Power

### Q26
A pump raises 200 kg of water per minute to a height of 15 m. Its power is

(a) 500 W &nbsp; (b) 750 W &nbsp; (c) 1000 W &nbsp; (d) 3000 W

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  W  =  m g h  =  200 x 10 x 15  =  30000 J  in 60 s

  P  =  30000 / 60  =  500 W
```
> **SHORTCUT:** For a pump,  P = (mass per second) x g x h. Convert "per
> minute" to "per second" FIRST, before doing anything else.

---

### Q27
A car of mass 1000 kg moves at a constant 15 m/s against a resistance of
500 N. The engine power is

(a) 3.75 kW &nbsp; (b) 7.5 kW &nbsp; (c) 15 kW &nbsp; (d) 150 kW

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Constant speed, so the engine force = the resistance = 500 N.

  P = F v = 500 x 15 = 7500 W = 7.5 kW

  (The mass is a distractor - it is not needed.)
```

---

### Q28
An engine of 10 kW pulls a train at a constant 20 m/s. The resistance to the
motion is

(a) 200 N &nbsp; (b) 500 N &nbsp; (c) 1000 N &nbsp; (d) 2000 N

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  P = F v      ->      F = P / v = 10000 / 20 = 500 N
```

---

### Q29
A body of mass 2 kg is lifted at a constant 3 m/s. The power required is

(a) 6 W &nbsp; (b) 20 W &nbsp; (c) 60 W &nbsp; (d) 30 W

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Constant speed, so the lifting force equals the weight:
        F = m g = 2 x 10 = 20 N

  P = F v = 20 x 3 = 60 W
```

# PATTERN 9 — Collisions

### Q31
A body of mass m moving at speed v collides head-on and elastically with an
identical body at rest. After the collision

(a) both move at v/2 &nbsp; (b) the first stops, the second moves at v
(c) both stop &nbsp; (d) they move together at v

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Equal masses in an elastic collision EXCHANGE velocities.

        v1 = 0  ,  v2 = u1 = v
```

---

### Q32
A body of mass 4 kg moving at 5 m/s collides with a stationary 6 kg body and
they stick together. The common velocity is

(a) 1 m/s &nbsp; (b) 2 m/s &nbsp; (c) 3 m/s &nbsp; (d) 5 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  m1 u1 = (m1 + m2) v

  4 x 5 = (4 + 6) v

     20 = 10 v      ->      v = 2 m/s
```

---

### Q33
In the previous question, the loss of kinetic energy is

(a) 20 J &nbsp; (b) 30 J &nbsp; (c) 50 J &nbsp; (d) 80 J

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Ki = (1/2)(4)(5)^2 = 2 x 25 = 50 J
  Kf = (1/2)(10)(2)^2 = 5 x 4 = 20 J

  Loss = 50 - 20 = 30 J

  CHECK with the formula:
        m1 m2                4 x 6
     ------------- u^2  =  --------- x 25  =  (24/20) x 25 = 30 J
      2 (m1 + m2)            2 x 10
```

### Q34
A ball dropped from 4 m rebounds to 1 m. The coefficient of restitution is

(a) 0.25 &nbsp; (b) 0.5 &nbsp; (c) 0.75 &nbsp; (d) 1

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  e = sqrt( h1 / h ) = sqrt( 1 / 4 ) = 0.5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** For rebound-height questions, e = sqrt(ratio of heights). And
> the fraction of energy retained is exactly e^2.

---

### Q35
A neutron collides head-on and elastically with a stationary nucleus of mass
number A. The fraction of energy transferred is maximum when A is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 12 &nbsp; (c) 100 &nbsp; (d) 238

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
                          4 m1 m2
  Fraction transferred = -------------
                         ( m1 + m2 )^2

  This is maximum (equal to 1) when m1 = m2.

  A neutron has mass number 1, so a target of A = 1 (hydrogen) gives
  100 percent transfer. This is why WATER is used as a moderator in
  nuclear reactors.
```

### Q36
A body at rest explodes into two pieces of masses 2 kg and 3 kg. If the 2 kg
piece moves at 6 m/s, the speed of the 3 kg piece is

(a) 2 m/s &nbsp; (b) 4 m/s &nbsp; (c) 6 m/s &nbsp; (d) 9 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Total momentum before the explosion = 0, so it must be 0 after:

        (2)(6)  =  (3)(v)

              v =  12 / 3  =  4 m/s   (in the opposite direction)
```
> **SHORTCUT:** An explosion is a collision run backwards. Momentum before
> is zero, so the two pieces must carry equal and opposite momenta.

---

### Q37
Which of the following is conserved in a perfectly inelastic collision?

(a) kinetic energy only &nbsp; (b) momentum only
(c) both &nbsp; (d) neither

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  MOMENTUM is conserved in every collision.
  KINETIC ENERGY is conserved only in an elastic collision.
  (Total energy is always conserved, but that is not "kinetic".)
```

---

### Q38
Two equal masses collide elastically but not head-on, one being at rest. The
angle between their velocities after the collision is

(a) 0 &nbsp; (b) 45 degrees &nbsp; (c) 90 degrees &nbsp; (d) 180 degrees

**ANSWER: (c)** For an oblique **elastic** collision between **equal** masses
with one at rest, the two bodies always separate at **90 degrees**.

The energy equivalent of 1 gram of matter is

# THE FASTEST ROUTE THROUGH ANY PROBLEM IN THIS CHAPTER

```
  +---------------------------------------+------------------------------+
  |  WHAT THE QUESTION GIVES YOU          |  THE TOOL TO REACH FOR       |
  +---------------------------------------+------------------------------+
  |  Force and distance, wants speed      |  W(net) = Kf - Ki            |
  |  Speeds, wants force or distance      |  W(net) = Kf - Ki            |
  |  Heights, wants speed (smooth)        |  KE + PE = constant          |
  |  Heights, wants speed (rough)         |  KE + PE + friction loss     |
  |  Work and time                        |  P = W / t                   |
  |  Constant speed and a resistance      |  P = F v                     |
  |  Two bodies hitting each other        |  momentum conservation first |
  |  A graph                              |  AREA under it               |
  |  A spring                             |  U = (1/2) k x^2             |
  |  "Just completes the loop"            |  sqrt(gL) and sqrt(5gL)      |
  +---------------------------------------+------------------------------+
```
