## Topic: Work

### Q A1
Define work. Give its SI unit and dimensional formula.

**ANSWER:**
```
  WORK is said to be done when a force acting on a body displaces it.

        W  =  F s cos theta

  where theta is the angle between the force and the displacement.

  SI unit             :  joule (J)
  Dimensional formula :  [ M L^2 T^-2 ]
  Nature              :  SCALAR
```

---

### Q A2
When is the work done by a force zero? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Work is zero in three situations:

    1.  s = 0        the body does not move
                     (pushing a wall that will not move)

    2.  F = 0        no force acts

    3.  theta = 90   the force is PERPENDICULAR to the displacement

  EXAMPLES of theta = 90 :
    - A coolie carrying a load on his head, walking on level ground.
    - The centripetal force on a body moving in a circle.
    - The tension in the string of a swinging pendulum.
```

---

### Q A3
Give one example each of positive work, negative work and zero work.

**ANSWER:**
```
  POSITIVE WORK  -  gravity acting on a body that is FALLING
                    (force and displacement both downward, theta = 0)

  NEGATIVE WORK  -  friction acting on a sliding block
                    (friction opposes the motion, theta = 180)

  ZERO WORK      -  a coolie carrying a load on his head on level ground
                    (theta = 90)
```

---

### Q A4
Can work done by a force be negative? Explain.

**ANSWER:** Yes.
```
  W = F s cos theta

  When theta is more than 90 degrees, cos theta is negative,
  so W is negative.

  Physically, negative work means the force is TAKING energy AWAY
  from the body, slowing it down. Friction and braking forces are
  the standard examples.
```

---

### Q A5
Define the joule.

**ANSWER:** One joule is the work done when a force of one newton displaces a
body through one metre **in the direction of the force**.
```
  1 J  =  1 N x 1 m       and      1 J  =  10^7 erg
```

## Topic: Energy and the work-energy theorem

### Q A6
Define kinetic energy. Write its expression.

**ANSWER:**
```
  KINETIC ENERGY is the energy possessed by a body BECAUSE OF ITS MOTION.

  It is measured by the work the body can do before coming to rest.

              1                        p^2
        K  = --- m v^2       or   K = -------
              2                        2 m

  SI unit: joule (J).   K can never be negative.
```

---

### Q A7
State the work-energy theorem.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The NET WORK done by all the forces acting on a body is equal to the
  CHANGE in its kinetic energy.

              W(net)  =  Kf  -  Ki

                          1              1
              W(net)  =  --- m v^2  -   --- m u^2
                          2              2

  It is valid for both constant and variable forces.
```

---

### Q A8
Define potential energy. Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  POTENTIAL ENERGY is the energy possessed by a body because of its
  POSITION or its CONFIGURATION (shape).

  EXAMPLES:
    - Water stored at a height behind a dam    (gravitational, U = mgh)
    - A stretched or compressed spring         (elastic, U = (1/2)kx^2)
    - A stretched bow, a wound clock spring
```

---

### Q A9
A body of mass m has momentum p. Write the relation between its kinetic
energy and its momentum.

**ANSWER:**
```
                   p^2
             K  = -------          and       p  =  sqrt( 2 m K )
                   2 m

  Note: for the SAME momentum, the LIGHTER body has MORE kinetic energy.
```

### Q A10
If the momentum of a body is doubled, by what factor does its kinetic
energy change?

**ANSWER:**
```
  K = p^2 / 2m , so K depends on p SQUARED.

  Doubling p  ->  K becomes  2^2 = FOUR times the original.
```

## Topic: Springs and potential energy

### Q A11
State Hooke's law for a spring and explain the negative sign.

**ANSWER:**
```
             F  =  - k x

  The restoring force of a spring is directly proportional to the
  displacement from its natural length.

  The MINUS sign shows that the force is always OPPOSITE to the
  displacement - the spring always pulls (or pushes) back towards
  its natural length. That is why it is called a RESTORING force.

  k = spring constant, unit N/m, dimensions [ M T^-2 ].
```

---

### Q A12
Write the expression for the potential energy stored in a stretched spring.

**ANSWER:**
```
              1
        U  = --- k x^2
              2

  x is measured from the NATURAL (unstretched) length.
  The same energy is stored for a stretch x or a compression x,
  because x is squared.
```

## Topic: Conservative forces and energy conservation

### Q A13
Distinguish between conservative and non-conservative forces with one
example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CONSERVATIVE     -  the work done is INDEPENDENT of the path, and the
                      work done in a closed loop is ZERO.
                      Example: the force of GRAVITY.

  NON-CONSERVATIVE -  the work done DEPENDS on the path, and the work
                      done in a closed loop is NOT zero.
                      Example: FRICTION.
```

---

### Q A14
State the law of conservation of mechanical energy.

**ANSWER:**
```
  If only CONSERVATIVE forces act on a body, the total mechanical energy
  (kinetic energy + potential energy) of the body remains CONSTANT.

              K  +  U   =   constant

  It does not hold when friction or air resistance is present.
```

### Q A20
Which quantity is conserved in ALL collisions?

**ANSWER:** **LINEAR MOMENTUM** is conserved in every collision (and so is the
total energy). Kinetic energy is conserved only in an **elastic** collision.

### Q A21
Two bodies of equal mass make a head-on elastic collision, one being at rest.
What happens?

**ANSWER:**
```
  They EXCHANGE their velocities.

        v1 = 0        (the moving one stops dead)
        v2 = u1       (the stationary one moves off with the
                       first one's original speed)

  Seen in carrom, billiards and Newton's cradle.
```

```
        E = energy released (joule)
        m = mass converted (kg)
        c = speed of light in vacuum = 3 x 10^8 m/s

  It is the basis of nuclear fission and fusion.
```

### Q B1
Define work. Explain positive work, negative work and zero work with examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION

        W  =  F s cos theta   =   F . s

  where F is the force, s the displacement and theta the angle
  between them. Work is a SCALAR, measured in joule.


  POSITIVE WORK  (theta between 0 and 90, cos theta positive)

        The force helps the motion; the body GAINS energy.

              F --->
            [ ]------> s          theta = 0 , W = + F s

        Examples: gravity on a falling stone;
                  an engine driving a car forward.


  NEGATIVE WORK  (theta between 90 and 180, cos theta negative)

        The force opposes the motion; the body LOSES energy.

           <--- F
            [ ]------> s          theta = 180 , W = - F s

        Examples: friction on a sliding block;
                  gravity on a body thrown upward;
                  braking force on a car.


  ZERO WORK      (theta = 90, or F = 0, or s = 0)

              F ^
                |
            [ ]------> s          theta = 90 , W = 0

        Examples: a coolie carrying a load on his head on level ground;
                  the centripetal force on a body in a circle;
                  pushing a wall that does not move.
```

### Q B2
Explain how to find the work done by a variable force. Illustrate with a
graph.

**ANSWER:**
```
  W = F s cos theta applies only to a CONSTANT force.
  When the force changes with position, we use the following idea.

  STEP 1  Divide the whole displacement into a very large number of
          very small steps, each of size ds.

  STEP 2  Over such a small step the force is practically constant,
          so the small work done is

              dW  =  F  ds

          which is the AREA of a thin strip under the F - s graph.

  STEP 3  Add up all the strips:

                       s2
              W  =  INTEGRAL   F ds
                      s1

  STEP 4  Therefore
          +----------------------------------------------------------+
          |  WORK DONE = AREA UNDER THE FORCE - DISPLACEMENT GRAPH   |
          +----------------------------------------------------------+


  GRAPH

    F (N)
      ^
      |         .-'''-.
      |      .-'   |   '-.
      |    .'      |      '.
      |   /      | | |      \
      |  /       | | |       \
      | /        | | |        \
      |/         |ds |         \
      +----------+---+----------+-------> s (m)
      0         s1              s2

      The shaded total area between s1 and s2 is the work done.

      Area ABOVE the axis  ->  POSITIVE work
      Area BELOW the axis  ->  NEGATIVE work

  EXAMPLE: for a spring, F = kx is a straight line through the origin,
  so the area is a triangle:
                                  1                    1
              W  =  area  =      --- x (x) x (k x) =  --- k x^2
                                  2                    2
```

### Q B3
State and prove the work-energy theorem for a constant force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT

  The net work done by all the forces acting on a body is equal to the
  change in its kinetic energy.

              W(net)  =  Kf  -  Ki


  PROOF (constant force)

  Let a constant force F act on a body of mass m, changing its speed
  from u to v over a displacement s, giving it an acceleration a.

  STEP 1  From the third equation of motion,

              v^2  =  u^2  +  2 a s

  STEP 2  Rearranging,
                                v^2  -  u^2
              a s    =   ------------------------
                                     2

  STEP 3  Multiply both sides by the mass m:

                                m ( v^2  -  u^2 )
              m a s  =   -------------------------------
                                       2

  STEP 4  But  m a = F  (Newton's second law), so the left-hand side
          is  F s , which is the work done W.

  STEP 5  Splitting the right-hand side,

                        1              1
              W    =   --- m v^2  -   --- m u^2
                        2              2

                   =   Kf  -  Ki

                                                     HENCE PROVED
```

### Q B4
Prove the work-energy theorem for a variable force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1  For a very small displacement ds, the small work done is

              dW  =  F ds

  STEP 2  By Newton's second law,

                         dv
              F  =  m  ------
                         dt

          so
                         dv
              dW  =  m ------  ds
                         dt

  STEP 3  Rearrange, noting that  ds / dt  =  v :

                          ds
              dW  =  m  ------ dv    =    m v dv
                          dt

  STEP 4  Integrate from the initial speed u to the final speed v:

                        v
              W  =  INTEGRAL   m v dv
                       u

  STEP 5  The integral of v dv is v^2 / 2 :

                     +-             -+ v
                     |     m v^2     |
              W  =   |   ---------   |
                     |        2      | u
                     +-             -+

  STEP 6  Applying the limits,

                      1             1
              W  =   --- m v^2  -  --- m u^2   =   Kf  -  Ki
                      2             2

                                                     HENCE PROVED

  So the theorem is valid for a variable force as well.
```

### Q B5
Distinguish between conservative and non-conservative forces. Give the
tests for a conservative force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THE THREE TESTS FOR A CONSERVATIVE FORCE

    1.  The work done in moving a body between two points is
        INDEPENDENT OF THE PATH followed.

    2.  The work done in a COMPLETE ROUND TRIP (a closed loop) is ZERO.

    3.  A POTENTIAL ENERGY function can be defined for it,
        with  F = - dU / dx .

  A force that fails ANY of these is non-conservative.


  COMPARISON

  +--------------------------+------------------+--------------------+
  |  POINT                   |  CONSERVATIVE    |  NON-CONSERVATIVE  |
  +--------------------------+------------------+--------------------+
  |  Work depends on path    |  NO              |  YES               |
  |  Work in a closed loop   |  ZERO            |  NOT zero          |
  |  Potential energy exists |  YES             |  NO                |
  |  Energy recoverable      |  fully           |  lost as heat      |
  |  Mechanical energy       |  CONSERVED       |  NOT conserved     |
  |  Examples                |  gravity, spring |  friction, air     |
  |                          |  electrostatic   |  drag, viscosity   |
  +--------------------------+------------------+--------------------+


  WHY FRICTION FAILS THE CLOSED-LOOP TEST

      A --------------------> B     friction acts left  , W = - f d
      A <-------------------- B     friction acts right , W = - f d

      Total for the round trip  =  - 2 f d , which is NOT zero.

      (Gravity, by contrast, always points downwards, so the work
      done going up exactly cancels the work done coming down.)
```

### Q B6
Derive the expression for the potential energy stored in a stretched
spring, and state Hooke's law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HOOKE'S LAW

              F  =  - k x

  The restoring force is proportional to the displacement x from the
  natural length, and is directed OPPOSITE to it (hence the minus sign).
  k is the spring constant, in N/m.


  DERIVATION OF THE STORED ENERGY

  To stretch the spring we must apply a force equal and opposite to
  the restoring force, that is  F(applied) = + k x .

  This force is NOT constant - it grows as x grows - so we use the
  area under the graph.

    F
      ^
      |                    /|
      |                  /  |
      |                /    |
    kx|. . . . . . ./       |
      |          /  |       |
      |        /    |       |
      |      /      |       |
      |    /        |       |
      |  /          |       |
      +/------------+-------+------> x
      0                     x

  The graph is a straight line through the origin, so the area is a
  triangle:

              1
      W  =   --- x base x height
              2

              1
         =   --- x  ( x )  x  ( k x )
              2

              1
         =   --- k x^2
              2

  By integration, the same result:

              x                    +-           -+ x
      W = INTEGRAL  k x dx     =   |   k x^2 / 2  |     =  (1/2) k x^2
             0                     +-           -+ 0

  This work is stored in the spring as elastic potential energy:

          +-----------------------------------+
          |          U  =  (1/2) k x^2        |
          +-----------------------------------+

  It is the same for a stretch x and a compression x, because x is
  squared.
```

### Q B7
A body is whirled in a vertical circle of radius L. Derive the minimum
speed at the highest point and at the lowest point.

**ANSWER:**
```
                      v(top)
                     ----->
                  ......T.......
              ...     o TOP     ...
            ..        |            ..
          ..          | mg           ..
         .            v                .
        .              O  centre        .      radius = L
         .                             .
          ..         ^                ..
            ...      | T           ...
                 ....o.........
                  BOTTOM  | mg
                          v
                       v(bottom)

  AT THE TOP

  Both the tension T and the weight mg point DOWNWARD, towards the
  centre, and together they supply the centripetal force:

                                m v(top)^2
              T  +  m g   =   --------------
                                    L

  A string can only PULL, never push, so T cannot be negative.
  The slowest possible case is when the string just goes slack, T = 0 :

                                m v(top)^2
                    m g   =   --------------
                                    L

                v(top)^2  =  g L

          +---------------------------------------+
          |     v(top, min)  =  sqrt( g L )       |
          +---------------------------------------+


  AT THE BOTTOM

  Apply conservation of mechanical energy between the bottom and the
  top. The top is a height 2L above the bottom.

       (1/2) m v(bot)^2  =  (1/2) m v(top)^2  +  m g (2 L)

  Substituting  v(top)^2 = g L  and cancelling m :

       (1/2) v(bot)^2  =  (1/2) g L  +  2 g L

             v(bot)^2  =  g L  +  4 g L   =   5 g L

          +---------------------------------------+
          |     v(bot, min)  =  sqrt( 5 g L )     |
          +---------------------------------------+


  TENSION AT THE BOTTOM

                             m v(bot)^2         m (5 g L)
              T  =  m g  +  --------------  =  ----------- + m g  =  6 m g
                                  L                 L

  Also, for ANY speed,   T(bottom)  -  T(top)  =  6 m g .
```

### Q B9
Show that in a perfectly inelastic collision kinetic energy is lost, and
find the loss.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BEFORE                                AFTER

   u1 ->       u2 ->                     v ->
  ( m1 )      ( m2 )                   ( m1 + m2 )
  ---------------------->              ---------------------->

  STEP 1  In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies stick together
          and move with one common velocity v.

  STEP 2  Momentum is conserved:

              m1 u1  +  m2 u2   =   ( m1 + m2 ) v

                         m1 u1  +  m2 u2
              v    =   ---------------------
                            m1  +  m2

  STEP 3  Kinetic energy before:

              Ki  =  (1/2) m1 u1^2  +  (1/2) m2 u2^2

  STEP 4  Kinetic energy after:

              Kf  =  (1/2) ( m1 + m2 ) v^2

  STEP 5  Substituting v and simplifying gives the standard result:

                              +-               -+
                              |    m1  m2       |
              Ki  -  Kf   =   | --------------- |  ( u1  -  u2 )^2
                              |   2( m1 + m2 )  |
                              +-               -+

  STEP 6  The right-hand side is a SQUARE multiplied by positive
          masses, so it is ALWAYS POSITIVE.

          Therefore kinetic energy is ALWAYS LOST in a perfectly
          inelastic collision. The lost energy appears as heat,
          sound and permanent deformation.

          The loss is the MAXIMUM possible loss for the given masses
          and speeds.

  NOTE: momentum is still perfectly conserved. Only kinetic energy
        is lost. Total energy is also conserved.
```

### Q B12
A body of mass 2 kg initially at rest is acted on by a force of 10 N for
5 s on a frictionless surface. Using the work-energy theorem, find its final
kinetic energy.

**ANSWER:**
```

                 F        10
            a = ---  =  ------  =  5 m/s^2
                 m         2

          K = (1/2)(2)(25)^2 = 1 x 625 = 625 J.   Agrees.

  ANSWER:  final kinetic energy = 625 J
```

### Q B14
Find the work done by a force F = 5 i + 3 j - 2 k newton in displacing a
body through s = 2 i - j + 3 k metre. Also find the angle between them.

**ANSWER:**

### Q C1
State and prove the law of conservation of mechanical energy in the case of
a freely falling body.

**ANSWER:**

```
  STATEMENT

  If only conservative forces act on a body, the total mechanical energy
  of the body - the sum of its kinetic energy and its potential energy -
  remains CONSTANT throughout the motion.

              K  +  U   =   constant
```

```
  SET-UP

  A body of mass m is released from REST at a point A, at a height h
  above the ground. Air resistance is neglected, so the only force
  acting is GRAVITY, which is a conservative force.
  Take the GROUND as the zero level for potential energy.


        A  o  <-----------------------  v = 0        height = h
           |
           |          x
           |
        B  o  <-----------------------  v1           height = h - x
           |
           |
           |
  //////// C ///////////////////////     v2          height = 0
                  GROUND
```

```
  AT THE POINT A  (the top, height h)

      The body starts from rest, so v = 0.

          KE  =  (1/2) m (0)^2   =   0
          PE  =  m g h

          TOTAL  =  0  +  m g h   =   m g h                 ... (1)
```

```
  AT THE POINT B  (after falling a distance x, height h - x)

      Using  v^2 = u^2 + 2 a s  with u = 0 , a = g , s = x :

          v1^2  =  0  +  2 g x   =   2 g x

                       1                 1
          KE  =       --- m v1^2   =    --- m ( 2 g x )   =   m g x
                       2                 2

          PE  =  m g ( h - x )   =   m g h  -  m g x

          TOTAL  =  m g x  +  m g h  -  m g x

                 =  m g h                                    ... (2)
```

```
  AT THE POINT C  (the ground, height 0)

      Using  v^2 = u^2 + 2 a s  with u = 0 , a = g , s = h :

          v2^2  =  2 g h

                       1                 1
          KE  =       --- m v2^2   =    --- m ( 2 g h )   =   m g h
                       2                 2

          PE  =  m g ( 0 )   =   0

          TOTAL  =  m g h  +  0   =   m g h                  ... (3)
```

```
  CONCLUSION

  +--------------+---------------+----------------+------------------+
  |  POINT       |  KE           |  PE            |  TOTAL           |
  +--------------+---------------+----------------+------------------+
  |  A  (top)    |      0        |     m g h      |     m g h        |
  |  B  (middle) |    m g x      |   m g (h - x)  |     m g h        |
  |  C  (ground) |    m g h      |       0        |     m g h        |
  +--------------+---------------+----------------+------------------+

  From (1), (2) and (3), the total mechanical energy is the same,
  m g h , at every point of the fall.

  HENCE THE MECHANICAL ENERGY OF A FREELY FALLING BODY IS CONSERVED.
                                                     PROVED
```

```
  ENERGY GRAPH  (draw this - it carries marks)

    Energy
      ^
      |  PE
 mgh  |----\--------------------------  TOTAL (constant)
      |     \                    /
      |      \                  /
      |       \                /
      |        \              /
      |         \            /
      |          \          /
      |           \        /
      |            \      /   KE
      |             \    /
      0 +------------\/------------------> distance fallen
        top                        ground

  PE decreases in a straight line, KE increases in a straight line,
  and their SUM is a horizontal straight line.

  NOTE:  KE = PE exactly at the MIDDLE of the fall, at height h/2.

  LIMITATION:  the proof assumes NO air resistance. With air
  resistance present, part of the energy becomes heat, and the
  mechanical energy is no longer conserved (although the TOTAL
  energy still is).
```

### Q C2
Derive the expressions for the final velocities of two bodies undergoing a
one-dimensional elastic collision. Discuss the three special cases.

**ANSWER:**

```
  SET-UP

  BEFORE                                    AFTER

   u1 ->        u2 ->                        v1 ->      v2 ->
  ( m1 )       ( m2 )                       ( m1 )     ( m2 )
  ------------------------>                 ------------------------>

  Two smooth spheres of masses m1 and m2 move along the same straight
  line with velocities u1 and u2 (with u1 greater than u2, so they
  collide). After the collision their velocities are v1 and v2.
  The collision is ELASTIC, so both momentum and kinetic energy are
  conserved.
```

```
  STEP 1  CONSERVATION OF LINEAR MOMENTUM

              m1 u1  +  m2 u2   =   m1 v1  +  m2 v2

          Grouping the m1 terms and the m2 terms:

              m1 ( u1  -  v1 )   =   m2 ( v2  -  u2 )        ... (1)
```

```
  STEP 2  CONSERVATION OF KINETIC ENERGY (allowed, since elastic)

        (1/2) m1 u1^2 + (1/2) m2 u2^2 = (1/2) m1 v1^2 + (1/2) m2 v2^2

          Cancelling 1/2 and grouping:

              m1 ( u1^2  -  v1^2 )  =   m2 ( v2^2  -  u2^2 )  ... (2)
```

```
  STEP 3  DIVIDE (2) BY (1)

          Factorise both sides of (2) using a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b) :

              m1 ( u1 - v1 )( u1 + v1 )        m2 ( v2 - u2 )( v2 + u2 )
              --------------------------  =   ---------------------------
                   m1 ( u1 - v1 )                    m2 ( v2 - u2 )

          Cancelling,

              u1  +  v1   =   v2  +  u2

          which rearranges to

              u1  -  u2   =   v2  -  v1                      ... (3)

          IN WORDS:  in an elastic collision the relative velocity of
          APPROACH equals the relative velocity of SEPARATION.
          (This is exactly why e = 1 for an elastic collision.)
```

```
  STEP 4  SOLVE FOR v1

          From (3),      v2  =  v1  +  u1  -  u2

          Substituting this into the momentum equation

              m1 u1 + m2 u2  =  m1 v1 + m2 ( v1 + u1 - u2 )

              m1 u1 + m2 u2  =  v1 ( m1 + m2 ) + m2 u1 - m2 u2

              v1 ( m1 + m2 ) =  m1 u1 - m2 u1 + m2 u2 + m2 u2

              v1 ( m1 + m2 ) =  ( m1 - m2 ) u1  +  2 m2 u2

          +---------------------------------------------------------+
          |          +-              -+           +-        -+      |
          |          |   m1  -  m2    |           |  2 m2    |      |
          |  v1  =   | -------------- | u1   +    | -------- | u2   |
          |          |   m1  +  m2    |           | m1 + m2  |      |
          |          +-              -+           +-        -+      |
          +---------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  STEP 5  SOLVE FOR v2  (by the same method, or by swapping 1 and 2)

          +---------------------------------------------------------+
          |          +-              -+           +-        -+      |
          |          |   m2  -  m1    |           |  2 m1    |      |
          |  v2  =   | -------------- | u2   +    | -------- | u1   |
          |          |   m1  +  m2    |           | m1 + m2  |      |
          |          +-              -+           +-        -+      |
          +---------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  THE THREE SPECIAL CASES   (take the target at rest, u2 = 0)


  CASE 1 - EQUAL MASSES,  m1 = m2 = m

                m - m                   2 m
        v1  =  ------- u1  =  0 ,  v2 = ------- u1  =  u1
                m + m                   m + m

        +--------------------------------------------------------+
        |  THE TWO BODIES EXCHANGE VELOCITIES.                   |
        |  The first stops dead; the second moves off with the   |
        |  first one's original speed.                           |
        |  Seen in carrom, billiards and Newton's cradle, and    |
        |  in a neutron colliding with a proton.                 |
        +--------------------------------------------------------+


  CASE 2 - A HEAVY BODY STRIKES A LIGHT ONE AT REST,  m1 >> m2

        Neglecting m2 beside m1 :

                m1 - m2                          2 m1
        v1  =  --------- u1  =  u1  ,     v2 = --------- u1  =  2 u1
                m1 + m2                         m1 + m2

        +--------------------------------------------------------+
        |  v1 = u1     the heavy body carries on almost unchanged |
        |  v2 = 2 u1   the light body flies off at TWICE the      |
        |              speed of the heavy one                    |
        |  Example: a moving truck striking a football.          |
        +--------------------------------------------------------+


  CASE 3 - A LIGHT BODY STRIKES A HEAVY ONE AT REST,  m1 << m2

        Neglecting m1 beside m2 :

                m1 - m2                            2 m1
        v1  =  --------- u1  =  - u1  ,     v2 = --------- u1  =  0
                m1 + m2                           m1 + m2

        +--------------------------------------------------------+
        |  v1 = - u1   the light body BOUNCES STRAIGHT BACK with  |
        |              the same speed                            |
        |  v2 = 0      the heavy body remains practically at rest |
        |  Example: a rubber ball bouncing off a wall or floor.   |
        +--------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  SUMMARY PICTURE

  EQUAL MASSES            HEAVY hits LIGHT          LIGHT hits HEAVY

  ->o    o                ->[]   o                  ->o      []
   m1   m2                  M    m                    m       M

     o  ->o                 ->[]  ->->o             o<-       []
    stops  moves            keeps  flies off        bounces   barely
```

### Q C3
Define work, energy and power. Derive the work-energy theorem and apply it
to a body sliding down a smooth inclined plane.

**ANSWER:**

```
  DEFINITIONS

  WORK    -  work is done when a force displaces a body.
                 W  =  F s cos theta     ,  unit joule , scalar

  ENERGY  -  the capacity of a body to do work.
                 Kinetic   K = (1/2) m v^2
                 Potential U = m g h  or  (1/2) k x^2
                 unit joule , scalar , dimensions [ M L^2 T^-2 ]

  POWER   -  the rate of doing work.
                 P  =  W / t  =  F . v   ,  unit watt , scalar
```

```
  THE WORK-ENERGY THEOREM

  STATEMENT: the net work done by all the forces acting on a body
  equals the change in its kinetic energy,   W(net) = Kf - Ki .


  PROOF (constant force)

  STEP 1   v^2  =  u^2  +  2 a s

  STEP 2                v^2  -  u^2
           a s   =   ------------------
                            2

  STEP 3               m ( v^2  -  u^2 )
           m a s  =   ---------------------
                              2

  STEP 4   m a = F , so the left side is F s = W , giving

                     1              1
           W    =   --- m v^2  -   --- m u^2   =   Kf  -  Ki
                     2              2
                                                     PROVED
```

```
  APPLICATION - A BODY SLIDING DOWN A SMOOTH INCLINE

                        /|
                      /  |
                    /    |
              [m] /      | h
                /        |
              /  theta   |
            /____________|
                  smooth

  The body of mass m starts from rest at the top of a smooth incline
  of height h and inclination theta. The length of the incline is

              h
        L = ---------
            sin theta

  STEP 1  Forces acting:
            - the weight  m g  (vertically down)
            - the normal reaction N (perpendicular to the surface)
            The surface is SMOOTH, so there is no friction.

  STEP 2  Work done by the NORMAL REACTION:
            N is perpendicular to the motion, so theta = 90 and
            W(N) = 0.

  STEP 3  Work done by GRAVITY:
            The component of the weight along the incline is
            m g sin theta, and the distance moved is L.

                                                     h
            W(gravity) = ( m g sin theta ) x  ---------------
                                                sin theta

                       =  m g h

            (Note this is simply m g x vertical drop - which is
            exactly why gravity is a conservative force.)

  STEP 4  So the NET work done is  W(net) = m g h .

  STEP 5  Apply the theorem, with Ki = 0 (starts from rest):

              W(net)  =  Kf  -  Ki

              m g h   =  (1/2) m v^2  -  0

              v^2     =  2 g h

              +---------------------------------------------+
              |          v  =  sqrt( 2 g h )                |
              +---------------------------------------------+

  RESULT:  the speed at the bottom of a SMOOTH incline depends only
  on the HEIGHT h, and NOT on the angle of the incline and NOT on
  the mass. A steep slide and a gentle slide of the same height give
  the same final speed. This is the same result as for free fall.
```

### Q C4
Show that the total mechanical energy of a body oscillating on a spring is
conserved, and hence obtain its maximum speed.

**ANSWER:**

```
  SET-UP

  A block of mass m on a smooth horizontal surface is attached to a
  spring of force constant k. It is pulled aside a distance A from
  the natural length and released.

  |/\/\/\/\/\/\/[ m ]              natural length , x = 0
  |
  |/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/\/[ m ]      pulled to  x = A , then released
  |
  The only force doing work is the SPRING FORCE, F = - k x,
  which is CONSERVATIVE. (Gravity and the normal reaction are both
  perpendicular to the motion, so they do no work.)
```

```
  AT THE EXTREME POSITION,  x = A

        The block is momentarily at rest, v = 0.

            KE  =  0
            PE  =  (1/2) k A^2

            TOTAL  =  (1/2) k A^2                             ... (1)


  AT ANY INTERMEDIATE POSITION x , where the speed is v

            KE  =  (1/2) m v^2
            PE  =  (1/2) k x^2

            TOTAL  =  (1/2) m v^2  +  (1/2) k x^2             ... (2)


  AT THE MEAN POSITION,  x = 0 , where the speed is v(max)

            KE  =  (1/2) m v(max)^2
            PE  =  0

            TOTAL  =  (1/2) m v(max)^2                        ... (3)
```

```
  PROOF OF CONSERVATION

  Since the spring force is conservative, the total mechanical energy
  is the same at every position. Equating (1) and (2):

        (1/2) m v^2  +  (1/2) k x^2   =   (1/2) k A^2

  This shows that as x decreases, PE falls and KE rises by exactly
  the same amount, keeping the sum constant.

        +-------------------------------------------------------+
        |     KE  +  PE   =   (1/2) k A^2   =  CONSTANT         |
        +-------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  MAXIMUM SPEED

  Equate (1) and (3), which gives the speed at the mean position:

        (1/2) m v(max)^2   =   (1/2) k A^2

                                k
                v(max)^2  =    ---  A^2
                                m

        +-------------------------------------------------------+
        |                          k                            |
        |     v(max)   =   A sqrt( --- )                        |
        |                          m                            |
        +-------------------------------------------------------+


  SPEED AT ANY POSITION x , from the same equation:

                            k
            v  =  sqrt(  ------ ( A^2  -  x^2 )  )
                            m
```

### Q C5
Explain collisions. Distinguish elastic from inelastic collisions, define
the coefficient of restitution, and derive the common velocity and energy
loss in a perfectly inelastic collision.

**ANSWER:**

```
  WHAT IS A COLLISION

  A collision is a short, strong interaction between two bodies in
  which their momenta change appreciably in a very short time. The
  bodies need not physically touch (two magnets repelling, or a
  comet swinging past a planet, also count as collisions).

  IN EVERY COLLISION, LINEAR MOMENTUM IS CONSERVED, because the
  colliding pair forms an isolated system and the forces of impact
  are internal - equal and opposite by Newton's third law.
```

```
  CLASSIFICATION

                          COLLISIONS
                               |
             +-----------------+-----------------+
             |                                   |
         ELASTIC                            INELASTIC
   momentum conserved                 momentum conserved
   KE conserved                       KE NOT conserved
   e = 1                              e less than 1
                                             |
                              +--------------+--------------+
                              |                             |
                    PARTLY INELASTIC              PERFECTLY INELASTIC
                    0 < e < 1                     e = 0
                    most real collisions          bodies stick together
                                                  MAXIMUM loss of KE
```

```
  COMPARISON TABLE

  +---------------------------+-------------------+---------------------+
  |  POINT                    |  ELASTIC          |  INELASTIC          |
  +---------------------------+-------------------+---------------------+
  |  Momentum conserved       |  YES              |  YES                |
  |  Kinetic energy conserved |  YES              |  NO                 |
  |  Total energy conserved   |  YES              |  YES                |
  |  Coefficient e            |  1                |  less than 1        |
  |  Forces involved          |  conservative     |  non-conservative   |
  |  Deformation              |  temporary, fully |  permanent, or the  |
  |                           |  recovered        |  bodies stick       |
  |  Example                  |  collisions of    |  a lump of clay     |
  |                           |  atoms, molecules |  hitting the floor  |
  +---------------------------+-------------------+---------------------+
```

```
  COEFFICIENT OF RESTITUTION

         relative velocity of SEPARATION        v2  -  v1
   e  = ---------------------------------  =  -------------
         relative velocity of APPROACH          u1  -  u2

        e = 1        perfectly elastic
        0 < e < 1    partly inelastic
        e = 0        perfectly inelastic

  e has NO units and NO dimensions.

  For a ball dropped from height h and rebounding to h1 :

                        h1
            e  =  sqrt( ---- )
                         h
```

```
  PERFECTLY INELASTIC COLLISION - COMMON VELOCITY

  BEFORE                                AFTER

   u1 ->       u2 ->                     v ->
  ( m1 )      ( m2 )                   ( m1 + m2 )
  ---------------------->              ---------------------->

  STEP 1  The bodies stick together, so they move with a single
          common velocity v.

  STEP 2  Momentum is conserved:

              m1 u1  +  m2 u2   =   ( m1  +  m2 ) v

          +---------------------------------------------------+
          |                  m1 u1  +  m2 u2                  |
          |        v   =   ---------------------              |
          |                     m1  +  m2                     |
          +---------------------------------------------------+

          If the second body was at rest (u2 = 0):

                              m1 u1
                      v  =  -----------
                            m1  +  m2
```

```
  LOSS OF KINETIC ENERGY

  STEP 1  Ki  =  (1/2) m1 u1^2  +  (1/2) m2 u2^2

  STEP 2  Kf  =  (1/2) ( m1 + m2 ) v^2

  STEP 3  Substituting the value of v and simplifying:

          +---------------------------------------------------+
          |                  +-               -+              |
          |                  |    m1  m2       |              |
          |  Ki  -  Kf   =   | --------------- | ( u1 - u2 )^2|
          |                  |   2( m1 + m2 )  |              |
          |                  +-               -+              |
          +---------------------------------------------------+

  STEP 4  The right-hand side is a SQUARE multiplied by positive
          masses, so it is ALWAYS POSITIVE. Kinetic energy is
          therefore ALWAYS LOST.

          The lost energy is not destroyed - it appears as HEAT,
          SOUND and PERMANENT DEFORMATION. Total energy is still
          conserved.

          This loss is the MAXIMUM possible loss for the given
          masses and velocities.
```

```
  TWO-DIMENSIONAL COLLISIONS (brief)

  When the motion is not along one line, momentum is conserved
  SEPARATELY in each direction:

     ALONG X :  m1 u1  =  m1 v1 cos theta1  +  m2 v2 cos theta2
     ALONG Y :     0   =  m1 v1 sin theta1  -  m2 v2 sin theta2

  For an ELASTIC collision between two EQUAL masses with one at rest,
  the two bodies move off at RIGHT ANGLES to each other,
  that is  theta1 + theta2 = 90 degrees.
```
