## Topic: Heat, temperature and scales

### Q A1
Distinguish between heat and temperature.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HEAT         -  energy in TRANSIT from a hotter body to a colder body.
                  It is a form of ENERGY.   Unit: joule (J)

  TEMPERATURE  -  the degree of hotness or coldness of a body.
                  It DECIDES THE DIRECTION in which heat flows.
                  Unit: kelvin (K)
```

---

### Q A2
Convert 40 degC into the Fahrenheit and kelvin scales.

**ANSWER:**
```
  F = (9/5) C + 32 = (9/5)(40) + 32 = 72 + 32 = 104 degF

  K = C + 273 = 40 + 273 = 313 K
```

---

### Q A3
At what temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales give the same reading?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Let the common reading be x.

      x = (9/5) x + 32
      x - (9/5)x = 32
      -(4/5) x = 32
      x = -40

  ANSWER:  -40 degC = -40 degF
```

---

### Q A4
What is absolute zero? What is its value on the Celsius scale?

**ANSWER:** The lowest possible temperature, at which the pressure and volume of
an ideal gas would become zero and molecular motion would cease.
**0 K = -273.15 degC.**

---

## Topic: Thermal expansion

### Q A5
Define the coefficient of linear expansion. Give its unit.

**ANSWER:**
```
                dL
  alpha  =  -----------      = the increase in length per unit original
             L  x  dT          length per degree rise in temperature.

  UNIT:  per kelvin (K^-1)  or per degree celsius (degC^-1)
  DIMENSIONS: [ M^0 L^0 T^0 K^-1 ]
```

---

### Q A6
Write the relation between the coefficients of linear, areal and cubical
expansion.

**ANSWER:**
```
      beta = 2 alpha            gamma = 3 alpha

      alpha : beta : gamma  =  1 : 2 : 3

      alpha / 1  =  beta / 2  =  gamma / 3
```

---

### Q A7
A circular hole is drilled in a metal plate. What happens to the diameter of
the hole when the plate is heated?

**ANSWER:** The diameter **INCREASES**. Every linear dimension of the plate,
including the distance across a hole, grows by the factor `(1 + alpha dT)` —
exactly as if the hole were filled with the same metal.

---

### Q A8
Why is a gap left between two consecutive rails of a railway track?

**ANSWER:** To allow the rails to expand freely in summer. If they were joined
rigidly, they could not expand, an enormous thermal stress `Y alpha dT` would
develop, and the track would buckle.

---

### Q A9
What is thermal stress? Write its expression.

**ANSWER:** The stress developed in a rod that is heated (or cooled) while it is
rigidly clamped so that it cannot change its length.
```
      thermal stress  =  Y  alpha  dT
      thermal force   =  Y  A  alpha  dT

  It does NOT depend on the length of the rod.
```

---

### Q A10
Why does a pendulum clock go slow in summer?

**ANSWER:** The pendulum rod expands, so `L` increases. Since
`T = 2 pi sqrt(L/g)`, the time period increases, each swing takes longer, and
the clock **loses** time.

---

### Q A11
Write the relation between the real and apparent coefficients of expansion of a
liquid.

**ANSWER:**
```
      gamma(real)  =  gamma(apparent)  +  gamma(vessel)

  where  gamma(vessel) = 3 x alpha(vessel material)
```

---

### Q A12
What is the coefficient of volume expansion of an ideal gas at constant
pressure?

**ANSWER:** `gamma = 1/273 per degC = 0.00366 per degC`.
It is the **same for every gas** — it does not depend on the nature of the gas.

---

### Q A13
What is meant by the anomalous expansion of water?

**ANSWER:** Between 0 degC and 4 degC, water **contracts** when heated instead of
expanding. Consequently water has its **maximum density (1000 kg/m^3) at
4 degC** and its minimum volume there.

---

### Q A14
Why do fish survive in a lake even when its surface is frozen?

**ANSWER:** Because of the anomalous expansion of water. Water at 4 degC is the
densest, so it sinks and stays at the bottom. Colder water and ice, being less
dense, float on top. Ice is a poor conductor and acts as a blanket, so the water
below remains liquid at about 4 degC and the fish survive.

## Topic: Specific heat and latent heat

### Q A15
Define specific heat capacity. Give its SI unit.

**ANSWER:**
```
              Q
    c  =  ---------      = the heat required to raise the temperature of
           m  x  dT        1 kilogram of a substance through 1 kelvin.

    SI UNIT:  J kg^-1 K^-1
```

---

### Q A16
Define molar specific heat capacity and give the relation to specific heat.

**ANSWER:** The heat required to raise the temperature of **one mole** of a
substance through 1 kelvin.
```
              Q
    C  =  ---------  =  M c          UNIT: J mol^-1 K^-1
           n  x  dT
```

---

### Q A17
Why is water used as a coolant in car radiators?

**ANSWER:** Water has the **highest specific heat capacity** of any common
substance (4186 J/kg/K). It therefore absorbs a very large quantity of heat for
a small rise in its own temperature, which makes it the most efficient cheap
coolant.

---

### Q A18
Define latent heat of fusion and latent heat of vaporisation. Give their values
for water.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LATENT HEAT OF FUSION       -  the heat required to convert unit mass of a
                                 solid into liquid at its melting point,
                                 without any change of temperature.
                                 Lf(ice) = 3.33 x 10^5 J/kg = 80 cal/g

  LATENT HEAT OF VAPORISATION -  the heat required to convert unit mass of a
                                 liquid into vapour at its boiling point,
                                 without any change of temperature.
                                 Lv(water) = 22.6 x 10^5 J/kg = 540 cal/g
```

---

### Q A19
Why does a burn caused by steam at 100 degC hurt far more than one caused by
water at 100 degC?

**ANSWER:** Steam must first **condense** on the skin, and in doing so it
releases its latent heat of vaporisation, 540 cal per gram, *before* it even
begins to cool. Water at 100 degC delivers only the heat of cooling. Steam
therefore delivers about ten times more heat.

---

### Q A20
Why does the temperature of a substance remain constant during a change of
state?

**ANSWER:** Because the heat supplied is used entirely to **break the bonds**
between the molecules (increasing potential energy), not to increase their
kinetic energy. Since temperature measures average kinetic energy, it does not
change.

---

## Topic: Change of state

### Q A21
Define the triple point of water. Give its values.

**ANSWER:** The single temperature and pressure at which ice, water and water
vapour coexist in equilibrium.
```
      T = 273.16 K          P = 0.61 kPa  (about 0.006 atm)
```

---

### Q A22
What is regelation?

**ANSWER:** The phenomenon in which **ice melts under pressure and re-freezes
when the pressure is removed**. It is shown by the experiment in which a loaded
wire cuts right through a block of ice while leaving the block in one piece.

---

### Q A23
Why does food cook faster in a pressure cooker?

**ANSWER:** The pressure inside is about 2 atmospheres. Since the boiling point
rises with pressure, the water boils at about 120 degC instead of 100 degC, and
the higher temperature cooks the food much faster.

---

### Q A24
Give two examples of sublimation.

**ANSWER:** Dry ice (solid CO2), camphor, iodine, naphthalene balls — each goes
directly from solid to vapour without passing through the liquid state.

---

## Topic: Conduction and convection

### Q A25
Define the coefficient of thermal conductivity. Give its SI unit and
dimensions.

**ANSWER:**
```
              H  x  L
    K  =  ---------------   = the rate of flow of heat per unit area per unit
           A ( T1 - T2 )      temperature gradient, in the steady state.

    SI UNIT:     W m^-1 K^-1
    DIMENSIONS:  [ M L T^-3 K^-1 ]
```

---

### Q A26
Why does a metal chair feel colder than a wooden chair in the same room?

**ANSWER:** Both are at the **same temperature**. Metal has a much higher
thermal conductivity, so it conducts heat away from your skin far more quickly,
and the rapid loss of heat is what you interpret as "cold".

---

### Q A27
Why are two thin blankets warmer than a single blanket of the same total
thickness?

**ANSWER:** A layer of **still air** is trapped between the two blankets. Air is
one of the poorest conductors of heat (K = 0.024 W/m/K), so the trapped layer
greatly reduces the heat lost from the body.

---

### Q A28
Distinguish between natural and forced convection with one example each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NATURAL  -  the fluid moves only because of gravity acting on the density
              differences produced by heating.
              Example: boiling water in a pot, the sea breeze.

  FORCED   -  the fluid is driven by a pump, fan or blower.
              Example: a car radiator, blood circulated by the heart.
```

---

### Q A29
Why are ventilators provided near the ceiling of a room?

**ANSWER:** Warm, used air is less dense and rises to the top of the room. A
ventilator near the ceiling lets it escape, and fresh cool air enters from
below, setting up a convection current.

---

## Topic: Radiation

### Q A30
What is a perfectly black body?

**ANSWER:** A body that absorbs the whole of the radiation of every wavelength
falling on it, reflecting and transmitting none (`a = 1, r = 0, t = 0`). It is
also the best possible emitter at any given temperature (`e = 1`).

---

### Q A31
Distinguish between absorptive power and emissive power.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ABSORPTIVE POWER a  =  energy absorbed / energy incident
                      -> a pure RATIO, DIMENSIONLESS, NO UNIT
                      -> for a black body, a = 1

  EMISSIVE POWER e    =  energy emitted per unit area per unit time
                      -> UNIT: W m^-2 ,  DIMENSIONS: [ M T^-3 ]
```

---

### Q A32
State Kirchhoff's law of radiation.

**ANSWER:** At a given temperature and for a given wavelength, the ratio of the
emissive power to the absorptive power is the same for all bodies and is equal
to the emissive power of a perfectly black body at that temperature
(`e / a = E`). In short: **a good absorber is a good emitter.**

---

### Q A33
Why is the bottom of a cooking vessel blackened while its sides are polished?

**ANSWER:** By Kirchhoff's law a black surface is the best **absorber**, so the
blackened bottom takes in the maximum heat from the flame. A polished surface is
a poor **emitter**, so polished sides waste the least heat by radiation.

---

### Q A34
State the Stefan-Boltzmann law and give the value of Stefan's constant.

**ANSWER:** The total energy radiated per unit area per unit time by a perfectly
black body is directly proportional to the fourth power of its absolute
temperature.
```
      E  =  sigma T^4         sigma = 5.67 x 10^-8 W m^-2 K^-4
```

---

### Q A35
State Newton's law of cooling.

**ANSWER:** The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the
difference between its temperature and that of its surroundings, provided that
difference is small.
```
      - dT/dt  =  k ( T  -  Ts )
```

---

### Q A36
State Wien's displacement law.

**ANSWER:** The wavelength at which a black body radiates the maximum energy is
inversely proportional to its absolute temperature.
```
      lambda_m  x  T  =  b  =  2.9 x 10^-3  m K
```

---

### Q A37
What is the solar constant? Give its value.

**ANSWER:** The solar energy received per unit area per unit time by a surface
held perpendicular to the Sun's rays, just outside the Earth's atmosphere, at
the Earth's mean distance from the Sun.
```
      S  =  1.388 x 10^3  W/m^2   (about 1.4 kW/m^2)
```

---

### Q A38
Which mode of heat transfer requires no material medium? Give an example.

**ANSWER:** **RADIATION.** Example: the Sun's heat reaching the Earth across
150 million km of empty space.

### Q B1  *** MOST ASKED ***
Show that the coefficient of areal expansion is twice, and the coefficient of
cubical expansion is three times, the coefficient of linear expansion.

**ANSWER:**

```
  PART 1 :  beta = 2 alpha

  Take a square plate of side L, area A = L^2. Heat it through dT.

     Each side becomes      L' = L ( 1 + alpha dT )

     New area               A' = L'^2 = L^2 ( 1 + alpha dT )^2

     Expand                 ( 1 + alpha dT )^2
                              = 1 + 2 alpha dT + (alpha dT)^2

     Since alpha is about 10^-5, the term (alpha dT)^2 is about 10^-10
     and is NEGLECTED.

                            A' = A ( 1 + 2 alpha dT )

     Compare with           A' = A ( 1 + beta dT )

                                beta = 2 alpha


  PART 2 :  gamma = 3 alpha

  Take a cube of side L, volume V = L^3. Heat it through dT.

     Each side becomes      L' = L ( 1 + alpha dT )

     New volume             V' = L'^3 = L^3 ( 1 + alpha dT )^3

     Expand                 ( 1 + x )^3 = 1 + 3x + 3x^2 + x^3
                            with x = alpha dT

     Neglect x^2 and x^3 (about 10^-10 and 10^-15).

                            V' = V ( 1 + 3 alpha dT )

     Compare with           V' = V ( 1 + gamma dT )

                                gamma = 3 alpha


  HENCE           alpha : beta : gamma  =  1 : 2 : 3
```

> Write the sentence "higher powers of alpha dT are neglected because alpha is
> of the order 10^-5" — it is worth a mark on its own.

### Q B2
A steel rod of cross-sectional area 1 cm^2 is clamped rigidly between two walls
at 20 degC and heated to 60 degC. Find the thermal stress and the force on the
clamps. `Y = 2.0 x 10^11 N/m^2`, `alpha = 1.2 x 10^-5 /degC`.

**ANSWER:**
```
  dT = 60 - 20 = 40 degC
  A  = 1 cm^2 = 1 x 10^-4 m^2

  STRESS = Y alpha dT
         = (2.0 x 10^11)(1.2 x 10^-5)(40)
         = (2.4 x 10^6)(40)
         = 9.6 x 10^7 N/m^2

  FORCE  = stress x A
         = (9.6 x 10^7)(1 x 10^-4)
         = 9.6 x 10^3 N   =  9600 N
```

### Q B5
The apparent coefficient of expansion of mercury in a glass vessel is
1.50 x 10^-4 /degC. If `alpha(glass) = 0.9 x 10^-5 /degC`, find the real
coefficient of cubical expansion of mercury.

**ANSWER:**
```
  gamma(glass) = 3 x alpha(glass) = 3 x 0.9 x 10^-5
               = 2.7 x 10^-5 = 0.27 x 10^-4 /degC

  gamma(real) = gamma(apparent) + gamma(vessel)
              = 1.50 x 10^-4 + 0.27 x 10^-4
              = 1.77 x 10^-4 /degC

  ANSWER: about 1.8 x 10^-4 /degC
```

### Q B6
Explain the principle of calorimetry. 100 g of water at 90 degC is mixed with
400 g of water at 20 degC. Find the final temperature.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE OF CALORIMETRY
  When two bodies at different temperatures are mixed in an insulated
  vessel, heat flows from the hotter to the colder until they reach a
  common temperature, and

           HEAT LOST BY THE HOT BODY  =  HEAT GAINED BY THE COLD BODY

  This is simply the law of conservation of energy.

  NUMERICAL
  Let the final temperature be T.

     100 x c x (90 - T)  =  400 x c x (T - 20)      (c cancels)

     100(90 - T) = 400(T - 20)
     9000 - 100 T = 400 T - 8000
     9000 + 8000  = 500 T
     17000        = 500 T
     T            = 34 degC

  CHECK: 34 lies between 20 and 90.   ANSWER: 34 degC
```

### Q B8
50 g of ice at 0 degC is dropped into 200 g of water at 40 degC. Find the final
temperature. (`Lf = 80 cal/g`, `c(water) = 1 cal/g/degC`)

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1 - CHECK whether all the ice can melt.

     Heat needed to melt all the ice = 50 x 80 = 4000 cal
     Heat available if the water falls to 0 degC = 200 x 1 x 40 = 8000 cal

     8000 > 4000  ->  all the ice melts, with heat to spare.

  STEP 2 - find the final temperature T.

     HEAT GAINED = 4000 + 50 x 1 x (T - 0) = 4000 + 50 T
     HEAT LOST   = 200 x 1 x (40 - T)      = 8000 - 200 T

     4000 + 50 T = 8000 - 200 T
     250 T = 4000
     T = 16 degC

  ANSWER: 16 degC
```

### Q B9
10 g of steam at 100 degC is passed into 100 g of ice at 0 degC. What is the
final state of the mixture? (`Lv = 540 cal/g`, `Lf = 80 cal/g`)

**ANSWER:**
```
  HEAT AVAILABLE from the steam if it goes all the way to 0 degC:

     condensing        = 10 x 540 = 5400 cal
     cooling 100 -> 0  = 10 x 1 x 100 = 1000 cal
                                        --------
                              TOTAL   =  6400 cal

  HEAT NEEDED to melt ALL the ice = 100 x 80 = 8000 cal

  6400 < 8000  ->  NOT all the ice melts.

  Mass of ice melted = 6400 / 80 = 80 g

  FINAL STATE:
        temperature   =  0 degC
        ice left      =  100 - 80 = 20 g
        water present =  80 + 10  = 90 g
```

### Q B11
Two slabs of equal thickness and equal area, with thermal conductivities `K` and
`2K`, are joined face to face. The outer faces are kept at 100 degC and 0 degC.
Find the temperature of the junction and the equivalent conductivity.

**ANSWER:**
```
       100 degC  +--------+--------+  0 degC
                 |   K    |   2K   |
                 |   L    |   L    |
                 +--------+--------+
                          ^ junction at T

  The SAME heat flows through both slabs (series):

        K A (100 - T)        2K A (T - 0)
      ----------------  =  ----------------
              L                    L

        100 - T = 2 T
        100 = 3 T
        T = 33.3 degC

  EQUIVALENT CONDUCTIVITY (equal thicknesses):

        K_eq = 2 K1 K2 / (K1 + K2)
             = 2 (K)(2K) / (K + 2K)
             = 4K^2 / 3K
             = 1.33 K

  The junction lies nearer the cold end because the second slab
  conducts better and needs a smaller share of the temperature drop.
```

### Q B13
Explain the formation of land and sea breezes.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BASIS: water has a much HIGHER SPECIFIC HEAT than land, so land heats up
         and cools down far more quickly than the sea.

  SEA BREEZE (daytime, from sea to land)
     * The land warms rapidly; the air above it becomes hot and less dense
       and RISES.
     * The pressure over the land falls.
     * Cooler air from over the sea flows in to take its place.
     * This onshore wind is the SEA BREEZE.

                    hot air rises
                        ^ ^ ^
       ~~~~~~~~~~~~~    | | |    ############
       ~~ COOL SEA ~~   | | |    ## HOT LAND ##
              ----->  ----->  ----->     = SEA BREEZE

  LAND BREEZE (night-time, from land to sea)
     * At night the land cools quickly, but the sea stays warm.
     * The air above the sea is now warmer, less dense, and RISES.
     * Cooler air flows out from the land to replace it.
     * This offshore wind is the LAND BREEZE.

              <-----  <-----  <-----     = LAND BREEZE
       ~~ WARM SEA ~~            # COOL LAND #

  Both are examples of NATURAL CONVECTION.
```

### Q B14  *** MOST ASKED ***
State and prove Kirchhoff's law of radiation. Give two applications.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT
  At a given temperature and for a given wavelength, the ratio of the
  emissive power to the absorptive power is the same for all bodies, and
  is equal to the emissive power of a perfectly black body at that
  temperature.

                      e
                    -----  =  E
                      a

  PROOF
     Place a body B (absorptive power a, emissive power e) inside a
     perfectly black enclosure maintained at a constant temperature T.

        +-----------------------------------+
        |  black enclosure at temperature T |
        |        +-----------+              |
        |   -->  |  body B   |  -->         |
        |        |  (a, e)   |              |
        |        +-----------+              |
        +-----------------------------------+

     1. After some time B reaches thermal equilibrium with the enclosure
        and stays at the temperature T.

     2. At equilibrium its temperature does not change, so

              energy absorbed per second = energy emitted per second

     3. Let E be the black-body radiation falling on unit area of B per
        second from the enclosure.

              absorbed per unit area per second  =  a E
              emitted  per unit area per second  =  e

     4. Equating:      e  =  a E      ->      e / a  =  E

     5. E depends only on the temperature of the enclosure, not on the
        material of B. So e/a is the SAME for every body.

                            HENCE PROVED.

  MEANING:  A GOOD ABSORBER IS A GOOD EMITTER.

  APPLICATIONS
     1. The bottom of a cooking vessel is blackened (best absorber) while
        its sides are polished (poor emitter, so less heat is wasted).
     2. A thermos flask is silvered on both walls - a silvered surface is
        both a poor absorber and a poor emitter, so radiation loss is
        minimised.
     3. THE FRAUNHOFER LINES: the Sun's cooler chromosphere absorbs exactly
        those wavelengths that its own vapours would emit, producing the
        dark lines seen in the solar spectrum.
```

### Q B15
State the Stefan-Boltzmann law. A black sphere of radius 5 cm at 327 degC is
placed in surroundings at 27 degC. Find the net rate of heat loss.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT
  The energy radiated per unit area per unit time by a perfectly black body
  is proportional to the fourth power of its absolute temperature.

        E = sigma T^4 ,      sigma = 5.67 x 10^-8 W m^-2 K^-4

  For a body at T in surroundings at Ts:

        P(net) = e sigma A ( T^4 - Ts^4 )

  NUMERICAL
     T  = 327 + 273 = 600 K       Ts = 27 + 273 = 300 K
     A  = 4 pi r^2 = 4 x 3.1416 x (0.05)^2 = 0.031416 m^2

     T^4  = (600)^4 = 1.296 x 10^11
     Ts^4 = (300)^4 = 0.081 x 10^11
     difference     = 1.215 x 10^11

     P = 1 x (5.67 x 10^-8)(0.031416)(1.215 x 10^11)
       = (1.781 x 10^-9)(1.215 x 10^11)
       = 216.4 W

  ANSWER: about 216 W
```

### Q B16  *** MOST ASKED ***
State Newton's law of cooling and derive it from the Stefan-Boltzmann law.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT
  The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the
  difference between its temperature and that of its surroundings,
  provided that difference is SMALL.

              - dQ/dt  =  K ( T - Ts )

  DERIVATION FROM STEFAN'S LAW

  Step 1  For a body at temperature T in surroundings at Ts,

             - dQ/dt  =  e sigma A ( T^4  -  Ts^4 )

  Step 2  Let the excess temperature be small:  T = Ts + x  (x small)

             T^4 - Ts^4  =  ( Ts + x )^4  -  Ts^4

                         =  Ts^4 ( 1 + x/Ts )^4  -  Ts^4

  Step 3  Expand by the binomial theorem and keep only the first order
          term, since x/Ts is small:

             ( 1 + y )^4  =  1 + 4y + 6y^2 + ...   ~   1 + 4y

             T^4 - Ts^4  ~  Ts^4 ( 1 + 4x/Ts ) - Ts^4  =  4 Ts^3 x

  Step 4  Therefore

             - dQ/dt  =  4 e sigma A Ts^3  x  =  K ( T - Ts )

          where K = 4 e sigma A Ts^3 is a constant for a given body
          and given surroundings.

  Step 5  Since dQ = m c dT,

             - dT/dt  =  ( K / m c ) ( T - Ts )  =  k ( T - Ts )

                            HENCE PROVED.

  LIMITATION: because higher powers were neglected, the law holds only for
  SMALL excess temperatures (about 30 K or less).
```

### Q B19
Explain the greenhouse effect. Why is it important, and why is it now a problem?

**ANSWER:**
```
  WHAT IT IS
    1. Sunlight (short-wavelength, peak about 500 nm) passes easily through
       the atmosphere and warms the Earth's surface.
    2. The warm surface (about 288 K) re-radiates. By Wien's law its peak
       lies at 2.9e-3 / 288 = 1.0 x 10^-5 m = 10 micrometre, in the
       INFRARED.
    3. Greenhouse gases - CO2, water vapour, CH4, N2O, O3 and CFCs -
       strongly ABSORB infrared and re-radiate part of it back downwards.
    4. The surface therefore stays warmer than it otherwise would.

    It is called the greenhouse effect because a glass greenhouse behaves
    the same way: glass lets visible light in but is opaque to infrared.

  WHY IT MATTERS
    Without any greenhouse effect the Earth would average about -18 degC.
    With it, the average is about +15 degC. The natural greenhouse effect
    is worth about 33 degC and is what makes life possible.

  WHY IT IS A PROBLEM NOW
    Burning fossil fuels has sharply raised the CO2 concentration. The
    ENHANCED greenhouse effect traps more heat, causing GLOBAL WARMING:
    melting polar ice, rising sea level and disturbed monsoon patterns.
```

Explain thermal expansion of solids. Define the three coefficients, establish the

