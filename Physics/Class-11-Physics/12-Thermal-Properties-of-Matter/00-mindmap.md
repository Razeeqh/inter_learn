# Thermal Properties of Matter — Mind Map

**Heat is energy on the move. This chapter tells you what heat DOES to matter
(makes it bigger, makes it hotter, changes its state) and HOW it travels
(conduction, convection, radiation). It is mostly formulas + a few named laws,
so a weak student can score 8-10 marks here without being clever.**

## THE 60-SECOND VERSION

```
  1.  TEMPERATURE tells you how hot something is.
      HEAT is the energy that flows from hot to cold.
      They are NOT the same thing. (Sea = low temp, huge heat.
       Spark = high temp, tiny heat.)

  2.  Give a solid heat and it EXPANDS:
         length  by alpha        area by beta = 2 alpha
         volume  by gamma = 3 alpha

  3.  Give a body heat and its TEMPERATURE rises:
         Q = m c dT

  4.  Give a body heat while it CHANGES STATE and the temperature
      does NOT rise at all:
         Q = m L

  5.  Heat travels in exactly THREE ways:
         CONDUCTION  - through solids, particle to particle, no bulk motion
         CONVECTION  - fluid itself moves, carrying heat with it
         RADIATION   - electromagnetic waves, needs NO medium

  6.  For radiation there are FOUR named laws you must be able to state:
         Kirchhoff  ->  good absorber = good emitter
         Stefan     ->  E = sigma T^4
         Newton     ->  cooling rate is proportional to excess temperature
         Wien       ->  lambda_max x T = constant
```

That is the entire chapter. Everything else is detail.

## THE FIVE "WHY" QUESTIONS THE BOARD LOVES

```
  1. Why do fish survive under a frozen lake?
        -> anomalous expansion: 4 degC water is densest, sinks,
           ice floats on top and insulates the water below.

  2. Why does a metal chair feel colder than a wooden one on the
     same cold morning?
        -> metal has HIGH thermal conductivity, so it carries heat
           away from your skin fast. Both are at the SAME temperature.

  3. Why is a gap left between two rails / two slabs of a bridge?
        -> to allow for linear expansion in summer, otherwise huge
           thermal stress buckles them.

  4. Why does a pendulum clock lose time in summer?
        -> the rod expands, L increases, T = 2 pi sqrt(L/g) increases,
           so it ticks slower and loses time.

  5. Why is the bottom of a cooking vessel blackened?
        -> black surfaces are the best ABSORBERS of radiation
           (Kirchhoff: a good absorber is a good emitter).
```
