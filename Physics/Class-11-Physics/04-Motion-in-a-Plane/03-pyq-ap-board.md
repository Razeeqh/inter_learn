## Topic: Scalars, vectors and their types

### Q A1
Distinguish between a scalar and a vector. Give one example of each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SCALAR  -  has MAGNITUDE only.  Adds by ordinary arithmetic.
             Example: mass, 5 kg.

  VECTOR  -  has MAGNITUDE and DIRECTION. Adds by the parallelogram law.
             Example: force, 5 N due east.
```

---

### Q A2
Is electric current a scalar or a vector? Justify.

**ANSWER:** Electric current is a **SCALAR**. Although it has a direction of flow,
currents at a junction add **arithmetically**, not by the parallelogram law, so it
does not satisfy the definition of a vector.

---

Define a unit vector. Write its use.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A UNIT VECTOR is a vector whose magnitude is exactly ONE.

              A
    A-hat = -----          so       A  =  | A |  x  A-hat
            | A |

  USE: it specifies only the DIRECTION of a vector.
       It is dimensionless and has no unit.
```

What is a null vector? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A NULL (ZERO) vector has magnitude ZERO and an indeterminate direction.

  Examples:  1. A + ( -A )
             2. The displacement of a body that returns to its starting point.

**ANSWER:** A vector drawn from a fixed origin O to the position of the particle.
```
    r  =  x i  +  y j  +  z k          | r | = sqrt( x^2 + y^2 + z^2 )
```

**ANSWER:**

## Topic: Addition of vectors

### Q A8
State the parallelogram law of vector addition.

**ANSWER:** If two vectors acting simultaneously at a point are represented in
magnitude and direction by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from that
point, their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the **diagonal of
the parallelogram passing through that point**.

---

### Q A9
Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles. Find the magnitude of the resultant.

**ANSWER:**
```
  R = sqrt( A^2 + B^2 + 2AB cos 90 )      and  cos 90 = 0

    = sqrt( 3^2 + 4^2 )  =  sqrt( 9 + 16 )  =  sqrt(25)  =  5 N

  Direction:  tan alpha = 4/3 = 1.333  ->  alpha = 53 deg 8 min from the 3 N force.
```

---

### Q A10
Two forces of 5 N and 10 N act at a point. What are the maximum and minimum possible
values of their resultant?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MAXIMUM  (theta = 0)    :  R = 5 + 10  =  15 N
  MINIMUM  (theta = 180)  :  R = |5 - 10| =   5 N
```

### Q A11
When is | A + B | = | A - B | ?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A^2 + B^2 + 2AB cos theta  =  A^2 + B^2 - 2AB cos theta

           4 AB cos theta  =  0     ->     cos theta  =  0

           theta  =  90 degrees

  The two vectors must be PERPENDICULAR.
```

### Q A12
Two equal forces have a resultant equal in magnitude to either of them. Find the
angle between them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  For two equal vectors of magnitude A:      R = 2 A cos ( theta / 2 )

  Given R = A :        A = 2 A cos ( theta / 2 )

                       cos ( theta / 2 ) = 1/2   ->   theta / 2 = 60 deg

                       theta  =  120 degrees
```

### Q A13
Find the angle between the vectors ( i + j ) and ( i - j ).

**ANSWER:**
```
  A . B  =  (1)(1) + (1)(-1)  =  1 - 1  =  0

  Since the dot product is zero, the angle is  90 degrees.
```

---

### Q A14
Can the resultant of two unequal vectors be zero? Give a reason.

**ANSWER:** **No.** The resultant is zero only when the two vectors are equal in
magnitude and exactly opposite in direction. If A is not equal to B, the minimum
resultant is `|A - B|`, which is not zero.

## Topic: Projectile motion

### Q A15
Define a projectile and its trajectory.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PROJECTILE  -  a body thrown into space which then moves ONLY under
                 the action of gravity (air resistance neglected).

  TRAJECTORY  -  the path followed by the projectile. It is a PARABOLA.
```

---

### Q A16
What is the velocity of a projectile at the highest point of its path?

**ANSWER:**
```
  At the top the VERTICAL component is zero, but the horizontal component
  is unchanged.

        v (at top)  =  u cos theta      -  purely HORIZONTAL

  It is NOT zero (except for a body thrown vertically upward).
```

---

### Q A17
What is the acceleration of a projectile at its highest point?

**ANSWER:** It is **g = 9.8 m/s^2 directed vertically downwards**. The acceleration
of a projectile is g at every instant of the flight, including the topmost point.

### Q A19
Name two angles of projection for which the horizontal range is the same.

**ANSWER:** **theta and (90 - theta)**, because
`sin 2(90 - theta) = sin(180 - 2theta) = sin 2theta`.
Example: 30 degrees and 60 degrees.

### Q A20
A ball is projected with a speed of 20 m/s at 30 degrees to the horizontal.
Find its time of flight. (g = 10 m/s^2)

**ANSWER:**
```
        2 u sin theta      2 x 20 x sin 30      2 x 20 x 0.5      20
   T =  --------------- =  ----------------- =  ------------- =  ----  =  2 s
              g                   10                  10          10
```

---

### Q A21
A body is projected horizontally from a height of 20 m with a speed of 10 m/s.
How long does it take to reach the ground? (g = 10 m/s^2)

**ANSWER:**
```
   For a horizontal projectile the initial VERTICAL velocity is zero, so

        T = sqrt( 2 h / g ) = sqrt( 2 x 20 / 10 ) = sqrt(4) = 2 seconds

   (The horizontal speed of 10 m/s does not affect the time.)
```

---

### Q A22
Two bodies are dropped and fired horizontally from the same height at the same
instant. Which one lands first?

**ANSWER:** **Both land at the same time.** The vertical motion is independent of the
horizontal motion, and both have zero initial vertical velocity, so both take
`T = sqrt(2h/g)`.

## Topic: Circular motion and relative velocity

### Q A23
Define angular velocity. Give its SI unit and dimensions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement.

        omega  =  d theta / dt

  SI unit     :  radian per second (rad/s)
  Dimensions  :  [ T^-1 ]        (radian is dimensionless)
```

---

### Q A24
Write the relation between linear velocity and angular velocity.

**ANSWER:**
```
        v  =  r omega

  where r is the radius of the circular path and omega is in rad/s.
```

---

### Q A25
What is centripetal acceleration? Write its expression and direction.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The acceleration of a body in uniform circular motion, which continuously
  changes the DIRECTION of the velocity.

        a_c  =  v^2 / r   =   r omega^2

  DIRECTION: along the radius, always TOWARDS THE CENTRE of the circle.
```

---

### Q A26
A body moves in a circle of radius 2 m with a constant speed of 4 m/s. Find its
centripetal acceleration.

**ANSWER:**
```
        v^2       4^2       16
  a  = ----- =  ------  =  ----  =  8 m/s^2   directed towards the centre.
         r         2         2
```

---

### Q A27
Does the centripetal force do any work on a body in uniform circular motion?

**ANSWER:** **No.** The centripetal force is always perpendicular to the
displacement, so `W = F s cos 90 = 0`. That is why the speed stays constant.

---

### Q A28
Find the angular velocity of the seconds hand of a clock.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The seconds hand completes one revolution in T = 60 s.

           2 pi        2 x 3.14
  omega = ------  =  -------------  =  0.105  rad / s
             T             60
```

### Q A29
Define relative velocity. Write its expression for two bodies A and B.

**ANSWER:** The velocity of one body as it appears to an observer moving with the
other body.
```
        v(AB)  =  v(A)  -  v(B)        and       v(BA) = - v(AB)
```

---

### Q A30
A man walking in vertically falling rain has to tilt his umbrella. In which
direction, and why?

**ANSWER:** **Forward, in the direction of his motion.** Relative to the man, the
rain has a horizontal component `-v_m` (backwards), so the rain appears to come from
the front at an angle `tan theta = v_m / v_r` from the vertical.

### Q B1
State the parallelogram law of vector addition and derive an expression for the
magnitude of the resultant of two vectors.

**ANSWER:**

**Statement:** If two vectors acting simultaneously at a point are represented in
magnitude and direction by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from that
point, their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the diagonal of
the parallelogram passing through that point.

```
                         B                          C
                          *------------------------*
                         /|                       /|
                    B   / |                  R   / | B sin theta
                       /  |                     /  |
                      /theta                   /alpha
                     *--------------------------*---*
                     O            A             A    D
                                            <---------->
                                             B cos theta

  DERIVATION
     OA = A ,  OB = AC = B ,  angle AOB = angle CAD = theta ,  OC = R

     In triangle ACD :    CD = B sin theta        AD = B cos theta

     In right triangle ODC :

        OC^2  =  OD^2  +  CD^2
        R^2   =  ( A + B cos theta )^2  +  ( B sin theta )^2
              =  A^2 + 2AB cos theta + B^2 cos^2 theta + B^2 sin^2 theta
              =  A^2 + 2AB cos theta + B^2 ( cos^2 theta + sin^2 theta )

  +--------------------------------------------------------+
  |      R  =  sqrt( A^2  +  B^2  +  2 A B cos theta )     |
  +--------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Q B2
Derive an expression for the direction of the resultant of two vectors, and discuss
the cases theta = 0, 90 and 180 degrees.

**ANSWER:**
```
  From the same figure, in right triangle ODC:

                       CD                B sin theta
        tan alpha  =  ------   =   ---------------------
                       OD           A  +  B cos theta

  where alpha is the angle the resultant makes with A.

  SPECIAL CASES

     theta = 0    :  R = sqrt(A^2 + B^2 + 2AB) = A + B          MAXIMUM
                     tan alpha = 0 / (A+B) = 0  ->  alpha = 0
                     The resultant is along A.

     theta = 90   :  R = sqrt(A^2 + B^2)
                     tan alpha = B / A

     theta = 180  :  R = sqrt(A^2 + B^2 - 2AB) = | A - B |      MINIMUM
                     The resultant is along the larger vector.

  Hence for any two vectors:      | A - B |  <=  R  <=  A + B
```

### Q B3
What is meant by the resolution of a vector? Obtain the rectangular components of a
vector in a plane.

**ANSWER:**

**Resolution** is the process of splitting a single vector into two or more vectors
which together produce the same effect. When the parts are mutually perpendicular
they are called **rectangular components**.

```
              y
              ^
              |                    * P
              |                  / |
              |         A     /    |
              |            /       |  Ay
              |         /          |
              | theta/             |
              O--------------------+------> x
              |<------- Ax ------->|

  Drop perpendiculars from the head of A onto the two axes.

  In the right-angled triangle:

        Ax                                   Ay
       ----  = cos theta                    ----  = sin theta
        A                                    A

  +---------------------------------------------------------+
  |     Ax  =  A cos theta          Ay  =  A sin theta       |
  |                                                         |
  |     A   =  Ax i  +  Ay j                                |
  |                                                         |
  |     A   =  sqrt( Ax^2 + Ay^2 )    tan theta = Ay / Ax   |
  +---------------------------------------------------------+

  In three dimensions:   A = Ax i + Ay j + Az k
                         A = sqrt( Ax^2 + Ay^2 + Az^2 )
```

### Q B6
Show that the trajectory of a projectile fired at an angle to the horizontal is a
parabola.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Take the point of projection as the origin.
  Launch speed u at angle theta.  ux = u cos theta ,  uy = u sin theta.

  HORIZONTAL (ax = 0):        x  =  ( u cos theta ) t

                                              x
                              ->      t  =  ------------          ... (1)
                                            u cos theta

  VERTICAL (ay = -g):         y  =  ( u sin theta ) t  -  (1/2) g t^2   ... (2)

  Put (1) into (2):

                         /      x      \       1     /      x      \ 2
     y = u sin theta x  |  -----------  |  -  --- g |  -----------  |
                         \ u cos theta /       2     \ u cos theta /

  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                        g                            |
  |     y  =  ( tan theta ) x    -    -------------------  x^2          |
  |                                    2 u^2 cos^2 theta                |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+

  This has the form   y  =  A x  -  B x^2   with A and B constants:
  second degree in x, first degree in y.

        ***  Hence the trajectory is a PARABOLA.  ***
```

### Q B9
Derive the relation v = r omega, and hence obtain the expression for centripetal
acceleration.

**ANSWER:**
```
  RELATION v = r omega
     For a particle on a circle of radius r turning through an angle theta,

              arc  s  =  r theta

     Differentiate with respect to time (r is constant):

              ds            d theta
             ----  =  r  x  ---------           ->      v  =  r omega
              dt              dt

  CENTRIPETAL ACCELERATION
     Let the particle move from P to Q in a small time dt, sweeping an angle
     d.theta. The speed stays v but the direction turns through d.theta.

     The velocity triangle is SIMILAR to the position triangle, so

              | dv |          | dr |                   v
             --------  =  ----------      ->  | dv | = --- | dr |
                v              r                       r

     Divide by dt and let dt -> 0 (then | dr | / dt -> v):

  +-------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                v^2                                          |
  |     a_c   =   -----   =   r omega^2   =   v omega           |
  |                 r                                           |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------+

     DIRECTION: as d.theta -> 0, dv becomes perpendicular to v, i.e. along
     the radius pointing TOWARDS THE CENTRE. Hence "centripetal".
```

### Q B10
Explain non-uniform circular motion. Write the expressions for the tangential and
radial accelerations and for the net acceleration.

**ANSWER:**
```
  In NON-UNIFORM circular motion the SPEED also changes with time, so there
  are two perpendicular accelerations at every instant.

                         a_t
                          ^
                          |
              . . . . . . *. . . . .
          .               |\         .
        .            a_r  | \  a     .
      .            <------*  \        .
     *              O                 *

  RADIAL (centripetal)      a_r  =  v^2 / r  =  r omega^2
        - directed to the centre
        - changes only the DIRECTION of the velocity

  TANGENTIAL                a_t  =  dv / dt  =  r alpha
        - directed along the tangent
        - changes only the MAGNITUDE (the speed)

  NET ACCELERATION          a  =  sqrt( a_r^2  +  a_t^2 )

                            tan phi  =  a_t / a_r

  If a_t = 0 the motion is UNIFORM circular motion and a = v^2 / r only.
```

### Q B11
A river 400 m wide flows at 2 m/s. A boat can row at 4 m/s in still water.
(a) Find the shortest time to cross and the drift.
(b) Find the direction in which the boat must be rowed to reach the point directly
opposite, and the time taken.

**ANSWER:**
```
  GIVEN:  d = 400 m ,  v_r = 2 m/s ,  v_b = 4 m/s

  (a) SHORTEST TIME — row straight across
          t  =  d / v_b  =  400 / 4  =  100 s
          drift  =  v_r x t  =  2 x 100  =  200 m downstream
          resultant speed = sqrt( 16 + 4 ) = sqrt(20) = 4.47 m/s

  (b) SHORTEST PATH — land directly opposite
          The upstream component must cancel the current:

              v_b sin theta  =  v_r
              sin theta  =  2 / 4  =  0.5     ->    theta = 30 degrees

          Row 30 degrees UPSTREAM from the perpendicular to the bank.

          speed across  =  sqrt( v_b^2 - v_r^2 )  =  sqrt( 16 - 4 )
                        =  sqrt(12)  =  3.46 m/s

          t  =  400 / 3.46  =  115.6 s

  Note: the shortest PATH takes longer than the shortest TIME.
```

            H  =  R(max) / 4  =  100 / 4  =  25 m

       CHECK directly:
            H = u^2 sin^2 45 / 2g = 1000 x 0.5 / 20 = 500/20 = 25 m   correct.
```

### Q C1  *** THE MOST REPEATED LONG ANSWER IN THIS CHAPTER ***

**A body is projected with a speed u at an angle theta with the horizontal. Show that
its path is a parabola, and derive expressions for its time of flight, maximum height
and horizontal range.**

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT (how the examiner awards the 8 marks)
     Diagram + components ................ 1
     Proof that the path is a parabola ... 2
     Time of flight ...................... 2
     Maximum height ...................... 1.5
     Horizontal range .................... 1.5
```

**DIAGRAM**

```
        y
        ^
        |                    v = u cos theta
        |                    ....*....
        |               ....           ....
        |            ..                    ..
        |          ..     |                  ..
   u    |        ..       | H                  ..
    \   |      ..         |                      ..
     \  |    ..           |                        ..
      \ |  ..             |                          ..
       \|.theta           |                            ..
   -----O------------------+------------------------------*--------> x
        |<-------------------- R ------------------------>|
```

**SET-UP**

```
  Take the point of projection O as origin, the horizontal as x, vertical as y.

     Initial horizontal velocity   ux  =  u cos theta
     Initial vertical velocity     uy  =  u sin theta

     Horizontal acceleration       ax  =  0        (gravity has no horizontal part)
     Vertical acceleration         ay  =  -g

  ASSUMPTION: the horizontal and vertical motions are completely INDEPENDENT.
              They share only the same time t.
```

**PART 1 — THE PATH IS A PARABOLA**

```
  Horizontal:      x  =  ( u cos theta ) t

                                     x
                          t   =   ------------                    ... (1)
                                  u cos theta

  Vertical:        y  =  ( u sin theta ) t  -  (1/2) g t^2        ... (2)

  Substituting (1) in (2):

                             /     x     \      1      /     x     \ 2
      y  =  ( u sin theta ) |  ---------  |  -  --- g  |  ---------  |
                             \ u cos th. /      2       \ u cos th. /

  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                          g                          |
  |       y   =   ( tan theta ) x    -   -------------------  x^2       |
  |                                       2 u^2 cos^2 theta             |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------------+

  For a given u and theta, tan theta and g/(2u^2 cos^2 theta) are constants,
  so the equation is of the form   y = A x - B x^2 .

  It is of the SECOND degree in x and the FIRST degree in y.

        ******  THEREFORE THE PATH IS A PARABOLA.  ******
```

**PART 2 — TIME OF FLIGHT**

```
  The projectile lands at the same horizontal level from which it was thrown,
  so the NET vertical displacement over the whole flight is ZERO.

     Using   y = uy t - (1/2) g t^2   with  y = 0 and t = T :

        0  =  ( u sin theta ) T  -  (1/2) g T^2

        (1/2) g T^2  =  ( u sin theta ) T

     Dividing by T (T is not zero):

  +-------------------------------------+
  |                  2 u sin theta      |
  |         T   =   ----------------    |
  |                        g            |
  +-------------------------------------+

  Time of ascent = time of descent = T/2 = ( u sin theta ) / g.
```

**PART 3 — MAXIMUM HEIGHT**

```
  At the highest point the vertical component of velocity becomes zero.

     Using   vy^2 = uy^2 - 2 g y   with  vy = 0 , y = H :

        0  =  ( u sin theta )^2  -  2 g H

  +----------------------------------------+
  |                  u^2 sin^2 theta       |
  |         H   =   -------------------    |
  |                        2 g             |
  +----------------------------------------+
```

**PART 4 — HORIZONTAL RANGE**

```
  The horizontal velocity is constant throughout, so

        R  =  ( horizontal velocity )  x  ( time of flight )
           =  ( u cos theta )  x  T

                                     2 u sin theta
           =  ( u cos theta )  x   -----------------
                                          g

              2 u^2 sin theta cos theta
           =  ---------------------------
                          g

  Using   2 sin theta cos theta = sin 2 theta :

  +----------------------------------------+
  |                  u^2 sin 2 theta       |
  |         R   =   -------------------    |
  |                        g               |
  +----------------------------------------+
```

**CONCLUSIONS WORTH ADDING (they often carry the last mark)**

```
  1.  R is maximum when sin 2 theta = 1, i.e. theta = 45 deg,
      and then R(max) = u^2 / g.

  2.  theta and (90 - theta) give the same range.

  3.  At the highest point v = u cos theta (NOT zero) and a = g downward.

  4.  The speed on landing equals the speed of projection u.
```

### Q C2

**State the parallelogram law of vector addition. Derive expressions for the
magnitude and the direction of the resultant of two vectors, and discuss the special
cases.**

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
     Statement ............................ 1
     Labelled figure ...................... 1
     Derivation of R ...................... 3
     Derivation of alpha .................. 2
     Special cases ........................ 1
```

**STATEMENT:** If two vectors acting simultaneously at a point are represented in
magnitude and direction by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from that
point, then their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the diagonal
of the parallelogram passing through that point.

```
  FIGURE

                         B                          C
                          *------------------------*
                         /|                       /|
                        / |                      / |
                   B   /  |                 R   /  |  B sin theta
                      /   |                    /   |
                     /theta                   /alpha
                    *--------------------------*---*
                    O            A             A    D
                                            <-------->
                                            B cos theta

     OA = A ,  OB = B , angle AOB = theta , OC = R , alpha = angle COA
     AC = OB = B  (opposite sides of a parallelogram)
     CD is perpendicular to OA produced.

  DERIVATION OF THE MAGNITUDE

     Since AC is parallel to OB,  angle CAD = theta.

     In right-angled triangle ACD:
            CD  =  AC sin theta  =  B sin theta
            AD  =  AC cos theta  =  B cos theta

     In right-angled triangle ODC:
            OC^2  =  OD^2 + CD^2
            R^2   =  ( OA + AD )^2  +  CD^2
                  =  ( A + B cos theta )^2  +  ( B sin theta )^2
                  =  A^2 + 2 A B cos theta + B^2 cos^2 theta + B^2 sin^2 theta
                  =  A^2 + B^2 + 2 A B cos theta

  +----------------------------------------------------------+
  |        R  =  sqrt( A^2  +  B^2  +  2 A B cos theta )     |
  +----------------------------------------------------------+

  DERIVATION OF THE DIRECTION

     In right-angled triangle ODC:

                          CD               B sin theta
            tan alpha = ------  =  ---------------------------
                          OD          A   +   B cos theta

  +----------------------------------------------------------+
  |                        B sin theta                       |
  |        tan alpha =  ---------------------                |
  |                      A  +  B cos theta                   |
  +----------------------------------------------------------+

  SPECIAL CASES

     +-------------+----------------------+--------------------------+
     |  theta      |   R                  |   alpha                  |
     +-------------+----------------------+--------------------------+
     |    0 deg    |   A + B  (MAXIMUM)   |   0  (along A)           |
     |   90 deg    |   sqrt(A^2 + B^2)    |   tan alpha = B / A      |
     |  180 deg    |   | A - B | (MINIMUM)|   along the larger one   |
     +-------------+----------------------+--------------------------+

     Hence     | A - B |   <=   R   <=   A + B

     If A = B :   R = 2 A cos ( theta / 2 )  and the resultant bisects
                  the angle between them.
```

### Q C3

**A body is projected horizontally from the top of a tower of height h with a speed u.
Show that its path is a parabola. Derive expressions for its time of flight,
horizontal range and the velocity with which it strikes the ground. Also state why a
bomb released from a moving aeroplane does not fall vertically below the point of
release as seen from the ground.**

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
     Diagram + set-up ..................... 1
     Proof of the parabola ................ 2
     Time of flight ....................... 2
     Range ................................ 1
     Velocity on striking ................. 1
     Aeroplane explanation ................ 1
```

```
  DIAGRAM

        u
       ---->*----------------.
            |    .            .
            |         .        .
          h |             .     .
            |                .   .
            |                  .  .   vx = u
            |                   .  .  |
       -----+--------------------.--*-v vy = gT
            |<-------- R ---------->|

  SET-UP  (taking downward as positive)
        ux = u        uy = 0        ax = 0        ay = g
```

```
  PART 1 — THE PATH IS A PARABOLA

     Horizontal:     x  =  u t        ->     t  =  x / u

     Vertical:       y  =  uy t + (1/2) g t^2  =  0 + (1/2) g t^2

     Substituting:

                          1     / x \ 2              g
                  y  =   --- g |-----|    =    ------------  x^2
                          2     \ u /            2 u^2

  +-----------------------------------------+
  |                    g                    |
  |          y  =   --------  x^2           |
  |                  2 u^2                  |
  +-----------------------------------------+

     y is proportional to x^2 , hence the path is a PARABOLA
     (only the descending half of it).
```

```
  PART 2 — TIME OF FLIGHT

     The body falls the full height h in the time of flight T:

           h  =  (1/2) g T^2      ->      T^2 = 2h / g

  +--------------------------------+
  |       T  =  sqrt( 2 h / g )    |
  +--------------------------------+

     T does NOT contain u.  A body dropped from rest and a body fired
     horizontally from the same height reach the ground at the SAME instant.
```

```
  PART 3 — HORIZONTAL RANGE

     The horizontal velocity is unchanged, so

  +---------------------------------------+
  |     R  =  u T  =  u sqrt( 2 h / g )   |
  +---------------------------------------+
```

```
  PART 4 — VELOCITY ON STRIKING THE GROUND

     vx  =  u                        (unchanged)
     vy  =  g T  =  g sqrt(2h/g)  =  sqrt( 2 g h )

  +-----------------------------------------------------+
  |     v  =  sqrt( vx^2 + vy^2 )  =  sqrt( u^2 + 2gh ) |
  |                                                     |
  |                       sqrt( 2 g h )                 |
  |     tan beta   =   -------------------              |
  |                            u                        |
  +-----------------------------------------------------+

     where beta is the angle below the horizontal.
```

```
  PART 5 — THE BOMB AND THE AEROPLANE

     When the bomb is released it already possesses the aeroplane's
     HORIZONTAL velocity u. Gravity cannot change that horizontal velocity.

     -> To an observer on the GROUND the bomb travels forward a distance
        R = u sqrt(2h/g) while falling, so it follows a parabola and does
        NOT land vertically below the release point.

     -> To the PILOT (who keeps moving with the same horizontal velocity)
        the bomb appears to fall STRAIGHT DOWN, always staying directly
        below the aeroplane.
```

### Q C4

**What is uniform circular motion? Define angular displacement, angular velocity and
angular acceleration. Derive v = r omega and obtain an expression for the centripetal
acceleration. Write the expressions for the time period and frequency.**

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
     Definition of UCM + diagram .......... 1
     Angular quantities (3 definitions) ... 2
     Derivation of v = r omega ............ 1.5
     Derivation of a_c .................... 2.5
     Time period and frequency ............ 1
```

```
  UNIFORM CIRCULAR MOTION
     Motion of a body along a circular path with CONSTANT SPEED.
     The velocity is NOT constant because its direction keeps changing;
     hence the motion is ACCELERATED.

                        v2
                        ^
                        |
              . . . . . *. . . . . .
          .             |            .
        .          a_c  |              .
      .         <-------*               .
     *  <--- v1         O                *
      .                                 .
        .                             .
          . . . . . . . . . . . . .

     Velocity  -> always along the TANGENT
     Acceleration -> always along the RADIUS, towards the CENTRE
```

```
  DEFINITIONS

     ANGULAR DISPLACEMENT theta
        The angle swept out by the radius vector.
              theta  =  s / r          unit: radian

     ANGULAR VELOCITY omega
        The rate of change of angular displacement.
              omega  =  d theta / dt   unit: rad/s ,  dimensions [ T^-1 ]

     ANGULAR ACCELERATION alpha
        The rate of change of angular velocity.
              alpha  =  d omega / dt   unit: rad/s^2 , dimensions [ T^-2 ]
        For uniform circular motion alpha = 0.
```

```
  DERIVATION OF  v = r omega

        arc  s  =  r theta

        Differentiating with respect to t, with r constant:

              ds              d theta
             ----   =   r  x  ---------
              dt                dt

  +----------------------+
  |     v  =  r omega    |          and similarly    a_t = r alpha
  +----------------------+
```

```
  DERIVATION OF CENTRIPETAL ACCELERATION

     Let the particle move from P to Q in a small time interval dt,
     turning through a small angle d.theta. Its speed stays v.

        POSITION DIAGRAM                  VELOCITY DIAGRAM
             Q                                    v2
             *                                    /|
            / \                                  / |
        r  /   \  r                          v  /  |  dv
          / d.th \                              /   |
         *--------*                            *----
         O         P                              v1

     Both triangles are ISOSCELES with the SAME apex angle d.theta
     (each velocity is perpendicular to its own radius), so the two
     triangles are SIMILAR:

              | dv |          | dr |
             --------   =   ----------
                 v               r

                                v
              | dv |    =     -----  | dr |
                                r

     Dividing by dt :

              | dv |         v      | dr |
             --------  =   -----  x --------
                dt           r         dt

     As dt -> 0 ,   | dr | / dt -> v   and   | dv | / dt -> a .

  +-------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                 v^2                                         |
  |      a_c   =   -----   =   r omega^2   =   v omega          |
  |                  r                                          |
  +-------------------------------------------------------------+

     DIRECTION: as d.theta -> 0 the vector dv becomes perpendicular to v,
     i.e. it points along the radius TOWARDS THE CENTRE.
```

```
  TIME PERIOD AND FREQUENCY

     TIME PERIOD T : the time for one complete revolution.

              2 pi r        2 pi
        T  =  --------  =  -------            unit: second
                 v          omega

     FREQUENCY n : the number of revolutions per second.

               1        omega
        n  =  ---  =  ---------               unit: hertz (Hz)
               T        2 pi

     Hence      omega = 2 pi n = 2 pi / T
     and        a_c   = 4 pi^2 n^2 r = 4 pi^2 r / T^2
```

### Q C5

**Define relative velocity. Obtain an expression for the relative velocity of two
bodies moving in a plane. Apply it to (a) a boat crossing a river and (b) a man
walking in the rain.**

**ANSWER:**

```
  MARK SPLIT
     Definition + general expression ...... 2
     River-boat: shortest time ............ 2
     River-boat: shortest path ............ 2
     Rain-and-umbrella .................... 2
```

```
  DEFINITION
     The relative velocity of a body A with respect to a body B is the
     velocity with which A appears to move to an observer moving with B.

  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                               |
  |      v(AB)  =  v(A)  -  v(B)          v(BA)  =  - v(AB)       |
  |                                                               |
  |  If theta is the angle between v(A) and v(B) :                |
  |                                                               |
  |    | v(AB) |  =  sqrt( vA^2  +  vB^2  -  2 vA vB cos theta )  |
  |                                                               |
  |  Same direction  (0 deg)   ->  | vA - vB |                    |
  |  Opposite        (180 deg) ->    vA + vB                      |
  |  Perpendicular   (90 deg)  ->  sqrt( vA^2 + vB^2 )            |
  |                                                               |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
  (a) BOAT CROSSING A RIVER
      Width d , river speed v_r , boat speed in still water v_b .

  CASE 1 : SHORTEST TIME  (row perpendicular to the bank)

              <------------- v_r ------------->
      far   ==================================
              ^              /
              |            /
            d |          /  actual path
              |        /
      near  ==*======/=======================
              |<drift>|

            time      t  =  d / v_b            (this is the MINIMUM time)
            drift     x  =  v_r t  =  v_r d / v_b
            resultant speed  =  sqrt( v_b^2 + v_r^2 )

  CASE 2 : SHORTEST PATH  (land directly opposite)

              <------------- v_r ------------->
      far   ==================================
                          ^
                          |  resultant
                 v_b   \  |
                    \     |
      near  ==========\===*==============
                       theta

            The upstream component must cancel the current:

                  v_b sin theta  =  v_r      ->    sin theta = v_r / v_b

            Row at angle theta UPSTREAM of the perpendicular.

            speed across  =  v_b cos theta  =  sqrt( v_b^2 - v_r^2 )

                                     d
            time    t  =  --------------------------
                           sqrt( v_b^2  -  v_r^2 )

            Possible only if  v_b > v_r .
```

```
  (b) MAN WALKING IN THE RAIN
      Rain falls vertically with speed v_r ; the man walks with speed v_m .

            v(rain rel. man)  =  v(rain)  -  v(man)

                        |theta
                        | /
                   v_r  |/         - v_m
                        *<-----------------
                        |\
                        | \
                        |  \  v(rel)
                        v   v

            | v(rel) |  =  sqrt( v_r^2  +  v_m^2 )

                              v_m
            tan theta  =    -------          (measured from the VERTICAL)
                              v_r

            The man must hold his umbrella tilted through this angle
            FORWARD, in the direction in which he is walking.

            The faster he walks, the more he must tilt it forward.
```
