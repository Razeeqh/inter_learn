## Topic: Work function and emission

### Q A1
What is the "work function" of a metal? On what does it depend?

**ANSWER:**
```
  WORK FUNCTION (W0)  -  the MINIMUM energy required to just remove
  an electron from the surface of a metal.

  It depends ONLY on:
     (i)  the nature of the metal
     (ii) the condition of its surface

  It does NOT depend on the incident light in any way.
  Unit: joule, but usually quoted in eV.
```

---

### Q A2
Name the four types of electron emission and state the energy source for each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  THERMIONIC emission   ->  energy supplied by HEAT
  2.  FIELD (cold cathode)  ->  a strong ELECTRIC FIELD (~10^8 V/m)
  3.  PHOTOELECTRIC         ->  LIGHT (photons)
  4.  SECONDARY emission    ->  fast-moving ELECTRONS striking the surface
```

---

### Q A3
What is the photoelectric effect? Who discovered it?

**ANSWER:** The emission of electrons from the surface of a metal when light
of suitable frequency falls on it. It was discovered by **Hertz (1887)**,
studied in detail by **Lenard**, and explained by **Einstein**.

---

### Q A4
Define threshold frequency and threshold wavelength.

**ANSWER:**
```
  THRESHOLD FREQUENCY (nu0)  -  the MINIMUM frequency of incident
  light below which no photoemission occurs, however intense the light.

  THRESHOLD WAVELENGTH (lambda0)  -  the MAXIMUM wavelength of the
  incident light that can still cause photoemission.

                 W0  =  h nu0        lambda0  =  c / nu0
```

---

### Q A5
Define stopping potential. What is its significance?

**ANSWER:**
```
  STOPPING POTENTIAL (V0)  -  the minimum NEGATIVE potential applied
  to the anode at which the photoelectric current becomes ZERO.

  Significance:  it MEASURES the maximum kinetic energy of the
  photoelectrons, because

                e V0  =  K_max
```

---

### Q A6
On what factors does the stopping potential depend, and on what does it NOT
depend?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEPENDS ON      :  the FREQUENCY of the incident light
                     and the WORK FUNCTION of the metal

  DOES NOT DEPEND :  the INTENSITY of the incident light
  ON

                 V0  =  (h/e) nu  -  W0/e
```

---

### Q A7
What is saturation current? What does it depend on?

**ANSWER:** The maximum photoelectric current, reached when **all** the emitted
photoelectrons are collected by the anode. It is **directly proportional to the
intensity** of the incident light (frequency being constant).

---

### Q A8
Write Einstein's photoelectric equation and explain each term.

**ANSWER:**
```
              h nu   =   W0   +   K_max

     h nu   =  energy of the incident photon
     W0     =  work function of the metal (the exit fee)
     K_max  =  maximum kinetic energy of the emitted photoelectron
            =  (1/2) m v_max^2  =  e V0
```

## Topic: Photons

### Q A9
Write any four properties of a photon.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  Energy   E  =  h nu  =  h c / lambda
  2.  Momentum p  =  h / lambda  =  E / c
  3.  REST MASS is ZERO ; it always travels with speed c
  4.  It is electrically NEUTRAL, so it is NOT deflected by
      electric or magnetic fields
  (5. In a collision, energy and momentum are conserved, but the
      number of photons is not.)
```

---

### Q A10
A photon has zero rest mass. Then how can it have momentum?

**ANSWER:** Because for a photon the momentum comes from its energy, not from
a rest mass:
```
              E        h nu          h
     p   =   ---  =   ------  =  ----------
              c          c        lambda

  The relation p = m v applies to particles WITH rest mass. A photon
  obeys the relativistic relation E = p c instead.
```

---

### Q A11
Are photons deflected by electric and magnetic fields? Why?

**ANSWER:** **No.** A photon carries **zero charge**, and electric and magnetic
fields exert forces only on charged particles. Hence the path of a photon is
unaffected by them.

## Topic: Matter waves

### Q A16
State de Broglie's hypothesis. Write the expression for the de Broglie
wavelength.

**ANSWER:** Every moving material particle has a wave associated with it
(a matter wave), of wavelength
```
                 h          h
     lambda  =  ---   =   ------
                 p         m v
```

Write the expression for the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated
through a potential difference of V volts.

**ANSWER:**

                     h                    12.27
     lambda  =  ---------------   =   -----------  angstrom
                sqrt(2 m e V)            sqrt(V)
```

### Q A18
Find the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 400 V.

**ANSWER:**
```
                12.27          12.27
     lambda  =  --------  =  --------  =  0.61  angstrom
                sqrt(400)       20

              =  0.61 x 10^-10  m
```

### Q A19
Why is the wave nature of a moving cricket ball not observed?

**ANSWER:**
```
  For a cricket ball  lambda = h/(mv) is about 10^-34 m, because
  h = 6.63 x 10^-34 is extremely small while m is comparatively huge.

  Diffraction can only be observed with an obstacle comparable in size
  to the wavelength, and no obstacle that small exists. So the wave
  nature is UNDETECTABLE (not absent).
```

---

### Q A20
An electron and a proton have the same kinetic energy. Which has the larger
de Broglie wavelength?

**ANSWER:**
```
     lambda  =  h / sqrt(2 m K)     ->    lambda ~ 1/sqrt(m)

  The ELECTRON, because it is lighter.
  Ratio = sqrt(1836) = about 43 times longer.
```

---

### Q A21
What is the de Broglie wavelength of a particle at rest?

**ANSWER:** **Infinite.** For a particle at rest v = 0, so p = 0, and
lambda = h/p becomes infinite. In other words, a stationary particle has
**no matter wave**. Matter waves exist only for moving particles.

---

### Q A22
What did the Davisson-Germer experiment establish?

**ANSWER:** It showed that a beam of electrons is **diffracted** by a nickel
crystal, producing an intensity peak at **54 V and 50 degrees**. The measured
wavelength (1.65 A) agreed with the de Broglie prediction (1.67 A), thereby
**experimentally confirming the wave nature of the electron**.

---

### Q A23
State Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

**ANSWER:** It is impossible to determine simultaneously and exactly both the
position and the momentum of a particle. The product of the uncertainties
satisfies
```
     (delta x)(delta p)   >=   h / (4 pi)
```

---

### Q A24
What is a photocell? State one use.

**ANSWER:** A photocell is a device that converts light energy into electrical
energy using the photoelectric effect; the current it produces is proportional
to the light intensity. **Use:** in burglar alarms (or in cinema sound
reproduction, automatic doors, exposure meters).

  6.  By changing the intensity, the frequency and the potential
      independently, the four laws of photoelectric emission are
      established.
```

### Q B2
State and explain the laws (experimental results) of photoelectric emission.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW 1 -- EFFECT OF INTENSITY
  For a fixed frequency, the saturation photoelectric current is
  DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL to the intensity of the incident light.

        I ^                          *
          |                    *
          |              *
          |        *
          |  *
          +-------------------------> intensity

  LAW 2 -- EFFECT OF INTENSITY ON ENERGY
  The stopping potential (and hence Kmax) is INDEPENDENT of the
  intensity.

  LAW 3 -- EFFECT OF FREQUENCY
  Kmax and the stopping potential increase LINEARLY with the frequency
  of the incident light, and are independent of intensity.

       V0 ^                    /
          |                   /   slope = h/e
          |                  /
      ----+-----------------*----------> nu
          |                nu0

  LAW 4 -- THRESHOLD FREQUENCY
  Below a certain minimum frequency nu0, characteristic of the metal,
  NO emission occurs however intense the light or however long it
  is applied.

  LAW 5 -- INSTANTANEOUS EMISSION
  The emission is instantaneous; the time lag is less than 10^-9 s
  even for very feeble light.
```

### Q B4
Explain the effect of frequency on the stopping potential. Draw the graph and
explain how Planck's constant can be found from it.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Keeping the intensity fixed and increasing the frequency, the
  stopping potential increases. Plotting V0 against nu gives a
  STRAIGHT LINE:

       V0 ^                                *
          |                           *
          |                      *          slope = h / e
          |                 *                     = 4.14 x 10^-15 V s
          |            *

          |

                                     h            W0

### Q B5
Explain why the wave theory of light fails to explain the photoelectric effect.

**ANSWER:**
```
  According to the wave theory the energy of light is spread
  CONTINUOUSLY over the wavefront and depends on the amplitude
  (intensity), not on the frequency. Four contradictions follow.

  1.  NO THRESHOLD FREQUENCY SHOULD EXIST
      Wave theory: any frequency should eject electrons if the light
      is made intense enough.
      Experiment : below nu0 there is NO emission at all, however
      intense the light.

  2.  Kmax SHOULD DEPEND ON INTENSITY
      Wave theory: a more intense (larger amplitude) wave should shake
      the electron harder and give it more energy.
      Experiment : Kmax and V0 are completely independent of intensity.

  3.  Kmax SHOULD NOT DEPEND ON FREQUENCY
      Wave theory: frequency has nothing to do with the energy carried.
      Experiment : Kmax increases LINEARLY with frequency.

  4.  THERE SHOULD BE A LONG TIME LAG
      Wave theory: with feeble light an electron would need hours or
      days to absorb enough energy from the thinly spread wavefront.
      Experiment : emission is instantaneous ( < 10^-9 s ).

  CONCLUSION: the wave theory fails completely, and the particle
  (photon) theory of Einstein is required.
```

### Q B6
State and derive Einstein's photoelectric equation. How does it explain the
experimental results?

**ANSWER:**
```
  EINSTEIN'S PHOTON POSTULATE
  Light of frequency nu travels as packets (photons) of energy
  E = h nu. One photon is absorbed COMPLETELY by ONE electron --
  it is an all-or-nothing process.

  DERIVATION
  1.  A photon of energy h nu is completely absorbed by a single
      electron in the metal.
  2.  Part of this energy, W0, is used in freeing the electron from
      the surface.
  3.  The remainder appears as the kinetic energy of the electron.
  4.  An electron at the very surface loses only W0 and therefore
      escapes with the MAXIMUM kinetic energy.
  5.  By conservation of energy:

           +--------------------------------------+
           |     h nu   =   W0   +   K_max        |
           +--------------------------------------+

      i.e.  K_max = h nu - W0 = h(nu - nu0) = e V0

  EXPLANATION OF THE RESULTS
  (i)   THRESHOLD: if h nu < W0 the packet is too small to pay the
        exit fee, and since one electron takes only one photon, extra
        photons cannot help. Hence no emission below nu0.
  (ii)  CURRENT vs INTENSITY: intensity = number of photons per
        second; each frees at most one electron, so the current is
        proportional to intensity.
  (iii) Kmax INDEPENDENT OF INTENSITY: brighter light means MORE
        photons, not more energetic ones, so h nu and hence Kmax
        are unchanged.
  (iv)  Kmax LINEAR IN FREQUENCY: Kmax = h nu - W0 is the equation
        of a straight line of slope h.
  (v)   INSTANTANEOUS: a single photon-electron collision is a
        one-shot event, so there is no accumulation time.
```

### Q B11
State de Broglie's hypothesis and derive the expression for the de Broglie
wavelength.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HYPOTHESIS
  Since radiation shows a dual (wave-particle) nature and nature is
  symmetrical, MATTER should also show a dual nature. Every moving
  material particle has a wave -- a MATTER WAVE -- associated with it.

  DERIVATION
  1.  For a photon of frequency nu,        E = h nu
  2.  Treating it as a particle,           E = m c^2
  3.  Equating:                            h nu  =  m c^2

                                               h nu       h
                                     m c  =   ------  =  --------
                                                 c        lambda
                                     (using nu / c = 1 / lambda)

  4.  But m c is the momentum p of the photon, so

                    h                             h
          p  =  --------      i.e.   lambda  =  -----
                 lambda                           p

  5.  de Broglie proposed that this applies to ALL particles.
      For a particle of mass m moving with velocity v, p = m v :

          +------------------------------------------+
          |               h            h             |
          |   lambda  =  ---   =   --------          |
          |               p          m v             |
          +------------------------------------------+

  In terms of kinetic energy K (since p = sqrt(2 m K)) :

                        h
          lambda  =  ------------
                     sqrt(2 m K)
```

### Q B13
Describe the Davisson-Germer experiment. How did it confirm the wave nature
of the electron?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ARRANGEMENT
   +-------------------------------------------------------------+
   |               EVACUATED  CHAMBER                            |
   |  [ ]== FILAMENT (thermionic source of electrons)            |
   |   |                                                          |
   |  ===  ANODE at variable accelerating voltage V              |
   |   |                                                          |
   |  [|]  COLLIMATOR -> fine electron beam                      |
   |   |                              .-''-.                      |
   |   v            scattered       /DETECTOR\   movable on       |
   |   |            electrons      |  (movable)|  a circular arc  |
   |   |               \            \        /                    |
   |   v                \    phi     '-....-'                     |
   |  ------------------------------------------                  |
   |  ///////  NICKEL  CRYSTAL  TARGET  ////////                  |
   |  ------------------------------------------                  |
   +-------------------------------------------------------------+

  METHOD
  1.  Electrons from a heated filament are accelerated through a
      known potential V and collimated into a fine beam.
  2.  The beam strikes a single crystal of NICKEL, whose regularly
      spaced atoms act as a three-dimensional diffraction grating.
  3.  A movable detector measures the intensity of the scattered
      electrons at each scattering angle phi, for several values
      of V.

  OBSERVATION
       I ^      SHARP PEAK
         |        /\
         |       /  \
         |   \  /    \___
         +---------------------> phi
              phi = 50 deg  at  V = 54 volt

  A sharp maximum appeared at a SCATTERING ANGLE OF 50 DEGREES when
  the ACCELERATING VOLTAGE WAS 54 VOLTS. Sharp maxima and minima are
  the signature of DIFFRACTION, which only waves can produce.

  CALCULATION
     de Broglie prediction:  lambda = 12.27/sqrt(54) = 1.67 A

     Experimental value (Bragg's law, d = 0.91 A for nickel,
     glancing angle theta = (180-50)/2 = 65 deg):
              lambda = 2 d sin theta = 2(0.91)(0.906) = 1.65 A

  CONCLUSION
  The two values agree to within about 1 percent. This EXPERIMENTALLY
  CONFIRMED de Broglie's hypothesis that moving electrons behave like
  waves. G.P. Thomson obtained the same result independently by
  diffracting electrons through a thin gold foil.
```

### Q B14
Explain the four types of electron emission in detail.

**ANSWER:**
```
  An electron is held inside a metal by the attraction of the positive
  ions. The minimum energy needed to free it is the WORK FUNCTION W0.
  That energy can be supplied in four ways.

  1.  THERMIONIC EMISSION
      The metal is heated (typically to 2000 K or more). The thermal
      energy of the free electrons increases until some of them exceed
      W0 and escape.
      Example: the tungsten filament of a cathode ray tube or X-ray
      tube.

  2.  FIELD EMISSION (cold cathode emission)
      A very strong electric field, of order 10^8 V/m, is applied at
      the metal surface. The field pulls the electrons out directly,
      without any heating.
      Example: field emission microscope, spark plugs, lightning.

  3.  PHOTOELECTRIC EMISSION
      Light of frequency greater than nu0 falls on the metal. Each
      photon of energy h nu is absorbed by one electron, which escapes
      if h nu > W0.
      Example: photocells, solar-powered light switches.

  4.  SECONDARY EMISSION
      Fast-moving electrons (or ions) strike the metal surface and
      transfer their kinetic energy to the bound electrons, knocking
      them out.
      Example: photomultiplier tubes and electron multipliers, where
      the effect is used to amplify a weak signal.
```

### Q B15
Write any four properties of matter waves. How do they differ from
electromagnetic waves?

**ANSWER:**
```
  PROPERTIES OF MATTER WAVES
  1.  They are associated ONLY with moving particles; a particle at
      rest (p = 0) has no matter wave.
  2.  lambda = h/p , so a lighter or slower particle has a LONGER
      wavelength.
  3.  The wavelength is INDEPENDENT of the charge of the particle;
      it depends only on the momentum.
  4.  The amplitude of the wave at a point gives the PROBABILITY of
      finding the particle there.
  5.  Their wavelength is measurable only for very light particles
      (electrons, neutrons) -- for macroscopic bodies it is ~10^-34 m
      and undetectable.

  DIFFERENCES FROM ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  MATTER WAVES             |  ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES             |
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
  |  Produced by a moving     |  Produced by an accelerated CHARGE |
  |  particle, charged or not |                                    |
  |  Not electromagnetic; no  |  Consist of oscillating electric   |
  |  electric/magnetic field  |  and magnetic fields               |
  |  Travel slower than light |  Always travel at c in vacuum      |
  |  Wavelength depends on    |  Wavelength depends on the source  |
  |  the momentum             |  frequency                         |
  +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
```

       ^  all three curves reach zero at the SAME -V0

       |  -> the saturation current DOES depend on intensity

       V0 ^                              *
          |                        *
          |                  *         SLOPE = h/e = 4.14e-15 V s
          |            *
      ----+-----------*-------------------------> nu
          |          /|  nu0 = threshold frequency
    -W0/e +........./ |  y-intercept = -W0/e

```

```
  PART 3 -- EINSTEIN'S EQUATION AND THE EXPLANATION          [3 marks]

  Postulate: light travels as photons of energy E = h nu, and one
  photon is absorbed completely by one electron.

  Energy conservation for a surface electron:

           +----------------------------------------+
           |       h nu   =   W0   +   K_max        |
           |                                        |
           |  K_max = h nu - W0 = h(nu - nu0) = eV0 |
           +----------------------------------------+

  EXPLANATIONS
  (i)   Threshold frequency: if h nu < W0 the single packet is too
        small, and a second photon cannot be added because one
        electron absorbs only one photon. So there is NO emission
        below nu0 however intense the light.
  (ii)  Current vs intensity: intensity is the number of photons per
        second; each can free at most one electron, so the current is
        proportional to intensity.
  (iii) Kmax independent of intensity: increasing intensity increases
        the NUMBER of photons but not the energy h nu of any one, so
        Kmax = h nu - W0 is unchanged.
  (iv)  Kmax linear in frequency: Kmax = h nu - W0 is a straight line
        of slope h; dividing by e gives V0 = (h/e)nu - W0/e, which is
        exactly the observed graph (c).
  (v)   Instantaneous emission: a single photon-electron collision is
        a one-shot event, so there is no accumulation time.
```
