## Topic: Energy bands and materials

### Q A1
What is the energy band gap? Give the values for germanium and silicon.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ENERGY BAND GAP (Eg) is the forbidden energy range between the top
  of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band. No
  electron can have an energy inside this gap.

       Germanium  ->  Eg = 0.72  eV
       Silicon    ->  Eg = 1.1   eV
```

---

### Q A2
On the basis of energy bands, how do you distinguish a conductor, a
semiconductor and an insulator?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CONDUCTOR      -  valence and conduction bands OVERLAP, Eg = 0
  SEMICONDUCTOR  -  small gap, Eg about 1 eV
  INSULATOR      -  large gap, Eg more than 3 eV
```

---

### Q A3
Why does a pure semiconductor behave as an insulator at 0 K?

**ANSWER:** At 0 K all the covalent bonds are complete, so the valence band
is completely full and the conduction band is completely empty. There are no
free electrons or holes, so no current can flow.

---

### Q A4
What is a hole? What charge does it carry?

**ANSWER:** A hole is the **vacancy left in a covalent bond** when an electron
escapes from it. It behaves like a particle carrying a positive charge of
**+1.6 x 10^-19 C**.

---

## Topic: Intrinsic and extrinsic

### Q A5
What is an intrinsic semiconductor? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A semiconductor in its PURE form, with no impurity added.

  In it,   n(e)  =  n(h)  =  n(i)

  Examples:  pure germanium (Ge),  pure silicon (Si)
```

---

### Q A6
What is doping? Why is it done?

**ANSWER:** Doping is the deliberate addition of a small, controlled amount
of a suitable impurity to a pure semiconductor. It is done to **increase the
conductivity** of the semiconductor in a controlled way. A typical level is
1 impurity atom per 10^6 semiconductor atoms.

---

### Q A7
What is an n-type semiconductor? Name the dopants used.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A semiconductor doped with a PENTAVALENT impurity, in which
  ELECTRONS are the majority carriers and holes the minority carriers.

  Dopants:  Phosphorus (P), Arsenic (As), Antimony (Sb), Bismuth (Bi)
```

---

### Q A8
What is a p-type semiconductor? Name the dopants used.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A semiconductor doped with a TRIVALENT impurity, in which
  HOLES are the majority carriers and electrons the minority carriers.

  Dopants:  Boron (B), Aluminium (Al), Indium (In), Gallium (Ga)
```

---

### Q A9
What are donor and acceptor impurities?

**ANSWER:**
```
  DONOR     -  a pentavalent impurity. It DONATES a free electron
               to the crystal. Creates a donor level just below the
               conduction band.

  ACCEPTOR  -  a trivalent impurity. It ACCEPTS an electron from a
               nearby bond, creating a hole. Creates an acceptor level
               just above the valence band.
```

---

### Q A10
State the mass action law for a semiconductor.

**ANSWER:**
```
              n(e)   x   n(h)   =   n(i)^2

  The product of the electron and hole concentrations at a given
  temperature is a constant, equal to the square of the intrinsic
  carrier concentration.
```

---

### Q A11
Is an n-type semiconductor negatively charged? Justify.

**ANSWER:** **No.** It is electrically **neutral**. Each donor atom supplies
one free electron but also keeps one extra positive nuclear charge, so the
crystal as a whole has zero net charge. The letter "n" only tells us that the
majority carriers are negative.

---

### Q A12
How does the conductivity of a semiconductor change with temperature?

**ANSWER:** It **increases** with temperature. Heating breaks more covalent
bonds, creating more electron-hole pairs, so the number of charge carriers
rises rapidly. A semiconductor therefore has a **negative temperature
coefficient of resistance**.

---

## Topic: p-n junction and diode

### Q A13
What is a depletion region?

**ANSWER:** The narrow region on either side of a p-n junction that has been
emptied of **mobile charge carriers** by diffusion and recombination, leaving
only fixed negative acceptor ions on the p side and fixed positive donor ions
on the n side. Its width is about 10^-6 m.

---

### Q A14
What is the barrier potential? Give its value for Ge and Si.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The potential difference developed across the depletion region,
  which opposes the further diffusion of majority carriers.

       Germanium  ->  0.3  V
       Silicon    ->  0.7  V
```

---

### Q A15
Distinguish between diffusion current and drift current in a p-n junction.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DIFFUSION current  -  due to MAJORITY carriers, caused by the
                        concentration difference, flows from p to n.

  DRIFT current      -  due to MINORITY carriers, caused by the
                        junction electric field, flows from n to p.

  At equilibrium the two are equal, so the net current is ZERO.
```

---

### Q A16
What happens to the width of the depletion layer in forward bias and in
reverse bias?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FORWARD bias  ->  depletion layer becomes THINNER,
                    barrier potential DECREASES.

  REVERSE bias  ->  depletion layer becomes WIDER,
                    barrier potential INCREASES.
```

---

### Q A17
Define knee voltage (cut-in voltage) of a diode.

**ANSWER:** The forward voltage at which the diode current begins to increase
rapidly. **Ge = 0.3 V, Si = 0.7 V.**

---

### Q A18
Define breakdown voltage.

**ANSWER:** The reverse voltage at which the reverse current through a p-n
junction increases suddenly and very sharply. Beyond it an ordinary diode is
damaged.

---

### Q A19
What is reverse saturation current? Which carriers cause it?

**ANSWER:** The very small, almost constant current that flows through a
reverse-biased p-n junction. It is caused by **minority carriers**. It is of
the order of microamperes in Ge and nanoamperes in Si, and it increases
strongly with temperature.

---

## Topic: Rectifiers and special diodes

### Q A20
What is rectification? Which property of a diode makes it possible?

**ANSWER:** Rectification is the conversion of **AC into DC**. It is possible
because a p-n junction diode conducts appreciably in **one direction only**
(forward bias) and blocks the other direction (reverse bias).

---

### Q A21
Give the ripple factor and efficiency of half-wave and full-wave rectifiers.

**ANSWER:**
```
  +---------------+------------------+---------------+
  |               |  RIPPLE FACTOR   |  EFFICIENCY   |
  +---------------+------------------+---------------+
  |  Half-wave    |      1.21        |    40.6 %     |
  |  Full-wave    |      0.48        |    81.2 %     |
  +---------------+------------------+---------------+
```

---

### Q A22
What is the frequency of the output of a full-wave rectifier if the input
supply is 50 Hz?

**ANSWER:** **100 Hz.** A full-wave rectifier output has twice the input
frequency (2f). (A half-wave rectifier output would be 50 Hz.)

---

### Q A23
What is a Zener diode? How is it always connected in a circuit?

**ANSWER:** A heavily doped p-n junction diode designed to operate safely in
the **reverse breakdown region**. It is always connected in **reverse bias**,
in parallel with the load, with a series resistance to limit the current.

---

### Q A24
What is a photodiode and in which bias is it used? Why?

**ANSWER:** A photodiode is a p-n junction that converts light into an
electrical signal. It is used in **reverse bias**, because in reverse bias the
current is very small and is dominated by the light-generated carriers, so the
change produced by light is easy to measure.

---

### Q A25
What is an LED? Name two materials used to make LEDs.

**ANSWER:** A heavily doped p-n junction operated in **forward bias**, which
emits light when electrons and holes recombine at the junction. Materials:
**Gallium Arsenide (GaAs)** and **Gallium Phosphide (GaP)**. Silicon and
germanium are not used because they release the energy as heat, not light.

---

### Q A26
Why are Si and Ge not used to make LEDs?

**ANSWER:** Their band gaps are small and their recombination process releases
the energy mostly as **heat (lattice vibrations)** rather than as photons, so
they do not emit useful light.

---

## Topic: Transistor and gates

### Q A27
Why is the base of a transistor made thin and lightly doped?

**ANSWER:** So that very few of the carriers injected by the emitter recombine
in the base. About 95 to 98 per cent of them pass straight through to the
collector, which makes I(C) nearly equal to I(E), I(B) very small, and hence
the current gain large.

---

### Q A28
Write the relation between the three transistor currents.

**ANSWER:**
```
              I(E)   =   I(B)   +   I(C)

  with  I(C) much greater than I(B).
```

---

### Q A29
Define alpha and beta of a transistor and write the relation between them.

**ANSWER:**
```
                I(C)                        I(C)
   alpha  =  ---------        beta   =   ---------
                I(E)                        I(B)

                  alpha                        beta
   beta   =  --------------     alpha  =  -------------
               1  -  alpha                 1  +  beta

   alpha is always less than 1;  beta is always much greater than 1.
```

---

### Q A30
In which region must a transistor be operated when it is used as an
amplifier, and how are its junctions biased?

**ANSWER:** In the **ACTIVE region**. The emitter-base junction is **forward
biased** and the collector-base junction is **reverse biased**.

---

### Q A31
What is meant by phase reversal in a CE amplifier?

**ANSWER:** In a common-emitter amplifier the output voltage is **180 degrees
out of phase** with the input voltage. This follows from
V(CE) = V(CC) - I(C) R(C): when the input rises, I(C) rises and V(CE) falls.

---

### Q A32
What is a logic gate? Name the three basic gates.

**ANSWER:** A digital circuit that follows a definite logical relationship
between its inputs and its output, using only two levels (0 and 1). The three
basic gates are **OR, AND and NOT**.

---

Why are NAND and NOR called universal gates?

**ANSWER:** Because **any** other logic gate (NOT, AND, OR) can be constructed

### Q B4
Explain why the conductivity of a semiconductor increases with temperature
while that of a metal decreases.

**ANSWER:**

```
  SEMICONDUCTOR

     T increases
         -> more covalent bonds break
         -> more electron-hole PAIRS are created
         -> the NUMBER of charge carriers increases rapidly
         -> conductivity INCREASES, resistance DECREASES
         -> NEGATIVE temperature coefficient of resistance

  METAL

     T increases
         -> the number of free electrons does NOT change
            (it is already at its maximum)
         -> but the lattice ions vibrate more violently
         -> electrons collide more often, relaxation time falls
         -> conductivity DECREASES, resistance INCREASES
         -> POSITIVE temperature coefficient of resistance
```

**The key difference:** in a semiconductor heating CREATES NEW CARRIERS, and
that gain far outweighs the extra scattering. In a metal no new carriers can
be created, so only the extra scattering shows up.

```
     R                                R
     |     METAL                      |  SEMICONDUCTOR
     |          /                     |\
     |         /                      | \
     |       /                        |   \
     |     /                          |      \____
     |   /                            |            -------____
     +----------------------- T       +------------------------- T
```

### Q B5
Explain the formation of a p-n junction, the depletion region and the barrier
potential.

**ANSWER:**

```
        p - REGION          |          n - REGION
                            |
    o   o   o   o | - - - - | + + + + | o   o   o   o
    o   o   o   o | - - - - | + + + + | o   o   o   o
    o   o   o   o | - - - - | + + + + | o   o   o   o
                  |         |         |
    holes         |<------ DEPLETION ->|      free electrons
                  |        REGION      |
       negative acceptor ions      positive donor ions
                  |                    |
                  E  <-----------------
                  |<---- V(B) ---->|
```

```
  1.  On the p side holes are in the majority, on the n side electrons.
      The huge concentration difference makes holes DIFFUSE from p to n
      and electrons diffuse from n to p.

  2.  Near the junction these carriers RECOMBINE, so the region is left
      with no mobile carriers - it is DEPLETED.

  3.  The donor atoms left behind on the n side become fixed POSITIVE
      ions; the acceptor atoms on the p side become fixed NEGATIVE
      ions. These ions cannot move.

  4.  This double layer of fixed charge sets up an electric field
      pointing from n to p. The potential difference across it is the
      BARRIER POTENTIAL V(B).
              Ge = 0.3 V      Si = 0.7 V

  5.  The field opposes further diffusion. When the drift current due
      to minority carriers exactly balances the diffusion current due
      to majority carriers, equilibrium is reached and the NET CURRENT
      IS ZERO.

  Width of the depletion layer:  about 10^-6 m.
```

### Q B6
Explain the action of a p-n junction diode in forward bias and in reverse
bias. Draw the circuit diagrams.

**ANSWER:**

```
       FORWARD BIAS                        REVERSE BIAS
   (p to +, n to -)                     (p to -, n to +)

    +-------( mA )------+                +-------( uA )------+
    |                   |                |                   |
  +-+---+---+           |              +-+---+---+           |
  |  p  |  n |          |              |  p  |  n |          |
  +-----+---+           |              +-----+---+           |
    |                   |                |                   |
    |      R            |                |      R            |
    +---/\/\/\----------+                +---/\/\/\----------+
    |                   |                |                   |
    |    + |    -       |                |    - |    +       |
    +------| |----------+                +------| |----------+
           | |                                  | |
        battery                              battery
```

```
  FORWARD BIAS
    - The applied voltage opposes the barrier potential.
    - Effective barrier = V(B) - V.
    - The depletion layer becomes THINNER.
    - Majority carriers cross the junction easily.
    - A LARGE current (milliamperes) flows.
    - Junction resistance is LOW.

  REVERSE BIAS
    - The applied voltage supports the barrier potential.
    - Effective barrier = V(B) + V.
    - The depletion layer becomes WIDER.
    - Majority carriers cannot cross; only minority carriers drift
      across.
    - A very small reverse saturation current (microamperes) flows.
    - Junction resistance is VERY HIGH.
```

Draw and explain the V-I characteristic curve of a p-n junction diode.

                            I (forward, mA)

                                 |         /
                                 |        /
                                 |       /
                                 |      /

      carried by minority carriers.
    - At the BREAKDOWN VOLTAGE the reverse current increases suddenly
      and the ordinary diode may be destroyed.
```

### Q B8
Explain the working of a Zener diode as a voltage regulator with a circuit
diagram.

**ANSWER:**

```
                R(s)
      +-------/\/\/\-------+-----------+
      |                    |           |
      |                   _|_         R(L)
   unregulated            /_\  Zener  \/\/\    regulated
   input V(i)              |  (reverse) |      output V(o) = V(z)
      |                    |           |
      +--------------------+-----------+
```

```
  The Zener diode is connected in REVERSE bias, in parallel with the
  load, with a series resistance R(s).

  It operates in the breakdown region, where the voltage across it
  stays constant at V(z) even though the current through it changes
  a great deal.

  CASE 1 - INPUT VOLTAGE RISES
      -> Zener current I(z) increases sharply
      -> the total current through R(s) increases
      -> the drop across R(s) increases by the same amount
      -> the OUTPUT stays at V(z).

  CASE 2 - LOAD CURRENT INCREASES
      -> Zener current I(z) decreases by the same amount
      -> the total current through R(s) is unchanged
      -> the drop across R(s) is unchanged
      -> the OUTPUT stays at V(z).

  EQUATIONS
       V(i)  =  I R(s)  +  V(z)
       I     =  I(z)  +  I(L)
       I(L)  =  V(z) / R(L)

  ROLE OF R(s): it limits the current through the Zener and absorbs
  the excess input voltage. Without it the Zener would be destroyed.
```

### Q B9
Describe the working of an LED. State four advantages of LEDs.

**ANSWER:**

```
   SYMBOL                   CIRCUIT

     ----|>|----                    R
           |  \ \             +--/\/\/\--+
              \ \  light      |          |
                              |        --|>|--   forward biased
                              |          |
                              +----||----+
```

```
  WORKING
    1. An LED is a heavily doped p-n junction operated in FORWARD bias.
    2. Forward bias injects electrons from n into p and holes from p
       into n.
    3. Near the junction electrons and holes RECOMBINE.
    4. Each recombination releases energy equal to the band gap Eg.
       In suitable materials this energy is emitted as a PHOTON.

              Eg  =  h c / lambda

    5. The COLOUR of the light therefore depends on the BAND GAP of
       the material.

  MATERIALS
       GaAs   -> infrared
       GaAsP  -> red / yellow
       GaP    -> green / red
       GaN    -> blue
    (Si and Ge are not used: they release the energy as heat.)

  ADVANTAGES
    1. Low operating voltage and very low power consumption.
    2. Very fast switching, of the order of nanoseconds; no warm-up.
    3. Long life and high reliability - no filament to burn out.
    4. Small, rugged and light in weight.
    5. Nearly monochromatic light of the required colour.
    6. Low cost.
```

### Q B10
Describe a photodiode and a solar cell. How do they differ?

**ANSWER:**

```
  PHOTODIODE
    - A p-n junction operated in REVERSE bias.
    - Light with photon energy greater than Eg falls on the junction
      and generates electron-hole pairs.
    - The junction field sweeps them across, so the reverse current
      increases in proportion to the light intensity.
    - Used in reverse bias because the change caused by light is then
      easy to detect against the small dark current.
    - Uses: light detectors, exposure meters, optical communication.

  SOLAR CELL
    - A p-n junction with NO external bias at all.
    - Sunlight generates electron-hole pairs at the junction.
    - The built-in field pushes electrons to the n side and holes to
      the p side, so a PHOTOVOLTAGE appears across the terminals.
    - The top layer is made very thin so that light reaches the
      junction.
    - Material band gap should be about 1.0 to 1.8 eV.
    - Uses: calculators, satellites, rooftop power.

  DIFFERENCE
    A photodiode DETECTS light and needs an external battery.
    A solar cell GENERATES power and needs no battery.
```

### Q B11
Describe the structure of an npn transistor and explain its working. Why is
the base thin and lightly doped?

**ANSWER:**

```
        n p n   TRANSISTOR                     SYMBOL

              e-  e-  e-  e-                       C
        +------+---+------+                        |
        |  n   | p |  n   |                  B ----+
        |  E   | B |  C   |                        |\
        +------+---+------+                        | \
           |     |     |                           |  v  (arrow OUT)
           -     |     +                           E
           |     |     |
           +--||-+--||-+
            V(EB)   V(CB)
          forward   reverse
```

```
  WORKING
    1. The emitter-base junction is FORWARD biased, so the emitter
       (heavily doped) injects a large number of ELECTRONS into
       the base.
    2. The base is thin and lightly doped, so only about 2 to 5 per
       cent of these electrons recombine with holes there. That small
       part is the base current I(B).
    3. The collector-base junction is REVERSE biased. Its positive
       potential attracts the remaining 95 to 98 per cent of the
       electrons through into the collector, giving I(C).
    4. Therefore    I(E)  =  I(B)  +  I(C)   with  I(C) >> I(B).

  WHY THE BASE IS THIN AND LIGHTLY DOPED
    A thin, lightly doped base contains very few holes, so very few
    of the injected electrons are lost by recombination. Almost all
    of them reach the collector. This keeps I(B) very small and makes
    beta = I(C)/I(B) large, which is what gives the transistor its
    high current amplification.
```

### Q B12
Define alpha and beta of a transistor and derive the relation between them.

**ANSWER:**

```
                   I(C)
   alpha  =     ---------      (common base current gain,
                   I(E)         at constant V(CB))     alpha < 1


                   I(C)
   beta   =     ---------      (common emitter current gain,
                   I(B)         at constant V(CE))     beta >> 1

  DERIVATION

     Start from      I(E)  =  I(B)  +  I(C)

     Divide every term by I(C):

           I(E)         I(B)         I(C)
         --------  =  --------  +  --------
           I(C)         I(C)         I(C)


            1            1
         -------  =   --------   +   1
          alpha         beta

     Rearranging,

                   alpha                      beta
        beta  =  ------------     alpha  =  ------------
                  1 - alpha                  1 + beta
```

Example: if alpha = 0.98 then beta = 0.98 / 0.02 = 49.

### Q C1  *** THE MOST REPEATED 8-MARK QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER ***
What is rectification? With the help of a circuit diagram, explain the working
of a HALF-WAVE rectifier. Draw the input and output waveforms.

**ANSWER:**

**RECTIFICATION**

Rectification is the process of converting alternating current (AC) into
direct current (DC). A p-n junction diode can do this because it offers a very
low resistance in forward bias and a very high resistance in reverse bias, so
it conducts appreciably in one direction only.

**CIRCUIT DIAGRAM**

```
        H A L F - W A V E    R E C T I F I E R

                          D
     o------+        +---|>|---+---------+
            |        |         |         |
            )||(     |         |         |
   AC       )||(     |        R(L)      output
   input    )||(     |        \/\/\      (DC)
            )||(     |         |         |
     o------+        +---------+---------+
            |
       transformer
       (primary)     (secondary)

   D    = p-n junction diode
   R(L) = load resistance; the output is taken across it
```

**WORKING**

```
  POSITIVE HALF CYCLE of the input
      - The upper end of the secondary is positive.
      - The p side of the diode is positive  ->  FORWARD biased.
      - The diode conducts.
      - Current flows through R(L) and an output voltage appears
        across it.

  NEGATIVE HALF CYCLE of the input
      - The upper end of the secondary is negative.
      - The p side of the diode is negative  ->  REVERSE biased.
      - The diode does not conduct (only a negligible reverse
        saturation current flows).
      - No current through R(L); the output is zero.

  Thus only ONE HALF of every input cycle appears at the output. The
  output current is unidirectional, so it is DC - but it is pulsating,
  not steady.
```

**WAVEFORMS**

```
   INPUT

     V
     |     ___              ___              ___
     |    /   \            /   \            /   \
     |   /     \          /     \          /     \
   --+--/-------\--------/-------\--------/-------\------> t
     |           \      /         \      /         \
     |            \___/            \___/            \___


   OUTPUT across R(L)

     V
     |     ___              ___              ___
     |    /   \            /   \            /   \
     |   /     \          /     \          /     \
   --+--/-------\________/-------\________/-------\______> t
     |
     |      only the POSITIVE halves survive
```

**IMPORTANT RESULTS**

```
                     V(m)                   I(m)
       I(m)   =  --------------   I(dc) = ---------
                  r(f) + R(L)                pi

                     I(m)
       I(rms) =  ----------      ripple factor  r  =  1.21
                       2

       maximum efficiency   =  40.6 %
       output frequency     =  f  (same as the input)
       PIV                  =  V(m)
       number of diodes     =  1
```

**NEED FOR A FILTER**

The output is pulsating DC. A capacitor connected in parallel with the load
charges to the peak voltage and then discharges slowly through R(L) during the
gaps, filling in the dips and giving a much smoother output.

**DISADVANTAGES OF THE HALF-WAVE RECTIFIER**

```
   1.  Half of the input power is wasted.
   2.  Very high ripple factor (1.21) - poor quality DC.
   3.  Low efficiency (only 40.6 %).
   4.  Low output frequency, so filtering is harder.
```

**STRUCTURE AND WORKING OF AN npn TRANSISTOR**

```
              e-  e-  e-  e-                       SYMBOL
        +------+---+------+                            C
        |  n   | p |  n   |                            |

```
  1. The emitter is heavily doped, the base is very thin and lightly

  2. The emitter-base junction is FORWARD biased, so the emitter

```

**alpha AND beta**

```
                   I(C)
   alpha  =     ---------      common base current gain,  alpha < 1
                   I(E)

                   I(C)
   beta   =     ---------      common emitter current gain, beta >> 1
                   I(B)

  DERIVATION OF THE RELATION

     I(E)  =  I(B)  +  I(C)

     divide throughout by I(C):

            1            1
         -------  =   --------   +   1
          alpha         beta

     so
                   alpha                      beta
        beta  =  ------------     alpha  =  ------------
                  1 - alpha                  1 + beta
```

**TRANSISTOR AS A CE AMPLIFIER**

```
                                  +---------+------- +V(CC)
                                  |         |
                                 R(B)      R(C)
                                  |         |
              C(in)               |         +--------+
    input  ---| |-----------------+         |        |
    signal                        |         |      C(out)
    (AC)                          |    C    |        |
                                  B---------+     output
                                  |    \             signal
                              [TRANSISTOR]           (AC)
                                  |                    |
                                  E                    |
     ---------------------------- + ------------------ + -----  ground
```

```
  WORKING
    1. The DC bias sets the operating point in the middle of the
       ACTIVE region (E-B forward, B-C reverse).
    2. The AC input signal adds to V(BE) and makes it rise and fall.
    3. A small change in V(BE) produces an appreciable change in I(B).
    4. Since I(C) = beta x I(B), the collector current changes beta
       times as much.
    5. This large current change flows through the large collector
       resistor R(C), producing a large voltage change across it.
    6. From    V(CE)  =  V(CC)  -  I(C) R(C)   the output voltage
       changes greatly - the signal has been AMPLIFIED.

  GAINS

       A(i)  =  beta

                          R(C)
       A(v)  =  beta  x  --------
                          r(i)

                                                   R(C)
       A(p)  =  A(v) x A(i)  =  beta^2  x     ------------

     INPUT                    OUTPUT (inverted and larger)
       ___                       \             /

hence the **highest power gain** of the three configurations.

### Q C4
Explain the classification of solids into conductors, semiconductors and
insulators on the basis of energy bands. Explain intrinsic and extrinsic
semiconductors, and describe how n-type and p-type materials are formed.

**ANSWER:**

**ENERGY BANDS**

When a large number of atoms are brought together to form a solid, the sharp
energy levels of the isolated atoms split and merge into ENERGY BANDS. The
highest filled band is the VALENCE BAND and the next higher band is the
CONDUCTION BAND. The gap between them, in which no electron energy is allowed,
is the FORBIDDEN GAP Eg.

```
     CONDUCTOR (METAL)        SEMICONDUCTOR            INSULATOR

  ///////////////////      ///////////////////     ///////////////////
  ////  CONDUCTION //      ////  CONDUCTION //     ////  CONDUCTION //
  ////    BAND     //      ////    BAND     //     ////    BAND     //
  ///////////////////      ///////////////////     ///////////////////
  ///////////////////              ^                        ^
  ////  VALENCE    //              |  Eg ~ 1 eV             |  Eg > 3 eV
  ////    BAND     //              v                        v
  ///////////////////      ///////////////////     ///////////////////
                           ////  VALENCE    //     ////  VALENCE    //
   Bands OVERLAP, Eg = 0   ////    BAND     //     ////    BAND     //
                           ///////////////////     ///////////////////
```

```
  CONDUCTOR      Eg = 0. The bands overlap, so electrons find empty
                 states immediately. Always conducts.
                 Resistance INCREASES with temperature.

  SEMICONDUCTOR  Eg is small (Ge 0.72 eV, Si 1.1 eV). An insulator at
                 0 K, but on heating some electrons cross the gap and
                 it conducts. Resistance DECREASES with temperature.

  INSULATOR      Eg is large (more than 3 eV; diamond about 6 eV).
                 No electron can cross. Practically no conduction.
```

**INTRINSIC SEMICONDUCTOR**

```
  A pure semiconductor, with no added impurity.

  At 0 K every covalent bond is complete and it behaves as a perfect
  insulator. On heating, bonds break; each broken bond produces one
  FREE ELECTRON and one HOLE (the vacancy left behind, which behaves
  like a positive charge of magnitude 1.6 x 10^-19 C).

  Because carriers are always created in pairs,

           n(e)  =  n(h)  =  n(i)

  Total current  I  =  I(electrons)  +  I(holes)

  The conductivity is far too low for practical devices.
```

**EXTRINSIC SEMICONDUCTOR AND DOPING**

```
  DOPING is the deliberate addition of a small, controlled amount of
  a suitable impurity (about 1 atom in 10^6) to raise the
  conductivity. The result is an EXTRINSIC semiconductor.
```

**n-TYPE**

```
        Si  ==  Si  ==  Si
        ||      ||      ||               Pentavalent atom (P, As, Sb, Bi)
        Si  ==  As  ==  Si               forms 4 bonds; the FIFTH electron
        ||      ||      ||               is loosely bound and becomes FREE.
        Si  ==  Si  ==  Si
                |                        The impurity DONATES an electron:
               (e-)  free                it is a DONOR.

   ///////////////////////
   ///  CONDUCTION BAND ///
   ///////////////////////
            ^  about 0.01 eV
   - - - - - - - - - - -    DONOR LEVEL (just below the CB)
            |
   ///////////////////////
   ///  VALENCE BAND    ///
   ///////////////////////

   Majority carriers: ELECTRONS.   Minority carriers: HOLES.
```

**p-TYPE**

```
        Si  ==  Si  ==  Si
        ||      ||      ||               Trivalent atom (B, Al, In, Ga)
        Si  ==   B  --  Si               forms only 3 bonds; the fourth
        ||      ||      (o) hole         bond is incomplete, leaving a HOLE.
        Si  ==  Si  ==  Si
                                         The impurity ACCEPTS an electron:
                                         it is an ACCEPTOR.

   ///////////////////////
   ///  CONDUCTION BAND ///
   ///////////////////////

   - - - - - - - - - - -    ACCEPTOR LEVEL (just above the VB)
            ^  about 0.01 eV
   ///////////////////////
   ///  VALENCE BAND    ///
   ///////////////////////

   Majority carriers: HOLES.       Minority carriers: ELECTRONS.
```

**MASS ACTION LAW**

```
            n(e)  x  n(h)  =  n(i)^2

  At a fixed temperature the product of the two carrier
  concentrations is constant. So doping that increases one type of
  carrier automatically decreases the other.

  Both n-type and p-type crystals are electrically NEUTRAL overall.

### Q C5
Draw the circuit of a Zener diode voltage regulator and explain its working.
Also describe the construction and working of an LED and a solar cell.

**ANSWER:**

**ZENER DIODE VOLTAGE REGULATOR**

```
                R(s)
      +-------/\/\/\-------+-----------+
      |                    |           |
      |                   _|_         R(L)
   unregulated            /_\  Zener  \/\/\    regulated
   input V(i)              |  (reverse) |      output V(o) = V(z)
      |                    |           |
      +--------------------+-----------+
```

```
  A Zener diode is a heavily doped p-n junction, so its depletion
  layer is very thin and the breakdown occurs at a low, sharp,
  well-defined reverse voltage V(z). In the breakdown region the
  voltage across it stays constant while the current through it
  varies widely. That is the property used for regulation.

  WORKING
    If V(i) INCREASES  -> I(z) increases sharply -> the current through
    R(s) increases -> the drop across R(s) increases -> the extra
    voltage is absorbed by R(s) -> the output stays at V(z).

    If the LOAD CURRENT increases -> I(z) decreases by exactly the same
    amount -> the total current through R(s) is unchanged -> the drop
    across R(s) is unchanged -> the output stays at V(z).

  EQUATIONS
       V(i)  =  I R(s)  +  V(z)
       I     =  I(z)  +  I(L)
       I(L)  =  V(z) / R(L)

  ROLE OF R(s): limits the Zener current and absorbs the excess
  input voltage. It is essential.
```

**LIGHT EMITTING DIODE**

```
  A heavily doped p-n junction operated in FORWARD bias.

  Forward bias injects electrons into the p side and holes into the
  n side. Near the junction they RECOMBINE, and in suitable materials
  the released energy (equal to Eg) is emitted as a PHOTON:

           Eg  =  h c / lambda

  So the colour depends on the band gap of the material.

  Materials:  GaAs (infrared), GaAsP (red / yellow),
              GaP (green / red), GaN (blue).
  Si and Ge are NOT used - they release the energy as heat.

  Advantages: low voltage and power, very fast switching, long life,
  small and rugged, nearly monochromatic light, low cost.
```

**SOLAR CELL**

```
           sunlight  \  \  \
                      v  v  v
            +=====================+   <-- very THIN top n layer so
            |     n  region       |       that light reaches the
            +---------------------+       junction
            |     p  region       |
            +=====================+
              |                 |
              +-----( load )----+
```

```
  A solar cell is a p-n junction with NO external bias.

  1. Photons with energy greater than Eg generate electron-hole pairs
     at or near the junction.
  2. The built-in junction field separates them: electrons are driven
     to the n side and holes to the p side.
  3. The n side becomes negative and the p side positive, so a
     PHOTOVOLTAGE appears across the terminals.
  4. Connecting a load makes current flow - solar energy has been
     converted directly into electrical energy.

  A good solar-cell material needs a band gap of about 1.0 to 1.8 eV,
  high optical absorption and good conductivity. Common materials are
  Si, GaAs, CdTe and CuInSe2.
```
