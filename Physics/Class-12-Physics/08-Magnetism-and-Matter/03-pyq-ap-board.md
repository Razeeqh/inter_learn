## Topic: The bar magnet and poles

### Q A1
What happens to a bar magnet when it is cut into two equal pieces
(i) perpendicular to its length (ii) parallel to its length?

**ANSWER:**
```
  In BOTH cases each piece becomes a COMPLETE magnet with its own
  N pole and S pole. Isolated poles are never obtained.

  (i)  CUT PERPENDICULAR to the length
          pole strength  m   ->  UNCHANGED
          magnetic length    ->  HALVED
          magnetic moment M  ->  HALVED  ( M/2 )

  (ii) CUT PARALLEL to the length
          pole strength  m   ->  HALVED
          magnetic length    ->  UNCHANGED
          magnetic moment M  ->  HALVED  ( M/2 )
```

---

### Q A2
Why do magnetic monopoles not exist? / Can an isolated N pole be obtained?

**ANSWER:** No. Magnetism arises from **circulating currents** inside atoms, and every
current loop necessarily has two faces (one N, one S). Whenever a magnet is broken,
each fragment develops a fresh pair of poles. Experimentally, the net magnetic flux
through any closed surface is always zero, which is exactly the statement that
**isolated magnetic poles do not exist**.

---

### Q A3
Define magnetic dipole moment. Give its SI unit.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MAGNETIC DIPOLE MOMENT  =  (pole strength)  x  (magnetic length)

              M  =  m  x  2l

  Equivalently: the TORQUE experienced by the magnet when it is placed
  PERPENDICULAR to a magnetic field of UNIT strength.

              M  =  tau(max) / B

  SI UNIT :  ampere metre-squared  ( A m^2 )   also written  J / T
  It is a VECTOR, directed from S to N inside the magnet.
```

---

### Q A4
Define pole strength and give its SI unit.

**ANSWER:** The pole strength `m` of a magnetic pole is the force it experiences in a
magnetic field of unit strength: `F = m B`. **SI unit: ampere metre (A m).**

---

### Q A5
What is the sure test of magnetism — attraction or repulsion? Why?

**ANSWER:** **REPULSION.** A magnet attracts unmagnetised iron as well, so attraction
proves nothing. Only another magnet can be **repelled**, so repulsion is the sure test.

## Topic: Field lines

### Q A6
State any four properties of magnetic field lines.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  They are CONTINUOUS CLOSED CURVES: outside the magnet N -> S,
      inside the magnet S -> N.
  2.  The TANGENT at any point gives the direction of B at that point.
  3.  The CROWDING of the lines is proportional to the STRENGTH of B;
      the lines are densest at the poles.
  4.  Two field lines NEVER INTERSECT (otherwise B would have two
      directions at one point).
  5.  They behave like stretched elastic threads - they contract
      lengthwise and repel each other sideways.
```

### Q A7
Give two differences between electric field lines and magnetic field lines.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRIC LINES                     MAGNETIC LINES
  ------------------------------     ------------------------------
  Start on +q and end on -q          No start and no end - closed loops
  Isolated source (a charge) is      Isolated source (a monopole) is
  possible                           impossible
  Flux through a closed surface      Flux through a closed surface is
  = q/e0 , can be non-zero           ALWAYS zero
  Shielding is possible              No magnetic insulator exists
```

### Q A16
State Gauss's law in magnetism. What does it imply?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The net magnetic flux through ANY CLOSED SURFACE is ZERO.

              ->    ->

## Topic: Earth's magnetism

### Q A17
Define **magnetic declination**.

**ANSWER:** The **angle between the geographic meridian and the magnetic meridian** at a
place. In simple words, it is the angle by which a freely suspended magnet deviates from
the true geographic north-south direction.

---

### Q A18
Define the **angle of dip** (inclination). What is its value at the magnetic poles and
at the magnetic equator?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ANGLE OF DIP = the angle that the EARTH'S TOTAL MAGNETIC FIELD makes
                 with the HORIZONTAL at that place.

  At the MAGNETIC EQUATOR :   dip = 0 degrees    ( B(V) = 0 , B = B(H) )
  At the MAGNETIC POLES   :   dip = 90 degrees   ( B(H) = 0 , B = B(V) )
```

---

### Q A19
Define the horizontal component of the earth's magnetic field and write the relation
between B, B(H) and the angle of dip.

**ANSWER:**
```
  B(H) is the component of the earth's total field along the horizontal
  direction, lying in the magnetic meridian.

        B(H)  =  B cos(delta)          B(V)  =  B sin(delta)

        tan(delta)  =  B(V) / B(H)     B  =  sqrt( B(H)^2 + B(V)^2 )
```

---

### Q A20
At a place the angle of dip is 60 degrees. Find the ratio of the vertical component to
the horizontal component of the earth's field.

**ANSWER:**
```
  tan(dip)  =  B(V) / B(H)

  B(V) / B(H)  =  tan(60)  =  sqrt(3)  =  1.732
```

---

### Q A21
An ordinary magnetic compass fails to work near the magnetic poles of the earth. Why?

**ANSWER:** At the magnetic poles the angle of dip is 90 degrees, so the **horizontal
component B(H) is zero**. A compass needle is constrained to move horizontally, and with
no horizontal field there is no torque to align it — so it cannot point in any
definite direction.

---

### Q A22
What are neutral points?

**ANSWER:** Points near a bar magnet where the **magnetic field of the magnet exactly
cancels the horizontal component of the earth's field**, so the resultant magnetic field
there is **zero**. If the magnet's N pole points geographic north, the neutral points lie
on the **equatorial line**; if the N pole points geographic south, they lie on the
**axial line**.

A material has a relative permeability of 0.9995. What type of material is it?

**ANSWER:**

### Q A27
Define retentivity and coercivity.

**ANSWER:**
```
  RETENTIVITY (remanence) :  the magnetic flux density B that REMAINS
                             in the material when the magnetising field
                             H is reduced to ZERO.   Unit : tesla.

  COERCIVITY              :  the REVERSE magnetising field H that must
                             be applied to reduce that residual B to
                             ZERO.   Unit : ampere / metre.
```

---

### Q A28
What does the area of a hysteresis loop represent?

**ANSWER:** The **energy lost as heat per unit volume of the material per cycle of
magnetisation** (hysteresis loss). Its unit is **J / m^3 per cycle**.

---

### Q A29
Why is soft iron preferred for the core of a transformer, and steel for a permanent
magnet?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SOFT IRON :  small hysteresis loop area  ->  small energy loss per cycle
               low coercivity              ->  demagnetises the instant
                                               the current stops
               very high permeability      ->  large B for small current
               ==> ideal for TRANSFORMER CORES and ELECTROMAGNETS

  STEEL     :  high coercivity             ->  very hard to demagnetise
               ==> ideal for PERMANENT MAGNETS
```

### Q B1
**Derive an expression for the magnetic field on the axial line of a bar magnet.**

**ANSWER:**
```
  Let a bar magnet have poles -m (S) and +m (N) separated by 2l, magnetic
  moment M = m(2l). Take a point P on the axis at distance r from the
  centre O.

        +-----------------------+
        |    S             N    |- - - - - - - - - ->  P
        +-----------------------+
       -m                       +m
        |<-------- r ---------->|<---- (r - l) ---->|
        |<-------- (r + l) ------------------------>|

  Field at P due to N pole (away from N):

              mu0        m
      B(N) = ------ x ---------          along  O -> P
              4 pi    (r - l)^2

  Field at P due to S pole (towards S):

              mu0        m
      B(S) = ------ x ---------          along  P -> O
              4 pi    (r + l)^2

  These are opposite, so

              mu0      +-      1              1       -+
      B    = ------ m  | -----------  -  ----------- |
              4 pi     +- (r - l)^2       (r + l)^2  -+

              mu0        (r+l)^2 - (r-l)^2       mu0         4 r l
           = ------ m x --------------------  = ------ m x ------------
              4 pi         (r^2 - l^2)^2         4 pi      (r^2-l^2)^2

  Using   m (2l) = M :

              mu0          2 M r
      B    = ------  x  ---------------
              4 pi       (r^2 - l^2)^2

  For a SHORT magnet ( r >> l ), neglect l^2 :

              mu0        2 M
      B    = ------  x  -------           DIRECTED ALONG M
              4 pi       r^3
```

### Q B3
**Show that a bar magnet is equivalent to a solenoid. Hence obtain the magnetic
moment of a current loop.**

**ANSWER:**
```
  Consider a solenoid of length 2l, radius a, with n turns per unit
  length carrying a current I. Find B at an axial point P at distance r
  from the centre, with r >> l and r >> a.

           |<---------- 2l ---------->|
           +--------------------------+
           |))))))))))))))))))))))))))|- - - - - - - -> P
           +--------------------------+
           -l       O      x  dx      +l
                    |<--------- r ------------>|

  An element of thickness dx at distance x contains n dx turns.
  Its axial field at P is

                    mu0 (n dx) I a^2
        dB  =   ---------------------------
                2 [ (r - x)^2 + a^2 ]^(3/2)

  Integrating from  x = -l  to  x = +l :

                  mu0 n I a^2     +l              dx
        B  =    --------------  INT    ------------------------
                       2         -l    [(r-x)^2 + a^2]^(3/2)

  Since r >> x and r >> a, the bracket is approximately r^3 :

                  mu0 n I a^2                    mu0 n (2l) I a^2
        B  =    --------------  x  (2l)   =    --------------------
                    2 r^3                            2 r^3

  Multiplying and dividing by 2 pi :

                  mu0        2 [ n (2l) I (pi a^2) ]        mu0      2 M
        B  =    ------  x  --------------------------  =   ------ x -----
                  4 pi                r^3                   4 pi     r^3

  This is EXACTLY the axial field of a bar magnet of moment M.
  Hence a bar magnet is equivalent to a solenoid, with

              M  =  n (2l) I (pi a^2)  =  N I A

  So the MAGNETIC MOMENT OF A CURRENT LOOP of N turns, area A,
  carrying current I is        M  =  N I A .
```

### Q B4
**Derive an expression for the torque acting on a magnetic dipole placed in a uniform
magnetic field.**

**ANSWER:**
```
  A bar magnet of pole strength m and magnetic length 2l is placed in a
  uniform field B, its axis making an angle theta with B.

                             F = mB
                             ^
                  +----------+
                  |       N  |
                  |     /    |          B ------->
                  |   / th   |          B ------->
                  | /--------|------->  B ------->
                  | S        |
                  +----------+
                  |
                  v  F = mB

  Force on the N pole  =  m B  along  +B
  Force on the S pole  =  m B  along  -B

  NET FORCE  =  mB - mB  =  ZERO
      -> the magnet does not translate in a UNIFORM field.

  The two forces are equal, opposite and NOT collinear, so they form
  a COUPLE.

      torque = (force) x (perpendicular distance between the forces)
             = (m B) x ( 2l sin theta )
             = ( m x 2l ) B sin theta

              tau  =  M B sin(theta)                (since M = m x 2l)

                                  ->    ->     ->
      In vector form :           tau  =  M  x  B

  SPECIAL CASES
      theta =  0    ->  tau = 0        (stable equilibrium)
      theta = 90    ->  tau = M B      (maximum)
      theta = 180   ->  tau = 0        (unstable equilibrium)
```

### Q B5
**Obtain an expression for the potential energy of a magnetic dipole in a uniform
magnetic field. Discuss the positions of stable and unstable equilibrium.**

**ANSWER:**
```
  Work done in turning the magnet through a small angle d(theta)
  against the torque :

        dW  =  tau d(theta)  =  M B sin(theta) d(theta)

  Total work from theta1 to theta2 :

                   theta2
        W  =   INT         M B sin(theta) d(theta)
                   theta1

           =   M B [ - cos(theta) ]  from theta1 to theta2

        W  =   M B ( cos theta1  -  cos theta2 )

  This work is stored as potential energy. Choosing U = 0 at
  theta = 90 degrees :

        U  =  M B ( cos 90  -  cos theta )

        U  =  - M B cos(theta)   =   -  M . B

  EQUILIBRIUM

    theta = 0     ->  U = - M B     MINIMUM energy   ->  STABLE
                      (M parallel to B; a compass needle rests here)

    theta = 180   ->  U = + M B     MAXIMUM energy   ->  UNSTABLE
                      (the smallest disturbance flips it back to 0)

    theta = 90    ->  U = 0

  Work needed to flip the magnet completely :   W = 2 M B
```

### Q B7
**State and explain Gauss's law in magnetism. What is its significance?**

**ANSWER:**
```
  STATEMENT
  The net magnetic flux through any CLOSED surface is always ZERO.

              ->    ->
      $   B  .   dS   =   0

  EXPLANATION
  Magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops. Any line that
  enters a closed surface must leave it somewhere, because it has no
  place to begin and no place to end. Hence the inward flux exactly
  equals the outward flux and the net flux is zero.

          . - - - - - - - - .
        .                     .
       .    +-----------+      .
       .    |  S     N  |------.------->  lines OUT
   ----.----|           |      .
   ----.----+-----------+      .
       .                       .
        .                     .
          ' - - - - - - - - '

  SIGNIFICANCE
  1.  MAGNETIC MONOPOLES DO NOT EXIST. If a single N pole existed, a
      surface drawn around it would show only outgoing lines and a
      non-zero flux, which is never observed.
  2.  The simplest magnetic entity in nature is the DIPOLE.
  3.  Magnetic field lines form CLOSED LOOPS.
  4.  It is one of MAXWELL'S FOUR EQUATIONS.

  CONTRAST with electrostatics :   $ E . dS  =  q(enclosed) / e0
  which is non-zero when the surface encloses net charge - because
  isolated CHARGES do exist.
```

### Q B10
**Explain hysteresis with the help of a B-H curve. Define retentivity and coercivity.**

**ANSWER:**
```
  HYSTERESIS means "lagging behind". When a ferromagnetic material is
  taken through a full cycle of magnetisation, the magnetic flux
  density B always LAGS BEHIND the magnetising field H.

                              B
                              |
                         b    |
                          ----+---- a          a = SATURATION
                        /     |     \
                       /      |      \
                      /       |       \
        -------------+--------+--------+-------------> H
             -Hc    c|        O        |  +Hc
                     |        |       /
                     \        |      /
                      \       |     /
                       ------+------
                        d     |   e
                              |

    O -> a   H increased: B rises and reaches SATURATION.
    a -> b   H reduced to zero, but B does NOT fall to zero.
             Ob = RETENTIVITY (remanence) Br.
    b -> c   H reversed; at Oc, B finally becomes zero.
             Oc = COERCIVITY Hc.
    c -> d   reverse saturation.
    d -> e -> a   H returned; the curve does NOT retrace its path.

  RETENTIVITY : the value of B that REMAINS when H is brought back to
                zero. Unit : tesla.

  COERCIVITY  : the REVERSE value of H needed to reduce that residual
                B to zero. Unit : ampere / metre.

  The AREA of the loop = ENERGY LOST as heat per unit volume per cycle.
```

                            +-       -+
                            |    I    |
          T  =  2 pi  sqrt  | ------- |
                            |   M B   |
                            +-       -+

     For a bar magnet of mass W, length L and breadth b,
     I = W ( L^2 + b^2 ) / 12.

     T is independent of the amplitude for small oscillations.
```

### Q C5
**Show that a bar magnet is equivalent to a solenoid, and hence obtain the magnetic
moment of a current loop. State Gauss's law in magnetism and explain what it tells us
about magnetic monopoles.**

```
  MARK SPLIT
  ------------------------------------------------------------
  Ampere's hypothesis + comparison of field patterns  1 mark
  Diagram and set-up of the solenoid                  1 mark
  Full integration and approximation                  3 marks
  Result B = (mu0/4pi) 2M/r^3 with M = N I A          1 mark
  Gauss's law in magnetism (statement + explanation)  1 mark
  Non-existence of monopoles + closed loops           1 mark
  ------------------------------------------------------------
                                          TOTAL       8 marks
```

**MODEL ANSWER**

```
  PART 1 — THE BAR MAGNET AS AN EQUIVALENT SOLENOID

  Ampere's hypothesis : all magnetism arises from circulating electric
  currents. So a bar magnet should behave exactly like a solenoid.
  A compass moved around a current-carrying solenoid traces the same
  pattern as around a bar magnet of the same size.

  Solenoid : length 2l, radius a, n turns per unit length, current I.
  Point P on the axis at distance r from the centre, with r >> l, a.

           |<---------- 2l ---------->|
           +--------------------------+
           |))))))))))))))))))))))))))|- - - - - - - -> P
           +--------------------------+
           -l       O      x  dx      +l
                    |<--------- r ------------>|

  Element dx contains n dx turns; its axial field at P is

                  mu0 (n dx) I a^2
        dB  =  ----------------------------
               2 [ (r - x)^2 + a^2 ]^(3/2)

  Integrating x from -l to +l :

                mu0 n I a^2     +l               dx
        B  =  --------------  INT   -------------------------
                     2          -l   [ (r-x)^2 + a^2 ]^(3/2)

  Since r >> x and r >> a, the bracket is approximately r^3 :

                mu0 n I a^2                 mu0 n (2l) I a^2
        B  =  --------------  x (2l)  =  ----------------------
                   2 r^3                        2 r^3

  Multiplying and dividing by 2 pi :

                mu0        2 [ n (2l) I (pi a^2) ]      mu0     2 M
        B  =  ------  x  --------------------------  = ------ x -----
                4 pi                r^3                 4 pi    r^3

  which is exactly the axial field of a bar magnet of moment

              M  =  n (2l) I (pi a^2)  =  N I A

  Hence a bar magnet is equivalent to a solenoid, and the magnetic
  moment of a current loop of N turns and area A carrying current I is
  M = N I A.

  PART 2 — GAUSS'S LAW IN MAGNETISM

  STATEMENT : the net magnetic flux through any closed surface is zero.

              ->    ->
        $     B  .  dS   =   0

  EXPLANATION : magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops, so
  every line entering a closed surface must also leave it. Inward flux
  = outward flux, so the net flux is zero.

  WHAT IT TELLS US

  1.  MAGNETIC MONOPOLES DO NOT EXIST. A surface enclosing a single N
      pole would show only outgoing lines, giving a non-zero flux -
      which is never observed.
  2.  This follows naturally from Part 1 : a magnet is a current loop,
      and a loop always has TWO faces. You cannot make a loop with only
      one face, so you cannot make a single pole.
  3.  Cutting a magnet always produces two complete magnets.
  4.  The simplest magnetic entity in nature is the DIPOLE.
  5.  Contrast : $ E.dS = q/e0 can be non-zero, because isolated
      electric CHARGES do exist. The magnetic law has ZERO on the
      right-hand side, permanently.
  6.  It is one of Maxwell's four equations of electromagnetism.
```
