# Nuclei — Every Question Pattern That Can Be Asked

A "pattern" is a question SHAPE. The numbers change every year. The shape does not.

For each one, learn:
- **TRIGGER** — how to recognise it in one glance
- **METHOD** — the fixed steps
- **TRAP** — where marks get lost

---

```
  PATTERN 1 — ISOTOPES / ISOBARS / ISOTONES                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are isotopes / isobars / isotones? Give examples."
            or "1H^3 and 2He^3 are ______"
  METHOD    1. Give the DEFINITION in one sentence.
            2. Give TWO examples with the full zX^A symbol.
            Memory hook:
               isoTOPes  -> P for PROTON same  (Z same)
               isoBARs   -> A for mAss same    (A same)
               isoTONes  -> N for NEUTRON same (N same)
  TRAP      Giving only the examples with no definition loses half the mark.
            Also: N = A - Z, so compute N before calling two nuclei isotones.
```

```
  PATTERN 2 — READ Z, A AND N OFF A SYMBOL                        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "How many neutrons are in 92U^238?"
  METHOD    Z = bottom-left = protons.  A = top-right = nucleons.
            N = A - Z.
  TRAP      Swapping Z and A. A is ALWAYS the bigger number (except for
            1H^1 where they are equal).
```

```
  PATTERN 3 — NUCLEAR RADIUS FROM R = R0 A^(1/3)                2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the radius of the 13Al^27 nucleus."
  METHOD    1. R = R0 A^(1/3) , R0 = 1.2 fermi.
            2. Take the CUBE ROOT of A.
            3. Multiply by 1.2 and write the answer in fermi
               AND in metres (x 10^-15).
  HELP      Cubes: 2^3=8, 3^3=27, 4^3=64, 5^3=125, 6^3=216, 7^3=343.
  TRAP      Using A instead of A^(1/3). Forgetting the unit.
```

```
  PATTERN 4 — RATIO OF RADII OR VOLUMES OF TWO NUCLEI             2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The mass numbers of two nuclei are in the ratio 1 : 8.
            Find the ratio of their radii / volumes."
  METHOD    RADII    ->  R1/R2 = (A1/A2)^(1/3)
            VOLUMES  ->  V1/V2 = A1/A2       (no cube root!)
            DENSITY  ->  always 1 : 1
  TRAP      Applying the cube root to a VOLUME question. Volume is
            directly proportional to A, full stop.
```

```
  PATTERN 5 — SHOW NUCLEAR DENSITY IS INDEPENDENT OF A            2-4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Show that the density of a nucleus does not depend on its
            mass number" / "Calculate the density of nuclear matter."
  METHOD    1. mass of nucleus = A m
            2. volume = (4/3) pi R^3 = (4/3) pi R0^3 A
            3. rho = A m / [(4/3) pi R0^3 A] -- the A CANCELS.
            4. Put numbers: m = 1.66 x 10^-27 kg , R0 = 1.2 x 10^-15 m
               (4/3) pi R0^3 = 7.24 x 10^-45 m^3
               rho = 2.3 x 10^17 kg/m^3
            5. State: the SAME for every nucleus, 10^14 times the
               density of water.
  TRAP      Forgetting to actually SHOW the cancellation. The marks are
            for the cancellation, not for the final number.
```

```
  PATTERN 6 — DEFINE THE ATOMIC MASS UNIT / PROVE 1 u = 931.5 MeV  2-4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define atomic mass unit. Show that 1 u is equivalent to
            931.5 MeV."
  METHOD    1. Definition: 1/12 of the mass of one neutral C-12 atom
               = 1.66 x 10^-27 kg.
            2. E = m c^2 = 1.66 x 10^-27 x (3 x 10^8)^2 = 1.494 x 10^-10 J
            3. Divide by 1.6 x 10^-19 J/eV -> 9.34 x 10^8 eV
            4. = about 931.5 MeV.
  TRAP      Forgetting the joule-to-eV conversion in step 3, and stopping
            at 1.494 x 10^-10 J.
```

```
  PATTERN 7 — MASS DEFECT AND BINDING ENERGY NUMERICAL            4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the binding energy (per nucleon) of 2He^4 / 8O^16 /
            1H^2, given the atomic masses."
  METHOD    A rigid four-step recipe. Write all four lines every time.
            1. Write Z, A and A - Z.
            2. Mass of the free parts = Z(1.007825) + (A-Z)(1.008665)
            3. dm = (mass of parts) - (given mass M)
            4. BE = dm x 931.5 MeV ;  BE/A = BE / A
  TRAP      * Rounding in the middle -- keep 6 decimals to the end.
            * Forgetting to DIVIDE by A when "per nucleon" is asked.
            * Multiplying by BOTH 931.5 and c^2.
```

```
  PATTERN 8 — WHICH NUCLEUS IS MORE STABLE?                       2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The binding energies of P, Q, R are ... Which is most stable?"
  METHOD    Compute BE / A for each. HIGHEST BE/A wins.
  TRAP      Comparing TOTAL binding energy. U-235 has 1784 MeV of total BE
            but is LESS stable than He-4 with only 28.3 MeV, because
            BE/A is 7.59 against 7.07 -- and both lose to Fe-56 at 8.79.
```

```
  PATTERN 9 — DRAW AND EXPLAIN THE BE-PER-NUCLEON CURVE           8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw a graph of binding energy per nucleon versus mass number
            and explain its features" / "...and explain how it accounts
            for fission and fusion."
  METHOD    1. DRAW the graph. Label the y-axis "BE per nucleon (MeV)"
               from 0 to 9, and the x-axis "mass number A" from 0 to 240.
            2. Mark FIVE points: H-2 (1.1), He-4 (7.07), O-16 (7.98),
               Fe-56 (8.8 -- the PEAK), U-235 (7.6).
            3. Write the FIVE features:
               steep rise for A < 20 ; peaks at A = 4, 8, 12, 16 ;
               flat at 8.5 MeV for A = 30 to 170 (SATURATION) ;
               MAXIMUM 8.8 MeV at A = 56 (IRON, most stable) ;
               slow fall beyond A = 170 (Coulomb repulsion).
            4. The rule: any change TOWARDS the peak releases energy.
               Left side -> join -> FUSION.  Right side -> split -> FISSION.
            5. Give the numbers: 236 x (8.5 - 7.6) = 212 MeV for fission ;
               26.7 MeV for 4 H -> He.
  TRAP      Drawing the graph with no axis labels, or forgetting to mark
            the peak at A = 56 and name IRON. Those two omissions cost
            2 marks on their own.
```

```
  PATTERN 10 — PROPERTIES OF NUCLEAR FORCES                       4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write four properties of nuclear forces" / "What are nuclear
            forces? Why must they exist?"
  METHOD    Start with WHY they must exist (protons repel; gravity is far
            too weak). Then list, each with one explaining line:
            1. Strongest force in nature (100 x electrostatic).
            2. Short range (2 to 3 fm), NOT inverse-square.
            3. Charge independent (p-p = n-n = p-n).
            4. Saturated (nearest neighbours only).
            5. Spin dependent.
            6. Attractive above 0.8 fm, repulsive below it.
  TRAP      Writing bare one-word points. Each property needs a short
            explaining clause to earn its mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 11 — POTENTIAL ENERGY vs SEPARATION GRAPH               4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Draw the variation of potential energy of a pair of nucleons
            with their separation and explain."
  METHOD    1. Draw PE (MeV) on the y-axis, separation r (fm) on the x-axis.
            2. Mark the MINIMUM at r0 = 0.8 fm with PE about -100 MeV.
            3. Show the curve shooting steeply POSITIVE for r < 0.8 fm.
            4. Show it rising towards zero and flattening for r > 3 fm.
            5. Interpret:
                 r < 0.8 fm  -> REPULSIVE (hard core, stops collapse)
                 r = 0.8 fm  -> equilibrium, force zero
                 r > 0.8 fm  -> ATTRACTIVE
                 r > 3 fm    -> force practically zero (short range)
  TRAP      Not labelling r0 = 0.8 fm. That single label carries a mark.
```

```
  PATTERN 12 — COMPARE ALPHA, BETA AND GAMMA RAYS                 4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between alpha, beta and gamma rays."
  METHOD    Draw a THREE-COLUMN TABLE and fill at least eight rows:
            nature, charge, mass, speed, penetrating power, ionising power,
            effect of electric field, effect of magnetic field, range in air.
  TRAP      Writing paragraphs instead of a table -- you will run out of
            time and the examiner will miss your points.
            Also: penetrating and ionising powers are OPPOSITE.
            alpha ionises most and penetrates least.
```

```
  PATTERN 13 — WRITE A DECAY EQUATION (DISPLACEMENT LAW)          2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Write the alpha decay of 92U^238" / "What is the daughter
            nucleus when 6C^14 undergoes beta decay?"
  METHOD    alpha      -> Z - 2 , A - 4 , plus 2He^4
            beta minus -> Z + 1 , A same , plus -1e^0 + antineutrino
            beta plus  -> Z - 1 , A same , plus +1e^0 + neutrino
            gamma      -> Z and A both UNCHANGED
            Then CHECK: mass numbers balance AND atomic numbers balance.
  TRAP      Forgetting the antineutrino in beta-minus decay.
            Forgetting to balance both rows before moving on.
```

```
  PATTERN 14 — COUNT THE ALPHAS AND BETAS IN A DECAY CHAIN        2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "92U^238 decays to 82Pb^206. How many alpha and beta particles
            are emitted?"
  METHOD    1. a = (A_parent - A_daughter) / 4
            2. b = 2a - (Z_parent - Z_daughter)
            Example: a = 32/4 = 8 ; b = 16 - 10 = 6.
  TRAP      Using the Z difference to find the alphas. Only A gives alphas,
            because only alpha changes A.
```

```
  PATTERN 15 — Q VALUE OF A DECAY OR REACTION                     2-4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Calculate the Q value / the energy released in
            92U^238 --> 90Th^234 + 2He^4 , given the masses."
  METHOD    1. Total mass of the LEFT side.
            2. Total mass of the RIGHT side.
            3. dm = left - right.
            4. Q = dm x 931.5 MeV.
            5. State: Q positive -> the decay is spontaneous, and the
               energy appears as the kinetic energy of the products.
  TRAP      Subtracting the wrong way round. If Q comes out negative for a
            natural decay, you have made a sign error.
```

```
  PATTERN 16 — STATE AND DERIVE THE RADIOACTIVE DECAY LAW         8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "State the law of radioactive decay and derive N = N0 e^(-lt)."
            THE SINGLE MOST ASKED LONG QUESTION IN THIS CHAPTER.
  METHOD    1. STATE the law in words.
            2. -dN/dt is proportional to N , so -dN/dt = lambda N.
            3. Separate: dN/N = -lambda dt.
            4. Integrate: ln N = -lambda t + C.
            5. Apply t = 0, N = N0 -> C = ln N0.
            6. ln(N/N0) = -lambda t.
            7. N = N0 e^(-lambda t).
            8. Draw the exponential decay curve, marking T, 2T, 3T on the
               t-axis and N0/2, N0/4, N0/8 on the N-axis.
  TRAP      Skipping the "apply the initial condition" step. Also, the
            MINUS sign in -dN/dt is worth a mark on its own -- it shows N
            is decreasing.
```

```
  PATTERN 17 — DERIVE T = 0.693 / lambda AND tau = 1.44 T         4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define half life and mean life and derive their relations
            with the decay constant."
  METHOD    HALF LIFE
              Put N = N0/2 at t = T in N = N0 e^(-lambda t)
              1/2 = e^(-lambda T) -> 2 = e^(lambda T) -> ln 2 = lambda T
              T = 0.693 / lambda
            MEAN LIFE
              tau = 1/lambda = T/0.693 = 1.44 T
            End with the sentence: MEAN LIFE IS ALWAYS GREATER THAN
            HALF LIFE.
  TRAP      Writing tau = 0.693 T (upside down). Sanity check every time:
            tau MUST be bigger than T.
```

```
  PATTERN 18 — FRACTION REMAINING AFTER A GIVEN TIME              2-4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The half life is 20 min. What fraction remains after 1 hour?"
  METHOD    1. n = t / T   (number of half lives)
            2. Fraction REMAINING = (1/2)^n
            3. Fraction DECAYED   = 1 - (1/2)^n
  TABLE     n=1 -> 1/2 ; n=2 -> 1/4 ; n=3 -> 1/8 ; n=4 -> 1/16 ;
            n=5 -> 1/32 ; n=6 -> 1/64
  TRAP      Answering the "remaining" fraction when the question says
            "decayed". Underline the word in the question before you start.
```

```
  PATTERN 19 — FIND THE HALF LIFE FROM TWO READINGS               2-4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The activity falls from 8000 to 1000 counts/min in 30 minutes.
            Find the half life."
  METHOD    1. Divide: R/R0 = 1000/8000 = 1/8.
            2. Recognise it as (1/2)^n -> n = 3.
            3. T = t / n = 30 / 3 = 10 minutes.
  TRAP      If the ratio is NOT a power of 1/2 (e.g. 20 %, 1/3, 30 %),
            switch to lambda = (2.303 / t) log10 (N0 / N) and then
            T = 0.693 / lambda.
```

```
  PATTERN 20 — DECAY CONSTANT AND MEAN LIFE FROM THE HALF LIFE    2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "The half life of radon is 3.8 days. Find lambda and tau."
  METHOD    1. lambda = 0.693 / T
            2. tau = 1 / lambda  (= 1.44 T -- use this as your check)
            3. If the answer must be in SI, convert T to SECONDS FIRST.
  TRAP      Mixing units. If T is in days, lambda comes out per day,
            NOT per second.
```

```
  PATTERN 21 — ACTIVITY IN BECQUEREL AND CURIE                    4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the activity of 2 g of a substance of half life 5 days"
            / "Express the activity in curie."
  METHOD    1. N = (mass in g / mass number) x 6.022 x 10^23
            2. Convert T to SECONDS.
            3. lambda = 0.693 / T
            4. R = lambda N   -> answer in becquerel
            5. Divide by 3.7 x 10^10 -> answer in curie
  TRAP      Skipping step 2. Becquerel is per SECOND, so the half life
            must be in seconds.
```

```
  PATTERN 22 — DEFINE ACTIVITY, BECQUEREL, CURIE, DECAY CONSTANT  2 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Define activity and give its units" / "Define the curie."
  METHOD    ACTIVITY  R = lambda N = disintegrations per second.
            BECQUEREL = 1 disintegration per second (SI).
            CURIE     = 3.7 x 10^10 Bq = activity of 1 g of radium-226.
            RUTHERFORD = 10^6 Bq.
            DECAY CONSTANT = reciprocal of the time in which N falls to
            1/e (36.8 %) of N0 ; also the probability of decay per
            nucleus per second.
  TRAP      Quoting the curie without its numerical value.
```

```
  PATTERN 23 — EXPLAIN NUCLEAR FISSION WITH ITS EQUATION          4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is nuclear fission? Write the equation and state the
            energy released and its source."
  METHOD    1. Definition in one sentence (heavy nucleus + SLOW neutron
               -> two nearly equal fragments + 2 or 3 neutrons + energy).
            2. Equation:
               92U^235 + 0n^1 --> 56Ba^141 + 36Kr^92 + 3 0n^1 + Q
            3. Balance both rows and say so.
            4. Q = about 200 MeV.
            5. SOURCE: BE/A rises from 7.6 to 8.5 MeV;
               236 x 0.9 = about 212 MeV. Equivalently dm = 0.2154 u.
  TRAP      Writing an unbalanced equation. Always check 236 = 141+92+3
            and 92 = 56 + 36.
```

```
  PATTERN 24 — CHAIN REACTION, k, CRITICAL MASS                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is a chain reaction? Distinguish controlled and
            uncontrolled" / "Define critical mass."
  METHOD    1. Chain reaction: each fission gives about 2.5 neutrons which
               cause further fissions -> self-sustaining.
            2. k = neutrons in one generation / previous generation.
               k < 1 dies out ; k = 1 reactor ; k > 1 bomb.
            3. Controlled: control rods absorb surplus neutrons, steady
               energy, used in a reactor.
               Uncontrolled: all neutrons cause fission, energy in
               microseconds, an atom bomb.
            4. Critical mass: minimum mass for which neutron leakage from
               the surface is small enough for k = 1. A sphere is used.
  TRAP      Defining critical mass as "the mass of uranium in a bomb".
            The word MINIMUM must appear.
```

```
  PATTERN 25 — THE NUCLEAR REACTOR                                8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Describe the construction and working of a nuclear reactor
            with a labelled diagram."
  METHOD    1. Principle: controlled chain reaction, k held at 1.
            2. DIAGRAM: core with fuel rods, control rods, moderator,
               surrounded by shielding; coolant loop to a heat exchanger,
               then turbine and generator.
            3. THE FIVE PARTS, each with its MATERIAL and its FUNCTION:
                 FUEL      enriched U-235 -- supplies fissile nuclei
                 MODERATOR heavy water/graphite -- SLOWS the neutrons
                 CONTROL   cadmium/boron -- ABSORBS surplus neutrons
                 COOLANT   water/liquid sodium -- carries heat away
                 SHIELDING concrete/lead -- stops gamma and neutrons
            4. Working: heat -> steam -> turbine -> generator -> electricity.
  TRAP      Confusing the moderator with the control rods. Moderator SLOWS
            (keeps the neutrons); control rods SWALLOW (remove them).
            Also: naming the parts without giving their functions gets
            only half the marks.
```

```
  PATTERN 26 — NUCLEAR FUSION AND WHY IT NEEDS HIGH TEMPERATURE   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What is nuclear fusion? Why is a very high temperature
            required?"
  METHOD    1. Definition + two example equations with their Q values.
            2. Both nuclei are POSITIVE -> Coulomb repulsion.
            3. They must approach to about 2 fm; the barrier is about
               400 keV.
            4. Average KE = (3/2) k T, so an enormous T is needed;
               with quantum tunnelling about 10^7 K suffices.
            5. Hence the name THERMONUCLEAR reaction. The Sun's core is
               at 1.5 x 10^7 K.
  TRAP      Saying "high temperature is needed to melt the nuclei".
            The reason is ELECTROSTATIC REPULSION, nothing else.
```

```
  PATTERN 27 — SOURCE OF STELLAR ENERGY: p-p AND CNO CYCLES       4-8 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Explain how the Sun produces its energy" / "Describe the
            proton-proton cycle and the carbon-nitrogen cycle."
  METHOD    1. State: fusion of hydrogen into helium in the core.
            2. Write the FOUR steps of the p-p cycle with their energies.
            3. Write the NET reaction:
               4(1H^1) -> 2He^4 + 2 positrons + 2 neutrinos + 26.7 MeV
            4. Write the SIX steps of the CNO cycle and state that carbon
               is a CATALYST -- it returns unchanged.
            5. Note the net result is IDENTICAL for both cycles, and that
               the p-p cycle dominates in cooler stars like the Sun while
               the CNO cycle dominates in hotter, more massive stars.
  TRAP      Forgetting to say the net result is 26.7 MeV, and forgetting
            the word CATALYST for carbon. Both are direct mark-earners.
```

```
  PATTERN 28 — COMPARE FISSION AND FUSION                         4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Distinguish between nuclear fission and nuclear fusion."
  METHOD    A TWO-COLUMN TABLE with at least six rows:
            process, nuclei involved, trigger, energy per event,
            energy per nucleon, controllability, waste, where it occurs.
  TRAP      Writing "fusion gives more energy" without qualifying it.
            Fission gives more per EVENT (200 vs 27 MeV);
            fusion gives more per NUCLEON (6.7 vs 0.85 MeV).
```

```
  PATTERN 29 — ENERGY FROM A GIVEN MASS OF FUEL                   4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "Find the energy released by the complete fission of 1 kg of
            U-235" / "How much U-235 does a 1000 MW reactor use per day?"
  METHOD    ENERGY FROM MASS
              1. N = (mass in g / 235) x 6.022 x 10^23
              2. E = N x 200 MeV
              3. Convert: 1 MeV = 1.6 x 10^-13 J
            MASS FROM POWER
              1. Energy per fission = 200 x 1.6 x 10^-13 = 3.2 x 10^-11 J
              2. Fissions per second = P / 3.2 x 10^-11
              3. Multiply by the time, then convert nuclei to grams
                 using (N / 6.022 x 10^23) x 235.
  KNOW      1 g of U-235  -> 8.2 x 10^10 J
            1 kg of U-235 -> 8.2 x 10^13 J
            1000 MW reactor -> about 1 kg of U-235 per day
  TRAP      Forgetting the MeV-to-joule conversion at the end.
```

```
  PATTERN 30 — RADIATION HAZARDS AND NUCLEAR HOLOCAUST            2-4 marks
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TRIGGER   "What are the hazards of nuclear radiation?" / "Write a note
            on nuclear holocaust."
  METHOD    1. Radiation IONISES living cells and damages DNA.
            2. SOMATIC effects (the exposed person): burns, hair loss,
               fall in white cells, sterility, leukaemia, cancer.
            3. GENETIC effects: mutations passed to later generations.
            4. Units: gray (1 J/kg), sievert; safe limit a few mSv/year.
            5. Holocaust: Hiroshima and Nagasaki 1945; blast, heat,
               fallout, NUCLEAR WINTER, genetic damage.
            6. Protection: distance, short exposure time, shielding
               (paper for alpha, aluminium for beta, lead for gamma).
  TRAP      Only listing effects with no protection measures.
```
