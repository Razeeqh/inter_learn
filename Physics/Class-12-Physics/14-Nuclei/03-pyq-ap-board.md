## Topic: Composition and size

### Q A1
What are isotopes? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ISOTOPES are nuclei that have the SAME atomic number Z (same element)
  but DIFFERENT mass number A (different number of neutrons).

  Examples:   1H^1 , 1H^2 , 1H^3      (hydrogen, deuterium, tritium)
              17Cl^35 and 17Cl^37
```

---

### Q A2
What are isobars? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ISOBARS are nuclei that have the SAME mass number A
  but DIFFERENT atomic number Z.

  Examples:   1H^3 and 2He^3
              18Ar^40 and 20Ca^40
```

---

### Q A3
What are isotones? Give two examples.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ISOTONES are nuclei that have the SAME number of NEUTRONS (N = A - Z)
  but different atomic number and different mass number.

  Examples:   1H^3 (N = 2)  and  2He^4 (N = 2)
              6C^14 (N = 8) and  8O^16 (N = 8)
```

---

### Q A4
Define atomic mass unit. Give its value in kg and in MeV.

**ANSWER:**
```
  One ATOMIC MASS UNIT is 1/12 of the mass of one neutral carbon-12 atom.

     1 u  =  1.66 x 10^-27 kg
     1 u  =  931.5 MeV
```

---

### Q A5
What is the value of R0 in R = R0 A^(1/3)? What does the formula tell you
about nuclear volume?

**ANSWER:**
```
  R0  =  1.2 fermi  =  1.2 x 10^-15 m

  Cubing:   V = (4/3) pi R0^3 A ,  so the VOLUME of a nucleus is
  DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL to its mass number A.
```

---

### Q A6
Calculate the radius of the nucleus 13Al^27.

**ANSWER:**
```
  R = R0 A^(1/3) = 1.2 x (27)^(1/3) = 1.2 x 3 = 3.6 fermi

  R = 3.6 x 10^-15 m
```

### Q A7
Show that nuclear density is independent of the mass number.

**ANSWER:**
```
                mass          A m                 m
  density  =  --------  =  --------------  =  -------------
               volume      (4/3)pi R0^3 A     (4/3)pi R0^3

  A cancels completely, so the density does not depend on A.

  Value  =  2.3 x 10^17 kg/m^3 , the same for every nucleus.
```

### Q A8
Two nuclei have mass numbers in the ratio 8 : 125. What is the ratio of
their nuclear radii?

**ANSWER:**
```
  R1/R2 = (A1/A2)^(1/3) = (8/125)^(1/3) = 2/5

  Ratio  =  2 : 5
```

## Topic: Mass defect and binding energy

### Q A9
Define mass defect. Write its expression.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MASS DEFECT is the difference between the total mass of the free
  nucleons and the actual mass of the nucleus formed by them.

     dm  =  [ Z mp  +  (A - Z) mn ]  -  M

  It is always positive for a stable nucleus.
```

---

### Q A10
Define binding energy of a nucleus.

**ANSWER:** The **binding energy** is the energy required to break a nucleus
completely into its individual free nucleons. Equivalently, it is the energy
released when the free nucleons combine to form the nucleus.
`BE = dm x 931.5 MeV`.

---

### Q A11
What is binding energy per nucleon? What is its significance?

**ANSWER:**
```
  BE per nucleon  =  (total binding energy) / A

  SIGNIFICANCE: it measures the STABILITY of a nucleus.
  The HIGHER the BE per nucleon, the MORE STABLE the nucleus.
  Maximum value is about 8.8 MeV at A = 56 (iron), so iron is the
  most stable nucleus.
```

---

### Q A12
Which nucleus has the highest binding energy per nucleon? What is its value?

**ANSWER:** **Iron, 26Fe^56**, with a binding energy per nucleon of about
**8.8 MeV**. It is therefore the most stable nucleus.

### Q A13
Calculate the binding energy of a deuteron (1H^2).
Given m(1H^2) = 2.014102 u, mp = 1.007825 u, mn = 1.008665 u.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Mass of parts  =  1.007825 + 1.008665  =  2.016490 u
  dm             =  2.016490 - 2.014102  =  0.002388 u
  BE             =  0.002388 x 931.5     =  2.224 MeV

  BE = 2.22 MeV     (BE per nucleon = 1.11 MeV)
```

## Topic: Nuclear forces

### Q A14
Write any two properties of nuclear forces.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  They are the STRONGEST forces in nature, about 100 times stronger
      than the electrostatic force.
  2.  They are SHORT RANGE, acting only up to about 2 to 3 fermi, and
      they do not obey the inverse square law.
  (Also acceptable: charge independent; saturated; spin dependent;
   attractive above 0.8 fm but repulsive below it.)
```

### Q A15
What is meant by "nuclear forces are charge independent"?

**ANSWER:** The nuclear force between two nucleons is the **same** whether the
pair is proton-proton, neutron-neutron or proton-neutron. The force does not
depend on the electric charge of the nucleons.

## Topic: Decay law

### Q A22
State the law of radioactive decay.

**ANSWER:** The number of nuclei disintegrating per second is directly
proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present at that instant.
`- dN/dt = lambda N`, which gives `N = N0 e^(-lambda t)`.

---

### Q A23
Define half life. Write its relation with the decay constant.

**ANSWER:**
```
  HALF LIFE is the time in which HALF the nuclei of a radioactive
  sample disintegrate.

       T  =  0.693 / lambda
```

---

### Q A24
Define mean life. How is it related to half life?

**ANSWER:**
```
  MEAN LIFE is the average lifetime of all the nuclei in the sample.

       tau  =  1 / lambda  =  T / 0.693  =  1.44 T

  Mean life is ALWAYS greater than half life.
```

---

### Q A25
Define the decay constant.

**ANSWER:** The decay constant `lambda` is the **reciprocal of the time** in which
the number of undecayed nuclei falls to **1/e (36.8 %)** of its initial value.
It is also the probability of decay per nucleus per second. Unit: s^-1.

---

### Q A26
Define activity. Name its SI unit and the practical unit with their relation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ACTIVITY  R  =  lambda N  =  the number of disintegrations per second.

  SI unit    :  BECQUEREL (Bq)  =  1 disintegration per second
  Practical  :  CURIE (Ci)      =  3.7 x 10^10 Bq
                (the activity of 1 gram of radium-226)
```

---

### Q A27
What fraction of a radioactive sample remains after 5 half lives?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Fraction = (1/2)^5 = 1/32 = 0.03125 = 3.125 %
  The fraction that has DECAYED is 31/32 = 96.875 %
```

### Q A28
The half life of a radioactive substance is 20 minutes. Find its decay constant.

**ANSWER:**
```
  lambda = 0.693 / T = 0.693 / (20 x 60 s) = 0.693 / 1200
         = 5.775 x 10^-4  s^-1
```

## Topic: Fission and fusion

### Q A29
What is nuclear fission? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NUCLEAR FISSION is the splitting of a heavy nucleus into two nearly equal
  lighter nuclei, with the release of neutrons and a large amount of energy,
  when it is struck by a slow neutron.

     92U^235 + 0n^1 --> 56Ba^141 + 36Kr^92 + 3 0n^1 + 200 MeV
```

---

### Q A30
What is a chain reaction?

**ANSWER:** A **chain reaction** is a self-sustaining sequence of fissions in
which the neutrons released by one fission go on to cause further fissions, so
that the reaction continues on its own and multiplies.

---

### Q A31
Define critical mass.

**ANSWER:** The **minimum mass** of fissile material required so that the neutrons
produced are not all lost from the surface and a **self-sustaining chain reaction**
can be maintained (k = 1). For pure U-235 it is about 10 kg.

---

### Q A32
What is the function of a moderator in a nuclear reactor? Name two moderators.

**ANSWER:** A **moderator slows down** the fast neutrons produced in fission to
thermal speeds, because U-235 undergoes fission efficiently only with **slow**
neutrons. Examples: **heavy water (D2O)** and **graphite**.

---

### Q A33
What is the function of control rods? Name the materials used.

**ANSWER:** Control rods **absorb the surplus neutrons** so that the neutron
multiplication factor is kept exactly at k = 1 and the reaction stays steady.
They are made of **cadmium** or **boron**.

---

### Q A34
What is nuclear fusion? Give one example.

**ANSWER:**
```
  NUCLEAR FUSION is the combining of two very light nuclei into a heavier
  nucleus with the release of a large amount of energy.

     1H^2 + 1H^3  -->  2He^4 + 0n^1 + 17.59 MeV
```

---

### Q A35
Why does nuclear fusion require a very high temperature?

**ANSWER:** Both nuclei are positively charged and repel each other strongly.
They must be given enough kinetic energy to overcome this **Coulomb barrier**
(about 400 keV) and come within 2 fermi. Only a temperature of the order of
**10^7 K** gives them that energy — hence the name *thermonuclear* reaction.

---

### Q A36
What is a thermonuclear reaction? Give an example.

**ANSWER:** A nuclear fusion reaction that is made possible by very **high
temperature**. Example: the **proton-proton cycle** in the Sun,
`4 (1H^1) --> 2He^4 + 2 positrons + 2 neutrinos + 26.7 MeV`.

---

### Q A37
What is the source of the Sun's energy?

**ANSWER:** **Nuclear fusion** of hydrogen into helium, through the
**proton-proton cycle** (and the carbon-nitrogen cycle in hotter stars). Four
protons fuse to form one helium-4 nucleus, releasing about **26.7 MeV**.

---

### Q A38
Name two biological hazards of nuclear radiation.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SOMATIC effects  -  burns, hair loss, fall in white blood cells,
                      leukaemia and other cancers in the exposed person.
  GENETIC effects  -  mutations in the reproductive cells that are
                      passed on to future generations.
```

### Q B1
Define mass defect and binding energy. How are they related? Explain binding
energy per nucleon.

**ANSWER:**
```
  MASS DEFECT (1 mark)
     The mass of a nucleus is always LESS than the sum of the masses of the
     free nucleons that make it. The difference is the mass defect.

        dm  =  [ Z mp  +  (A - Z) mn ]  -  M

  BINDING ENERGY (1 mark)
     The energy required to split the nucleus completely into free nucleons;
     equally, the energy released when they combine.

  RELATION (1 mark)
     The missing mass is converted into energy by Einstein's relation:

        BE  =  dm c^2       or, with dm in u,      BE = dm x 931.5 MeV

  BINDING ENERGY PER NUCLEON (1 mark)
        BE/A = BE / A .  It measures how tightly EACH nucleon is held.
        Higher BE/A means a more stable nucleus.
        Maximum is 8.8 MeV at A = 56 (iron).
```

### Q B2
Calculate the binding energy and binding energy per nucleon of 2He^4.
Given m(2He^4) = 4.002603 u, mp = 1.007825 u, mn = 1.008665 u.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Z = 2 ,  A = 4 ,  A - Z = 2

  Mass of the free nucleons
      2 x 1.007825  =  2.015650
      2 x 1.008665  =  2.017330
                       ---------
                       4.032980 u

  Mass defect
      dm  =  4.032980  -  4.002603  =  0.030377 u

  Binding energy
      BE  =  0.030377 x 931.5  =  28.30 MeV

  Binding energy per nucleon
      BE/A  =  28.30 / 4  =  7.07 MeV

  ANSWER:  BE = 28.30 MeV ,  BE/A = 7.07 MeV per nucleon
```

### Q B4
Write any four properties of nuclear forces.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  STRONGEST FORCE IN NATURE
      About 100 times stronger than the electrostatic force between
      protons and about 10^38 times stronger than gravitation.

  2.  SHORT RANGE
      They act only within about 2 to 3 fermi. Beyond about 10 fermi
      they are effectively zero. They do NOT obey the inverse square law.

  3.  CHARGE INDEPENDENT
      The force is identical for p-p, n-n and p-n pairs. It does not
      depend on charge.

  4.  SATURATED
      Each nucleon interacts only with its nearest neighbours, not with
      every other nucleon. This is why BE per nucleon is nearly constant
      and why nuclear volume is proportional to A.

  (5) SPIN DEPENDENT -- stronger when the nucleon spins are parallel.
  (6) ATTRACTIVE beyond 0.8 fm, strongly REPULSIVE below 0.8 fm.
```

### Q B6
State the displacement laws of alpha and beta decay with one example each.
What is emitted along with the beta particle and why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ALPHA DISPLACEMENT LAW
     zX^A  -->  (z-2)Y^(A-4)  +  2He^4
     Z decreases by 2, A decreases by 4; the daughter moves two places
     to the LEFT in the periodic table.
     Example:   92U^238  -->  90Th^234  +  2He^4

  BETA-MINUS DISPLACEMENT LAW
     zX^A  -->  (z+1)Y^A  +  -1e^0  +  antineutrino
     Z increases by 1, A is unchanged; the daughter moves one place
     to the RIGHT.
     Example:   90Th^234  -->  91Pa^234  +  -1e^0  +  antineutrino

  WHY AN ANTINEUTRINO IS ALSO EMITTED
     Beta particles come out with a CONTINUOUS range of energies, not a
     single fixed value. If only an electron were emitted, energy and
     momentum would not be conserved. Pauli therefore proposed a third,
     almost massless and chargeless particle -- the neutrino (antineutrino
     in beta-minus decay) -- which carries away the remaining energy.
```

### Q B7
Derive the relation between half life and decay constant. Also obtain the
relation between mean life and half life.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1   From the decay law         N  =  N0 e^(-lambda t)

  STEP 2   At  t = T (half life),  N = N0 / 2

              N0 / 2  =  N0 e^(-lambda T)

  STEP 3       1/2    =  e^(-lambda T)

  STEP 4   Inverting:   2  =  e^(lambda T)

  STEP 5   Taking natural logarithms:

               ln 2   =  lambda T

               T  =  ln 2 / lambda  =  0.693 / lambda

  MEAN LIFE
      The mean life is the reciprocal of the decay constant:

               tau  =  1 / lambda

      Substituting lambda = 0.693 / T :

               tau  =  T / 0.693  =  1.44 T
               or        T  =  0.693 tau

      Since 1.44 > 1, the MEAN LIFE is always LONGER than the HALF LIFE.
```

The half life of radium is 1600 years. Calculate its decay constant, its mean
life, and the activity of 1 g of radium-226.

**ANSWER:**
```

      R  =  lambda N  =  1.372 x 10^-11 x 2.665 x 10^21
                      =  3.66 x 10^10 disintegrations per second

  ANSWER: about 3.7 x 10^10 Bq = 1 CURIE, which is how the curie
          was originally defined.
```

### Q B9
A radioactive sample has an activity of 8000 disintegrations per second. After
30 minutes the activity falls to 1000 per second. Find the half life, the decay
constant and the mean life.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1   R / R0  =  1000 / 8000  =  1/8  =  (1/2)^3
           So n = 3 half lives have passed.

  STEP 2   n T = 30 min   ->   T  =  30 / 3  =  10 minutes

  STEP 3   lambda = 0.693 / T = 0.693 / (10 x 60 s)
                  = 1.155 x 10^-3 s^-1

  STEP 4   tau = 1/lambda = 866 s = 14.4 minutes
           (check: 1.44 x 10 = 14.4 min)

  ANSWER:  T = 10 min ,  lambda = 1.155 x 10^-3 s^-1 ,  tau = 14.4 min
```

### Q B10
Explain nuclear fission. Write the equation for U-235 and state the amount of
energy released and where it comes from.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION (1 mark)
     Nuclear fission is the breaking up of a heavy nucleus, on capture of a
     slow neutron, into two nearly equal lighter nuclei with the emission of
     2 or 3 neutrons and a large amount of energy.

  EQUATION (1 mark)
     92U^235 + 0n^1 --> [92U^236]* --> 56Ba^141 + 36Kr^92 + 3 0n^1 + Q

     Check:  235 + 1 = 141 + 92 + 3 = 236
              92 + 0 =  56 + 36     =  92

  ENERGY (1 mark)
     Q = about 200 MeV per fission = 3.2 x 10^-11 J.

  SOURCE OF THE ENERGY (1 mark)
     The products are more tightly bound than the parent.
     BE per nucleon rises from 7.6 MeV (U-236) to about 8.5 MeV
     (fragments). The gain is 236 x 0.9 = about 212 MeV, which appears
     as the kinetic energy of the fragments (about 165 MeV) plus the
     neutrons, gamma rays and beta particles.
     Equivalently, the products weigh about 0.2154 u less than the
     reactants, and 0.2154 x 931.5 = 200 MeV.
```

### Q B11
What is a chain reaction? Distinguish between controlled and uncontrolled chain
reactions. What is meant by the neutron multiplication factor?

**ANSWER:**
```
  CHAIN REACTION
     Each fission of U-235 releases about 2.5 neutrons. If these neutrons
     produce further fissions, and their neutrons produce still more, the
     process becomes self-sustaining. This is a chain reaction.

  NEUTRON MULTIPLICATION FACTOR k
          neutrons produced in one generation
     k = --------------------------------------

     k = 1  ->  critical        ->  steady reaction
     k > 1  ->  super-critical  ->  explosive growth

  +-----------------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  CONTROLLED (k = 1)         |  UNCONTROLLED (k > 1)         |
  +-----------------------------+-------------------------------+
  |  Surplus neutrons absorbed  |  All neutrons allowed to      |
  |  by cadmium/boron control   |  cause further fissions       |
  |  rods                       |                               |
  |  Energy released slowly and |  Energy released in a few     |
  |  steadily                   |  microseconds                 |
  |  Used in a NUCLEAR REACTOR  |  Used in an ATOM BOMB         |
  |  to generate electricity    |                               |
  +-----------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

### Q B12
Explain nuclear fusion. Why does it require a very high temperature? Give two
examples of fusion reactions.

**ANSWER:**
```
  DEFINITION
     Nuclear fusion is the process in which two very light nuclei combine
     to form a heavier nucleus with the release of a large amount of energy.
     Energy is released because the product sits HIGHER on the binding
     energy per nucleon curve than the reactants.

  WHY VERY HIGH TEMPERATURE IS NEEDED
     Both nuclei carry POSITIVE charge and repel each other. To fuse, they
     must approach to within about 2 fermi, which requires them to overcome
     a COULOMB BARRIER of roughly 400 keV. The only way to give every
     nucleus in a gas that much kinetic energy is to raise the temperature,
     since average KE = (3/2) k T. A temperature of the order of 10^7 K is
     required (helped by quantum tunnelling). Such reactions are therefore
     called THERMONUCLEAR reactions.

  EXAMPLES
     1H^2 + 1H^2  -->  2He^3 + 0n^1  +  3.27 MeV
     1H^2 + 1H^3  -->  2He^4 + 0n^1  + 17.59 MeV
```

### Q B13
How is the energy of the Sun produced? Explain the proton-proton cycle.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The Sun's energy comes from the NUCLEAR FUSION of hydrogen into helium
  in its core, where the temperature is about 1.5 x 10^7 K. The dominant
  route is the PROTON-PROTON CYCLE:

     1H^1 + 1H^1   -->  1H^2  + +1e^0 + neutrino  +  0.42 MeV
     +1e^0 + -1e^0 -->  gamma + gamma             +  1.02 MeV
     1H^2 + 1H^1   -->  2He^3 + gamma             +  5.49 MeV
     2He^3 + 2He^3 -->  2He^4 + 1H^1 + 1H^1       + 12.86 MeV

  (the first three steps happen twice, to make the two He-3 nuclei
   needed for the last step)

  NET REACTION
     4 (1H^1) + 2(-1e^0)  -->  2He^4 + 2 neutrinos + 6 gamma + 26.7 MeV

  So four hydrogen nuclei become one helium nucleus and 26.7 MeV of energy.
  Because the first step is extremely slow, the Sun burns steadily and has
  lasted 4.5 billion years.
```

### Q B14
Compare nuclear fission and nuclear fusion (any six points).

**ANSWER:**

### Q C1  *** most asked ***
State the law of radioactive decay. Derive N = N0 e^(-lambda t). Define half
life and mean life and obtain their relations with the decay constant. Define
activity and its units.

**ANSWER — mark split shown**

```
  (i)  STATEMENT OF THE LAW                                     [1 mark]

       The rate of disintegration of a radioactive sample at any instant is
       directly proportional to the number of undecayed nuclei present at
       that instant, and depends on nothing else.

  (ii) DERIVATION                                               [3 marks]

       Let N be the number of undecayed nuclei at time t, and dN the change
       in a small interval dt.

           - dN / dt   is proportional to   N

           - dN / dt   =   lambda N            (lambda = decay constant)

       Separating the variables:

               dN / N   =   - lambda dt

       Integrating both sides:

               INT (dN / N)  =  - lambda INT dt

               ln N   =   - lambda t  +  C

       At t = 0, N = N0 , so   ln N0 = C .   Substituting:

               ln N  -  ln N0   =   - lambda t

               ln ( N / N0 )    =   - lambda t

       Taking exponentials of both sides:

           +-------------------------------------+
           |        N   =   N0 e^(- lambda t)    |
           +-------------------------------------+

  (iii) THE DECAY CURVE                                         [1 mark]

        N
        |
      N0*
        |  *
        |     *
     N0/2 - - - *
        |   :     *
     N0/4 - :- - - -*
        |   :   :     *  *
     N0/8 - :- -:- -:- - - -* * *
        |   :   :   :             * * * * * * * *
      0 +---+---+---+---+---+---+---+---+---+---> t
        0   T   2T  3T  4T  5T

        The curve is exponential and never reaches zero.

  (iv) HALF LIFE                                                [1.5 marks]

       DEFINITION: the time in which half the nuclei present decay.

       Put N = N0/2 at t = T :

               N0/2  =  N0 e^(-lambda T)
                 1/2 =  e^(-lambda T)
                   2 =  e^(lambda T)
               ln 2  =  lambda T

               T  =  0.693 / lambda

  (v)  MEAN LIFE                                                [1 mark]

       DEFINITION: the average lifetime of all the nuclei of the sample.

               tau  =  1 / lambda  =  T / 0.693  =  1.44 T

       Mean life is always GREATER than half life.

  (vi) ACTIVITY AND ITS UNITS                                   [1.5 marks]

       ACTIVITY  R  =  - dN/dt  =  lambda N
                    =  the number of disintegrations per second.

       It also decays exponentially:   R = R0 e^(-lambda t)

       UNITS
          becquerel (Bq)  = 1 disintegration per second   [SI unit]
          curie (Ci)      = 3.7 x 10^10 Bq
                          = activity of 1 g of radium-226
          rutherford (Rd) = 10^6 Bq
```

### Q C2  *** most asked ***
Draw and explain the graph of binding energy per nucleon against mass number.
Explain how the shape of the curve accounts for both nuclear fission and
nuclear fusion.

**ANSWER — mark split shown**

```
  (i)  THE GRAPH                                                [3 marks]

   BE/A
  (MeV)
    |
  9 |                Fe-56 (8.8 MeV at A = 56)  <-- PEAK
    |                  *
  8 |          O-16  * * * * * * *
    |            *                * * * * * *
    |         *                              * * * * *  U-235 (7.6)
  7 |    He-4
    |     *
  6 |
    |
  5 |    Li-7
    |     *
  4 |
  3 |
    |  H-3
  2 |   *
  1 | H-2 *  (1.1 MeV)
    |*
  0 +--+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+----+--> A
    0  20   40   60   80  100  120  140  160  180  200  240

  (ii) FEATURES OF THE CURVE                                    [3 marks]

     1. For very light nuclei (A < 20) BE/A is small -- only 1.1 MeV for
        the deuteron -- and the curve rises steeply. Such nuclei are loosely
        bound because most of their nucleons are on the surface with few
        neighbours.

     2. There are sharp peaks at A = 4, 8, 12 and 16 (He-4, Be-8, C-12,
        O-16) -- these "alpha-like" nuclei are exceptionally stable.

     3. Between A = 30 and A = 170 the curve is almost FLAT at about
        8.5 MeV. This near-constancy is direct proof that the nuclear force
        is SATURATED: each nucleon binds only to its nearest neighbours.

     4. The MAXIMUM is 8.8 MeV at A = 56, IRON. Iron is the most stable
        nucleus; a star stops producing energy once its core becomes iron.

     5. Beyond A = 170 the curve falls slowly, reaching 7.6 MeV at U-235,
        because the long-range Coulomb repulsion between the many protons
        begins to overcome the short-range nuclear attraction.

  (iii) HOW THE SHAPE EXPLAINS FISSION AND FUSION               [2 marks]

     THE RULE: any change that moves nuclei TOWARDS the peak of the curve
     increases the total binding energy, and the surplus is released as
     energy.

     FISSION (right-hand side, falling)
        A heavy nucleus at A = 236 has BE/A = 7.6 MeV.
        Splitting it into two fragments near A = 118 raises BE/A to 8.5 MeV.
        Gain = 236 x (8.5 - 7.6) = about 212 MeV, released as energy.

     FUSION (left-hand side, rising)
        Four separate protons have BE/A = 0.
        Fusing them into He-4 gives BE/A = 7.07 MeV.
        Gain = about 26.7 MeV, released as energy.

     Fission gives more energy per EVENT; fusion gives far more energy
     per NUCLEON, and therefore per kilogram of fuel.
```

### Q C3
Explain the working of a nuclear reactor with a labelled diagram. Describe the
function of each part.

**ANSWER — mark split shown**

```
  (i)  PRINCIPLE                                                [1 mark]

     A nuclear reactor produces energy by a CONTROLLED chain reaction of
     nuclear fission, holding the neutron multiplication factor at exactly
     k = 1 so that the energy comes out steadily instead of explosively.

  (ii) DIAGRAM                                                  [3 marks]

  +==========================================================================+
  |                        CONCRETE SHIELDING                                |
  |  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+  |
  |  |                                                                    |  |
  |  |   ||   ||   ||   ||   <-- CONTROL RODS (cadmium / boron)           |  |
  |  |  +--+ +--+ +--+ +--+                                               |  |
  |  |  |FU| |FU| |FU| |FU|  <-- FUEL RODS (enriched uranium-235)         |  |
  |  |  |EL| |EL| |EL| |EL|                                               |  |
  |  |  +--+ +--+ +--+ +--+                                               |  |
  |  |   ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~   <-- MODERATOR (heavy water / graphite)      |  |
  |  |        REACTOR CORE                                                |  |
  |  +------------|-------------------------------------------------------+  |
  +===============|==========================================================+
                  |  hot coolant out
                  v
            +-----------+        steam       +---------+      +-----------+
            |   HEAT    | ---------------->  | TURBINE | ---> | GENERATOR |
            | EXCHANGER |                    +---------+      +-----------+
            +-----------+ <----- water ----------+                  |
                  ^                                                 v
                  +-------------- PUMP <---------------      ELECTRICITY

  (iii) THE PARTS AND THEIR FUNCTIONS                           [4 marks]

     1. FUEL  -- uranium enriched to 2-3 % in U-235 (or Pu-239, U-233).
        It supplies the fissile nuclei. Natural uranium contains only
        0.7 % U-235, too little for most designs.

     2. MODERATOR -- heavy water (D2O), graphite or ordinary water.
        It SLOWS DOWN the fast (2 MeV) neutrons produced in fission to
        thermal energy (about 0.025 eV) by repeated elastic collisions,
        because U-235 undergoes fission efficiently only with SLOW
        neutrons. Light nuclei are used because a neutron loses most of
        its energy when it collides with a particle of its own mass.

     3. CONTROL RODS -- cadmium or boron, which are strong neutron
        absorbers. They are pushed in or pulled out to ABSORB surplus
        neutrons and hold k at exactly 1. Pushing them fully in shuts
        the reactor down.

     4. COOLANT -- water, heavy water, liquid sodium or carbon dioxide,
        circulated through the core. It CARRIES THE HEAT away to the heat
        exchanger, where it boils water into steam to drive the turbine,
        and prevents the core from overheating.

     5. SHIELDING -- 2 to 3 metres of concrete, with lead. It PREVENTS the
        intense gamma radiation and neutrons from escaping and harming
        the operators.

  (iv) OUTPUT
     The steam turns a turbine coupled to a generator, producing
     electricity. Indian reactors include Tarapur, Kalpakkam, Kaiga,
     Narora and Kudankulam.
```

### Q C4
What is nuclear fusion? Explain why it needs a high temperature. Describe the
proton-proton cycle and the carbon-nitrogen cycle as the sources of stellar
energy. Compare fission and fusion.

**ANSWER — mark split shown**

```
  (i)  DEFINITION                                               [1 mark]

     Nuclear fusion is the combining of two very light nuclei to form a
     heavier nucleus with the release of a large amount of energy, because
     the product lies higher on the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve.

        1H^2 + 1H^2  -->  2He^3 + 0n^1  +  3.27 MeV
        1H^2 + 1H^3  -->  2He^4 + 0n^1  + 17.59 MeV

  (ii) WHY A HIGH TEMPERATURE IS REQUIRED                       [2 marks]

     Both nuclei are positively charged and repel each other. To fuse, they
     must approach within about 2 fermi, where the nuclear attraction can
     act. The electrostatic potential barrier to be crossed is about
     400 keV.

     Potential
     energy
       |        COULOMB BARRIER (about 400 keV)
       |            ****
       |          **    **
       |        **        ***
       |     ***             ******
     0 +--------------------------|---------------> separation r
       |                          |
       |                     *****  nuclear well (attraction)

     Since the average kinetic energy of a particle is (3/2) k T, an
     enormous temperature is needed. With quantum tunnelling, about
     10^7 K suffices; the Sun's core is at 1.5 x 10^7 K. Because heat
     drives the reaction, it is called a THERMONUCLEAR reaction.

  (iii) PROTON-PROTON CYCLE                                     [2 marks]

        1H^1 + 1H^1   -->  1H^2  + +1e^0 + neutrino  +  0.42 MeV
        +1e^0 + -1e^0 -->  gamma + gamma             +  1.02 MeV
        1H^2 + 1H^1   -->  2He^3 + gamma             +  5.49 MeV
        2He^3 + 2He^3 -->  2He^4 + 1H^1 + 1H^1       + 12.86 MeV

        NET:  4(1H^1) + 2(-1e^0) --> 2He^4 + 2 neutrinos + 26.7 MeV

        This dominates in the Sun and in cooler stars.

  (iv) CARBON-NITROGEN (CNO) CYCLE                              [2 marks]

        6C^12 + 1H^1  -->  7N^13 + gamma
        7N^13         -->  6C^13 + +1e^0 + neutrino
        6C^13 + 1H^1  -->  7N^14 + gamma
        7N^14 + 1H^1  -->  8O^15 + gamma
        8O^15         -->  7N^15 + +1e^0 + neutrino
        7N^15 + 1H^1  -->  6C^12 + 2He^4     <-- carbon comes back

        Carbon acts purely as a CATALYST. Adding all six steps gives the
        same net result:

        NET:  4(1H^1) --> 2He^4 + 2(+1e^0) + 2 neutrinos + 26.7 MeV

        This dominates in hotter, more massive stars (above about
        1.7 x 10^7 K).

  (v)  FISSION versus FUSION                                    [1 mark]

     +-------------------+------------------+---------------------+
     | Process           | heavy SPLITS     | light JOIN          |
     | Trigger           | slow neutron     | 10^7 K temperature  |
     | Energy per event  | 200 MeV          | 27 MeV              |
     | Energy per nucleon| 0.85 MeV         | 6.7 MeV (more)      |
     | Controllable      | yes (reactor)    | not yet on Earth    |
     | Waste             | radioactive      | harmless helium     |
     | Occurs in         | reactor, A-bomb  | stars, H-bomb       |
     +-------------------+------------------+---------------------+
```

### Q C5
Define mass defect and binding energy. Explain the nature of nuclear forces and
their properties. Draw the potential energy versus separation graph for two
nucleons and explain it.

**ANSWER — mark split shown**

```
  (i)  MASS DEFECT AND BINDING ENERGY                           [2 marks]

     MASS DEFECT   dm = [ Z mp + (A - Z) mn ] - M
        the difference between the total mass of the free nucleons and the
        actual mass of the nucleus.

     BINDING ENERGY  BE = dm c^2 = dm x 931.5 MeV
        the energy needed to separate the nucleus completely into free
        nucleons; equally, the energy released when they combine.

     BE per nucleon = BE / A measures stability; the maximum is 8.8 MeV
     at A = 56 (iron).

  (ii) WHY A NUCLEAR FORCE MUST EXIST                           [1 mark]

     Inside a nucleus, protons only a few fermi apart repel each other
     electrostatically with enormous force, and gravity is 10^36 times
     too weak to hold them. A third, far stronger, attractive force must
     therefore exist -- the NUCLEAR FORCE.

  (iii) PROPERTIES OF NUCLEAR FORCES                            [3 marks]

     1. STRONGEST force in nature -- about 100 times the electrostatic
        force and 10^38 times gravitation.
     2. VERY SHORT RANGE -- effective only up to 2 to 3 fermi, zero beyond
        about 10 fermi; it does NOT obey the inverse square law.
     3. CHARGE INDEPENDENT -- identical for p-p, n-n and p-n pairs.
     4. SATURATED -- each nucleon binds only to its nearest neighbours,
        which is why BE/A is nearly constant and volume is proportional
        to A.
     5. SPIN DEPENDENT -- stronger when the nucleon spins are parallel.
     6. ATTRACTIVE for r > 0.8 fm but strongly REPULSIVE for r < 0.8 fm;
        this hard core prevents the nucleus from collapsing.
     7. EXCHANGE in nature -- mediated by mesons (Yukawa's theory).

  (iv) POTENTIAL ENERGY GRAPH                                   [2 marks]

     Potential
     energy
     (MeV)
       |
   +100|  *
       |  *      REPULSIVE region (r < 0.8 fm)
       |   *
       |    *
     0 +----*-------------------------------------------> r (fm)
       |0   |*     1     2     3     4     5
       |    | *
       |    |  *          . . . . . . . . . . . .
       |    |   * . . . .      force falls to zero
   -100|    +----*   <-- MINIMUM at r0 = 0.8 fm, PE about -100 MeV

     READING THE GRAPH
       * At r = r0 = 0.8 fm the potential energy is a MINIMUM (about
         -100 MeV). The force here is ZERO and this is the equilibrium
         separation at which nucleons sit.
       * For r LESS than 0.8 fm the potential energy rises very steeply
         and becomes positive: the force is strongly REPULSIVE, which
         stops the nucleus collapsing.
       * For r GREATER than 0.8 fm the potential energy rises towards
         zero: the force is ATTRACTIVE.
       * Beyond about 3 fm the curve is almost flat at zero: the force
         has effectively vanished, showing the short range.
```

### Q C6
Explain radioactivity. Compare alpha, beta and gamma radiations. State the
displacement laws with examples and define the Q value of a decay.

**ANSWER — mark split shown**

```
  (i)  RADIOACTIVITY                                            [2 marks]

     Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of alpha, beta or gamma
     radiation from the unstable nucleus of an atom.

     Discovered by HENRI BECQUEREL in 1896 in uranium salts; Marie and
     Pierre Curie then isolated polonium and radium.

     KEY FEATURES
        * It is a NUCLEAR phenomenon, not a chemical one.
        * It is SPONTANEOUS and RANDOM.
        * It is UNAFFECTED by temperature, pressure, chemical combination,
          or electric and magnetic fields.
        * All elements with Z > 82 are naturally radioactive.

  (ii) COMPARISON OF THE THREE RADIATIONS                       [3 marks]

     +---------------+--------------+--------------+---------------+
     | PROPERTY      | ALPHA        | BETA         | GAMMA         |
     +---------------+--------------+--------------+---------------+
     | Nature        | 2He^4 nucleus| electron     | EM photon     |
     | Charge        | + 2e         | - 1e         | zero          |
     | Mass          | 4 u          | 1/1836 u     | zero rest mass|
     | Speed         | 0.05 c       | up to 0.99 c | c             |
     | Penetration   | least        | medium       | highest       |
     | Ionisation    | highest      | medium       | least         |
     | Electric field| to -ve plate | to +ve plate | undeflected   |
     | Magnetic field| small bend   | large bend,  | undeflected   |
     |               |              | opposite way |               |
     +---------------+--------------+--------------+---------------+

  (iii) DISPLACEMENT LAWS                                       [2 marks]

     ALPHA:   zX^A --> (z-2)Y^(A-4) + 2He^4
              Z falls by 2, A falls by 4.
              92U^238 --> 90Th^234 + 2He^4

     BETA-MINUS: zX^A --> (z+1)Y^A + -1e^0 + antineutrino
              Z rises by 1, A unchanged; inside, 0n^1 --> 1p^1 + e- + v-bar
              6C^14 --> 7N^14 + -1e^0 + antineutrino

     BETA-PLUS:  zX^A --> (z-1)Y^A + +1e^0 + neutrino
              11Na^22 --> 10Ne^22 + +1e^0 + neutrino

     GAMMA:   zX^A(excited) --> zX^A(ground) + gamma
              Neither Z nor A changes.
              27Co^60 --> 28Ni^60* + beta ; 28Ni^60* --> 28Ni^60 + gamma
              (1.17 MeV and 1.33 MeV)

  (iv) Q VALUE                                                  [1 mark]

     Q = [ mass of parent - total mass of products ] x 931.5 MeV

     If Q is positive, energy is released and the decay occurs
     spontaneously; the Q value appears as the kinetic energy of the
     products.

     EXAMPLE  92U^238 --> 90Th^234 + 2He^4
        dm = 238.05079 - (234.04363 + 4.00260) = 0.00456 u
        Q  = 0.00456 x 931.5 = 4.25 MeV
```
