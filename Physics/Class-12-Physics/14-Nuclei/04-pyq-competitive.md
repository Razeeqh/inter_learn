# PATTERN 1 — NUCLEAR RADIUS FROM R = R0 A^(1/3)

### Q1
The radius of the nucleus 13Al^27 is 3.6 fm. The radius of 52Te^125 is
(a) 6.0 fm  (b) 9.0 fm  (c) 12.0 fm  (d) 4.8 fm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  R1/R2 = (A1/A2)^(1/3)
  R_Te  = 3.6 x (125/27)^(1/3) = 3.6 x (5/3) = 6.0 fm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Both 27 and 125 are perfect cubes. Take cube roots first
> (3 and 5), then use the simple ratio. Never touch a calculator.

---

### Q2
The ratio of the radii of two nuclei with mass numbers 8 and 64 is
(a) 1 : 8  (b) 1 : 4  (c) 1 : 2  (d) 1 : 6

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  (8/64)^(1/3) = (1/8)^(1/3) = 1/2      ->  1 : 2
```

---

### Q3
If the nuclear radius of 2He^4 is 2 fm, the radius of 92U^238 is nearly
(a) 4.0 fm  (b) 5.8 fm  (c) 7.8 fm  (d) 9.6 fm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  R = 2 x (238/4)^(1/3) = 2 x (59.5)^(1/3)
  3.9^3 = 59.3 , so (59.5)^(1/3) = 3.9
  R = 2 x 3.9 = 7.8 fm
```

---

### Q4
The volume of a nucleus is proportional to
(a) A  (b) A^2  (c) A^(1/3)  (d) A^(2/3)

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  V = (4/3) pi R^3 = (4/3) pi R0^3 A     ->    V is proportional to A
```

---

### Q5
Two nuclei have volumes in the ratio 27 : 1. The ratio of their radii is
(a) 27 : 1  (b) 9 : 1  (c) 3 : 1  (d) 1 : 3

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  V is proportional to R^3 , so R1/R2 = (27)^(1/3) = 3   ->  3 : 1
```

# PATTERN 2 — NUCLEAR DENSITY

### Q6
The nuclear density of 8O^16 compared with that of 92U^238 is
(a) 2 times  (b) 14.9 times  (c) equal  (d) 1/14.9 times

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Nuclear density is INDEPENDENT of A. It is the same for all nuclei.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** If a question asks you to *compare nuclear densities*,
> the answer is **always "equal"** unless the options say otherwise.

---

### Q7
The order of magnitude of nuclear density is
(a) 10^3 kg/m^3  (b) 10^10 kg/m^3  (c) 10^17 kg/m^3  (d) 10^24 kg/m^3

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  rho = m / [(4/3) pi R0^3]
      = 1.66 x 10^-27 / 7.24 x 10^-45
      = 2.3 x 10^17 kg/m^3
```

---

### Q8
The nucleus of an atom has radius R and mass number A. If the mass number is
increased 8 times, the density becomes
(a) 8 rho  (b) 2 rho  (c) rho/8  (d) rho

**ANSWER: (d)** Mass rises 8 times, but the volume also rises 8 times
(V is proportional to A), so the density is unchanged.

# PATTERN 4 — MASS DEFECT AND BINDING ENERGY

### Q12
The mass defect of a nucleus with A = 4 is 0.03 u. Its binding energy per
nucleon is nearly
(a) 28 MeV  (b) 14 MeV  (c) 7 MeV  (d) 3.5 MeV

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  BE   = 0.03 x 931.5 = 27.9 MeV
  BE/A = 27.9 / 4 = 6.99 = about 7 MeV
```

---

### Q13
The binding energy per nucleon is MAXIMUM for
(a) 2He^4  (b) 26Fe^56  (c) 92U^235  (d) 1H^2

**ANSWER: (b)** Iron-56, at about 8.8 MeV per nucleon. It is the most
stable nucleus.

---

### Q14
Which of the following has the LOWEST binding energy per nucleon?
(a) 1H^2  (b) 2He^4  (c) 8O^16  (d) 26Fe^56

**ANSWER: (a)** The deuteron, only 1.11 MeV per nucleon.

---

### Q15
The binding energies per nucleon of nuclei P, Q, R and S are 5.6, 7.6, 8.8 and
7.0 MeV. The MOST STABLE nucleus is
(a) P  (b) Q  (c) R  (d) S

**ANSWER: (c)** Highest BE per nucleon = most stable.

---

### Q16
In the reaction `1H^2 + 1H^3 --> 2He^4 + 0n^1`, the binding energies of
1H^2, 1H^3 and 2He^4 are a, b and c respectively (in MeV). The energy released is
(a) a + b + c  (b) c + a - b  (c) c - a - b  (d) a + b - c

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Energy released = (BE of products) - (BE of reactants)
                  = c - (a + b)  =  c - a - b
  (the neutron is a free nucleon, so its binding energy is zero)
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Energy released is **always BE(after) − BE(before)**.
> If your answer comes out negative you have subtracted the wrong way.

---

### Q17
A heavy nucleus of mass number 240 with BE/A = 7.6 MeV splits into two fragments
of mass number 120 each with BE/A = 8.5 MeV. The energy released is
(a) 804 MeV  (b) 216 MeV  (c) 108 MeV  (d) 2040 MeV

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Before:  240 x 7.6 = 1824 MeV
  After :  240 x 8.5 = 2040 MeV
  Released = 2040 - 1824 = 216 MeV
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Energy released = A x (change in BE/A) = 240 x 0.9 = 216 MeV.
> One line, no long multiplication.

---

### Q18
In the fusion `4 (1H^1) --> 2He^4 + 2 positrons + 26.7 MeV`, the energy released
per nucleon is about
(a) 26.7 MeV  (b) 13.35 MeV  (c) 6.7 MeV  (d) 0.85 MeV

**ANSWER: (c)**  `26.7 / 4 = 6.7 MeV per nucleon`

# PATTERN 5 — DECAY LAW: FRACTION AFTER n HALF LIVES

### Q19
The half life of a radioactive element is 20 minutes. The fraction left after
1 hour is
(a) 1/2  (b) 1/4  (c) 1/8  (d) 1/16

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  n = 60 / 20 = 3 half lives   ->   (1/2)^3 = 1/8
```

---

### Q20
Seven-eighths of a radioactive sample decays in 15 days. Its half life is
(a) 15 days  (b) 7.5 days  (c) 5 days  (d) 3 days

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Decayed 7/8  ->  REMAINING 1/8 = (1/2)^3  ->  n = 3
  n T = 15  ->  T = 5 days
```
> **TRAP:** the question says *decayed*, not *remaining*. Always convert to
> "fraction remaining" first.

---

### Q21
The activity of a sample falls from 6000 to 375 counts per minute in 40 minutes.
The half life is
(a) 5 min  (b) 10 min  (c) 20 min  (d) 8 min

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  375 / 6000 = 1/16 = (1/2)^4   ->  n = 4
  T = 40 / 4 = 10 minutes
```

---

### Q22
The half life of a radioactive substance is 20 minutes. The time between 33 %
decay and 67 % decay is
(a) 20 min  (b) 40 min  (c) 13 min  (d) 10 min

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  33 % decayed  ->  67 % remaining
  67 % decayed  ->  33 % remaining

  The amount remaining has HALVED (67 % -> 33.5 %), so exactly ONE
  half life has passed = 20 minutes.
```

---

### Q23
Two radioactive samples X and Y have half lives 20 min and 40 min and initially
contain equal numbers of nuclei. After 80 minutes, N_X : N_Y is
(a) 1 : 4  (b) 4 : 1  (c) 1 : 2  (d) 1 : 16

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  X:  n = 80/20 = 4  ->  N0/16
  Y:  n = 80/40 = 2  ->  N0/4
  Ratio = (1/16) : (1/4) = 1 : 4
```

# PATTERN 6 — DECAY CONSTANT, HALF LIFE, MEAN LIFE

### Q24
The relation between half life T and mean life tau is
(a) tau = 0.693 T  (b) T = 1.44 tau  (c) tau = 1.44 T  (d) tau = T

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  tau = 1/lambda ,  T = 0.693/lambda   ->   tau = T/0.693 = 1.44 T
  MEAN LIFE IS ALWAYS LONGER THAN HALF LIFE.
```

---

### Q25
The half life of a radioactive nucleus is 50 days. Its decay constant is
(a) 0.0139 per day  (b) 0.693 per day  (c) 34.65 per day  (d) 72 per day

**ANSWER: (a)**  `lambda = 0.693 / 50 = 0.01386 per day`

---

### Q26
After one mean life, the fraction of nuclei that has DECAYED is
(a) 1/e  (b) 1 - 1/e  (c) 1/2  (d) e

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  At t = tau = 1/lambda :   N = N0 e^-1 = 0.368 N0  (remaining)
  Decayed = 1 - 0.368 = 0.632 = 1 - 1/e = 63.2 %
```
> **SHORTCUT:** remember the pair — after one MEAN life 36.8 % remains,
> after one HALF life 50 % remains.

---

### Q27
A nucleus can decay by two routes with decay constants lambda1 and lambda2. Its
effective half life is
(a) 0.693/(lambda1 + lambda2)  (b) 0.693/(lambda1 lambda2)
(c) 0.693 (lambda1 + lambda2)  (d) 1.44/(lambda1 + lambda2)

**ANSWER: (a)** Parallel decay routes ADD their decay constants:
`lambda = lambda1 + lambda2`, so `T = 0.693 / (lambda1 + lambda2)`.

---

### Q28
A sample falls to 20 % of its original amount in 100 s. The decay constant is
(a) 1.61 x 10^-2 s^-1  (b) 6.93 x 10^-3 s^-1
(c) 2.0 x 10^-2 s^-1   (d) 4.6 x 10^-3 s^-1

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  0.2 = e^(-100 lambda)
  ln 0.2 = -1.609 = -100 lambda
  lambda = 0.01609 = 1.61 x 10^-2 s^-1
```

1 curie is equal to
(a) 3.7 x 10^7 Bq  (b) 3.7 x 10^10 Bq  (c) 10^6 Bq  (d) 6.02 x 10^23 Bq

**ANSWER: (b)**  `1 Ci = 3.7 x 10^10 disintegrations per second`

### Q30
The SI unit of radioactivity is
(a) curie  (b) rutherford  (c) becquerel  (d) roentgen

**ANSWER: (c)** 1 becquerel = 1 disintegration per second.

---

### Q31
A sample contains 10^16 nuclei of half life 10 days. Its activity is nearly
(a) 8.0 x 10^9 Bq  (b) 8.0 x 10^6 Bq  (c) 6.9 x 10^14 Bq  (d) 1.16 x 10^11 Bq

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  T = 10 x 86400 = 8.64 x 10^5 s
  lambda = 0.693 / 8.64 x 10^5 = 8.02 x 10^-7 s^-1
  R = lambda N = 8.02 x 10^-7 x 10^16 = 8.02 x 10^9 Bq
```
> **TRAP:** convert the half life into SECONDS before you compute lambda,
> otherwise the activity will not be in becquerel.

---

### Q32
The activity of 1 g of radium-226 (half life 1600 y) is about
(a) 1 Bq  (b) 1 curie  (c) 1 rutherford  (d) 10^20 Bq

**ANSWER: (b)** It is 3.7 x 10^10 Bq = 1 curie. This is precisely how the
curie was defined.

# PATTERN 8 — DISPLACEMENT LAWS AND DECAY CHAINS

### Q33
When a nucleus emits one alpha particle and two beta-minus particles, the
resulting nucleus is
(a) an isotope of the original  (b) an isobar of the original
(c) an isotone of the original  (d) a completely different element

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  alpha:   Z - 2 , A - 4
  2 beta:  Z + 2 , A unchanged
  NET:     Z unchanged , A reduced by 4    ->  SAME element, different A
           ->  an ISOTOPE
```

---

### Q34
In the decay chain 92U^238 --> 82Pb^206, the numbers of alpha and beta particles
emitted are
(a) 8 and 6  (b) 6 and 8  (c) 8 and 8  (d) 6 and 6

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  alphas  a = (238 - 206)/4 = 32/4 = 8
  betas   b = 2a - (Z1 - Z2) = 16 - (92 - 82) = 16 - 10 = 6
```

---

### Q35
90Th^232 decays to 82Pb^208. The numbers of alpha and beta particles emitted are
(a) 6 and 4  (b) 4 and 6  (c) 6 and 6  (d) 8 and 4

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  a = (232 - 208)/4 = 24/4 = 6
  b = 2(6) - (90 - 82) = 12 - 8 = 4
```

---

### Q36
In beta-minus decay, the electron that is emitted comes from
(a) the K shell  (b) the outermost orbit
(c) the nucleus, when a neutron converts to a proton
(d) the surrounding air

**ANSWER: (c)** `0n^1 --> 1p^1 + -1e^0 + antineutrino`. The electron is
**created inside the nucleus** at the instant of decay.

---

### Q37
In gamma decay, which of the following changes?
(a) Z only  (b) A only  (c) both Z and A  (d) neither Z nor A

**ANSWER: (d)** Only the energy state of the nucleus changes.

---

### Q38
92U^238 emits an alpha particle. The daughter nucleus is
(a) 90Th^234  (b) 92U^234  (c) 93Np^238  (d) 88Ra^234

**ANSWER: (a)**  `Z: 92 - 2 = 90 ; A: 238 - 4 = 234`

# PATTERN 9 — PROPERTIES OF ALPHA, BETA, GAMMA AND NUCLEAR FORCES

### Q39
Which radiation has the greatest penetrating power and the least ionising power?

**ANSWER: (c)** Gamma. Penetrating power and ionising power are always

### Q40
Which of the following is NOT deflected by a magnetic field?
(a) alpha  (b) beta  (c) gamma  (d) protons

### Q41
Nuclear forces are
(a) charge dependent and long range
(b) charge independent and short range
(c) charge independent and long range
(d) weaker than gravitational forces

**ANSWER: (b)** They are the same for p-p, n-n and p-n, and act only up to

### Q42
The nuclear force between two nucleons becomes REPULSIVE when their separation
is less than about
(a) 0.8 fm  (b) 2 fm  (c) 10 fm  (d) 100 fm

**ANSWER: (a)** Below r0 = 0.8 fm there is a repulsive hard core that
prevents the nucleus from collapsing.

---

### Q43
Radioactivity is NOT affected by
(a) temperature  (b) pressure  (c) chemical combination  (d) all of these

**ANSWER: (d)** It is a purely nuclear process and is independent of all

# PATTERN 10 — FISSION, REACTORS AND FUSION

### Q44
In a nuclear reactor, the function of the moderator is to
(a) absorb neutrons  (b) slow down fast neutrons
(c) cool the core  (d) shield the workers

**ANSWER: (b)** Slowing neutrons is the moderator's job; **absorbing** them
is the **control rod's** job.
> **SHORTCUT:** Moderator = SLOW (heavy water, graphite).
> Control rods = SWALLOW (cadmium, boron). Never mix the two.

---

### Q45
Cadmium rods are used in a nuclear reactor to
(a) slow the neutrons  (b) absorb the surplus neutrons
(c) transfer heat  (d) increase the reaction rate

**ANSWER: (b)**

---

### Q46
For a controlled chain reaction in a nuclear reactor, the neutron multiplication
factor k must be
(a) less than 1  (b) exactly 1  (c) greater than 1  (d) zero

**ANSWER: (b)** k = 1 gives a steady reaction; k > 1 gives a bomb.

---

### Q47
The energy released in the fission of one U-235 nucleus is about
(a) 2 MeV  (b) 20 MeV  (c) 200 MeV  (d) 2000 MeV

**ANSWER: (c)** About 200 MeV, i.e. 3.2 x 10^-11 J.

---

### Q48
The energy released by the complete fission of 1 g of U-235 (200 MeV per
fission) is nearly
(a) 8.2 x 10^10 J  (b) 8.2 x 10^13 J  (c) 5.1 x 10^23 J  (d) 2.6 x 10^21 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  N = (1/235) x 6.022 x 10^23 = 2.56 x 10^21 nuclei
  E = 2.56 x 10^21 x 200 x 1.6 x 10^-13 J
    = 8.2 x 10^10 J
```

---

### Q49
A nuclear reactor delivers 1000 MW of power. The number of fissions per second
(200 MeV each) is
(a) 3.1 x 10^19  (b) 3.1 x 10^16  (c) 5 x 10^21  (d) 6 x 10^23

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  Energy per fission = 200 x 1.6 x 10^-13 = 3.2 x 10^-11 J
  Fissions per second = 10^9 / 3.2 x 10^-11 = 3.125 x 10^19
```

---

### Q50
Nuclear fusion requires a very high temperature because
(a) the nuclei must be melted
(b) the nuclei must overcome the Coulomb repulsion between them
(c) the neutrons must be slowed
(d) the electrons must be removed

**ANSWER: (b)** The Coulomb barrier (about 400 keV) must be crossed so that
the nuclei come within range of the attractive nuclear force.

### Q51
The source of the Sun's energy is

**ANSWER: (c)** Mainly through the proton-proton cycle,
`4 (1H^1) --> 2He^4 + 26.7 MeV`.

### Q52
In the carbon-nitrogen cycle, carbon acts as
(a) fuel  (b) product  (c) catalyst  (d) moderator

**ANSWER: (c)** 6C^12 enters at the first step and reappears at the last
step unchanged.

### Q53
In the reaction `1H^2 + 1H^2 --> 2He^3 + 0n^1`, given
m(1H^2) = 2.014102 u, m(2He^3) = 3.016029 u, m(0n^1) = 1.008665 u,
the energy released is
(a) 3.27 MeV  (b) 17.6 MeV  (c) 0.42 MeV  (d) 200 MeV

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  Before = 2 x 2.014102 = 4.028204 u
  After  = 3.016029 + 1.008665 = 4.024694 u
  dm     = 0.003510 u
  Q      = 0.003510 x 931.5 = 3.27 MeV
```

### Q54
Compared with fission, fusion releases
(a) more energy per event but less per nucleon
(b) less energy per event but more per nucleon

**ANSWER: (b)** Fission gives 200 MeV per event but only 0.85 MeV per
nucleon; fusion gives 27 MeV per event but 6.7 MeV per nucleon.
