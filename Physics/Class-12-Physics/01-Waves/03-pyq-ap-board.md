## Topic: Nature and types of waves

### Q A1
What is a wave? Does a wave transport matter along with it?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A WAVE is a disturbance that travels through a medium and carries
  ENERGY and MOMENTUM from one point to another.

  NO — a wave does NOT transport matter. The particles of the medium
  only vibrate about their own mean positions.
```

---

### Q A2
Distinguish between a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave with one example
each.

**ANSWER:**
```
  TRANSVERSE   -  particles vibrate PERPENDICULAR to the direction
                  of propagation.
                  Example: a wave on a stretched string, light.

  LONGITUDINAL -  particles vibrate PARALLEL to the direction of
                  propagation.
                  Example: sound waves in air.
```

---

### Q A3
Why can transverse mechanical waves not travel through gases and liquids?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A transverse wave needs the medium to resist SHEARING, that is, the
  medium must possess a MODULUS OF RIGIDITY.

  Gases and liquids have NO modulus of rigidity, so there is no restoring
  force for a sideways displacement. Hence no transverse wave.
```

---

### Q A4
Sound cannot travel through vacuum but light can. Why?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Sound is a MECHANICAL wave. It needs a material medium whose particles
  can be compressed and rarefied.

  Light is an ELECTROMAGNETIC wave made of oscillating electric and
  magnetic fields, which need no medium. So light travels through vacuum.
```

---

### Q A5
Why is sound faster in solids than in gases?

**ANSWER:**
```
  v  =  sqrt( ELASTICITY / DENSITY )

  Going from a gas to a solid the ELASTICITY (modulus) increases enormously
  — far more than the density increases. So the ratio E / rho is much larger
  for a solid, and the speed is much greater.

  Steel 5100 m/s   >   Water 1490 m/s   >   Air 332 m/s
```

## Topic: Wave characteristics and v = f lambda

### Q A6
Define wavelength and amplitude of a wave.

**ANSWER:**
```
  WAVELENGTH (lambda) - the distance between two consecutive particles
                        of the medium that are in the SAME PHASE.
                        (= distance between two successive crests.)
                        Unit: metre.

  AMPLITUDE (A)       - the maximum displacement of a particle from its
                        mean position. Unit: metre.
```

---

### Q A7
A sound wave has a frequency of 400 Hz and a wavelength of 0.85 m. Find its speed.

**ANSWER:**
```
  v  =  f x lambda  =  400 x 0.85  =  340 m/s
```

---

### Q A8
A sound wave travels from air into water. What happens to its frequency,
wavelength and speed?

**ANSWER:**
```
  FREQUENCY   -  UNCHANGED  (it is fixed by the source)
  SPEED       -  INCREASES  (332 -> about 1490 m/s)
  WAVELENGTH  -  INCREASES in the same ratio, since lambda = v / f
```

---

### Q A9
Distinguish between wave velocity and particle velocity.

**ANSWER:**
```
  WAVE VELOCITY     -  the speed at which the disturbance travels.
                       It is CONSTANT for a given medium.

  PARTICLE VELOCITY -  the speed of one vibrating particle (dy/dt).
                       It KEEPS CHANGING — zero at the extreme positions
                       and maximum (= A omega) at the mean position.
```

## Topic: Speed of sound

### Q A12
State Newton's formula for the speed of sound in a gas. What value does it give?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Newton assumed the process is ISOTHERMAL, so the bulk modulus B = P.

           v  =  sqrt( P / rho )

  At NTP:  v  =  sqrt( 1.013 x 10^5 / 1.293 )  =  280 m/s

  This is about 16% LOWER than the experimental value of 332 m/s.
```

---

### Q A13
What is Laplace's correction?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Laplace showed the process is ADIABATIC, not isothermal, because air is a
  POOR CONDUCTOR OF HEAT and the compressions and rarefactions occur too
  rapidly for heat to be exchanged.

  For an adiabatic change,  B = gamma P , so

           v  =  sqrt( gamma P / rho )  =  332 m/s

  which agrees with experiment.
```

---

### Q A14
How does pressure affect the speed of sound in a gas?

**ANSWER:**
```
  It has NO EFFECT (at constant temperature).

  From  P V = m R' T  and  rho = m/V , the ratio  P / rho  is a constant at
  a fixed temperature. If P doubles, rho also doubles. Since
  v = sqrt(gamma P / rho), the speed is unchanged.
```

---

### Q A15
The speed of sound in air at 0 deg C is 330 m/s. What is it at 30 deg C?

**ANSWER:**
```
  v(t)  =  v(0)  +  0.61 t

        =  330  +  0.61 x 30   =  330 + 18.3   =  348.3 m/s
```

---

### Q A16
How does humidity affect the speed of sound? Give the reason.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Humidity INCREASES the speed of sound.

  Water vapour (molar mass 18) is LIGHTER than the air it replaces
  (molar mass about 29), so moist air is LESS DENSE than dry air.

  Since v ~ 1 / sqrt(rho), a smaller density gives a greater speed.
```

## Topic: Superposition, interference, stationary waves

### Q A17
State the principle of superposition of waves.

**ANSWER:**
```
  When two or more waves travel simultaneously through a medium, the
  resultant displacement of any particle is the VECTOR SUM of the
  displacements produced by the individual waves.

            y  =  y1  +  y2  +  y3  + ...
```

---

### Q A18
What is a node and an antinode? What is the distance between two consecutive
nodes?

**ANSWER:**
```
  NODE      -  a point in a stationary wave that is always AT REST
               (amplitude = zero).

  ANTINODE  -  a point vibrating with MAXIMUM amplitude (= 2A).

  Distance between two consecutive nodes  =  lambda / 2
```

---

### Q A19
In a stationary wave the distance between a node and the next antinode is 5 cm.
Find the wavelength.

**ANSWER:**
```
  node to next antinode  =  lambda / 4

  lambda / 4  =  5 cm      ->      lambda  =  20 cm  =  0.2 m
```

---

### Q A20
What happens to the phase of a wave when it is reflected at a rigid boundary?

**ANSWER:**
```
  It undergoes a PHASE CHANGE OF pi RADIANS (180 degrees), equivalent to a
  path difference of lambda/2. The reflected pulse is INVERTED — a crest
  returns as a trough. A NODE always forms at a rigid boundary.

  At a FREE boundary there is NO phase change and an ANTINODE forms.
```

## Topic: Strings and pipes

### Q A21
Why are only odd harmonics present in a closed organ pipe?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A DISPLACEMENT NODE must form at the closed end and an ANTINODE at the
  open end. The distance from a node to an antinode is always an ODD
  multiple of lambda/4.

  So L = lambda/4, 3 lambda/4, 5 lambda/4, ... giving frequencies in the
  ratio 1 : 3 : 5 : ...  — only ODD harmonics.
```

### Q A23
What is end correction? Give its value.

**ANSWER:**
```
  The antinode at an open end of a pipe forms slightly OUTSIDE the mouth,
  because the air just outside also vibrates. The small extra length that
  must be added to the pipe length is the END CORRECTION.

           e  =  0.6 r        (r = radius of the pipe)

  Closed pipe: add 0.6r once.   Open pipe: add 1.2r (both ends).
```

What is a sonometer used for?

**ANSWER:**

```
  A sonometer is used to study the transverse vibrations of a stretched

```

## Topic: Beats and Doppler effect

### Q A25
What are beats? Define beat frequency.

**ANSWER:**
```
  BEATS - the periodic rise and fall in the intensity of sound produced when
  two waves of SLIGHTLY DIFFERENT frequencies travelling in the SAME
  direction superpose.

  BEAT FREQUENCY - the number of beats heard per second.

              n  =  | f1  -  f2 |
```

---

### Q A26
Two tuning forks of frequencies 512 Hz and 516 Hz are sounded together. How many
beats are heard per second?

**ANSWER:**
```
  n  =  | 516  -  512 |  =  4 beats per second

  Time between two beats  =  1 / 4  =  0.25 s
```

---

### Q A27
Why are beats not heard when the frequency difference is large?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Because of the PERSISTENCE OF HEARING — the sensation of a sound remains
  in the ear for about 1/10 second.

  If |f1 - f2| is more than about 10 Hz the maxima come faster than the ear
  can separate them, so no throbbing is heard — just two separate notes.
```

---

### Q A28
What is the Doppler effect?

**ANSWER:**
```
  The APPARENT change in the frequency (pitch) of a wave observed when
  there is RELATIVE MOTION between the source and the observer.

  Approaching -> apparent frequency INCREASES
  Receding    -> apparent frequency DECREASES

  The actual frequency of the source never changes.
```

---

### Q A29
Why is there no Doppler effect when the source and the observer move
perpendicular to the line joining them?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Only the COMPONENT of velocity ALONG the line joining source and observer
  produces a Doppler shift.

  For perpendicular motion that component is  v cos(90 deg) = 0 , so the
  separation is momentarily not changing and

                        f'  =  f       (no shift)
```

---

### Q A30
Give two applications of the Doppler effect.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  RADAR speed guns — measuring the speed of a vehicle or an aircraft.
  2.  The RED SHIFT of light from distant galaxies, showing that the
      universe is expanding.
  (Also: SONAR, Doppler ultrasound for blood flow.)
```

### Q B1
Explain Newton's formula for the speed of sound in air. What is Laplace's
correction?

**ANSWER:**
```
  NEWTON'S FORMULA
  ----------------
  Newton assumed that when sound passes through air the compressions and
  rarefactions occur SLOWLY enough for the heat to escape, so the process
  is ISOTHERMAL and the temperature stays constant.

  For an isothermal change:      P V  =  constant
  Differentiating:               P dV  +  V dP  =  0

                                       dP
                        ->    P  =  - --------  =  B (isothermal)
                                      dV / V

  So B = P , and                v  =  sqrt( P / rho )

  At NTP,  P = 1.013 x 10^5 N/m^2 and rho = 1.293 kg/m^3 :

           v  =  sqrt( 1.013 x 10^5 / 1.293 )  =  280 m/s

  But the experimental value is 332 m/s — an error of about 16%,
  far too large to be an experimental error.

  LAPLACE'S CORRECTION
  --------------------
  Laplace pointed out that sound travels very fast and AIR IS A POOR
  CONDUCTOR OF HEAT. The heat produced in a compression has NO TIME to
  leak away. Hence the process is ADIABATIC, not isothermal.

  For an adiabatic change:       P V^gamma  =  constant

                        ->    gamma P  =  B (adiabatic)

  So                            v  =  sqrt( gamma P / rho )

  With gamma = 1.41 for air:

           v  =  sqrt( 1.41 x 1.013 x 10^5 / 1.293 )  =  332 m/s

  This agrees exactly with the experimental value.
```

### Q B2
Explain the factors that affect the speed of sound in a gas.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Start from Laplace's formula   v = sqrt( gamma P / rho ).

  1. DENSITY
     v ~ 1 / sqrt(rho).  A denser gas carries sound MORE SLOWLY.
     Sound travels about 4 times faster in hydrogen than in oxygen,
     because oxygen is 16 times denser.

  2. PRESSURE — NO EFFECT
     At constant temperature P / rho is a constant (from P V = m R' T).
     If P doubles, rho doubles too, so v is unchanged.

  3. TEMPERATURE
     Using P/rho = R T / M ,   v = sqrt( gamma R T / M )   ->   v ~ sqrt(T)
     with T in KELVIN. Sound travels FASTER on a hot day.
     Working rule:   v(t) = v(0) + 0.61 t   (metres per second per deg C)

  4. HUMIDITY
     Water vapour (M = 18) is lighter than dry air (M = 29), so moist air
     is LESS DENSE. Since v ~ 1/sqrt(rho), sound travels FASTER in
     humid air.

  5. WIND
     The wind velocity adds vectorially:  v(eff) = v + w cos(theta).

  NO EFFECT: amplitude, frequency, wavelength, phase, loudness, pitch.
```

### Q B5
Explain the reflection of a wave at a rigid boundary and at a free boundary.

**ANSWER:**
```
  AT A RIGID (FIXED) BOUNDARY
  ---------------------------
  The boundary cannot move, so the displacement there must always be zero.
  To make this possible the reflected wave must be INVERTED.

    - crest returns as a TROUGH
    - PHASE CHANGE of pi radians (180 deg), i.e. a path change of lambda/2
    - a NODE always forms at the rigid end
    - for sound, a compression returns as a COMPRESSION

     incident  /\                          |
    __________/  \__________               | wall
                                           |
     reflected ______________               |
                        \  /                |
                         \/   (inverted)    |


  AT A FREE BOUNDARY
  ------------------
  The end is free to move as much as it likes, so no inversion is needed.

    - crest returns as a CREST
    - PHASE CHANGE = ZERO
    - an ANTINODE always forms at the free end
    - for sound, a compression returns as a RAREFACTION

     incident  /\
    __________/  \__________ o (free ring)

     reflected      /\
    ______________/  \______ o   (upright)
```

### Q B7
What is interference of sound? State the conditions for constructive and
destructive interference.

**ANSWER:**
```
  INTERFERENCE is the redistribution of sound energy that occurs when two
  waves of the SAME FREQUENCY and a CONSTANT PHASE DIFFERENCE superpose,
  producing permanent regions of increased and decreased loudness.

  CONSTRUCTIVE (LOUD)
      phase difference   =  2 n pi           (0, 2pi, 4pi, ...)
      path  difference   =  n lambda         (n = 0, 1, 2, ...)
      resultant amplitude=  A1 + A2
      resultant intensity=  ( sqrt I1 + sqrt I2 )^2      MAXIMUM

  DESTRUCTIVE (QUIET)
      phase difference   =  ( 2n - 1 ) pi    (pi, 3pi, 5pi, ...)
      path  difference   =  ( 2n - 1 ) lambda / 2
      resultant amplitude=  | A1 - A2 |
      resultant intensity=  ( sqrt I1 - sqrt I2 )^2      MINIMUM

  If A1 = A2 the minima are points of COMPLETE SILENCE.

  CONDITIONS FOR A GOOD PATTERN
      1. the sources must be COHERENT (constant phase difference)
      2. they must have the SAME frequency
      3. their amplitudes should be equal or nearly equal
      4. they should be close together

  ENERGY IS NOT DESTROYED — it is only redistributed from the minima to
  the maxima. The total energy is conserved.
```

### Q B8
Derive the equation of a stationary wave and find the positions of the nodes and
antinodes.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Two identical waves travelling in opposite directions:

        y1  =  A sin( omega t  -  k x )
        y2  =  A sin( omega t  +  k x )

  By superposition:

        y   =  y1 + y2
            =  A [ sin(omega t - kx)  +  sin(omega t + kx) ]

  Using  sin C + sin D = 2 sin((C+D)/2) cos((C-D)/2) :

        +-----------------------------------------------+
        |    y  =  2 A cos( k x )  x  sin( omega t )    |
        +-----------------------------------------------+

  x and t have SEPARATED, so nothing travels — it is a stationary wave.
  The amplitude of the particle at x is   2 A cos(k x).

  NODES  (amplitude = 0)  ->  cos(kx) = 0

        k x  =  pi/2 , 3pi/2 , 5pi/2 , ...

        x    =  lambda/4 , 3 lambda/4 , 5 lambda/4 , ...
             =  ODD multiples of lambda / 4

  ANTINODES (amplitude = 2A)  ->  cos(kx) = +/- 1

        k x  =  0 , pi , 2pi , ...

        x    =  0 , lambda/2 , lambda , ...
             =  integer multiples of lambda / 2

  Distance between consecutive nodes      =  lambda / 2
  Distance between consecutive antinodes  =  lambda / 2
  Distance from a node to the next antinode = lambda / 4
```

### Q B10
A train sounds a whistle of frequency 640 Hz and moves at 20 m/s. The speed of
sound in air is 340 m/s. Find the frequency heard by a stationary observer
(i) as the train approaches (ii) after it has passed.

**ANSWER:**
```
  General formula:      f'  =  f ( v + vo ) / ( v - vs ) ,   vo = 0

  (i) APPROACHING — source moving TOWARDS, so the bottom takes a MINUS:

                     v                    340              340
       f'  =  f  ----------  =  640 x  ----------  = 640 x -----
                  v  -  vs              340 - 20            320

           =  680 Hz          (HIGHER, as expected)

  (ii) RECEDING — the bottom takes a PLUS:

                     v                    340              340
       f'  =  f  ----------  =  640 x  ----------  = 640 x -----
                  v  +  vs              340 + 20            360

           =  604.4 Hz        (LOWER, as expected)

  The pitch drops by  680 - 604.4  =  75.6 Hz as the train passes.
```

### Q B13
Two loudspeakers 3 m apart emit sound of wavelength 1.5 m in phase. A listener
stands 4 m from one speaker and 5.25 m from the other. Is the sound loud or quiet?

**ANSWER:**
```
  Path difference  =  5.25  -  4  =  1.25 m

                          1.25
  In wavelengths:      ----------  =  0.8333 lambda
                          1.5

  For a MAXIMUM we need a whole number of wavelengths (0, 1, 2 ...).
  For a MINIMUM we need 0.5, 1.5, 2.5 ... wavelengths.

  0.833 is NEITHER, so the point is between a maximum and a minimum.

  Phase difference  =  (2 pi / lambda) x path difference
                    =  (2 pi / 1.5) x 1.25  =  1.667 pi  =  300 degrees

  Resultant amplitude  =  sqrt( A^2 + A^2 + 2A^2 cos 300 )
                       =  A sqrt( 2 + 2(0.5) )  =  A sqrt(3)  =  1.73 A

  So the sound is LOUDER than one speaker alone but not at a maximum —
  it is a point of intermediate intensity.
```

### Q B14
Describe how a resonance tube (air column) experiment is used to find the speed
of sound in air. Show that the end correction cancels out.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A tube partly filled with water behaves as a CLOSED pipe whose length can
  be varied by changing the water level. A tuning fork of known frequency f
  is held over the mouth and the level is lowered until resonance (loudest
  sound) is heard.

  FIRST resonance at length l1 :      l1  +  e   =   lambda / 4
  SECOND resonance at length l2 :     l2  +  e   =   3 lambda / 4

  Subtract the first from the second:

           l2  -  l1   =   3 lambda/4  -  lambda/4   =   lambda / 2

           ->     lambda  =  2 ( l2  -  l1 )

           ->     v  =  f lambda  =  2 f ( l2  -  l1 )

  The end correction e has CANCELLED, which is exactly why two resonances
  are measured instead of one.

  If e is wanted:     e  =  ( l2  -  3 l1 ) / 2
```

### Q C1  *** MOST REPEATED LAQ FROM THIS CHAPTER ***
Explain the formation of stationary waves in a stretched string and hence deduce
the laws of transverse waves in stretched strings.

**ANSWER — MARK SPLIT: formation 2 + modes/diagram 3 + three laws 3**

```
  PART 1 — FORMATION OF STATIONARY WAVES              [2 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  A string is fixed rigidly at both ends and stretched with tension T.
  When it is plucked, a transverse wave travels along it, reflects from
  the fixed ends (with a phase change of pi) and travels back.

  The reflected wave superposes on the incident wave. Two identical waves
  travelling in opposite directions give:

        y1  =  A sin(omega t - k x)
        y2  =  A sin(omega t + k x)

        y   =  y1 + y2  =  2 A cos(k x) sin(omega t)

  x and t are separated, so nothing travels — a STATIONARY WAVE is formed.
  Points of zero amplitude are NODES; points of maximum amplitude 2A are
  ANTINODES. Consecutive nodes are lambda/2 apart.

  PART 2 — MODES OF VIBRATION                          [3 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Both ends are FIXED, so a NODE must form at each end. Therefore the
  length L must contain a whole number of half-wavelengths:

                       lambda(n)                     2 L
            L   =  n x -----------      ->  lambda = -----
                            2                          n

  Since   v = f lambda   and   v = sqrt( T / mu ) :

        +---------------------------------------------------+
        |             n         +-      -+                  |
        |   f(n)  =  ------  x  |  sqrt  |  T / mu          |
        |             2 L       +-      -+                  |
        |                                                   |
        |             n = 1, 2, 3, ...                      |
        +---------------------------------------------------+

  n = 1  FUNDAMENTAL (1st harmonic)      1 loop     f1
      N                          N
      |         ..........       |                 L = lambda/2
      +--------------------------+                 lambda = 2L
      |         ..........       |                       1
      N             A            N            f1 = ----- sqrt(T/mu)
                                                    2 L

  n = 2  FIRST OVERTONE (2nd harmonic)   2 loops    f2 = 2 f1
      N            N            N
      |    ...     |     ...    |                  L = lambda
      +------------+------------+                  lambda = L
      |    ...     |     ...    |
      N     A      N     A      N

  n = 3  SECOND OVERTONE (3rd harmonic)  3 loops    f3 = 3 f1
      N       N        N        N
      |  ...  |        |  ...   |                  L = 3 lambda/2
      +-------+--------+--------+                  lambda = 2L/3
      |       |  ...   |        |
      N   A   N    A   N    A   N

  ALL harmonics are present:    f1 : f2 : f3 : ...  =  1 : 2 : 3 : ...

  PART 3 — THE LAWS OF TRANSVERSE VIBRATIONS         [3 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Taking the fundamental,   f  =  (1 / 2L) sqrt( T / mu ).

  LAW OF LENGTH
     When T and mu are constant, the fundamental frequency is INVERSELY
     PROPORTIONAL to the vibrating length.
                f  ~  1 / L            f L  =  constant

  LAW OF TENSION
     When L and mu are constant, the fundamental frequency is DIRECTLY
     PROPORTIONAL to the SQUARE ROOT of the tension.
                f  ~  sqrt( T )        f / sqrt(T)  =  constant

  LAW OF MASS
     When L and T are constant, the fundamental frequency is INVERSELY
     PROPORTIONAL to the SQUARE ROOT of the linear density.
                f  ~  1 / sqrt( mu )   f sqrt(mu)  =  constant

  All three laws are verified experimentally with a SONOMETER, using a
  paper rider that is thrown off the wire at resonance.
```

### Q C2  *** MOST REPEATED LAQ ***
What are beats? Obtain an expression for the beat frequency. Where and how are
beats made use of?

**ANSWER — MARK SPLIT: definition 1 + derivation 4 + applications 3**

```
  PART 1 — WHAT ARE BEATS                              [1 mark]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  When two sound waves of SLIGHTLY DIFFERENT frequencies travelling in the
  SAME DIRECTION superpose, the resultant intensity at a point rises and
  falls periodically. This periodic waxing and waning of loudness is called
  BEATS. One beat = one maximum plus one minimum of loudness.

     LOUD       SOFT        LOUD       SOFT        LOUD
      ####        #          ####        #          ####
     ######      ##         ######      ##         ######
    ########    ###        ########    ###        ########
   ------------------------------------------------------------
    ########    ###        ########    ###        ########
     ######      ##         ######      ##         ######
      ####        #          ####        #          ####

      |<---------- one beat ---------->|

  PART 2 — EXPRESSION FOR THE BEAT FREQUENCY           [4 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Let two waves of equal amplitude A and frequencies f1 and f2 arrive at a
  point:

        y1  =  A sin( 2 pi f1 t )
        y2  =  A sin( 2 pi f2 t )

  By the principle of superposition:

        y  =  y1 + y2  =  A [ sin(2 pi f1 t) + sin(2 pi f2 t) ]

  Using  sin C + sin D  =  2 sin( (C+D)/2 ) cos( (C-D)/2 ) :

                     +-  2 pi (f1 - f2) t -+       +- 2 pi (f1 + f2) t -+
        y  =  2 A cos| ------------------- | x sin | ------------------ |
                     +-          2        -+       +-         2       -+

  This is a wave of frequency  ( f1 + f2 ) / 2  whose AMPLITUDE is

                        +-  2 pi (f1 - f2) t  -+
        R  =  2 A  cos  | ------------------- |
                        +-         2         -+

  INTENSITY IS MAXIMUM when  R = +/- 2A , i.e. when the cosine is +/- 1 :

        2 pi ( f1 - f2 ) t / 2   =   0 , pi , 2 pi , ...

                                      1        2
                      t   =   0 ,  ------- , ------- , ...
                                   f1 - f2   f1 - f2

  So successive maxima are separated by a time  1 / (f1 - f2) second, and
  the number of maxima per second is

        +--------------------------------------------+
        |    BEAT FREQUENCY   n   =   | f1 - f2 |    |
        +--------------------------------------------+

  Minima occur exactly halfway between the maxima, so the number of minima
  per second is also | f1 - f2 |.

  NOTE: beats are heard distinctly only if | f1 - f2 | is less than about
  10 Hz, because of the PERSISTENCE OF HEARING (1/10 second).

  PART 3 — USES OF BEATS                               [3 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  1. TUNING MUSICAL INSTRUMENTS
     A string is sounded with a standard tuning fork and its tension is
     adjusted until the beats vanish. Zero beats means the frequencies are
     exactly equal. This is the most sensitive method of tuning known.

  2. DETERMINING AN UNKNOWN FREQUENCY
     Sound the unknown fork with a known fork of frequency f and count the
     beats n. The unknown is either (f + n) or (f - n). Load the unknown
     fork with wax (which LOWERS its frequency) and count again:
        beats INCREASE  ->  the unknown was  (f - n)
        beats DECREASE  ->  the unknown was  (f + n)

  3. DETECTING A DANGEROUS GAS IN MINES
     Two identical whistles are blown, one with air from outside the mine
     and one with air from inside. If methane (marsh gas) is present the
     density of the air changes, the frequency of that whistle changes,
     and BEATS ARE HEARD. This warns the miners.

  4. IN RADIO RECEIVERS
     The superheterodyne receiver mixes the incoming signal with a local
     oscillator; the beat (intermediate) frequency is then amplified.

  5. Pilots synchronise twin propeller engines by adjusting them until the
     beats disappear.
```

### Q C3  *** MOST REPEATED LAQ ***
What is the Doppler effect? Obtain an expression for the apparent frequency of
sound heard when the source is in motion with respect to an observer at rest.
Discuss all the cases.

**ANSWER — MARK SPLIT: definition 1 + derivation 4 + other cases 2 + applications 1**

```
  PART 1 — DEFINITION                                  [1 mark]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  The DOPPLER EFFECT is the apparent change in the frequency of a wave
  observed whenever there is RELATIVE MOTION between the source of sound
  and the observer.

  When they APPROACH  ->  the apparent frequency INCREASES (pitch rises)
  When they RECEDE    ->  the apparent frequency DECREASES (pitch falls)

  The frequency actually emitted by the source never changes, and the
  speed of sound in the medium never changes.

  PART 2 — SOURCE MOVING, OBSERVER AT REST             [4 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Let    v  = speed of sound in air
         f  = true frequency of the source
         T  = 1/f = time period
         vs = speed of the source

  (a) SOURCE MOVING TOWARDS A STATIONARY OBSERVER

        S ---> vs                              O (at rest)
        ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~
        waves crowded in front of the source

     In one time period T the wave front moves a distance v T = lambda,
     but the source itself has moved forward a distance vs T.
     So the wave is squeezed into a shorter space:

        lambda'  =  v T  -  vs T   =  ( v - vs ) T

                                      v  -  vs
                            =      ------------
                                        f

     The observer receives these shortened waves at the normal speed v:

                    v               v f                +-      v     -+
        f'  =   ---------   =   -----------   =   f    |  ----------  |
                 lambda'          v  -  vs            +-   v  -  vs -+

     Since the denominator is SMALLER than v,   f'  >  f.
     THE PITCH RISES.

  (b) SOURCE MOVING AWAY FROM A STATIONARY OBSERVER

        O (at rest)                        vs <--- S
        ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~
        waves stretched out behind the source

        lambda'  =  ( v + vs ) T   =   ( v + vs ) / f

                    v                 +-      v     -+
        f'  =   ---------   =   f     |  ----------  |
                 lambda'             +-   v  +  vs -+

     Denominator is LARGER, so  f'  <  f.   THE PITCH FALLS.

  PART 3 — THE OTHER CASES                             [2 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  (c) OBSERVER MOVING TOWARDS A STATIONARY SOURCE
      Here the wavelength is NOT changed. Instead the observer runs into
      the waves, so their speed relative to him becomes ( v + vo ):

                 v  +  vo             +-   v + vo  -+
        f'  =  ------------   =   f   |  ---------- |        f' > f
                  lambda             +-      v     -+

  (d) OBSERVER MOVING AWAY FROM A STATIONARY SOURCE
      Relative speed = ( v - vo ):

                              +-   v - vo  -+
        f'  =           f     |  ---------- |                f' < f
                             +-      v     -+

  (e) BOTH SOURCE AND OBSERVER MOVING — the GENERAL formula:

        +---------------------------------------------------+
        |                      +-               -+          |
        |                      |   v   +   vo    |          |
        |         f'   =   f   |  -------------  |          |
        |                      |   v   -   vs    |          |
        |                      +-               -+          |
        +---------------------------------------------------+

     SIGN CONVENTION: take the direction from the SOURCE TOWARDS THE
     OBSERVER as positive. Velocities in that direction are positive,
     velocities against it are negative. In practice:

        observer moving TOWARDS  ->  + vo  in the numerator
        observer moving AWAY     ->  - vo  in the numerator
        source moving TOWARDS    ->  - vs  in the denominator
        source moving AWAY       ->  + vs  in the denominator

     Anything that brings them CLOSER makes f' BIGGER.

  IMPORTANT SPECIAL CASE
     If the source and the observer move PERPENDICULAR to the line joining
     them, the component of velocity along that line is v cos(90) = 0, so
     THERE IS NO DOPPLER EFFECT and  f' = f.

     The formula is valid only when vs and vo are LESS than v. If the
     source moves faster than sound, a shock wave (sonic boom) is produced
     instead.

  PART 4 — APPLICATIONS                                [1 mark]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
     - RADAR speed guns measure vehicle and aircraft speeds.
     - SONAR finds the speed of submarines and shoals of fish.
     - Doppler ultrasound measures blood flow and a foetal heartbeat.
     - The RED SHIFT of galaxy light proves the universe is expanding.
     - The rotation of the Sun is measured from the shift of its two edges.
```

### Q C4
Explain the formation of stationary waves in closed and open organ pipes.
Discuss the harmonics produced in each and explain end correction.

**ANSWER — MARK SPLIT: formation 2 + closed pipe 3 + open pipe 2 + end correction 1**

```
  PART 1 — FORMATION                                   [2 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  Sound sent into a pipe travels to the far end, reflects there, and the
  reflected wave superposes on the incident wave. Since the two waves have
  the same frequency and amplitude but travel in opposite directions, a
  STATIONARY WAVE is set up in the air column.

  The boundary conditions decide everything:

        CLOSED END  -  the air cannot move  ->  DISPLACEMENT NODE
        OPEN END    -  the air is free      ->  DISPLACEMENT ANTINODE

  PART 2 — CLOSED ORGAN PIPE                           [3 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
   FUNDAMENTAL          1st OVERTONE          2nd OVERTONE
   (1st harmonic)       (3rd harmonic)        (5th harmonic)

   +---------+ open     +---------+ open      +---------+ open
   |    A    |          |    A    |           |    A    |
   |    :    |          |    N    |           |    N    |
   |    :    |          |    :    |           |    A    |
   |    N    |          |    A    |           |    N    |
   +=========+ CLOSED   |    :    |           |    A    |
                        |    N    |           |    N    |
   L = lambda/4         +=========+ CLOSED    +=========+ CLOSED

                        L = 3 lambda/4        L = 5 lambda/4

        v                     3 v                   5 v
   f1 = ---            f2 = -------           f3 = -------
       4 L                    4 L                   4 L

  In general        f  =  ( 2n - 1 ) v / ( 4 L ),    n = 1, 2, 3, ...

        f1 : f2 : f3 : ...  =  1 : 3 : 5 : 7 : ...

  ONLY ODD HARMONICS ARE PRESENT. The even harmonics are missing, because
  the distance from a node (closed end) to an antinode (open end) is always
  an odd multiple of lambda/4.

  PART 3 — OPEN ORGAN PIPE                             [2 marks]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
   FUNDAMENTAL          1st OVERTONE          2nd OVERTONE
   (1st harmonic)       (2nd harmonic)        (3rd harmonic)

   +---------+ open     +---------+ open      +---------+ open
   |    A    |          |    A    |           |    A    |
   |    N    |          |    N    |           |    N    |
   |    A    |          |    A    |           |    A    |
   +---------+ open     |    N    |           |    N    |
                        |    A    |           |    A    |
   L = lambda/2         +---------+ open      |    N    |
                                              |    A    |
                        L = lambda            +---------+ open

                                              L = 3 lambda / 2

        v                    2 v                    3 v
   f1 = ---            f2 = -----            f3 = -------
       2 L                   2 L                    2 L

  In general        f  =  n v / ( 2 L ),      n = 1, 2, 3, ...

        f1 : f2 : f3 : ...  =  1 : 2 : 3 : 4 : ...

  ALL HARMONICS are present, so the note of an open pipe is RICHER and more
  musical than that of a closed pipe. For the same length, the open pipe's
  fundamental is TWICE that of the closed pipe (one octave higher).

  PART 4 — END CORRECTION                              [1 mark]
  -----------------------------------------------------------
  The antinode is not formed exactly at the open end; the air just outside
  the mouth also vibrates, so the antinode lies slightly OUTSIDE the pipe.
  The extra length to be added is the END CORRECTION:

                   e   =   0.6 r        (r = radius of the pipe)

                                    v
      CLOSED PIPE (1 open end):  f = -----------------
                                    4 ( L  +  0.6 r )

                                    v
      OPEN PIPE (2 open ends):   f = -----------------
                                    2 ( L  +  1.2 r )

  Because of the end correction the true frequency is slightly LOWER than
  the uncorrected value, and a wide pipe needs a larger correction.
```

### Q C5
Define a progressive wave and derive its equation. Explain the terms wave number,
angular frequency and phase, and obtain the relation between phase difference and
path difference. Discuss the speed of a transverse wave on a stretched string.

**ANSWER — SKELETON WITH MARK SPLIT**

```
  STEP 1  DEFINITION                                            [1 mark]
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  A PROGRESSIVE (travelling) wave is one that travels continuously forward
  through a medium, carrying energy and momentum with it, the particles of
  the medium vibrating about their mean positions.

  STEP 2  DERIVATION OF THE EQUATION                            [3 marks]
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Let the particle at the origin execute SHM:

        y  =  A sin( omega t )

  A particle at a distance x from the origin receives the disturbance
  LATER, after a time  x / v. Its phase therefore lags by

        phase lag  =  omega ( x / v )  =  k x

  Hence the displacement of the particle at x at time t is

        +-----------------------------------------------------+
        |     y   =   A  sin ( omega t   -   k x )            |
        +-----------------------------------------------------+

  MINUS sign -> the wave travels along +x.
  PLUS  sign -> the wave travels along -x.

  Equivalent forms:

                  +-   t          x     -+                 2 pi
     y = A sin 2pi|  ----  -   -------- |   ,  y = A sin -------- (vt - x)
                  +-   T        lambda  -+                lambda

  STEP 3  THE TERMS                                             [2 marks]
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  AMPLITUDE A        - maximum displacement from the mean position.

  ANGULAR FREQUENCY  omega = 2 pi f = 2 pi / T     (rad/s)
                     the phase covered per second.

  WAVE NUMBER / PROPAGATION CONSTANT
                     k = 2 pi / lambda             (rad/m)
                     the phase change per metre.

  PHASE              = ( omega t - k x ) — the complete state of vibration
                     of a particle (position in the cycle and direction).

  Also               v  =  omega / k  =  f lambda

  STEP 4  PHASE DIFFERENCE AND PATH DIFFERENCE                  [1 mark]
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two particles separated by a path difference (delta x) have phases
  differing by

        phi  =  k (delta x)  =  ( 2 pi / lambda ) x ( delta x )

  One whole wavelength of path  =  2 pi radians of phase.
        path lambda    -> phase 2 pi (in phase)
        path lambda/2  -> phase pi  (exactly opposite)

  STEP 5  SPEED ON A STRETCHED STRING                           [1 mark]
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
                     +-              -+
                     |       T        |
        v   =   sqrt |  ------------  |
                     |      mu        |
                     +-              -+

  T  = tension (N),  mu = mass per unit length (kg/m) = pi r^2 rho.

  Dimensional proof: assume v = k T^a mu^b. Substituting dimensions and
  equating the powers of M, L and T gives a = 1/2, b = -1/2, and
  experiment shows k = 1.
```
