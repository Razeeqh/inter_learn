## Topic: Charge and its properties

### Q A1
What are the properties of electric charge?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1. QUANTISATION  -  charge exists only as  Q = n e ,
                      where e = 1.6 x 10^-19 C and n is an integer.

  2. ADDITIVITY    -  charge is a SCALAR; total charge is the
                      algebraic sum of all charges with their signs.

  3. CONSERVATION  -  the total charge of an isolated system is
                      constant; charge is neither created nor destroyed.
```

---

### Q A2
What is meant by quantisation of charge? Write the relation.

**ANSWER:** Electric charge on any body exists only as an **integral
multiple of the elementary charge e**. It cannot take any arbitrary value.

```
        Q  =  n e          n = 0, ±1, ±2, ±3, ...

        e  =  1.6 x 10^-19  C
```

---

### Q A3
State the law of conservation of charge with one example.

**ANSWER:** The **total charge of an isolated system remains constant**;
charge can only be transferred, never created or destroyed.

```
  Example:  glass rubbed with silk
            BEFORE:  0  +  0  =  0
            AFTER:  +q  + (-q) =  0     Total unchanged.
```

---

### Q A4
A body has a charge of -3.2 x 10^-19 C. How many excess electrons does
it carry?

**ANSWER:**
```
          Q         3.2 x 10^-19
   n  =  ---   =  ----------------   =   2  electrons  (in excess)
          e         1.6 x 10^-19
```

---

### Q A5
Can a body have a charge of 1.6 x 10^-20 C? Why?

**ANSWER:** **No.**
```
          1.6 x 10^-20
   n  =  --------------   =   0.1     which is NOT an integer.
          1.6 x 10^-19

  Charge is quantised, so n must be a whole number.
```

---

### Q A6
Distinguish between charging by conduction and charging by induction.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CONDUCTION  -  requires CONTACT. The body acquires the SAME sign
                 of charge as the charging body. The charging body
                 loses some charge.

  INDUCTION   -  NO contact needed. The body acquires the OPPOSITE
                 sign of charge. The charging body loses nothing.
```

## Topic: Coulomb's law

### Q A7
State Coulomb's law in electrostatics.

**ANSWER:** The force of attraction or repulsion between two **point charges
at rest** is directly proportional to the **product of the magnitudes of the
charges** and inversely proportional to the **square of the distance**
between them, and acts along the line joining them.

```
              1        q1 q2
   F   =   --------- x -------
            4 pi e0      r^2
```

---

### Q A8
Write the vector form of Coulomb's law. Why is the vector form preferred?

**ANSWER:**
```
   ->            1        q1 q2      ^
   F12   =   --------- x -------- x  r21
              4 pi e0      r^2

   ^
   r21 = unit vector from charge 2 towards charge 1
```
It is preferred because **the sign of the product q1 q2 automatically
decides attraction or repulsion** — you do not have to decide by hand.
It also shows directly that F12 = - F21.

---

### Q A9
Define the permittivity of free space. Give its value and unit.

**ANSWER:** e0 is the constant that appears in Coulomb's law for vacuum; it
measures the ability of vacuum to permit electric field lines.

```
   e0  =  8.854 x 10^-12   C^2 N^-1 m^-2      (or F m^-1)

        1
   ---------  =  9 x 10^9   N m^2 C^-2
    4 pi e0
```

---

### Q A10
Define dielectric constant (relative permittivity). Give its unit.

**ANSWER:** The dielectric constant K of a medium is the ratio of the force
between two charges in vacuum to the force between the same charges at the
same separation in that medium.

```
          F_vacuum          e
   K  =  ----------   =   ------          K has NO UNITS.
          F_medium          e0

   K(vacuum) = 1 ,  K(water) = 80 ,  K(metal) = infinity
```

### Q A11
Two charges experience a force F in vacuum. What is the force if they are
placed in a medium of dielectric constant 5, at the same separation?

**ANSWER:**
```
              F         F
   F_med  =  ---   =   ---            The force becomes ONE-FIFTH.
              K         5
```

### Q A12
Give two differences between the Coulomb force and the gravitational force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  Coulomb force may ATTRACT or REPEL;
      gravitational force ONLY attracts.

  2.  Coulomb force DEPENDS on the medium (divide by K);
      gravitational force does NOT depend on the medium.

  (Also acceptable: the Coulomb force is about 10^36 times stronger,
   and it can be shielded whereas gravity cannot.)
```

## Topic: Electric field

### Q A13
Define electric field intensity. Give its SI unit and dimensions.

**ANSWER:** The electric field intensity at a point is the **force
experienced by a unit positive test charge** placed at that point.

```
   ->     ->
   E  =   F / q0            (as q0 -> 0)

   SI unit:      N C^-1   or   V m^-1
   Dimensions:   [ M L T^-3 A^-1 ]
   It is a VECTOR.
```

---

### Q A14
Why must the test charge used to define the electric field be
vanishingly small?

**ANSWER:** A large test charge would exert its own force on the source
charges and **redistribute or displace them**, thereby changing the very
field being measured. Taking the limit q0 -> 0 removes this disturbance.

---

### Q A15
State the principle of superposition of electric forces.

**ANSWER:** The force on any one charge due to a group of charges is the
**vector sum** of the forces exerted on it by each of the other charges
acting individually. **The presence of other charges does not alter the
force between any given pair.**

---

### Q A16
What is the direction of the electric field due to (i) a positive charge
(ii) a negative charge?

**ANSWER:**
```
   Positive charge  ->  radially OUTWARD (away from the charge)
   Negative charge  ->  radially INWARD  (towards the charge)
```

---

## Topic: Field lines

### Q A17
Write any four properties of electric lines of force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  They start on positive charges and end on negative charges.
  2.  The tangent at any point gives the direction of E there.
  3.  Two field lines NEVER intersect.
  4.  Their crowding indicates the strength of the field.
  (also: they never form closed loops; they are perpendicular to a
   conductor's surface and absent inside a conductor.)
```

---

### Q A18
Why do two electric field lines never intersect each other?

**ANSWER:** If they intersected, **two tangents could be drawn at the point
of intersection**, meaning the electric field would have **two directions at
the same point simultaneously**, which is impossible.

---

### Q A19
Electric field lines do not form closed loops. Why?

**ANSWER:** Because the electrostatic field is **conservative**. A closed
loop would allow a charge to gain energy by going round and round, which
violates conservation of energy. Lines must begin on +ve and end on -ve
charge.

## Topic: Dipole

### Q A20
Define electric dipole and electric dipole moment. Give the unit of
dipole moment.

**ANSWER:** An **electric dipole** is a pair of equal and opposite point
charges separated by a small distance.

```
   Dipole moment   p  =  q  x  ( 2a )

   Direction:  from  -q  TOWARDS  +q
   Unit:  coulomb metre  (C m)
   Dimensions:  [ L T A ]
   It is a VECTOR.
```

---

### Q A21
An electric dipole is placed in a uniform electric field. What is the net
force on it?

**ANSWER:** **ZERO.** The forces +qE and -qE on the two charges are equal
and opposite. However, since they act along different lines they form a
**couple**, so the dipole experiences a **torque** and rotates.

---

### Q A22
Write the expression for the torque on a dipole in a uniform field. When is
it maximum?

**ANSWER:**
```
   tau  =  p E sin(theta)             (vector form:  tau = p x E )

   MAXIMUM when theta = 90 degrees, i.e. when the dipole is
   PERPENDICULAR to the field.       tau_max  =  p E
```

---

### Q A23
Compare the axial and equatorial fields of a short dipole at the same
distance.

**ANSWER:**
```
   E_axial       =  2 k p / r^3       (parallel to p)
   E_equatorial  =    k p / r^3       (anti-parallel to p)

   E_axial   =   2  x  E_equatorial

   Both vary as  1 / r^3.
```

---

### Q A24
What is the potential energy of a dipole in a uniform field? When is it
minimum?

**ANSWER:**
```
   U  =  - p E cos(theta)             (vector form:  U = - p . E )

   MINIMUM ( = - p E ) when theta = 0, i.e. when the dipole is
   ALIGNED WITH the field. This is the STABLE position.
```

---

## Topic: Flux and Gauss's law

### Q A25
Define electric flux. Give its SI unit and state whether it is a scalar
or a vector.

**ANSWER:** Electric flux through a surface is the **total number of
electric field lines passing normally through that surface**.

```
   phi  =  E A cos(theta)          theta measured from the NORMAL

   SI unit:  N m^2 C^-1   (or V m)
   It is a SCALAR.
```

---

### Q A26
State Gauss's law in electrostatics.

**ANSWER:** The total electric flux through **any closed surface** is equal
to **1/e0 times the net charge enclosed** by the surface.

```
                                    q(enclosed)
   phi  =  closed integral(E.dA) = -------------
                                         e0
```

---

### Q A27
A charge q is placed at the centre of a cube. What is the flux through
one face?

**ANSWER:**
```
   Total flux  =  q / e0

   By symmetry the six faces share it equally:

                     q
   phi_face   =   --------
                   6 e0
```

---

### Q A28
What is the flux through a closed surface if a charge lies OUTSIDE it?

**ANSWER:** **ZERO.** Every field line that enters the surface also leaves
it, so the incoming (negative) flux exactly cancels the outgoing
(positive) flux.

---

### Q A29
Define linear, surface and volume charge densities and give their units.

**ANSWER:**
```
   lambda  =  q / L    ->  linear   charge density  ->  C m^-1
   sigma   =  q / A    ->  surface  charge density  ->  C m^-2
   rho     =  q / V    ->  volume   charge density  ->  C m^-3
```

---

### Q A30
What is the electric field inside a uniformly charged hollow spherical
shell? Give its practical use.

**ANSWER:** **E = 0** everywhere inside the shell, because the Gaussian
sphere drawn inside encloses **zero charge**.
Practical use: **electrostatic shielding** — a metal enclosure (car, plane,
Faraday cage) protects the inside from external fields, e.g. during
lightning.

### Q B1
State Coulomb's law. Write it in vector form and explain the meaning of
each symbol. What is the effect of a medium?

**ANSWER:**

**Statement:** The force between two point charges at rest is directly
proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges, inversely
proportional to the square of the distance between them, and acts along
the line joining them.

```
  SCALAR FORM
                    1        q1 q2
        F    =   --------- x -------
                  4 pi e0      r^2

  VECTOR FORM
        ->            1        q1 q2       ^
        F12   =   --------- x --------  x  r21
                   4 pi e0      r^2

  MEANING OF SYMBOLS
        q1, q2  =  the two point charges (put in WITH their signs)
        r       =  distance between them, in metres
        ^
        r21     =  unit vector from charge 2 towards charge 1
        e0      =  8.854 x 10^-12 C^2 N^-1 m^-2
        1/(4 pi e0)  =  9 x 10^9 N m^2 C^-2

  If q1 q2 is POSITIVE  ->  F along r  ->  REPULSION
  If q1 q2 is NEGATIVE  ->  F opposite r -> ATTRACTION

  Also    F12  =  - F21     (Newton's third law)


  EFFECT OF A MEDIUM

  In a medium of permittivity  e = e0 K :

                     1        q1 q2               F_vacuum
        F_med  =  --------- x -------    =     -------------
                   4 pi e       r^2                  K

  The force is REDUCED K times. K is the dielectric constant,
  a pure number, equal to 1 for vacuum and 80 for water.
```

### Q B2
Compare the electrostatic force with the gravitational force.

**ANSWER:**
```
  +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  ELECTROSTATIC (COULOMB) FORCE  |  GRAVITATIONAL FORCE            |
  +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+
  |  F = k q1 q2 / r^2              |  F = G m1 m2 / r^2              |
  |  Acts between CHARGES           |  Acts between MASSES            |
  |  ATTRACTIVE or REPULSIVE        |  ALWAYS ATTRACTIVE              |
  |  Depends on the medium (÷ K)    |  Independent of the medium      |
  |  Can be SHIELDED                |  Cannot be shielded             |
  |  k = 9 x 10^9                   |  G = 6.67 x 10^-11              |
  |  VERY STRONG                    |  VERY WEAK                      |
  +---------------------------------+---------------------------------+

  SIMILARITIES
  1.  Both obey the INVERSE SQUARE law.
  2.  Both are CENTRAL forces (act along the line joining the bodies).
  3.  Both are long-range forces and obey Newton's third law.
  4.  Both are conservative.

  For an electron and a proton:

        F_electric                     
       ------------   =   about  2.3 x 10^39
        F_gravity

  This is why gravity is completely ignored in atomic problems.
```

### Q B4
State and explain the principle of superposition. Three equal charges q
are at the corners of an equilateral triangle of side a. Find the force
on one of them.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PRINCIPLE
  The force on any charge due to a number of other charges is the
  VECTOR SUM of the individual forces, each calculated from Coulomb's
  law as if the others were absent. The presence of other charges does
  not alter the force between a given pair.

        ->     ->     ->            ->
        F1  =  F12  + F13  + ... +  F1n


  NUMERICAL

                       C (q)
                      / \
                     /   \
                a   /     \  a
                   /       \
                  /         \
          A (q)-------------- B (q)
                     a

  Force on the charge at A:

  From B :  F1 = k q^2 / a^2 , directed along B -> A (repulsion)
  From C :  F2 = k q^2 / a^2 , directed along C -> A (repulsion)

  Both have the same magnitude F = k q^2 / a^2 .
  The angle BETWEEN the two force vectors at A is 60 degrees.

  Resultant  =  2 F cos( 60 / 2 )
             =  2 F cos(30)
             =  2 F x ( sqrt(3) / 2 )
             =  sqrt(3) F


        +-----------------------------------------+
        |                    sqrt(3)  k q^2       |
        |     F_resultant =  ----------------     |
        |                          a^2            |
        +-----------------------------------------+

  DIRECTION: along the bisector of angle A, pointing away from
  the centre of the triangle.
```

### Q B5
Write the properties of electric lines of force. Draw the lines for
(i) a single positive charge (ii) two equal positive charges
(iii) a dipole.

**ANSWER:**
```
  PROPERTIES
  1. They start on POSITIVE charge and end on NEGATIVE charge.
     (For an isolated charge they start or end at infinity.)
  2. The TANGENT at any point gives the direction of E there.
  3. Two lines NEVER intersect, because E cannot have two directions

  6. They meet a conductor's surface PERPENDICULARLY, and there are
     NO lines inside a conductor.


  (i)  SINGLE POSITIVE CHARGE

              \      |      /
               \     |     /
                \    |    /
        ---------- ( + ) ----------
                /    |    \
               /     |     \
              /      |      \

        Radially outward.


  (ii) TWO EQUAL POSITIVE CHARGES

         \   |   /               \   |   /
          \  |  /                 \  |  /
        ---( + )---    .  N  .   ---( + )---
          /  |  \                 /  |  \
         /   |   \               /   |   \

        Lines bend AWAY from each other.
        N is the NEUTRAL POINT at the midpoint, where E = 0.
        No line passes through N.


  (iii) DIPOLE  (+q and -q)

                 .......................
               .                        .
             .      _______________       .
           .      /                 \      .
          .      /    _________      \      .

### Q B6
Derive an expression for the torque acting on an electric dipole placed
in a uniform electric field.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SET-UP

     Uniform field E, dipole at angle theta to it.

     ------------------------------------------->  E
     ------------------------------------------->

                       (+q)
                       /  ^ F = qE
                      /
                     /
              theta /
        ----------O---------------------  (direction of E)
                 /
                /
             (-q)
               |
               v  F = qE  (opposite direction)


  STEP 1 — FORCES
      On +q :   + q E   along E
      On -q :   - q E   opposite to E
      NET FORCE  =  qE - qE  =  ZERO
      So there is no translation.

  STEP 2 — THE TWO FORCES FORM A COUPLE
      They are equal, opposite, and act along DIFFERENT lines.
      A couple produces a TORQUE.

  STEP 3 — PERPENDICULAR DISTANCE between the two forces

           perpendicular distance  =  2a sin(theta)

  STEP 4 — TORQUE

        tau  =  (either force) x (perpendicular distance)
             =  ( q E ) x ( 2a sin(theta) )
             =  ( q x 2a ) E sin(theta)


        +---------------------------------------------+
        |                                             |
        |     tau   =   p E sin(theta)                |
        |                                             |
        |     ->      ->    ->                        |
        |     tau  =  p  x  E                         |
        |                                             |
        +---------------------------------------------+

  SPECIAL CASES
        theta = 0    ->  tau = 0     STABLE equilibrium
        theta = 90   ->  tau = pE    MAXIMUM torque
        theta = 180  ->  tau = 0     UNSTABLE equilibrium

  If theta = 90 degrees, tau = pE, so  p = tau / E.
  Hence dipole moment may also be defined as the MAXIMUM TORQUE
  experienced per unit electric field.
```

### Q B7
Derive an expression for the potential energy of a dipole in a uniform
electric field. Find the work done to rotate it from 0 to 180 degrees.

**ANSWER:**
```
  To rotate the dipole through a small angle d(theta) against the
  torque, the work done is

        dW  =  tau  d(theta)  =  p E sin(theta) d(theta)

  Total work in turning from theta1 to theta2:

        W  =  integral of  p E sin(theta) d(theta)   from t1 to t2

           =  p E [ - cos(theta) ]   from t1 to t2

           =  p E [ cos(theta1)  -  cos(theta2) ]

  This work is stored as POTENTIAL ENERGY. Taking U = 0 at
  theta = 90 degrees (the dipole perpendicular to the field):

        U  =  p E [ cos(90) - cos(theta) ]  =  - p E cos(theta)


        +---------------------------------------------+
        |                                             |
        |     U   =   - p E cos(theta)                |
        |                                             |
        |            ->   ->                          |
        |     U  =  - p .  E                          |
        |                                             |
        +---------------------------------------------+

  VALUES
        theta =   0   ->  U = - p E    MINIMUM  ->  STABLE
        theta =  90   ->  U =   0
        theta = 180   ->  U = + p E    MAXIMUM  ->  UNSTABLE

  WORK TO ROTATE FROM 0 TO 180 DEGREES

        W  =  p E [ cos(0) - cos(180) ]
           =  p E [ 1 - (-1) ]
           =  2 p E
```

### Q B8
Define electric flux. State Gauss's law and prove it for a point charge.

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRIC FLUX
  The total number of electric field lines passing normally through a
  surface.

        phi  =  E . A  =  E A cos(theta)

  theta is measured from the NORMAL to the surface.
  Unit: N m^2 C^-1. It is a SCALAR.


  GAUSS'S LAW
  The total electric flux through any CLOSED surface is 1/e0 times the
  NET CHARGE ENCLOSED by that surface.

                                     q(enclosed)
        phi  =  closed integral(E.dA) = ------------
                                          e0


  PROOF FOR A POINT CHARGE

  Take a point charge +q. Draw a SPHERE of radius r with the charge
  at its centre (this is the Gaussian surface).

                      . - - - - - - .
                   .        ^ dA       .
                 .          |            .
                .        E  |   ^         .
                .           |    \         .
                .     ------(+q)------>     .
                .           |    /          .
                 .          |   v          .
                   .        v            .
                      ' - - - - - - '

  On this sphere:
      (i)   E has the SAME magnitude everywhere  =  k q / r^2
      (ii)  E is RADIAL, i.e. parallel to dA everywhere, so
            the angle between E and dA is ZERO.

  Therefore

        phi  =  closed integral of E dA cos(0)

             =  E x (total surface area)

             =  E x  4 pi r^2

                    1        q
             =  --------- x -----  x  4 pi r^2
                 4 pi e0     r^2

                    q
             =   ------
                   e0

  Hence proved. Note that r has cancelled, so the flux does NOT
  depend on the size of the Gaussian surface.
```

### Q B9
What is a Gaussian surface? What are the conditions for choosing a good
one? Why is Gauss's law useful?

**ANSWER:**
```
  A GAUSSIAN SURFACE is an IMAGINARY closed surface drawn around a
  charge distribution, over which Gauss's law is applied.

  CONDITIONS FOR A GOOD CHOICE
  1.  It must be a CLOSED surface.
  2.  It must follow the SYMMETRY of the charge distribution, so that
      E has a CONSTANT magnitude over it.
  3.  E should be either PERPENDICULAR to the surface (full flux) or
      PARALLEL to it (zero flux) at every point.
  4.  It must NOT pass through a discrete point charge (E is infinite
      there).

  WHICH SURFACE FOR WHICH BODY
  +----------------------+---------------------------+
  |  Point charge/sphere |  concentric SPHERE        |
  |  Long wire           |  coaxial CYLINDER         |
  |  Plane sheet         |  CYLINDER / PILLBOX       |
  +----------------------+---------------------------+

  WHY IT IS USEFUL
  For symmetric distributions it turns a difficult vector integration
  into simple arithmetic, giving E in one or two lines. It is also
  valid for any surface of any shape, and shows immediately that
  charges outside contribute zero net flux.
```

### Q B10
Using Gauss's law, derive the field due to an infinitely long straight
uniformly charged wire.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Let the wire have linear charge density lambda (C/m).
  Find E at a perpendicular distance r.

  STEP 1 — SYMMETRY
  By symmetry E is RADIAL (perpendicular to the wire) and has the
  same magnitude at all points at distance r.

  STEP 2 — GAUSSIAN SURFACE
  A CYLINDER of radius r and length l, coaxial with the wire.

                      |<---------- l --------->|
        ==============+========================+============
          wire        |                        |
                   ___|________________________|___
                  /   |                        |   \
        cap ---> |    |         ^  E           |    | <--- cap
                 |    |         |              |    |
                 |    |  -------+----->        |    |
                  \___|________________________|___/
                      |                        |
        ==============+========================+============
                       curved surface

  STEP 3 — FLUX
  Curved surface : E is parallel to the area vector ->
                   flux =  E x (2 pi r l)
  Two flat caps  : E lies IN their plane (angle 90 degrees) ->
                   flux = 0

        Total flux  =  E x 2 pi r l

  STEP 4 — CHARGE ENCLOSED
        q  =  lambda  x  l

  STEP 5 — GAUSS'S LAW

                          lambda l
        E x 2 pi r l  =  ----------
                             e0

        +--------------------------------------------+
        |                                            |
        |               lambda           2 k lambda  |

  positive lambda. Note that l has cancelled, as it must.

### Q B14
Explain charging by induction, with diagrams, in steps.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Charging by induction produces a charge WITHOUT any contact, and the
  induced charge is OPPOSITE in sign to the charging body.

  STEP 1  Bring a positively charged rod near a neutral metal sphere
          standing on an insulating stand. Free electrons in the sphere
          are attracted to the near side.

              + + +           _________
              [ROD]          /  - - +  \
              + + +         |  -     +  |
                             \  - - +  /
                              ---------
          Near side NEGATIVE, far side POSITIVE. Total still ZERO.

  STEP 2  EARTH the sphere while the rod is still in place. Electrons
          flow UP from the earth and neutralise the positive far side.

              + + +           _________
              [ROD]          /  - - -  \
              + + +         |  -     -  |
                             \  - - -  /
                              ----+----
                                  |
                                -----  earth

  STEP 3  REMOVE THE EARTHING FIRST, keeping the rod in place.

  STEP 4  NOW remove the rod. The excess electrons spread uniformly.

                              _________
                             /  -   -  \
                            | -   -   - |
                             \  -   -  /
                              ---------
                      Sphere is now NEGATIVELY charged.

  KEY POINTS FOR MARKS
  *  The rod loses NO charge at all.
  *  The sphere acquires the OPPOSITE charge to the rod.
  *  The ORDER matters: remove the earth FIRST, the rod LAST.
```

### Q C1
Derive the expression for the electric field intensity due to an electric
dipole at a point on its **axial line**. Also obtain the result for a
short dipole.

**ANSWER:**

```
  DEFINITIONS FIRST (worth a mark)

  An electric dipole is a pair of equal and opposite point charges
  separated by a small distance 2a. Its dipole moment is

        p  =  q x 2a ,  directed from  -q  towards  +q.

  The AXIAL LINE is the line passing through both charges, extended
  on either side. A point on it is said to be in the "end-on" position.


  THE DIAGRAM

                       2a
          |<--------------------------->|

         (-q)            O            (+q)                     P
          A -------------|------------- B --------- r --------- .
                         |                                      |
                         |<------------- r -------------------->|
                         |
                    centre of the dipole

      Distance from  +q  to  P   =   r - a
      Distance from  -q  to  P   =   r + a


  STEP 1 — FIELD AT P DUE TO +q

  It is directed AWAY from +q, i.e. from B towards P (to the right):

                       1             q
        E1   =    ---------- x  -------------            (towards P)
                   4 pi e0       ( r - a )^2


  STEP 2 — FIELD AT P DUE TO -q

  It is directed TOWARDS -q, i.e. from P towards A (to the left):

                       1             q
        E2   =    ---------- x  -------------            (away from P)
                   4 pi e0       ( r + a )^2


  STEP 3 — RESULTANT

  P is nearer to +q, so E1 > E2 and the resultant points along the
  direction of p (to the right). The two are anti-parallel, so we
  SUBTRACT their magnitudes:

        E_axial  =  E1  -  E2

                              +-                            -+
                     q        |      1                1       |
                =  --------- x|  ------------  -  ------------|
                   4 pi e0    |  ( r - a )^2      ( r + a )^2 |
                              +-                            -+

  STEP 4 — SIMPLIFY

  Take the common denominator:

                              +-                              -+
                     q        |  ( r + a )^2  -  ( r - a )^2    |
                =  --------- x| ------------------------------- |
                   4 pi e0    |    ( r - a )^2 ( r + a )^2      |
                              +-                              -+

  Numerator:
        ( r + a )^2 - ( r - a )^2
             =  ( r^2 + 2ar + a^2 ) - ( r^2 - 2ar + a^2 )
             =  4 a r

  Denominator:
        [ ( r - a )( r + a ) ]^2   =   ( r^2 - a^2 )^2

  Therefore

                     q               4 a r
        E_axial = --------- x  --------------------
                   4 pi e0      ( r^2 - a^2 )^2


  STEP 5 — INTRODUCE THE DIPOLE MOMENT

        q x 4 a r  =  2 r x ( q x 2a )  =  2 p r

        +==========================================================+
        |                                                          |
        |                      1              2 p r                |
        |     E_axial   =  ---------- x  ------------------        |
        |                   4 pi e0        ( r^2 - a^2 )^2         |
        |                                                          |
        +==========================================================+


  STEP 6 — SHORT DIPOLE  ( r >> a , so a^2 is negligible )

        ( r^2 - a^2 )^2   ->   ( r^2 )^2   =   r^4

                      1          2 p r              1         2 p
        E_axial  =  --------- x  -------    =   --------- x  ------
                     4 pi e0       r^4           4 pi e0      r^3

        +==========================================================+
        |                                                          |
        |                      2 k p                               |
        |     E_axial   =    ---------                             |
        |                       r^3                                |
        |                                                          |
        |     DIRECTION: PARALLEL to the dipole moment p           |
        |                                                          |
        +==========================================================+


  NOTE (worth a mark)
  The field of a dipole falls as 1 / r^3 , faster than the 1 / r^2 of a
  single point charge, because the fields of the two opposite charges
  almost cancel at large distances.
```

### Q C2
Derive the expression for the electric field intensity due to an electric
dipole at a point on its **equatorial line**. Compare it with the axial
field.

**ANSWER:**

```
  The EQUATORIAL LINE is the perpendicular bisector of the dipole axis.
  A point on it is said to be in the "broadside-on" position.


  THE DIAGRAM

                                  P
                                  .
                                / | \
                              /   |   \
                            /     |     \
                          /       |       \
             sqrt(r^2+a^2)        r        sqrt(r^2+a^2)
                      /           |           \
                    /  theta      |      theta  \
                  /               |               \
        (-q) ----O----------------O----------------O---- (+q)
                 A                |                B
                 |<----- a ------>|<----- a ------>|

  Distance from EACH charge to P is the SAME:

        AP  =  BP  =  sqrt( r^2 + a^2 )


  STEP 1 — MAGNITUDES OF THE TWO FIELDS

  Because the distances are equal, the magnitudes are equal:

                                   1              q
        E1  =  E2  =  E  =    ---------- x  ---------------
                               4 pi e0        ( r^2 + a^2 )

  E1 (due to +q) points AWAY from B, i.e. along BP produced.
  E2 (due to -q) points TOWARDS A, i.e. along PA.


  STEP 2 — RESOLVE INTO COMPONENTS

  Resolve each field into a component along the dipole axis and a
  component perpendicular to it.

                   E2 \       |       / E1
                       \      |      /
                        \     |     /
                    <------- P -------->
                     (the components parallel to the axis
                      both point from + towards - : they ADD)

      PERPENDICULAR components:
            E1 sin(theta)  upward
            E2 sin(theta)  downward
            EQUAL and OPPOSITE  ->  they CANCEL.

      PARALLEL components (along the axis, from +q side to -q side):
            E1 cos(theta)  and  E2 cos(theta)
            SAME direction  ->  they ADD.


  STEP 3 — ADD THE SURVIVING COMPONENTS

        E_eq  =  E1 cos(theta)  +  E2 cos(theta)   =   2 E cos(theta)

  From the right-angled triangle:

                              a
        cos(theta)  =  --------------------
                        sqrt( r^2 + a^2 )

  Therefore

                             1              q                    a
        E_eq  =   2  x  ---------- x  -------------- x  --------------------
                         4 pi e0       ( r^2 + a^2 )     sqrt( r^2 + a^2 )

                             1            q ( 2a )
              =         ---------- x  --------------------
                         4 pi e0       ( r^2 + a^2 )^(3/2)


  STEP 4 — INTRODUCE p = q ( 2a )

        +==========================================================+
        |                                                          |
        |                      1                 p                 |
        |     E_eq   =     ---------- x  ---------------------     |
        |                   4 pi e0       ( r^2 + a^2 )^(3/2)      |
        |                                                          |
        +==========================================================+


  STEP 5 — SHORT DIPOLE  ( r >> a )

        ( r^2 + a^2 )^(3/2)   ->   ( r^2 )^(3/2)   =   r^3

        +==========================================================+
        |                                                          |
        |                       k p                                |
        |     E_eq    =       -------                              |
        |                       r^3                                |
        |                                                          |
        |     DIRECTION: ANTI-PARALLEL to p                        |
        |                (from the +q side towards the -q side)    |
        |                                                          |
        +==========================================================+


  STEP 6 — COMPARISON WITH THE AXIAL FIELD

  +--------------------+----------------------+----------------------+
  |                    |  AXIAL               |  EQUATORIAL          |
  +--------------------+----------------------+----------------------+
  |  Expression        |  2 k p / r^3         |  k p / r^3           |
  |  Direction         |  PARALLEL to p       |  ANTI-PARALLEL to p  |
  |  Magnitude         |  TWICE the other     |  HALF of axial       |
  |  Variation with r  |  1 / r^3             |  1 / r^3             |
  +--------------------+----------------------+----------------------+

              E_axial   =   2  x  E_equatorial

  Both fall as 1 / r^3, unlike the 1 / r^2 field of a point charge.
```

### Q C3
State Gauss's law. Using it, derive the expression for the electric field
due to (a) an infinitely long straight uniformly charged wire and
(b) a uniformly charged infinite plane sheet. Also find the field between
two oppositely charged parallel sheets.

**ANSWER:**

```
  GAUSS'S LAW

  The total electric flux through any CLOSED surface is equal to
  1/e0 times the NET CHARGE ENCLOSED by that surface.

                                          q(enclosed)
        phi  =  closed integral(E . dA) = -------------
                                               e0

  The imaginary closed surface used is called the GAUSSIAN SURFACE.
  Charges outside it contribute ZERO net flux.


  ==========================================================
  (a)  INFINITELY LONG STRAIGHT UNIFORMLY CHARGED WIRE
  ==========================================================

  Let lambda = linear charge density = charge per unit length (C/m).
  Find E at a perpendicular distance r from the wire.

  STEP 1 — SYMMETRY
  The wire looks identical in both directions, so E can have no
  component along the wire. E must be RADIAL and must have the same
  magnitude at every point at distance r.

  STEP 2 — GAUSSIAN SURFACE
  Choose a CYLINDER of radius r and length l, coaxial with the wire.

                        |<---------- l ---------->|
       =================+=========================+==============
         wire, lambda   |                         |
                     ___|_________________________|___
                    /   |                         |   \
        flat cap-> |    |          ^  E           |    | <-flat cap
                   |    |          |              |    |
                   |    |  --------+------>       |    |
                   |    |             r           |    |
                    \___|_________________________|___/
                        |                         |
       =================+=========================+==============
                          CURVED SURFACE

  STEP 3 — FLUX THROUGH EACH PART
     CURVED SURFACE : E is everywhere perpendicular to it, i.e.
                      parallel to the area vector (angle 0).
                      flux  =  E x ( 2 pi r l )

     TWO FLAT CAPS  : E is radial and therefore lies IN the plane
                      of each cap. The angle between E and the cap's
                      area vector is 90 degrees.
                      flux  =  0

     TOTAL FLUX     =  E  x  2 pi r l

  STEP 4 — CHARGE ENCLOSED
     Only the length l of wire lies inside:

            q  =  lambda  x  l

  STEP 5 — APPLY GAUSS'S LAW

                            lambda  l
        E x 2 pi r l   =   -----------
                                e0

        +========================================================+
        |                                                        |
        |               lambda                2 k lambda         |
        |     E   =   -------------     =    -------------       |
        |               2 pi e0 r                  r             |
        |                                                        |
        +========================================================+

     *  E varies as  1 / r  (NOT 1/r^2).
     *  Directed radially OUTWARD if lambda is positive,
        radially INWARD if lambda is negative.
     *  The length l cancels, as it must, since the answer cannot
        depend on the arbitrary length we chose.


  ==========================================================
  (b)  UNIFORMLY CHARGED INFINITE PLANE SHEET
  ==========================================================

  Let sigma = surface charge density = charge per unit area (C/m^2).

  STEP 1 — SYMMETRY
  The sheet looks the same from every point, so E must be
  PERPENDICULAR to the sheet and equal in magnitude on both sides
  at equal distances.

  STEP 2 — GAUSSIAN SURFACE
  A CYLINDER (a "pillbox") of cross-sectional area A, pushed
  symmetrically through the sheet so that its two flat faces are
  parallel to the sheet and equidistant from it.

                          |  SHEET  (sigma)
                          |
                          |
                    ______|______
                   /      |      \
                  |       |       |
             <----| E   A | A   E |---->
                  |       |       |
                   \______|______/
                          |
                CURVED SIDE: E lies ALONG it -> no flux
                          |

  STEP 3 — FLUX THROUGH EACH PART
     TWO FLAT CAPS : E is perpendicular to each and points outward
                     flux from each  =  E A
                     total from both =  2 E A

     CURVED SIDE   : E is parallel to the curved surface
                     (angle 90 degrees with its area vector)
                     flux  =  0

     TOTAL FLUX    =  2 E A

  STEP 4 — CHARGE ENCLOSED
     The piece of sheet trapped inside the pillbox has area A:

            q  =  sigma  x  A

  STEP 5 — APPLY GAUSS'S LAW

                       sigma A
        2 E A    =   ------------
                          e0

        +========================================================+
        |                                                        |
        |                    sigma                               |
        |        E   =    ---------                              |
        |                    2 e0                                |
        |                                                        |
        +========================================================+

     *  E is INDEPENDENT of the distance from the sheet.
        The field of an infinite sheet is UNIFORM.
     *  Directed away from the sheet if sigma is positive.
     *  Just outside a CHARGED CONDUCTOR the result is
        E = sigma / e0 (twice as large), because the conductor
        has charge on both faces and the field inside it is zero.


  ==========================================================
  (c)  TWO PARALLEL OPPOSITELY CHARGED SHEETS
  ==========================================================

           SHEET 1 (+sigma)             SHEET 2 (-sigma)
                 |                            |
                 |                            |
     REGION I    |        REGION II           |    REGION III
                 |                            |
                 |                            |

  Each sheet on its own produces a uniform field of magnitude
  sigma / (2 e0), pointing AWAY from the positive sheet and
  TOWARDS the negative sheet.

  +-----------+--------------------+--------------------+-----------+
  |  REGION   |  FIELD OF SHEET 1  |  FIELD OF SHEET 2  |  RESULT   |
  +-----------+--------------------+--------------------+-----------+
  |    I      |  sig/2e0 to LEFT   |  sig/2e0 to RIGHT  |    0      |
  |    II     |  sig/2e0 to RIGHT  |  sig/2e0 to RIGHT  |  sig/e0   |
  |   III     |  sig/2e0 to RIGHT  |  sig/2e0 to LEFT   |    0      |
  +-----------+--------------------+--------------------+-----------+

        +========================================================+
        |                                                        |
        |    BETWEEN the sheets :    E  =  sigma / e0            |
        |                            (directed from + to -)      |
        |                                                        |
        |    OUTSIDE both sheets:    E  =  0                     |
        |                                                        |
        +========================================================+

  This uniform field confined between the plates, with nothing
  outside, is exactly the arrangement of a PARALLEL PLATE CAPACITOR.
```

### Q C4
Using Gauss's law, derive the expression for the electric field due to a
uniformly charged thin spherical shell at a point (i) outside the shell
(ii) on its surface (iii) inside the shell. Draw the graph of E against r.

**ANSWER:**

```

  uniformly over its surface, so that

                        Q
        sigma  =   -------------
                    4 pi R^2

  By symmetry the field must be RADIAL, and must have the same
  magnitude at all points at the same distance r from the centre.

                     . - - - - - - - .

               .  +                    +  .
               . +           O          + .              P
               . +           |<--- R --->+.              .

            phi  =  E  x  4 pi r^2

  STEP 2 — CHARGE ENCLOSED
     The entire shell lies inside, so

            q(enclosed)  =  Q

                              Q
        E  x  4 pi r^2  =  --------
                              e0

        +========================================================+
        |                                                        |
        |                    1        Q            k Q           |
        |       E    =   --------- x -----   =   -------         |

        |                                                        |
        +========================================================+

     IMPORTANT CONCLUSION:
     This is exactly the field of a POINT CHARGE Q placed at the
     CENTRE. So for all external points, a uniformly charged shell
     behaves as if its whole charge were concentrated at its centre.


  ==========================================================
  (ii)  POINT ON THE SURFACE   ( r = R )
  ==========================================================

     Putting r = R in the above result:

        +========================================================+
        |                                                        |
        |                 k Q            sigma                   |

        |                 R^2             e0                     |
        |                                                        |
        +========================================================+

     This is the MAXIMUM value of the field.

     (Check:  kQ/R^2  =  Q/(4 pi e0 R^2)  =  sigma / e0 ,
      since sigma = Q / 4 pi R^2. )


  ==========================================================
  (iii)  POINT INSIDE THE SHELL   ( r < R )
  ==========================================================

                     . - - - - - - - .
                  .   + + + + + + + +  .
                .  +                  +  .
               .  +   . - - - - - .    +  .
               . +   .             .    + .
               . +  .       O       .    +.      <-- Gaussian sphere
               . +  .       |<- r ->.    +.          of radius r < R
               .  +  .             .    +  .
                .  +   ' - - - - -'     +  .
                  .   + + + + + + + +   .
                     ' - - - - - - - '

            phi  =  E  x  4 pi r^2

  STEP 2 — CHARGE ENCLOSED
     ALL the charge sits on the outer surface at radius R.
     Therefore NO charge lies inside the Gaussian sphere:

            q(enclosed)  =  0

  STEP 3 — GAUSS'S LAW

                              0
        E  x  4 pi r^2  =  --------   =   0
                              e0

        |                                                        |

        |                                                        |

     (a car, an aeroplane, a Faraday cage) keeps its interior free of
     external electric fields, which is why one is safe inside a car
     during a thunderstorm.


  ==========================================================
  THE GRAPH OF E AGAINST r
  ==========================================================

    E
    ^
    |
    |               * <---- maximum,  E = kQ/R^2 = sigma/e0
    |               |*
    |               |  *
    |               |     *
    |               |        *            E falls as 1/r^2
    |               |             *
    |               |                   *
    |               |                          *
    |               |                                  *

    O               R

        Outside (r > R):    E  =  k Q / r^2      (same as shell)

                                   k Q r          rho r

### Q C5
Define electric flux. State and prove Gauss's law. Apply it to find the
field due to an infinite plane sheet of charge and state two important
conclusions from Gauss's law.

**ANSWER:**

```
  PART 1 — ELECTRIC FLUX

  Electric flux through a surface is the total number of electric field
  lines passing NORMALLY through that surface.

        phi  =  E . A  =  E A cos(theta)

  where theta is the angle between E and the NORMAL to the surface.

  For a general surface:

        phi  =  integral ( E . dA )

  Unit: N m^2 C^-1  (or V m).   Dimensions: [ M L^3 T^-3 A^-1 ].
  It is a SCALAR quantity.

        theta = 0     ->  phi = E A     (maximum)
        theta = 90    ->  phi = 0
        theta = 180   ->  phi = - E A


  PART 2 — STATEMENT OF GAUSS'S LAW

  The total electric flux through any CLOSED surface is 1/e0 times the
  NET CHARGE ENCLOSED by that surface.

                                          q(enclosed)
        phi  =  closed integral(E . dA) = -------------
                                                e0


  PART 3 — PROOF FOR A POINT CHARGE

  Consider an isolated point charge +q. Draw an imaginary SPHERE of
  radius r with the charge at its centre.

                        . - - - - - - .
                     .        ^  dA      .
                   .          |            .
                  .       E   |    ^        .
                  .           |     \        .
                  .   <-------(+q)------->    .
                  .           |     /        .
                   .          |    v        .
                     .        v          .
                        ' - - - - - - '

  Why a sphere is the right choice:
     (i)  the magnitude of E is the SAME at every point on it, because
          every point is at the same distance r;
     (ii) E is RADIAL, so it is PARALLEL to the area vector dA at
          every point, i.e. the angle between them is ZERO.

  Consider a small area element dA. The flux through it is

        d(phi)  =  E dA cos(0)  =  E dA

  Total flux over the whole sphere:

        phi  =  closed integral of  E dA
             =  E  x  ( total area )              [E is constant]
             =  E  x  4 pi r^2

  Substituting the field of a point charge, E = q / (4 pi e0 r^2):

                       q
        phi  =   -------------- x  4 pi r^2
                  4 pi e0 r^2

                    q
             =   ------
                   e0

  Hence proved. Note that r has cancelled: the flux is INDEPENDENT
  of the radius of the Gaussian surface. Since field lines are
  continuous, the same total flux passes through ANY closed surface
  enclosing the charge, whatever its shape. This extends the proof
  to any closed surface.


  PART 4 — APPLICATION: INFINITE PLANE SHEET OF CHARGE

  Let the sheet have surface charge density sigma.

  SYMMETRY: E is perpendicular to the sheet and equal on both sides.

  GAUSSIAN SURFACE: a cylindrical pillbox of face area A pushed
  symmetrically through the sheet.

                          |  SHEET (sigma)
                    ______|______
                   /      |      \
              <---| E   A | A   E |--->
                   \______|______/
                          |
                curved side gives NO flux

  FLUX      =  E A  (left cap)  +  E A (right cap)  +  0 (curved)
            =  2 E A

  CHARGE ENCLOSED  =  sigma A

  GAUSS'S LAW:
                      sigma A
        2 E A    =  -----------
                        e0

        +--------------------------------------+
        |                sigma                 |
        |      E   =   ---------               |
        |                 2 e0                 |
        +--------------------------------------+

  E does not depend on the distance: the field is UNIFORM.


  PART 5 — TWO IMPORTANT CONCLUSIONS FROM GAUSS'S LAW

  1.  The flux through a closed surface depends ONLY on the charge
      ENCLOSED, not on the shape or size of the surface, and not on
      where inside the charge sits. A charge OUTSIDE the surface
      contributes ZERO net flux.

  2.  The electric field inside a charged conductor (or inside a
      hollow charged shell) is ZERO, since the enclosed charge is
      zero. All excess charge on a conductor resides on its OUTER
      SURFACE. This is the basis of ELECTROSTATIC SHIELDING.
