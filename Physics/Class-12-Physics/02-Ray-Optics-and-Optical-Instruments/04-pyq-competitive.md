# PATTERN 1 — Plane mirrors and inclined mirrors

### Q1
Two plane mirrors are inclined at 72 degrees. The number of images of a point
object placed between them (not on the bisector) is

(a) 4 &nbsp; (b) 5 &nbsp; (c) 6 &nbsp; (d) 3

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
        360      360
   N = ----- =  ----- =  5      (an ODD number)
          a       72

   Object NOT on the bisector  ->  n  =  N  =  5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** compute 360/a first. Even -> subtract 1. Odd -> read the
> question again to see whether the object is on the bisector.

---

### Q2
A ray strikes a plane mirror at an angle of 15 degrees with the mirror surface.
The angle between the incident and the reflected ray is

(a) 30 deg &nbsp; (b) 60 deg &nbsp; (c) 150 deg &nbsp; (d) 75 deg

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Angle of incidence  i  =  90 - 15  =  75 deg
   Angle between the two rays  =  i + r  =  75 + 75  =  150 deg
```
> **SHORTCUT:** "angle with the SURFACE" always means subtract from 90 first.
> This is the single most common trick in the reflection section.

---

### Q3
A plane mirror is rotated through 10 degrees keeping the incident ray fixed.
The reflected ray turns through

(a) 5 deg &nbsp; (b) 10 deg &nbsp; (c) 20 deg &nbsp; (d) 40 deg

**ANSWER: (c)** — the reflected ray turns through **2 theta = 20 degrees**.

---

### Q4
The minimum length of a plane mirror needed for a person of height 180 cm to
see his full image is

(a) 180 cm &nbsp; (b) 120 cm &nbsp; (c) 90 cm &nbsp; (d) 45 cm

**ANSWER: (c)** — the mirror needs to be only **half** the person's height,
`180 / 2 = 90 cm`, and this does not depend on how far he stands.

# PATTERN 2 — Mirror formula numericals

### Q5
An object is placed 20 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm.
The image distance is

(a) -60 cm &nbsp; (b) +60 cm &nbsp; (c) -30 cm &nbsp; (d) +12 cm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   u = -20 , f = -15

    1      1      1        1        1        -4  +  3        -1
   ---  = ---  - ----  = ------ - ------  = ----------  =  ------
    v      f      u       (-15)   (-20)         60            60

            v  =  -60 cm      (real image, 60 cm in front)
```

---

### Q6
The magnification produced by a convex mirror is always

(a) greater than 1 &nbsp; (b) less than 1 &nbsp; (c) equal to 1 &nbsp;
(d) negative

**ANSWER: (b)** — a convex mirror always produces a virtual, erect,
**diminished** image, so `0 < m < 1`.

---

### Q7
An object is placed at the centre of curvature of a concave mirror. The image is

(a) virtual and erect &nbsp; (b) real, inverted and of the same size
(c) real and diminished &nbsp; (d) at infinity

**ANSWER: (b)** — object at C gives an image at C, real, inverted, same size,
`m = -1`.

---

### Q8
A concave mirror of focal length 10 cm forms an image three times the size of
the object. The object may be at

(a) 6.67 cm or 13.33 cm &nbsp; (b) 30 cm only &nbsp; (c) 20 cm only
(d) 10 cm only

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Use   m  =  f / ( f - u )   with  f = -10

   CASE 1  real image,  m = -3
        -3  =  -10 / ( -10 - u )   ->   -10 - u  =  10/3
                                   ->   u  =  -13.33 cm

   CASE 2  virtual image,  m = +3
        +3  =  -10 / ( -10 - u )   ->   -10 - u  =  -10/3
                                   ->   u  =  -6.67 cm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** when a question says "magnified 3 times" without saying real
> or virtual, there are **two** answers. Look for an option pair.

---

### Q9
For a concave mirror, the minimum distance between a real object and its real
image is

(a) f &nbsp; (b) 2f &nbsp; (c) 4f &nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (c)** — the minimum occurs when both are at C, so the distance is
`2f + 2f = 4f`.

# PATTERN 3 — Refractive index and Snell's law

### Q10
Light of wavelength 600 nm in vacuum enters a medium of refractive index 1.5.
Its wavelength in the medium is

(a) 900 nm &nbsp; (b) 600 nm &nbsp; (c) 400 nm &nbsp; (d) 300 nm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
              lambda (vacuum)        600
   lambda  =  ----------------  =  -------  =  400 nm
                    mu               1.5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** on refraction, **frequency stays the same**; speed and
> wavelength both divide by mu.

---

### Q11
A ray goes from a medium of refractive index 1.5 into a medium of refractive
index 1.2. The ratio of the speed of light in the two media is

(a) 5 : 4 &nbsp; (b) 4 : 5 &nbsp; (c) 1 : 1 &nbsp; (d) 25 : 16

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   v is inversely proportional to mu :

     v1      mu2      1.2       4
    ----  = -----  = ------  = ---
     v2      mu1      1.5       5
```

---

### Q12
A ray falls on a glass surface at 60 degrees and the refraction angle is
30 degrees. The refractive index is

(a) sqrt(2) &nbsp; (b) sqrt(3) &nbsp; (c) 1.5 &nbsp; (d) 2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
           sin 60       0.866
   mu  =  --------  =  --------  =  1.732  =  sqrt(3)
           sin 30        0.5
```

---

### Q13
A coin lies at the bottom of a tank of water (mu = 4/3) of depth 4 m. Its
apparent depth is

(a) 3 m &nbsp; (b) 4 m &nbsp; (c) 5.33 m &nbsp; (d) 2 m

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
                       4          4 x 3
   apparent depth  =  -----  =  --------  =  3 m
                       4/3         4
```

---

### Q14
A vessel contains 8 cm of water (mu = 4/3) over 6 cm of a liquid of mu = 1.5.
The apparent depth seen from above is

(a) 10 cm &nbsp; (b) 14 cm &nbsp; (c) 12 cm &nbsp; (d) 9 cm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
     t1       t2        8         6
    ----  +  ----  =  -----  +  -----  =  6  +  4  =  10 cm
     mu1      mu2      4/3       1.5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** for stacked layers, just add `t / mu` for each layer.
> Never average the refractive indices.

# PATTERN 4 — Total internal reflection

### Q15
The critical angle for a medium of refractive index sqrt(2) is

(a) 30 deg &nbsp; (b) 45 deg &nbsp; (c) 60 deg &nbsp; (d) 90 deg

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
                1           1
   sin C  =  ------  =  ----------  =  0.7071      ->    C  =  45 deg
                mu        sqrt(2)
```

---

### Q16
If the critical angle for a medium is 45 degrees, the speed of light in it is

(a) 2.12 x 10^8 &nbsp; (b) 1.5 x 10^8 &nbsp; (c) 3 x 10^8 &nbsp;
(d) 2.5 x 10^8 m s^-1

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
             1           1
   mu  =  -------  =  --------  =  sqrt(2)  =  1.414
            sin C      sin 45

            c         3 x 10^8
   v  =  -------  =  -----------  =  2.12 x 10^8  m s^-1
            mu          1.414
```

---

### Q17
Total internal reflection can occur when light travels from

(a) air to glass &nbsp; (b) glass to air &nbsp; (c) air to water
(d) vacuum to glass

**ANSWER: (b)** — it needs **denser to rarer**, and glass is denser than air.

---

### Q18
As the refractive index of a medium increases, its critical angle

(a) increases &nbsp; (b) decreases &nbsp; (c) stays the same
(d) first increases then decreases

**ANSWER: (b)** — since `sin C = 1/mu`, a larger mu gives a smaller C.
That is exactly why a diamond (mu = 2.42, C = 24 deg) sparkles so much.

---

### Q19
A luminous point source is at a depth h below the surface of a liquid of
refractive index mu. The radius of the bright circle seen on the surface is

(a) `h tan C` &nbsp; (b) `h sin C` &nbsp; (c) `h / tan C` &nbsp; (d) `h mu`

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Light escapes only within the cone of half-angle C :

                        h                            h
   r  =  h tan C  =  ------------- ,  since  tan C = ----------
                      sqrt(mu^2-1)                    sqrt(mu^2 - 1)

   so also   r  =  h / sqrt( mu^2 - 1 )
```
> **SHORTCUT:** memorise `r = h / sqrt(mu^2 - 1)`. It appears every few years.

# PATTERN 5 — Lens maker's formula and focal length

### Q20
A biconvex lens has both radii 20 cm and is made of glass with mu = 1.5.
Its focal length is

(a) 10 cm &nbsp; (b) 20 cm &nbsp; (c) 30 cm &nbsp; (d) 40 cm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
    1                 +-  1       1  -+              2         1
   ---  =  ( 1.5 - 1) | ----  -  ---- |  =  0.5  x  ----  =  ----
    f                 +- 20      -20 -+              20        20

            f  =  +20 cm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** for a **biconvex lens with equal radii R** and mu = 1.5,
> `f = R` exactly. Recognise it and skip the algebra.

---

### Q21
A plano-convex lens of radius 15 cm is made of glass of mu = 1.5. Its focal
length is

(a) 15 cm &nbsp; (b) 30 cm &nbsp; (c) 45 cm &nbsp; (d) 7.5 cm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   R1 = +15 , R2 = infinity

    1                 +-  1        1     -+              1         1
   ---  =  ( 1.5 - 1) | ----  -  ------- |  =  0.5  x  -----  =  -----
    f                 +- 15      infinity-+             15         30

            f  =  30 cm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** for a plano-convex lens with mu = 1.5, `f = 2R`.

---

### Q22
A convex lens of focal length f in air is immersed in a liquid of the same
refractive index as the lens. Its new focal length is

(a) f &nbsp; (b) 2f &nbsp; (c) zero &nbsp; (d) infinite

**ANSWER: (d)** — `(mu(rel) - 1) = 0`, so `1/f = 0` and **f is infinite**.
The lens becomes invisible and stops bending light.

---

### Q23
A convex lens is cut into two halves along the principal axis. The focal length
of each half is

(a) f/2 &nbsp; (b) f &nbsp; (c) 2f &nbsp; (d) 4f

**ANSWER: (b)** — cutting **along** the axis does not change R1 or R2, so **f
is unchanged**. (Cutting **perpendicular** to the axis would give `2f`.)

---

# PATTERN 6 — Thin lens formula and magnification

### Q24
An object is 30 cm from a convex lens of focal length 20 cm. The image distance
is

(a) 60 cm &nbsp; (b) 12 cm &nbsp; (c) -60 cm &nbsp; (d) 15 cm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   u = -30 , f = +20

    1      1      1        1        1        3 - 2        1
   ---  = ---  + ---  =  -----  + ------  = -------  =  -----
    v      f      u       20      (-30)       60          60

            v  =  +60 cm       (real image, on the far side)
```

---

### Q25
A concave lens of focal length 20 cm forms an image of an object placed 30 cm
away. The image is

(a) real, 12 cm away &nbsp; (b) virtual, 12 cm away
(c) virtual, 60 cm away &nbsp; (d) real, 60 cm away

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   u = -30 , f = -20

    1      1      1        1         1        -3 - 2        -5
   ---  = ---  + ---  =  ------ + ------  =  --------  =  ------
    v      f      u       (-20)   (-30)         60          60

            v  =  -12 cm      (negative -> virtual, same side)
```
> **SHORTCUT:** a concave lens NEVER makes a real image of a real object.
> If two options say "real", cross them out before you calculate.

---

### Q26
The distance between an object and the real image formed by a convex lens of
focal length f is minimum when the object is at

(a) f &nbsp; (b) 2f &nbsp; (c) 3f &nbsp; (d) 4f

**ANSWER: (b)** — the minimum object-to-image distance for a convex lens is
`4f`, and it occurs when the object is at `2f` (so the image is also at `2f`).

# PATTERN 7 — Power and combination of lenses

### Q27
A lens has a power of -2.5 D. Its focal length is

(a) +40 cm &nbsp; (b) -40 cm &nbsp; (c) -25 cm &nbsp; (d) +25 cm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
          1          1
   f  =  ---  =  --------  =  -0.4 m  =  -40 cm      (a CONCAVE lens)
          P       (-2.5)
```

---

### Q28
Two lenses of powers +4 D and -2 D are in contact. The focal length of the
combination is

(a) 50 cm &nbsp; (b) 25 cm &nbsp; (c) 100 cm &nbsp; (d) 33.3 cm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   P  =  4  -  2  =  +2 D          ->      f  =  1/2  =  0.5 m  =  50 cm
```
> **SHORTCUT:** when powers are given, just ADD them. Never convert to focal
> lengths first — that wastes 30 seconds.

---

### Q29
Two thin lenses of focal lengths 20 cm and 30 cm are separated by 10 cm.
The equivalent focal length is

(a) 12 cm &nbsp; (b) 15 cm &nbsp; (c) 60 cm &nbsp; (d) 10 cm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
    1      1      1        d          1       1        10
   ---  = ----  + ---- - -------  =  ----- + ----- - --------
    F      f1      f2     f1 f2       20      30      20 x 30

           3  +  2         10        5         1        4  -  1        1
      =  ----------  -  -------  =  ----  -  ----  =  ----------  =  ----
             60            600       60       60          60           15

            F  =  15 cm
```

# PATTERN 8 — Prism and dispersion

### Q30
The angle of a prism is 60 degrees and the angle of minimum deviation is
30 degrees. The refractive index is

(a) 1.33 &nbsp; (b) 1.41 &nbsp; (c) 1.50 &nbsp; (d) 1.73

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
           sin ( (60 + 30)/2 )       sin 45        0.7071
   mu  =  ---------------------  =  --------  =  ----------  =  1.414
                sin ( 60/2 )         sin 30         0.5
```
> **SHORTCUT:** memorise the standard trio:
> A = 60, Dm = 30  ->  mu = sqrt(2) = 1.414
> A = 60, Dm = 60  ->  mu = sqrt(3) = 1.732
> A = 60, Dm = 40  ->  mu = 1.53

---

### Q31
For a prism of angle A, the minimum deviation is also A. The refractive index is

(a) `sin A` &nbsp; (b) `2 cos(A/2)` &nbsp; (c) `2 sin(A/2)` &nbsp;
(d) `cos(A/2)`

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
           sin ( (A + A)/2 )       sin A        2 sin(A/2) cos(A/2)
   mu  =  -------------------  =  --------  =  ---------------------
              sin ( A/2 )          sin(A/2)          sin(A/2)

       =  2 cos ( A / 2 )
```

---

### Q32
A thin prism of angle 5 degrees is made of glass of mu = 1.6. The deviation
produced is

(a) 2 deg &nbsp; (b) 3 deg &nbsp; (c) 4 deg &nbsp; (d) 8 deg

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   D  =  ( mu - 1 ) A  =  ( 1.6 - 1 ) x 5  =  0.6 x 5  =  3 degrees
```
> **SHORTCUT:** whenever A is 10 degrees or less, use `D = (mu - 1) A`.
> Do NOT go looking for the full prism formula.

---

### Q33
For a certain glass, mu(violet) = 1.66 and mu(red) = 1.62. The dispersive power
of a prism made from it, if mu(mean) = 1.64, is

(a) 0.0625 &nbsp; (b) 0.04 &nbsp; (c) 0.625 &nbsp; (d) 0.024

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
              mu(v) - mu(r)        1.66 - 1.62         0.04
   omega  =  ---------------  =  ---------------  =  --------  =  0.0625
                mu(y) - 1          1.64 - 1            0.64
```
> **SHORTCUT:** dispersive power does NOT contain A. If an option involves the
> prism angle, it is wrong.

---

### Q34
The maximum angle of a prism for a ray to be able to emerge from the second
face is

(a) C &nbsp; (b) 2C &nbsp; (c) C/2 &nbsp; (d) 90 - C

**ANSWER: (b)** — since `A = r1 + r2` and each of r1, r2 cannot exceed the
critical angle C without total internal reflection, `A <= 2C`.

# PATTERN 9 — Scattering

### Q35
According to Rayleigh's law, the intensity of scattered light is proportional to

(a) lambda &nbsp; (b) 1/lambda &nbsp; (c) 1/lambda^2 &nbsp; (d) 1/lambda^4

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   Blue (about 450 nm) versus red (about 650 nm):

      I(blue)      +- 650 -+ 4
      --------  =  | ----- |    ~=  4.4      (blue scatters far more)
      I(red)       +- 450 -+
```

---

### Q36
The sky appears black to an astronaut because

(a) there is no sunlight &nbsp; (b) there is no atmosphere to scatter light
(c) the temperature is very low &nbsp; (d) of total internal reflection

**ANSWER: (b)** — no air molecules means no scattering, so no light reaches
the eye except directly from the Sun and the stars.

# PATTERN 10 — Optical instruments

### Q37
A magnifying glass has a focal length of 10 cm. Its magnifying power when the
image is formed at the near point (D = 25 cm) is

(a) 2.5 &nbsp; (b) 3.5 &nbsp; (c) 1.5 &nbsp; (d) 25

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
              D            25
   m  =  1 + ---  =  1 +  -----  =  1  +  2.5  =  3.5
              f            10
```
> **SHORTCUT:** image at the near point -> `1 + D/f`.
> Image at infinity -> `D/f`. The near-point value is always 1 more.

---

### Q38
An astronomical telescope has fo = 60 cm and fe = 5 cm. Its magnifying power
in normal adjustment and its tube length are

(a) 12 , 65 cm &nbsp; (b) 12 , 55 cm &nbsp; (c) 300 , 65 cm &nbsp;
(d) 65 , 12 cm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
          fo       60
   M  =  ----  =  ----  =  12            L  =  fo + fe  =  60 + 5  =  65 cm
          fe        5
```

---

### Q39
In a compound microscope, to increase the magnifying power one should

(a) increase both fo and fe &nbsp; (b) decrease both fo and fe
(c) increase fo and decrease fe &nbsp; (d) decrease fo and increase fe

**ANSWER: (b)** — since `M ~= (L/fo)(D/fe)`, both focal lengths appear in the
denominator, so **both should be small**.

---

### Q40
The final image formed by a compound microscope is

(a) real, erect &nbsp; (b) real, inverted &nbsp; (c) virtual, erect
(d) virtual, inverted

**ANSWER: (d)** — the objective inverts once and the eyepiece (acting as a
simple microscope) does not invert again, so the final image is
**virtual and inverted**.

---

### Q41
The magnifying power of an astronomical telescope when the final image is at
the near point is

(a) fo/fe &nbsp; (b) `(fo/fe)(1 + fe/D)` &nbsp; (c) `(fo/fe)(1 + D/fe)`
(d) `fo fe / D`

**ANSWER: (b)**

# PATTERN 11 — The eye and its defects

### Q42
A person cannot see beyond 2 m. The power of the lens required is

(a) +0.5 D &nbsp; (b) -0.5 D &nbsp; (c) +2 D &nbsp; (d) -2 D

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   MYOPIA:  f  =  - (far point)  =  -2 m

              1        1
   P  =     -----  =  ----  =  -0.5 D          (CONCAVE lens)
              f        -2
```
> **SHORTCUT:** myopia -> answer is always NEGATIVE.
> Hypermetropia -> answer is always POSITIVE. That alone kills half the
> options before you calculate.

---

### Q43
A person's near point is 100 cm. The power of the lens needed to read at 25 cm
is

(a) +2 D &nbsp; (b) +3 D &nbsp; (c) +4 D &nbsp; (d) +1 D

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   HYPERMETROPIA:   u = -25 cm , v = -100 cm

    1     1     1          1          1         -1  +  4         3
   --- - --- = ---  ->  -------- - -------  =  ----------  =  --------
    v     u     f        (-100)     (-25)          100          100

            f  =  100/3  cm  =  0.333 m

              1         1
   P  =     -----  =  -------  =  +3 D           (CONVEX lens)
              f        0.333

   Quick formula check:   f = D N / (N - D) = 25 x 100 / 75 = 33.3 cm  OK
```

---

### Q44
Astigmatism is corrected using a

(a) convex lens &nbsp; (b) concave lens &nbsp; (c) cylindrical lens
(d) bifocal lens

**ANSWER: (c)**

# SPEED RULES FOR THE EXAM HALL

```
  +====================================+==================================+
  |  WHEN YOU SEE THIS                 |  DO THIS IMMEDIATELY             |
  +====================================+==================================+
  |  Any mirror numerical              |  1/v + 1/u = 1/f , u NEGATIVE    |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Any lens numerical                |  1/v - 1/u = 1/f , u NEGATIVE    |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Concave lens or convex mirror     |  the answer MUST be virtual,     |
  |                                    |  erect, diminished. Cross out    |
  |                                    |  every "real" option first.      |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  "angle with the surface"          |  subtract from 90 first          |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Two inclined mirrors              |  N = 360/a , even -> N-1         |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Powers of lenses given            |  just ADD them, do not convert   |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Biconvex, equal radii, mu = 1.5   |  f  =  R      (one second)       |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Plano-convex, mu = 1.5            |  f  =  2R     (one second)       |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Lens cut ALONG the axis           |  f unchanged                     |
  |  Lens cut ACROSS the axis          |  f doubles                       |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Lens in a liquid of the same mu   |  f = infinity, lens invisible    |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Prism angle 10 deg or less        |  D = ( mu - 1 ) A                |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  "symmetric passage" / "i = e"     |  it is MINIMUM DEVIATION         |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  A = 60 , Dm = 30                  |  mu = sqrt(2) = 1.414            |
  |  A = 60 , Dm = 60                  |  mu = sqrt(3) = 1.732            |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Dispersive power                  |  no A in the answer              |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Wavelength in a medium            |  divide by mu (frequency fixed)  |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Stacked liquid layers             |  add  t / mu  for each layer     |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Critical angle                    |  sin C = 1 / mu                  |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Bright circle on a liquid surface |  r = h / sqrt( mu^2 - 1 )        |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Magnifier at the near point       |  m = 1 + D/f                     |
  |  Magnifier, relaxed eye            |  m = D/f                         |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Telescope, normal adjustment      |  M = fo/fe , L = fo + fe         |
  +------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
  |  Myopia                            |  answer is NEGATIVE power        |
  |  Hypermetropia                     |  answer is POSITIVE power        |
  +====================================+==================================+
```

**In EAPCET there is no negative marking. If a Ray Optics question is taking
more than 60 seconds, mark the most likely option using the table above and
move on. Never leave one blank.**
