## Topic: Reflection and mirrors

### Q A1
State the laws of reflection of light.

**ANSWER:**
```
  LAW 1   The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.
                            i  =  r

  LAW 2   The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the
          point of incidence all lie in the SAME PLANE.
```

---

### Q A2
Two plane mirrors are inclined at an angle of 60 degrees. How many images of a
point object placed between them are formed?

**ANSWER:**
```
         360      360
   N  =  ----- =  -----  =  6       (an EVEN number)
           a       60

   n  =  N - 1  =  5 images
```

---

### Q A3
What is the focal length of a plane mirror? What is its magnification?

**ANSWER:** A plane mirror has an **infinite** focal length (R is infinite, and
f = R/2). Its magnification is **+1** — the image is the same size, erect and
virtual.

---

### Q A4
Why is a convex mirror used as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?

**ANSWER:** A convex mirror always forms an **erect, virtual, diminished**
image for any object position, so it gives a much **wider field of view** than
a plane mirror of the same size.

---

### Q A5
A concave mirror of focal length 10 cm forms an image at the same position as
the object. Where is the object?

**ANSWER:** At the **centre of curvature**, i.e. at a distance
`R = 2f = 20 cm` from the mirror. There the image is real, inverted and of the
same size.

### Q A9
A pond appears shallower than it really is. Why?

**ANSWER:** Because of **refraction**. Light from the bottom bends away from
the normal as it leaves the water, so the rays reaching the eye appear to come
from a point nearer the surface.
`apparent depth = real depth / mu`

## Topic: Total internal reflection

### Q A11
Define the critical angle. Write its relation with the refractive index.

**ANSWER:** The **critical angle C** is the angle of incidence in the denser
medium for which the angle of refraction in the rarer medium is 90 degrees.
```
                  1                        1
   sin C  =    ------          mu  =   ---------
                 mu                     sin C
```

---

### Q A12
State the conditions for total internal reflection.

**ANSWER:**
```
   1.  Light must travel from a DENSER medium to a RARER medium.
   2.  The angle of incidence must be GREATER than the critical angle.
```

---

### Q A13
Why does a diamond sparkle?

**ANSWER:** Diamond has a very high refractive index (2.42), so its critical
angle is only about 24 degrees. Light entering a cut diamond strikes the inner
faces at angles greater than 24 degrees and undergoes **total internal
reflection** many times before emerging, producing the sparkle.

---

### Q A14
What is an optical fibre? On what principle does it work?

**ANSWER:** A very thin thread of glass with a high-index **core** surrounded
by a lower-index **cladding**. Light entering one end strikes the core-cladding
boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle and is guided along the
fibre by repeated **total internal reflection**.

---

### Q A15
Give one advantage of a totally reflecting prism over a plane mirror.

**ANSWER:** A totally reflecting prism reflects **100 %** of the light, whereas
a silvered mirror absorbs some of it. Also, the silvering of a mirror can peel
off with time, but the prism does not deteriorate.

### Q A19
What happens to the focal length of a glass lens when it is immersed in a
liquid of the same refractive index?

**ANSWER:** The relative refractive index becomes 1, so `(mu - 1) = 0` and
`1/f = 0`. The focal length becomes **infinite** — the lens stops converging or
diverging, and it becomes **invisible** in the liquid.

### Q A20
A concave lens always forms what kind of image?

**ANSWER:** Always a **virtual, erect, diminished** image, formed between the
focus and the optical centre, on the same side as the object — for every
position of the object.

### Q A21

**ANSWER:**

   ANGULAR DISPERSION  =  the difference in deviation between the
                          violet and the red rays.
        theta  =  D(v) - D(r)  =  ( mu(v) - mu(r) ) A

   DISPERSIVE POWER    =  angular dispersion / mean deviation

                            mu(v)  -  mu(r)
        omega    =      ----------------------      (no units)
                              mu(y)  -  1
```

### Q A22
State the conditions for minimum deviation in a prism.

**ANSWER:**
```
   1.  The angle of incidence equals the angle of emergence   (i = e)
   2.  r1  =  r2  =  A / 2
   3.  The refracted ray inside the prism is PARALLEL to the base
```

---

### Q A23
Why does the sky appear blue?

**ANSWER:** Air molecules scatter light according to Rayleigh's law, where the
scattered intensity is proportional to `1 / lambda^4`. Blue light has a much
shorter wavelength than red, so it is scattered far more strongly and reaches
our eyes from all parts of the sky.

---

### Q A24
Why does the Sun appear red at sunrise and sunset?

**ANSWER:** At sunrise and sunset the sunlight travels through a much longer
thickness of atmosphere. Nearly all the short-wavelength blue and violet light
is scattered away, and only the long-wavelength **red and orange** light
reaches the observer.

## Topic: Optical instruments and the eye

### Q A25
What is the least distance of distinct vision for a normal human eye?

**ANSWER:** **25 cm** (denoted by D). The far point of a normal eye is at
infinity.

---

### Q A26
Define the magnifying power of a telescope. Write the expression in normal
adjustment.

**ANSWER:** The ratio of the angle subtended at the eye by the final image to
the angle subtended by the object at the unaided eye.
```
          fo
   M  =  -----          and the tube length   L  =  fo  +  fe
          fe
```

---

### Q A27
Name the defect of vision in which a person cannot see distant objects clearly.
How is it corrected?

**ANSWER:** **Myopia** (short-sightedness). The image forms in front of the
retina. It is corrected using a **concave (diverging) lens** of negative power.

---

### Q A28
What kind of lens is used to correct hypermetropia, and why?

**ANSWER:** A **convex (converging) lens**. In hypermetropia the image of a
near object would form behind the retina; a convex lens converges the rays
before they enter the eye so that the image falls on the retina.

### Q B1
Derive the relation `f = R / 2` for a concave mirror.

**ANSWER:**
```
   DIAGRAM

        parallel ray                                        )  M
     ---------------------------------------------------__--)
                                        theta      __--     )
                                              __--          )
     ---------C-------------------F------__-------------------)  P
                             __-- theta                     )
                       __--                                 )

   1.  A ray parallel to the principal axis strikes the mirror at M.
       MC is a radius, so it is the NORMAL at M.
       Let the angle of incidence be theta.

   2.  The parallel ray and the axis CP are parallel and MC is a
       transversal, so    angle MCP  =  theta   (alternate angles).

   3.  By the law of reflection the angle of reflection is also theta,
       so in triangle MCF:      angle FMC  =  angle FCM  =  theta.

   4.  A triangle with two equal angles is isosceles, therefore

                                FM  =  FC

   5.  For PARAXIAL rays, M lies close to P, so   FM  ~=  FP.

   6.  Hence     FP  =  FC ,  so F is the midpoint of C and P.

                          PC          R
                   PF  =  ----   ->  f = ---
                            2             2

   VALID ONLY for paraxial rays / small aperture.
```

### Q B5
Explain total internal reflection and derive the relation between the critical
angle and the refractive index. Give two applications.

**ANSWER:**
```
   DIAGRAM
                        AIR (rarer)
                                    r = 90                NO refraction
                     \        \    ____/                       ^
                      \        \___/                           |
   ====O===============O========O============O===================
      /|\             /|\      /|\          /|\           .    |
     / | \           / | \    / | \        / | \        .      |
    /i < C\         / i<C \  /  i=C \     / i > C `  . '  reflected
                                                        back into glass
                        GLASS (denser)

   DEFINITION
   When light travels from a denser to a rarer medium and the angle of
   incidence exceeds a certain value called the CRITICAL ANGLE, the light
   is completely reflected back into the denser medium. This is TOTAL
   INTERNAL REFLECTION.

   DERIVATION
   Applying Snell's law at the critical angle (r = 90 deg):

           mu (denser)  sin C   =   mu (rarer)  sin 90

   Taking the rarer medium as air ( mu = 1 ) and sin 90 = 1 :

                                             1
           mu  sin C  =  1    ->    sin C = ----
                                             mu

                              +------------------+
                              |          1       |
                              |   mu = -------   |
                              |         sin C    |
                              +------------------+

   CONDITIONS
   1.  denser -> rarer
   2.  i > C

   APPLICATIONS
   1.  OPTICAL FIBRE — the core has a higher refractive index than the
       cladding, so light bounces along the fibre with no loss and
       carries telephone and internet signals over long distances.
   2.  TOTALLY REFLECTING PRISM — a 45-90-45 glass prism turns light
       through 90 or 180 degrees with 100 % reflection; used in
       binoculars and periscopes.
   (Also acceptable: mirage, sparkle of a diamond, endoscopy.)
```

### Q B11
Derive the relation `A + D = i + e` for a prism.

**ANSWER:**

                             /\
                            /  \
                     N     /    \     N'

### Q B13
Explain dispersion by a prism and define dispersive power.

**ANSWER:**
```
   DIAGRAM
                        /\
                       /  \
      WHITE LIGHT     /    \
     ------------>---/------\------  RED    (least deviated)
                     \      /\----   ORANGE
                      \    /  \---   YELLOW
                       \  /    \--   GREEN
                        \/      \-   BLUE
                                 \   INDIGO
                                  \  VIOLET (most deviated)

   DISPERSION is the splitting of white light into its constituent
   colours when it passes through a prism. It happens because the
   refractive index of the prism material is DIFFERENT for different
   colours: mu is largest for violet and smallest for red, so violet
   is deviated most and red least.

   For a thin prism:      D(v) = ( mu(v) - 1 ) A
                          D(r) = ( mu(r) - 1 ) A

   ANGULAR DISPERSION
             theta  =  D(v) - D(r)  =  ( mu(v) - mu(r) ) A

   MEAN DEVIATION
             D(y)   =  ( mu(y) - 1 ) A

   DISPERSIVE POWER
                       angular dispersion       mu(v)  -  mu(r)
             omega  = ---------------------  = -------------------
                         mean deviation           mu(y)  -  1

   omega has NO units and depends ONLY on the material of the prism,
   not on the refracting angle A.
```

### Q B14
Explain why the sky appears blue and why the setting Sun appears red.

**ANSWER:**
```
   RAYLEIGH'S LAW OF SCATTERING

           intensity of scattered light  is proportional to  1 / lambda^4

   BLUE SKY
   The molecules of the atmosphere are much smaller than the wavelength
   of visible light, so Rayleigh scattering applies. Blue light has a
   short wavelength, so it is scattered roughly 16 times more strongly
   than red light. This scattered blue light comes to our eyes from every
   direction, so the sky looks blue.

   RED SUNSET / SUNRISE
   At sunrise and sunset the sunlight has to travel through a much
   greater thickness of atmosphere before reaching us. Almost all the
   short-wavelength blue and violet light is scattered out of the beam
   along the way. Only the long wavelengths — red and orange — survive
   the journey, so the Sun and the sky around it look red.

   (Related: the sky is black for an astronaut because there is no
    atmosphere to scatter light.)
```

        ue     ve     fe        (-25)     6.25         25            25

                ue  =  -5 cm

   STEP 2  vo  =  L  -  | ue |  =  15  -  5  =  10 cm

   STEP 3  Find uo for the objective.   vo = +10 cm , fo = +2 cm

        1      1      1        1       1        1  -  5        -4
       ---  = ---- - ----  =  ----- - -----  = ---------  =  -------
        uo     vo     fo       10       2          10           10

                uo  =  -2.5 cm

   STEP 4  Magnifying power

                vo   +-       D  -+       10    +-      25   -+
        M  =   ---- | 1  +  ----- |  =  ------- | 1  +  ----- |
                uo   +-       fe -+     (-2.5)  +-      6.25 -+

            =  ( -4 ) ( 1 + 4 )  =  -20

        The magnitude of the magnifying power is 20.
        (The minus sign only means the final image is inverted.)
```

### Q C1
Derive the expression for refraction at a single spherical surface. Hence
obtain the relation for a convex refracting surface separating two media of
refractive indices mu1 and mu2. Solve one numerical based on it.

**ANSWER:**
```
   DIAGRAM

                mu(1)              |          mu(2)
              (rarer)              |         (denser)
                                  N|
                              __--'|\  r
                        __--'      | \
                  __--'  i         |  \
      O---------------------+------+---+---------C--------I------
       \      alpha         P      gamma   \     beta    /
        \                                   \           /
         object                              refracted ray

     O = point object on the axis        I = image
     P = pole of the surface             C = centre of curvature
     N = point where the ray meets the surface
     CN is the NORMAL at N (a radius is normal to the sphere)
     R = PC        u = PO (negative)      v = PI (positive)

   ASSUMPTIONS:  the aperture is small and the rays are PARAXIAL,
                 so all angles are small and tan(x) ~= x.

   STEP 1   In triangle ONC, the exterior angle equals the sum of the
            two interior opposite angles:

                        i  =  alpha  +  gamma                 ... (1)

   STEP 2   In triangle INC, similarly:

                        gamma  =  r  +  beta
                    ->  r  =  gamma  -  beta                  ... (2)

   STEP 3   With h = height of N above the axis, and using tan x ~= x :

                          h                 h                h
            alpha  =  --------      beta = -----    gamma = -----
                        (-u)                 v                R

   STEP 4   Snell's law at N, using sin x ~= x for small angles:

                        mu(1)  i   =   mu(2)  r

            Substitute (1) and (2):

                mu(1) ( alpha + gamma )  =  mu(2) ( gamma - beta )

   STEP 5   Substitute the expressions from STEP 3:

                    +-  h        h  -+           +-  h       h  -+
            mu(1) x |  ----  +  ---  |  =  mu(2) | ---  -  ---   |
                    +- (-u)      R  -+           +-  R       v  -+

            Cancel h throughout:

                mu(1)     mu(1)        mu(2)     mu(2)
              -  -----  +  -----   =    -----  -  -----
                  u          R            R         v

   STEP 6   Rearranging:

              +====================================================+
              |     mu(2)      mu(1)        mu(2)  -  mu(1)        |
              |     ------  -  ------   =   -----------------      |
              |       v          u                  R              |
              +====================================================+

   NOTE:  the SAME formula covers a concave surface, a virtual object and
          a virtual image — just put in the correct signs.

   NUMERICAL
   A point object is 30 cm in front of a convex glass surface of radius
   10 cm. mu(glass) = 1.5, mu(air) = 1. Find the image.

        mu1 = 1 , mu2 = 1.5 , u = -30 cm , R = +10 cm

        1.5        1        1.5 - 1          1.5        1        1
        ----  -  ------  =  --------   ->    ----  =  ---- -  ------
         v        (-30)        10             v        20       30

        1.5       3  -  2        1
        ----  =  ---------  =  -----      ->    v  =  1.5 x 60  =  +90 cm
         v          60          60

   ANSWER: a REAL image 90 cm inside the glass.
```

### Q C2
Derive the **lens maker's formula** for a thin lens. State the assumptions
made. Hence obtain the thin lens formula.

**ANSWER:**
```
   DIAGRAM

              surface 1                surface 2
              radius R1                radius R2
                    | |
                    | |
                    | |
       O -----------|-|-------------> I1
       object       | |                 \
                    | |                  \-------> I  (final image)
                    | |
             mu(1)   mu(2)   mu(1)
             (air)   (glass) (air)

      u  = object distance          v1 = image distance for surface 1
      v  = final image distance

   ASSUMPTIONS
   1.  The lens is THIN — its thickness is negligible, so both surfaces
       are treated as having the same pole, the optical centre.
   2.  The rays are PARAXIAL.
   3.  The object is a POINT object on the principal axis.
   4.  The lens is in AIR.

   STEP 1   REFRACTION AT THE FIRST SURFACE
            Light goes from mu(1) into mu(2). The image is formed at I1.
            Using the single-surface formula:

                mu(2)      mu(1)         mu(2)  -  mu(1)
               -------  -  -------  =  --------------------      ... (1)
                  v1          u                 R1

   STEP 2   REFRACTION AT THE SECOND SURFACE
            I1 now acts as the OBJECT for surface 2. Light goes from
            mu(2) back into mu(1):

                mu(1)      mu(2)         mu(1)  -  mu(2)
               -------  -  -------  =  --------------------      ... (2)
                  v          v1                 R2

   STEP 3   ADD equations (1) and (2). The v1 terms cancel:

              mu(1)     mu(1)      mu(2) - mu(1)     mu(1) - mu(2)
             -------  - -------  = --------------  + --------------
                v          u             R1                R2

                                                    +-  1       1  -+
                                =  ( mu(2) - mu(1) )| ----  -  ---- |
                                                    +-  R1      R2 -+

   STEP 4   Divide throughout by mu(1) and write
            mu = mu(2) / mu(1) = refractive index of the lens material
            with respect to the surrounding medium:

                1        1                     +-   1        1   -+
               ---  -   ---   =   ( mu - 1 )   |  -----  -  ---- |   ...(3)
                v        u                     +-   R1       R2  -+

   STEP 5   If the object is at INFINITY ( u = infinity ), the image is
            formed at the principal focus ( v = f ). Then

              +=================================================+
              |    1                   +-   1        1     -+   |
              |   ---   =  ( mu - 1 )  |  -----  -  -----   |   |
              |    f                   +-   R1       R2    -+   |
              +=================================================+

            This is the LENS MAKER'S FORMULA.

   STEP 6   Substituting this back into equation (3):

                          +--------------------------+
                          |    1       1       1     |
                          |   ---  -  ---  =  ---    |
                          |    v       u       f     |
                          +--------------------------+

            This is the THIN LENS FORMULA.

   CONSEQUENCES WORTH ONE MARK EACH
   *  If mu(lens) = mu(surrounding liquid), then (mu - 1) = 0, so
      f = infinity: the lens stops working and becomes invisible.
   *  A convex lens has a LONGER focal length in water than in air.
   *  For a biconvex lens R1 = +R and R2 = -R, giving
      1/f = 2 (mu - 1) / R.
```

### Q C3
Draw the ray diagram of a **compound microscope** and derive an expression for
its magnifying power when the final image is formed (i) at the near point and
(ii) at infinity. Also write the length of the tube.

**ANSWER:**
```
   RAY DIAGRAM

        OBJECTIVE                        EYEPIECE
          /\                               /\
         /  \                             /  \
    B   |    |                           |    |
     \  |    |                           |    |
      \ |    |                           |    |
  -----\|----|-----+----------------+----|----|------+------------ eye
   A    |\   |     |       A'       |    |    |      |
        | \  |     |       +--------|----|----|------+------>
        |  \ |     |       |        |    |   \|
        |   \|     |       |        |    |    |\
        |    \     |       |        |    |    | \
        |     \    |       |        |    |    |  \
        |      \   |       |        |     \  /    \
        |       \  |       |        |      \/      \
        |        \ +-------+ B'     |               \
        |          (real, inverted, |                \
        |           magnified image |                 \
        |           formed by the   |                  \
        |           objective)      |                   \
        |                                                B''
        |                                       FINAL IMAGE:
        |                                       VIRTUAL, INVERTED,
        |                                       HIGHLY MAGNIFIED
        |<-- uo -->|<--------- vo ------->|<-- ue -->|

                    |<---------- L = vo + ue ------------>|

   CONSTRUCTION
   Objective:  a convex lens of VERY SHORT focal length fo and SMALL
               aperture, placed near the object.
   Eyepiece:   a convex lens of short focal length fe (but fe > fo),
               placed near the eye. Both are mounted at the two ends of
               a tube whose length can be adjusted.

   WORKING
   1.  The object AB is placed just BEYOND the focus Fo of the objective.
   2.  The objective forms a REAL, INVERTED, MAGNIFIED image A'B'
       inside the tube.
   3.  The tube length is adjusted so that A'B' falls just INSIDE the
       focus Fe of the eyepiece.
   4.  The eyepiece then acts as a SIMPLE MICROSCOPE and forms a
       VIRTUAL, MAGNIFIED image A''B''. Since the objective has already
       inverted the image once, the final image is INVERTED with
       respect to the object.

   MAGNIFYING POWER

        M   =   m(objective)  x  m(eyepiece)

                       vo
        m(objective) = ------
                       uo

   CASE (i)  FINAL IMAGE AT THE NEAR POINT ( D = 25 cm )

        For the eyepiece acting as a simple microscope with the image
        at D :
                                         D
                       m(eyepiece)  = 1 + -----
                                          fe

              +=================================================+
              |               vo    +-          D   -+          |
              |      M   =   ----   | 1   +   -----  |          |
              |               uo    +-          fe  -+          |
              +=================================================+

   CASE (ii)  FINAL IMAGE AT INFINITY (normal adjustment, relaxed eye)

        A'B' is placed exactly AT the focus of the eyepiece, so

                                        D
                       m(eyepiece)  =  -----
                                        fe

              +=================================================+
              |               vo          D                     |
              |      M   =   ----   x   -----                   |
              |               uo          fe                    |
              +=================================================+

   USEFUL APPROXIMATION
        When the tube is long, vo ~= L and uo ~= fo , so

                      L         D
             M   ~=  ----  x  -----
                      fo        fe

   LENGTH OF THE TUBE
             L   =   vo  +  ue          (final image at D)
             L   =   vo  +  fe          (final image at infinity)

   HOW TO INCREASE THE MAGNIFICATION
        Make fo SMALL and fe SMALL.
```

### Q C4
Draw the ray diagram of an **astronomical telescope** in normal adjustment and
derive an expression for its magnifying power. Write the length of the tube.
How does it differ from a compound microscope?

**ANSWER:**
```
   RAY DIAGRAM — NORMAL ADJUSTMENT (final image at infinity)

     parallel rays        OBJECTIVE                   EYEPIECE
     from a distant         /\                          /\
     object                /  \                        /  \
   ==================>    |    |                      |    |
                          |    |                      |    |    to
   -----------------------|    |----------+-----------|    |----------> the
      alpha               |    |     Fo   |  Fe       |    |     beta   eye
   ==================>    |    |          |           |    |
                           \  /        A' |            \  /
                            \/            |  B'         \/
                                     (real, inverted,
                                      diminished image at
                                      the COMMON focus)

          |<------------- fo ------------>|<---- fe ---->|
          |<---------------- L = fo + fe ---------------->|

   CONSTRUCTION
   Objective:  a convex lens of LARGE focal length fo and LARGE aperture,
               facing the distant object.
   Eyepiece:   a convex lens of SMALL focal length fe, near the eye.

   WORKING
   1.  The object is so far away that the rays reaching the objective
       are effectively PARALLEL.
   2.  The objective brings them to a focus and forms a REAL, INVERTED,
       DIMINISHED image A'B' at its focal plane Fo.
   3.  In normal adjustment the tube is set so that A'B' lies exactly at
       the focus Fe of the eyepiece.
   4.  The eyepiece therefore sends out a PARALLEL beam, so the final
       image is at INFINITY and the eye is fully relaxed.
       The final image is VIRTUAL and INVERTED.

   DERIVATION OF THE MAGNIFYING POWER

        Magnifying power  =  angle subtended at the eye by the FINAL image
                             -------------------------------------------
                             angle subtended at the unaided eye by the
                             OBJECT

                     beta
        M   =   -----------
                    alpha

        For small angles, taking the height of A'B' as h :

                          h                          h
        alpha   ~=      ------          beta  ~=   ------
                          fo                         fe

                     beta          h / fe          fo
        M   =   -----------  =  -----------  =   ------
                    alpha         h / fo           fe

              +=================================================+
              |                      fo                         |
              |            M    =   ------                      |
              |                      fe                         |
              |                                                 |
              |            L    =   fo   +   fe                 |
              +=================================================+

        (A minus sign is often written, M = - fo/fe , to show that the
         final image is INVERTED.)

   CASE — FINAL IMAGE AT THE NEAR POINT D

              +=================================================+
              |             fo    +-          fe  -+            |
              |    M  =   ------  | 1   +   -----  |            |
              |             fe    +-          D   -+            |
              |                                                 |
              |    L  =   fo   +   ue                           |
              +=================================================+

   HOW TO IMPROVE A TELESCOPE
   *  fo LARGE and fe SMALL  ->  greater magnifying power.
   *  a LARGE objective aperture  ->  more light gathered, brighter image
      and better resolving power.

   DIFFERENCE FROM A COMPOUND MICROSCOPE

   +----------------------+---------------------------+--------------------+
   |                      |  COMPOUND MICROSCOPE      |  TELESCOPE         |
   +----------------------+---------------------------+--------------------+
   |  Used for            |  very SMALL nearby objects|  very FAR objects  |
   |  Objective f (fo)    |  VERY SMALL               |  VERY LARGE        |
   |  Objective aperture  |  small                    |  LARGE             |
   |  Object position     |  just beyond Fo           |  at infinity       |
   |  Magnifying power    |  (vo/uo)(1 + D/fe)        |  fo / fe           |
   |  Tube length         |  vo + ue                  |  fo + fe           |
   +----------------------+---------------------------+--------------------+

   NUMERICAL
   fo = 150 cm , fe = 5 cm , normal adjustment.

        M  =  150 / 5  =  30            L  =  150 + 5  =  155 cm
```

### Q C5
Derive the prism formula `mu = sin((A + Dm)/2) / sin(A/2)`. Draw the graph
between the angle of incidence and the angle of deviation, and state the
conditions for minimum deviation.

**ANSWER:**
```
   RAY DIAGRAM

                              A
                             /\
                            /  \
                     N     /    \     N'
                      \   / r1 r2\   /
                        \/        \ /
             i          /\   D    /\      e
      ------>----------/--\------/--\------->
                      /    \    /    \
                     /      \  /      \
                    /        \/        \
                   B ------------------- C

   PART 1 — THE TWO BASIC RELATIONS

   In the quadrilateral formed by A, the two normals and the point O
   inside the prism, the two normals meet the faces at 90 degrees:

                 A  +  angle NON'  =  180 deg               ... (1)

   In triangle NON' :

                 r1 + r2 + angle NON'  =  180 deg           ... (2)

   From (1) and (2):
                         A  =  r1  +  r2                    ... (3)

   Deviation at the first face  = i - r1
   Deviation at the second face = e - r2

                 D  =  (i - r1) + (e - r2)
                    =  (i + e) - (r1 + r2)
                    =  (i + e) - A

                         A  +  D  =  i  +  e                ... (4)

   PART 2 — THE  i - D  GRAPH

     D
     ^
     |  \                                     /
     |   \                                   /
     |    \                                 /
     |     \___                         ___/
     |         \____               ____/
  Dm |..............\_____________/
     |                     :
     |                     :
     +---------------------+----------------------------> i
                          i = e

   *  As i is increased from a small value, D first DECREASES, reaches
      a MINIMUM value Dm, and then INCREASES again.
   *  For every value of D except Dm there are TWO angles of incidence
      that give that same deviation (i and e can be interchanged).
   *  At the minimum there is only ONE angle of incidence, which means
      i and e must be EQUAL there.

   PART 3 — CONDITIONS FOR MINIMUM DEVIATION

        1.  i  =  e
        2.  r1  =  r2  =  A / 2
        3.  the refracted ray inside the prism is PARALLEL to the base
        4.  D  =  Dm

   PART 4 — DERIVATION OF THE PRISM FORMULA

   At minimum deviation, put i = e and r1 = r2 = r in (3) and (4):

        From (3):        A  =  2 r        ->        r  =  A / 2

                                                          A  +  Dm
        From (4):        A  +  Dm  =  2 i    ->     i  =  ----------
                                                              2

   Applying Snell's law at the first face:

                     sin i
             mu  =  -------
                     sin r

        +===============================================================+
        |                          +-   A   +   Dm  -+                  |
        |                    sin   |  --------------  |                 |
        |                          +-        2       -+                 |
        |          mu    =    --------------------------------          |
        |                              +-    A   -+                     |
        |                        sin   |  ------  |                     |
        |                              +-    2   -+                     |
        +===============================================================+

   PART 5 — THIN PRISM SPECIAL CASE

   If A is small then Dm is also small, and sin x ~= x :

                  ( A + Dm ) / 2          A  +  Dm
        mu   =   -----------------   =   ----------
                       A / 2                  A

        ->        Dm   =   ( mu  -  1 )  A

   NUMERICAL
   A = 60 deg , Dm = 30 deg :

              sin ( (60 + 30)/2 )       sin 45      0.7071
        mu = ---------------------  =  --------  = --------  =  1.414
                  sin ( 60/2 )          sin 30       0.5
```
