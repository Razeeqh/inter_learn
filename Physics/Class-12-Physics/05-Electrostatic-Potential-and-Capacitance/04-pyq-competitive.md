# PATTERN 1 — Potential due to point charges

### Q1
The electric potential at a distance of 20 cm from a charge of 4 x 10^-7 C is

(a) 9000 V &nbsp; (b) 18000 V &nbsp; (c) 900 V &nbsp; (d) 1800 V

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
        k q       ( 9 x 10^9 )( 4 x 10^-7 )        3.6 x 10^3
  V  =  -----  =  --------------------------  =  --------------  =  18000 V
          r                  0.20                     0.20
```

---

### Q2
Two charges +q and -q are placed at a distance 2a apart. The potential at the
midpoint of the line joining them is

(a) zero &nbsp; (b) kq/a &nbsp; (c) 2kq/a &nbsp; (d) kq/2a

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  V  =  k(+q)/a  +  k(-q)/a  =  0

  The potential is zero but the FIELD there is 2kq/a^2 , not zero.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Potential is a SCALAR. Equal and opposite charges at equal
> distances always give V = 0. No vectors, no components, no thinking.

---

### Q3
Four charges each +q are placed at the corners of a square of side a. The
potential at the centre is

(a) zero &nbsp; (b) 4kq/a &nbsp; (c) 4 sqrt(2) kq / a &nbsp; (d) 2kq/a

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Distance from any corner to the centre  =  a / sqrt(2)

              k q            4 k q sqrt(2)      4 sqrt(2) k q
  V  =  4 x ----------  =  ---------------  =  ---------------
            a / sqrt(2)          a                    a
```

---

### Q4
The work done in moving a charge of 5 C from a point at 10 V to a point at 60 V
is

(a) 50 J &nbsp; (b) 250 J &nbsp; (c) 300 J &nbsp; (d) 350 J

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  W  =  q ( V2 - V1 )  =  5 ( 60 - 10 )  =  5 x 50  =  250 J
```

---

### Q5
A charge is moved from one point to another on an equipotential surface. The
work done is

(a) maximum &nbsp; (b) minimum &nbsp; (c) zero &nbsp; (d) infinite

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  The potential difference is zero, so W = q x 0 = 0.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** The words "equipotential surface" and "work done" in the same
> sentence always give the answer ZERO. Do not compute anything.

# PATTERN 2 — Dipole potential and dipole energy

### Q6
The electric potential at a point on the equatorial line of a short dipole is

(a) kp/r^2 &nbsp; (b) 2kp/r^3 &nbsp; (c) zero &nbsp; (d) kp/r

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  V = k p cos theta / r^2 , and theta = 90 degrees on the equatorial line,
  so cos 90 = 0 and V = 0.
```

---

### Q7
The potential due to a short dipole varies with distance as

(a) 1/r &nbsp; (b) 1/r^2 &nbsp; (c) 1/r^3 &nbsp; (d) 1/r^4

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  +---------------------+------------+------------+
  |                     |     V      |     E      |
  +---------------------+------------+------------+
  |  POINT CHARGE       |    1/r     |   1/r^2    |
  |  DIPOLE             |   1/r^2    |   1/r^3    |
  +---------------------+------------+------------+
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Memorise that little table. It answers a whole family of
> questions instantly. E is always one power steeper than V.

---

### Q8
A dipole of moment p is placed in a uniform field E. The work done in rotating
it from the stable position to the unstable position is

(a) pE &nbsp; (b) 2pE &nbsp; (c) pE/2 &nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  W  =  p E ( cos 0 - cos 180 )  =  p E ( 1 + 1 )  =  2 p E
```

---

### Q9
An electric dipole is in stable equilibrium when the angle between p and E is

(a) 0 degrees &nbsp; (b) 90 degrees &nbsp; (c) 180 degrees &nbsp; (d) 45 degrees

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  U = -pE cos theta is MINIMUM ( = -pE ) at theta = 0, which is the
  stable position.
```

---

### Q10
The torque acting on a dipole placed parallel to a uniform field is

(a) pE &nbsp; (b) 2pE &nbsp; (c) zero &nbsp; (d) pE/2

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  tau = p E sin theta , and sin 0 = 0.
```

# PATTERN 3 — Relation between E and V

### Q11
The potential in a region varies as V = 6x - 8xy - 8y + 6yz volt. The magnitude
of the electric field at the origin is

(a) 8 V/m &nbsp; (b) 10 V/m &nbsp; (c) 6 V/m &nbsp; (d) 2 sqrt(35) V/m

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Ex = -dV/dx = -( 6 - 8y )         at origin  =  -6
  Ey = -dV/dy = -( -8x - 8 + 6z )   at origin  =  +8
  Ez = -dV/dz = -( 6y )             at origin  =   0

  E  =  sqrt( 36 + 64 )  =  sqrt(100)  =  10 V/m
```

---

### Q12
Two parallel plates 2 cm apart have a potential difference of 100 V. The field
between them is

(a) 50 V/m &nbsp; (b) 200 V/m &nbsp; (c) 5000 V/m &nbsp; (d) 2 V/m

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
        V         100
  E  =  ---  =  --------  =  5000 V m^-1
        d         0.02
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Convert cm to m FIRST, before touching the calculator.
> Option (c) and option (a) differ only by that conversion — that is exactly
> the trap being set.

---

### Q13
In a certain region the electric potential is constant. The electric field
there is

(a) constant and non-zero &nbsp; (b) zero &nbsp; (c) increasing &nbsp; (d) infinite

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  E = -dV/dr .  V constant means dV/dr = 0 , so E = 0.
```

---

### Q14
The SI unit V m^-1 is equivalent to

(a) N C^-1 &nbsp; (b) J C^-1 &nbsp; (c) C N^-1 &nbsp; (d) N m^-1

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   V / m  =  ( J / C ) / m  =  ( N m / C ) / m  =  N / C
```

# PATTERN 4 — Capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor

### Q15
The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is doubled when

(a) the area is halved &nbsp; (b) the separation is doubled
(c) the separation is halved &nbsp; (d) the charge is doubled

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  C = e0 A / d .  Halving d doubles C.
  Note that C does NOT depend on the charge at all, so (d) is wrong.
```

---

### Q16
A parallel plate capacitor of capacitance C has its plate separation increased
to three times and the region filled with a dielectric of kappa = 6. The new
capacitance is

(a) 2C &nbsp; (b) 18C &nbsp; (c) C/2 &nbsp; (d) 3C

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
        kappa e0 A        6           e0 A
  C' = ------------  =  ----- x  ( --------- )  =  2 C
           3 d             3            d
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Never plug numbers into e0 A / d. Just multiply C by every
> factor that changed: x6 for kappa, /3 for d, giving x2.

---

### Q17
A capacitor of 5 pF has air between its plates. If the plates are pulled to
twice the original separation and mica (kappa = 6) is introduced, the new
capacitance is

(a) 15 pF &nbsp; (b) 30 pF &nbsp; (c) 60 pF &nbsp; (d) 2.5 pF

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  C'  =  5 x 6 / 2  =  15 pF
```

---

### Q18
The capacitance of an isolated conducting sphere of radius R is

(a) 4 pi e0 R &nbsp; (b) 4 pi e0 / R &nbsp; (c) e0 R &nbsp; (d) 4 pi e0 R^2

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
              Q            Q
   C  =  ---------  =  -----------  =  4 pi e0 R
              V         k Q / R
```

### Q19
A metal slab of thickness t is inserted between the plates of a parallel plate
capacitor of separation d. The new capacitance is

(a) e0A/d &nbsp; (b) e0A/(d-t) &nbsp; (c) e0A/(d+t) &nbsp; (d) e0At/d

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  A metal has kappa = infinity, so t/kappa = 0 in the slab formula:

               e0 A                        e0 A
  C  =  -----------------------   =    -----------
        ( d - t ) + t / kappa            d - t
```

# PATTERN 5 — Combination of capacitors

### Q20
Three capacitors of 2 uF, 4 uF and 4 uF are connected in series. The equivalent
capacitance is

(a) 10 uF &nbsp; (b) 1 uF &nbsp; (c) 2 uF &nbsp; (d) 0.5 uF

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   1       1      1      1        2 + 1 + 1        4
  ---  =  ---  + ---  + ---  =  ------------  =  -----  =  1
   C       2      4      4            4            4

   C  =  1 uF
```
> **SHORTCUT:** In series the answer must be SMALLER than the smallest
> capacitor (2 uF). Options (a) and (c) can be crossed out without any work.

---

### Q21
n identical capacitors each of capacitance C are connected first in series and
then in parallel. The ratio (parallel C) / (series C) is

(a) n &nbsp; (b) n^2 &nbsp; (c) 1/n &nbsp; (d) 1/n^2

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Parallel  =  n C          Series  =  C / n

  Ratio  =  ( n C ) / ( C / n )  =  n^2
```

---

### Q22
Two capacitors 3 uF and 6 uF are joined in series across 120 V. The voltage
across the 3 uF capacitor is

(a) 40 V &nbsp; (b) 60 V &nbsp; (c) 80 V &nbsp; (d) 120 V

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
             C2                 6
  V1  =  ---------- V   =   -------- x 120   =   80 V
          C1 + C2             3 + 6

  The SMALLER capacitor always takes the LARGER share of the voltage.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** In a series pair, the voltage divides in the INVERSE ratio of
> the capacitances. 3 : 6 capacitance means 2 : 1 voltage, so 80 V and 40 V.

---

### Q23
Two capacitors of 4 uF and 6 uF are connected in parallel to a 100 V supply.
The total charge stored is

(a) 1 mC &nbsp; (b) 0.24 mC &nbsp; (c) 2.4 mC &nbsp; (d) 10 mC

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  C  =  4 + 6  =  10 uF

  Q  =  C V  =  ( 10 x 10^-6 )( 100 )  =  1 x 10^-3 C  =  1 mC
```

---

### Q24
To obtain a capacitance of 1.5 uF from capacitors each of 1 uF, the minimum
number required is

(a) 2 &nbsp; (b) 3 &nbsp; (c) 4 &nbsp; (d) 6

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Two 1 uF in SERIES give 0.5 uF.
  Put a third 1 uF in PARALLEL with them:  0.5 + 1  =  1.5 uF

  Total used = 3.
```

# PATTERN 6 — Energy and energy density

### Q25
A capacitor of 10 uF is charged to 100 V. The energy stored is

(a) 0.05 J &nbsp; (b) 0.5 J &nbsp; (c) 5 J &nbsp; (d) 0.005 J

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  U  =  (1/2) C V^2  =  (1/2)( 10 x 10^-6 )( 10^4 )  =  5 x 10^-2  =  0.05 J
```

---

### Q26
A capacitor is charged by a battery and then the battery is REMOVED. A
dielectric slab of kappa = 2 is inserted. The stored energy becomes

(a) doubled &nbsp; (b) halved &nbsp; (c) unchanged &nbsp; (d) four times

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Battery removed  ->  Q is fixed  ->  use  U = Q^2 / ( 2 C )

  C doubles, so U halves.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** Battery REMOVED means Q is king: use U = Q^2/2C, so U falls.
> Battery CONNECTED means V is king: use U = (1/2)CV^2, so U rises.
> Everything else in these questions follows from that one line.

---

### Q27
The same capacitor, with the battery still CONNECTED, has a kappa = 2 slab
inserted. The stored energy becomes

(a) doubled &nbsp; (b) halved &nbsp; (c) unchanged &nbsp; (d) one quarter

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  Battery connected  ->  V is fixed  ->  use  U = (1/2) C V^2

  C doubles, so U doubles. The extra energy comes from the battery.
```

---

### Q28
The energy density in an electric field of 2 x 10^3 V/m in air is
(e0 = 8.85 x 10^-12)

(a) 1.77 x 10^-5 J/m^3 &nbsp; (b) 3.54 x 10^-5 J/m^3
(c) 1.77 x 10^-2 J/m^3 &nbsp; (d) 8.85 x 10^-6 J/m^3

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  u  =  (1/2) e0 E^2  =  (1/2)( 8.85 x 10^-12 )( 2 x 10^3 )^2

     =  (1/2)( 8.85 x 10^-12 )( 4 x 10^6 )

     =  (1/2)( 35.4 x 10^-6 )   =   1.77 x 10^-5  J m^-3
```

---

### Q29
A capacitor charged to V is connected to an identical uncharged capacitor. The
fraction of the original energy that is lost is

(a) 1/4 &nbsp; (b) 1/2 &nbsp; (c) 3/4 &nbsp; (d) 1

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  Common potential  =  V / 2       (identical capacitors, one uncharged)

  Before:   U  =  (1/2) C V^2

  After:    U' =  (1/2)( 2C )( V/2 )^2  =  (1/2) C V^2 / 2  =  U / 2

  HALF the energy is lost as heat.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** "Identical capacitor, one uncharged" always loses exactly
> HALF the energy, and the common potential is always V/2. Memorise the
> result and skip the algebra.

### Q30
Two capacitors C1 = 2 uF at 100 V and C2 = 3 uF at 50 V are joined
positive-to-positive. The common potential is

(a) 50 V &nbsp; (b) 70 V &nbsp; (c) 75 V &nbsp; (d) 100 V

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
        C1 V1 + C2 V2       ( 2 )( 100 ) + ( 3 )( 50 )       200 + 150
  V  = ---------------  =  ----------------------------  =  -----------
          C1 + C2                    2 + 3                       5

     =  350 / 5  =  70 V
```

# PATTERN 7 — Conductors, dielectrics and mixed concepts

### Q31
Inside a charged hollow metal sphere

(a) E = 0, V = 0 &nbsp; (b) E = 0, V is constant and non-zero
(c) E is constant, V = 0 &nbsp; (d) both E and V vary

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
  E = 0 inside a conductor.
  V stays constant at the SURFACE value  k Q / R  everywhere inside.
```
> **SHORTCUT:** This exact question appears almost every year. E = 0 does NOT
> mean V = 0.

---

### Q32
The dielectric constant of a conductor is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) zero &nbsp; (c) infinity &nbsp; (d) 6

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  kappa = E0 / E , and the field E inside a conductor is zero,
  so kappa is infinite.
```

---

### Q33
Which of the following is a polar molecule?

(a) O2 &nbsp; (b) CO2 &nbsp; (c) H2O &nbsp; (d) N2

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
  Water is bent in shape, so the centres of positive and negative charge
  do not coincide and it has a permanent dipole moment.
  O2, N2 and CO2 are symmetric and therefore non-polar.
```

### Q35
An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 200 V.
Its kinetic energy is

(a) 200 eV &nbsp; (b) 200 J &nbsp; (c) 100 eV &nbsp; (d) 400 eV

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
  KE  =  q V  =  e x 200 V  =  200 eV  =  200 x 1.6 x 10^-19 J

      =  3.2 x 10^-17  J
```
> **SHORTCUT:** A charge of e accelerated through V volts always gains
> exactly V electron-volts. No calculation is needed at all.
