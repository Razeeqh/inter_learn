# TOPIC 0 — WHAT QUESTION IS THIS CHAPTER ANSWERING?

By 1900 everyone knew two facts:

```
  FACT 1   Atoms contain ELECTRONS (J. J. Thomson, 1897, cathode rays).
           Electrons are negative and very light.

  FACT 2   A whole atom is ELECTRICALLY NEUTRAL.
```

So there must be an equal amount of **positive** charge somewhere inside.
The whole chapter is one long argument about **where that positive charge sits**
and **what the electrons are doing**.

There is also a second, stranger fact that nobody could explain:

```
  Heat up hydrogen gas and it glows.
  Pass that glow through a prism and you do NOT get a rainbow.
  You get a few SHARP COLOURED LINES with total darkness in between.

     Sunlight through a prism:   |||||||||||||||||||||||||||  (continuous)

     Hydrogen through a prism:   |     |   |  ||             (a LINE spectrum)
                                red  b-g  b  v
```

Any correct model of the atom must explain those lines. Thomson's could not.
Rutherford's could not. Bohr's could. That is the story.

# TOPIC 1 — THOMSON'S MODEL (THE "PLUM PUDDING")

## The idea

Thomson said: the positive charge is spread **uniformly** through a sphere of
radius about 10^-10 m (the size of the whole atom), like a jelly. The tiny
negative electrons are stuck inside it like seeds in a watermelon.

```
        THOMSON'S "PLUM PUDDING" / "WATERMELON" MODEL

              . - - - - - - - - - .
           .    +   +   +   +  (-)  .
         .   +   (-)  +   +   +   +   .
        .  +   +   +   (-)  +   +   +  .
        .    (-)  +   +   +   +  (-)   .
         .  +   +   +   +   (-)  +    .
           .   +   (-)  +   +   +   .
              ' - - - - - - - - - '

        +   = positive charge, SPREAD OUT like jelly
        (-) = electron, embedded inside

        Radius of the whole ball  =  10^-10 m  =  1 angstrom
```

Other names for it: the **plum pudding model**, the **watermelon model**,
the **uniform sphere model**.

## What it got right

- The atom is neutral overall.
- The atom is about 10^-10 m in size.
- Electrons vibrating about their positions could radiate light.

## Why it FAILED (this is the exam point)

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  If the positive charge is SPREAD OUT over the whole atom, then   |
  |  the electric field anywhere inside is WEAK.                      |
  |                                                                   |
  |  A fast, heavy alpha particle passing through would feel only a    |
  |  gentle push and would be deflected by a FRACTION OF A DEGREE.     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  It could NEVER be turned back through 180 degrees.                |
  |                                                                   |
  |  But Geiger and Marsden SAW alpha particles coming straight back.  |
  |  ==>  THOMSON'S MODEL IS WRONG.                                    |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Also, it could not explain the sharp line spectrum of hydrogen at all.

> **TRAP:** In a 2-mark question "Why did Thomson's model fail?" do NOT
> just write "because of the scattering experiment". Write the reason:
> *a uniformly spread positive charge produces too weak a field to cause
> large-angle scattering.*

# TOPIC 2 — THE GEIGER–MARSDEN ALPHA PARTICLE SCATTERING EXPERIMENT

This is the most important experiment in the chapter. Learn the apparatus,
the observations, and the conclusion drawn from **each** observation.

## What is an alpha particle?

```
  Alpha particle  =  a helium nucleus,  He2+
                  =  2 protons + 2 neutrons
                  charge  =  +2e  =  +3.2 x 10^-19 C
                  mass    =  4 u  =  6.64 x 10^-27 kg
                         (about 7300 times the mass of an electron)
```

Because it is so heavy compared with an electron, an electron can no more
deflect an alpha particle than a mosquito can deflect a bowling ball.
**So any deflection must come from the positive charge.** Remember that line.

## The apparatus

Geiger and Marsden did the experiment in 1911 under Rutherford's direction.

```
   THE ALPHA PARTICLE SCATTERING EXPERIMENT  (Geiger - Marsden, 1911)

                                                  ZnS screen bent
                                                  round in a circle
        LEAD BLOCK                                        .  .
       +---------+                                    .          .
       |  ///////|                                 .                .
       | / 214Bi |                              .   \                 .
       | ///////\|                            .      \  (few bend       .
       +---------\                           .        \   a lot)         .
                  \  narrow beam of          .         \                  .
                   \ alpha particles         .          \                 .
                    \                        .           \                .
    +----------------\-----------------------.------------\---------------.
    |                 \                      .   MOST GO STRAIGHT THROUGH .
    |   COLLIMATOR ->  ====== ========= ==========================>       .
    |                   (slits)          |   .                            .
    |                                    |   .          /                 .
    |                                    |   .         /                  .
    |                        GOLD FOIL ->|   .        /                   .
    |                        thickness   |   .       /                    .
    |                        2.1 x 10^-7 m   .      /                     .
    |                        (about 400  |   .     /                      .
    |                         atoms      |   .   /                        .
    |                         thick)     |   .  /                         .
    |     <---  1 in 8000 COMES BACK  ---|   . /                          .
    |          (deflected > 90 deg)      |    .                           .
    +------------------------------------+----.---------------------------.
                                                .                       .
             WHOLE THING INSIDE A VACUUM           .                  .
             CHAMBER (else air would scatter          .  .  .  .  .
             the alphas)
                                              ROTATING MICROSCOPE
                                              slides round the screen
                                              and COUNTS the flashes
                                              at each angle theta
```

Part list you should be able to name in an exam:

| Part | What it is | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Radioactive Bismuth-214 (or Radon-222) | Emits alpha particles of about 5.5 MeV |
| Lead block | Thick lead with a hole | Absorbs alphas going in all other directions |
| Collimator | Two lead slits | Makes a thin, parallel, narrow beam |
| Target | Gold foil, 2.1 x 10^-7 m thick | Gold is malleable, can be beaten super thin; high Z gives strong scattering |
| Detector | Zinc sulphide (ZnS) screen | Each alpha hitting it makes a tiny flash (scintillation) |
| Microscope | Rotatable, on a turntable | Counts flashes at each scattering angle theta |
| Vacuum | Whole apparatus evacuated | Air molecules would scatter the alphas and spoil the result |

## Why GOLD? (a favourite 2-mark question)

```
  1.  Gold is the MOST MALLEABLE metal - it can be beaten into a foil only a
      few hundred atoms thick, so an alpha suffers essentially ONE collision.
  2.  Gold has a HIGH atomic number (Z = 79), so the nuclear charge is large
      and the scattering is strong enough to measure.
  3.  Gold does not oxidise, so the foil stays pure.
```

## THE THREE OBSERVATIONS AND WHAT EACH ONE PROVES

This exact table is the 4-mark answer. Learn it as a table.

```
  +----+-------------------------------+--------------------------------------+
  | No |  OBSERVATION                  |  CONCLUSION DRAWN FROM IT            |
  +----+-------------------------------+--------------------------------------+
  | 1  | MOST alpha particles passed   | Most of the atom is EMPTY SPACE.     |
  |    | straight through, undeviated. |                                      |
  +----+-------------------------------+--------------------------------------+
  | 2  | A SMALL fraction (about 0.14  | There is a small, CONCENTRATED       |
  |    | %) were deflected through     | region of POSITIVE charge that       |
  |    | more than 1 degree.           | repels the positive alpha particle.  |
  +----+-------------------------------+--------------------------------------+
  | 3  | About 1 in 8000 was deflected | That region carries essentially the  |
  |    | through MORE THAN 90 degrees, | WHOLE MASS of the atom and is        |
  |    | a few came straight BACK      | extremely TINY. A head-on hit        |
  |    | (180 degrees).                | bounces the alpha straight back.     |
  +----+-------------------------------+--------------------------------------+
```

Rutherford's own famous sentence about observation 3:

> "It was almost as incredible as if you fired a 15-inch shell at a piece of
> tissue paper and it came back and hit you."

## Extra quantitative result

The number of alpha particles N scattered at an angle theta was found to obey

```
                     1
      N(theta)  proportional to  ---------------------
                                   sin^4 ( theta / 2 )
```

So the count drops off extremely fast as theta increases — which is exactly
what a point-charge Coulomb repulsion predicts. This agreement is the proof
that the scattering really is Coulomb repulsion by a point nucleus.

## The trajectory of an alpha particle

The path is a **hyperbola** (the nucleus sits at the focus).

```
        TRAJECTORIES FOR DIFFERENT IMPACT PARAMETERS

     alpha in                                          theta = small
     ---------------------------------------------->  ---------->
                                                        (b large)
     ---------------------------------------->  \
                                                  \      theta bigger
                                                    \--->
     ------------------------------->  \
                                          \
                                            \   theta = 90 deg
                                              |
                                              v
     ----------------------->  <---------------------  theta = 180 deg
                              N                        (b = 0, HEAD ON)
                          (nucleus)

     b = IMPACT PARAMETER = the perpendicular distance of the initial
         line of motion of the alpha from the CENTRE of the nucleus

              +----------------------------------------+
              |   b LARGE   ->  theta SMALL             |
              |   b SMALL   ->  theta LARGE             |
              |   b = 0     ->  theta = 180 deg         |
              |               (straight back)           |
              +----------------------------------------+
```

The formula (you should be able to quote it):

```
                      Z e^2  cot ( theta / 2 )
      b   =   ----------------------------------------
                    4 pi eps0  x  ( 1/2 m v^2 )


      where  K = (1/2) m v^2  is the kinetic energy of the alpha particle.
```

Check it makes sense: if theta = 180 deg, then theta/2 = 90 deg,
cot 90 = 0, so **b = 0**. Correct — a head-on hit comes straight back.
If theta is tiny, cot(theta/2) is huge, so b is huge. Also correct.

> **TRAP:** The impact parameter is measured from the **centre of the nucleus**,
> perpendicular to the **original direction** of the alpha — not to its final
> direction, and not to the surface of the atom.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 2.1

**Q.** An alpha particle is scattered through 90 degrees by a gold nucleus
(Z = 79). Its kinetic energy is 5.0 MeV. Find the impact parameter.

```
  K  =  5.0 MeV  =  5.0 x 1.6 x 10^-13 J  =  8.0 x 10^-13 J

  theta = 90 deg  ->  cot(45 deg) = 1

              (9 x 10^9) x 79 x (1.6 x 10^-19)^2 x 1
  b   =   ---------------------------------------------
                        8.0 x 10^-13

  Numerator = 9e9 x 79 x 2.56e-38
            = 9e9 x 2.0224e-36
            = 1.820 x 10^-26

  b   =   1.820e-26 / 8.0e-13   =   2.28 x 10^-14 m
```

**ANSWER: b = 2.28 x 10^-14 m (about 22.8 fermi).**
Note how small that is — you must aim within 20 fermi of a nucleus to bend
the alpha by even 90 degrees. That is why only 1 in 8000 does it.

# TOPIC 3 — DISTANCE OF CLOSEST APPROACH

## The idea in plain English

Fire the alpha **straight at** the nucleus (b = 0). Both are positive, so the
nucleus pushes back. The alpha slows down, stops for an instant, and is thrown
back. The distance from the nucleus at which it **momentarily stops** is called
the **distance of closest approach**, written r0 or d.

```
                   alpha slows ...     stops     ... thrown back
     ------------------>  --->  -->  ->  |  <-  <---  <---------
                                          |
     |<--------------- r0 ---------------->|
                                        (+)
                                      NUCLEUS
                                      charge Ze

     At the stopping point:   ALL kinetic energy has become
                              ELECTROSTATIC potential energy.
```

## The derivation (write exactly this in the exam)

```
  STEP 1   Initial energy, far from the nucleus:
              KE = K = (1/2) m v^2 ,      PE = 0

  STEP 2   At the distance of closest approach r0:
              KE = 0  (it has stopped)
              PE  =   1        q1 q2         1       (2e)(Z e)
                    ------- x  ------   =  ------- x -----------
                    4 pi eps0    r0        4 pi eps0      r0

              (alpha charge = 2e ,  nucleus charge = Ze)

  STEP 3   Conservation of energy:  initial KE = final PE

              1                  1        2 Z e^2
             --- m v^2   =   --------- x  ---------
              2              4 pi eps0        r0

  STEP 4   Rearrange:

     +--------------------------------------------------------------+
     |                                                              |
     |                 2 Z e^2                    4 Z e^2           |
     |     r0  =  --------------------    =   ------------------    |
     |             4 pi eps0  x  K            4 pi eps0 m v^2       |
     |                                                              |
     |     where  K = (1/2) m v^2   is the KE of the alpha particle |
     +--------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Two things to notice, both worth marks:

```
  r0  is INVERSELY proportional to the kinetic energy K.
      -> a FASTER alpha gets CLOSER.

  r0  is PROPORTIONAL to Z.
      -> a heavier nucleus stops it further out.
```

## Why this matters

r0 gives an **upper estimate of the size of the nucleus**. The alpha never
touched the nucleus (it stopped and turned around), so the nucleus must be
**smaller than r0**. That is how Rutherford first estimated the nuclear size
as about 10^-14 m, later refined to about 10^-15 m.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 3.1 — the standard board numerical

**Q.** An alpha particle of energy 7.7 MeV is fired head-on at a gold nucleus
(Z = 79). Find the distance of closest approach.

```
  Data:   Z = 79 ,   e = 1.6 x 10^-19 C ,  1/(4 pi eps0) = 9 x 10^9
          K = 7.7 MeV = 7.7 x 1.6 x 10^-13 J = 1.232 x 10^-12 J

              2 Z e^2
  r0  =  -----------------  x  ( 1 / 4 pi eps0 )
                 K

  Numerator:  2 x 79 x (1.6e-19)^2 x 9e9
            = 158 x 2.56e-38 x 9e9
            = 4.045e-36 x 9e9
            = 3.640 x 10^-26

  r0  =  3.640e-26 / 1.232e-12  =  2.95 x 10^-14 m
```

**ANSWER: r0 = 2.95 x 10^-14 m, about 3 x 10^-14 m (30 fermi).**

## WORKED EXAMPLE 3.2

**Q.** Repeat for a 5.5 MeV alpha particle on the same gold nucleus.
Comment on the change.

```
  K  =  5.5 x 1.6 x 10^-13  =  8.8 x 10^-13 J

  r0 =  3.640e-26 / 8.8e-13  =  4.14 x 10^-14 m
```

**ANSWER: r0 = 4.14 x 10^-14 m.**
The energy went **down** (7.7 -> 5.5 MeV) so the distance went **up**
(2.95 -> 4.14 x 10^-14 m). A slower alpha is stopped further out. Correct.

> **SPEED TRICK:** r0 x K = constant for the same target. So if you already
> know r0 for one energy, just scale: r0(new) = r0(old) x K(old)/K(new).
> Check: 2.95e-14 x (7.7/5.5) = 2.95e-14 x 1.4 = 4.13e-14. Matches.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 3.3

**Q.** A proton of 2 MeV is fired at a silver nucleus (Z = 47). Find r0.

```
  For a PROTON the charge is  +e , not +2e.  So the formula becomes

              Z e^2
  r0  =  ----------------  x  (1 / 4 pi eps0)          <-- note: no factor 2
               K

  K = 2 x 1.6e-13 = 3.2e-13 J

  Numerator = 47 x (1.6e-19)^2 x 9e9 = 47 x 2.56e-38 x 9e9
            = 1.2032e-36 x 9e9 = 1.083e-26

  r0 = 1.083e-26 / 3.2e-13 = 3.38 x 10^-14 m
```

**ANSWER: 3.38 x 10^-14 m.**

> **TRAP:** The "2" in `2 Z e^2` comes from the alpha particle's charge +2e.
> If the projectile is a proton or a deuteron, that 2 becomes 1.
> Read the question.

# TOPIC 4 — RUTHERFORD'S NUCLEAR MODEL OF THE ATOM

## The model

Putting the three conclusions together, Rutherford proposed (1911):

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  1.  The whole POSITIVE charge and nearly the whole MASS of the     |
  |      atom are concentrated in a very small central core called      |
  |      the NUCLEUS.                                                   |
  |                                                                     |
  |  2.  The nucleus is about 10^-15 m across; the atom is about        |
  |      10^-10 m across. The rest is EMPTY SPACE.                      |
  |                                                                     |
  |  3.  The electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular orbits,   |
  |      like planets round the Sun. Hence the name the                 |
  |      "PLANETARY MODEL".                                             |
  |                                                                     |
  |  4.  The electrostatic attraction between the nucleus and the       |
  |      electron supplies the necessary CENTRIPETAL FORCE.             |
  |                                                                     |
  |  5.  The number of electrons equals the number of protons, so the   |
  |      atom is neutral.                                               |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

```
             RUTHERFORD'S NUCLEAR (PLANETARY) MODEL

                     . - - - - - - - - - .
                  .                        .
                .          - - - - .         .
              .         .            .         .
             .        .      (+)       .        .   <- 10^-10 m
             .        .   NUCLEUS      .        .
             .         .   10^-15 m   .         .
              .    (e-)  ' - - - - - '           .
                .          \                   .
                  .          orbit           .
                     ' - - - - - - - - - '

        Nucleus : Atom  =  10^-15 : 10^-10  =  1 : 100000

        If the nucleus were a 1 cm marble at the centre of a stadium,
        the electrons would be at the outer wall, 1 km away.
        Everything in between is EMPTY.
```

## Size of the nucleus — the numbers to quote

```
  Size of the atom     ~  10^-10 m   =  1 angstrom
  Size of the nucleus  ~  10^-15 m   =  1 fermi

  Ratio of radii       =  10^-5
  Ratio of VOLUMES     =  (10^-5)^3  =  10^-15

  So the nucleus occupies only about  1 part in 10^15  of the atom's volume,
  yet it holds  99.9 %  of its mass.
  ==>  Nuclear density is enormous, about 10^17 kg/m^3.
```

## The centripetal condition (needed for the Bohr derivation)

An electron of charge -e going round a nucleus of charge +Ze in a circle of
radius r must have the Coulomb attraction acting as the centripetal force:

```
   +--------------------------------------------------------------+
   |         m v^2            1            Z e^2                   |
   |        -------   =   ---------  x   ---------                 |
   |           r          4 pi eps0         r^2                    |
   |                                                               |
   |   ==>                        Z e^2                            |
   |          m v^2   =   ---------------------                    |
   |                        4 pi eps0  x  r                        |
   +--------------------------------------------------------------+
```

From this, for hydrogen (Z = 1):

```
                    1        e^2                        e^2
   KINETIC   K  =  --- m v^2 = -----------------  =  --------------
                    2          8 pi eps0 r            8 pi eps0 r

                            - e^2
   POTENTIAL U  =  --------------------          (negative: attraction)
                     4 pi eps0 r

                                    - e^2
   TOTAL     E = K + U  =  ----------------------
                             8 pi eps0 r
```

Three facts that come out of this and are asked directly:

```
  +--------------------------------------------------+
  |     U  =  -2 K            (potential = -2 x KE)  |
  |     E  =  -K              (total = minus the KE) |
  |     E  =   U / 2          (total = half the PE)  |
  +--------------------------------------------------+
```

The total energy is **negative**, which means the electron is **bound**.
You must supply energy to pull it out. Good.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 4.1

**Q.** In a hydrogen atom the electron orbits at r = 0.53 x 10^-10 m.
Find its speed.

```
             e^2                            (1.6e-19)^2 x 9e9
  m v^2 = ------------- ,   so  v^2 = ------------------------------
          4 pi eps0 r                  9.1e-31 x 0.53e-10

  Numerator = 2.56e-38 x 9e9 = 2.304e-28
  Denominator = 9.1e-31 x 5.3e-11 = 4.823e-41

  v^2 = 2.304e-28 / 4.823e-41 = 4.777e12
  v   = 2.19 x 10^6 m/s
```

**ANSWER: v = 2.19 x 10^6 m/s** — about c/137, i.e. 0.73 % of the speed of
light. (Fast, but slow enough that we may ignore relativity.)

# TOPIC 5 — THE TWO FAILURES OF RUTHERFORD'S MODEL

This is a certain 4-mark question. Two drawbacks. Learn both with reasons.

## DRAWBACK 1 — THE STABILITY PROBLEM

```
  Classical electromagnetism (Maxwell) says:

      ANY ACCELERATED CHARGE RADIATES ELECTROMAGNETIC WAVES.

  An electron moving in a circle is ALWAYS accelerating
  (centripetal acceleration v^2 / r, direction always changing).

  So it MUST continuously radiate energy.

  Radiating -> losing energy -> total energy E becomes more negative
            -> since E = - e^2 / (8 pi eps0 r), a more negative E means
               a SMALLER r
            -> the orbit shrinks
            -> it shrinks faster and faster
            -> the electron SPIRALS INTO THE NUCLEUS.
```

```
      THE DEATH SPIRAL PREDICTED BY CLASSICAL PHYSICS

              start here
                  \
              . - -\- - - .
           .        \       .
         .    . - - -\- .     .
        .   .    . -\- .  .    .
        .  .   .   ((+))  .  .  .
        .   .    ' - - '   .    .
         .    ' - - - - '     .
           .               .
              ' - - - - '

        Calculated time to collapse:   about  10^-8  SECONDS

        But atoms are stable for BILLIONS of years.
        ==> Rutherford's model contradicts the existence of matter.
```

**How to state it in the exam:** *An orbiting electron is accelerated, so by
classical electromagnetic theory it must continuously radiate energy, spiral
inwards and fall into the nucleus in about 10^-8 s. Hence the atom would be
unstable, which contradicts observation.*

## DRAWBACK 2 — THE CONTINUOUS SPECTRUM PROBLEM

```
  As the electron spirals in, r decreases CONTINUOUSLY.

  Its frequency of revolution  f = v / (2 pi r)  therefore also changes
  CONTINUOUSLY, increasing all the way in.

  Classically, the radiated frequency equals the frequency of revolution.

  ==>  The atom should emit a CONTINUOUS spectrum, a smeared rainbow whose
       frequency rises as the electron falls in.

  But the OBSERVED hydrogen spectrum is a set of SHARP DISCRETE LINES.

      PREDICTED:   ||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||||   (continuous)
      OBSERVED:    |      |    |   ||                   (line spectrum)
```

## DRAWBACK 3 (mention it for a bonus mark)

The model says nothing about **how the electrons are arranged** — it cannot
explain the periodic table, chemical bonding, or the sizes of atoms.

> **TRAP:** Students write only "the electron falls into the nucleus".
> That is half the answer. You must ALSO write the spectrum drawback.
> 4-mark question = 2 marks for each drawback.

# TOPIC 6 — ATOMIC SPECTRA

## Emission spectrum vs absorption spectrum

```
  EMISSION LINE SPECTRUM
  ----------------------
  Excite a gas (heat it or pass a discharge). It emits light.
  Through a prism you see BRIGHT COLOURED LINES on a DARK background.

     source ==> [gas discharge tube] ==> [prism] ==>  |   |  |    ||
                                                     BRIGHT lines,
                                                     dark background


  ABSORPTION LINE SPECTRUM
  ------------------------
  Pass WHITE light through the COOL gas. The gas absorbs exactly the
  wavelengths it would have emitted. You see a continuous rainbow with
  DARK LINES missing from it.

     white light ==> [cool gas] ==> [prism] ==>  |||| |||| ||||||||
                                                     ^     ^
                                                  DARK lines at exactly
                                                  the same places
```

```
  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  KEY FACT:  The dark lines of the absorption spectrum fall at  |
  |  EXACTLY the same wavelengths as the bright lines of the       |
  |  emission spectrum of the same element.                        |
  |                                                                |
  |  Because of this, a line spectrum is a FINGERPRINT of the      |
  |  element. That is how we know what stars are made of.          |
  +---------------------------------------------------------------+
```

(The dark lines in the Sun's spectrum are called **Fraunhofer lines** — they
are made by cooler gas in the Sun's own atmosphere.)

## The hydrogen spectrum and the Rydberg formula

Hydrogen is the simplest atom, so its spectrum was measured first.
In 1885 Balmer found a formula for the visible lines by pure trial and error.
Rydberg generalised it:

```
  +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                 |
  |       1               1          1                              |
  |    --------  =   R ( ------  -  ------ )        n2  >  n1       |
  |     lambda            n1^2       n2^2                           |
  |                                                                 |
  |    R = RYDBERG CONSTANT = 1.097 x 10^7 per metre                |
  |                                                                 |
  |    n1 = the lower level (where the electron LANDS)              |
  |    n2 = the upper level (where the electron STARTS)             |
  |                                                                 |
  |    1 / lambda  is called the WAVE NUMBER, symbol nu-bar         |
  +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## THE FIVE SERIES — learn this table cold

```
  +-----------+------+-----------------+---------------+--------------------+
  |  SERIES   |  n1  |  n2 values      |  REGION       |  DISCOVERED / USE  |
  +-----------+------+-----------------+---------------+--------------------+
  |  LYMAN    |  1   |  2, 3, 4, ...   |  ULTRAVIOLET  |  1906              |
  |  BALMER   |  2   |  3, 4, 5, ...   |  VISIBLE      |  1885 (first found)|
  |  PASCHEN  |  3   |  4, 5, 6, ...   |  INFRARED     |  1908              |
  |  BRACKETT |  4   |  5, 6, 7, ...   |  INFRARED     |  1922              |
  |  PFUND    |  5   |  6, 7, 8, ...   |  FAR INFRARED |  1924              |
  +-----------+------+-----------------+---------------+--------------------+

  Only the BALMER series is VISIBLE to the human eye.
  Lyman is the ONLY ultraviolet one. Everything below Balmer is infrared.
```

```
      L  y  m  a  n        Lazy
      B  a  l  m  e  r     Boys
      P  a  s  c  h  e  n  Play
      B  r  a  c  k  e  t  Basket
      P  f  u  n  d        Ball

          "LAZY  BOYS  PLAY  BASKET  BALL"
             L     B     P     B      P        with n1 = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
```

And for the regions: **U V I I I** — "Ultraviolet, Visible, Infrared,
Infrared, Infrared". Only the first two are different; everything else is IR.

## Longest and shortest wavelength in a series

```
  In ANY series, n1 is FIXED. Only n2 changes.

  LONGEST wavelength  (= smallest energy jump = FIRST line of the series)
        ->  take  n2 = n1 + 1
        ->  this makes  (1/n1^2 - 1/n2^2)  as SMALL as possible
        ->  lambda as LARGE as possible

  SHORTEST wavelength  (= biggest energy jump = the SERIES LIMIT)
        ->  take  n2 = INFINITY , so 1/n2^2 = 0
        ->  1/lambda = R / n1^2
        ->  lambda(min) = n1^2 / R
```

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------+
  |            n1^2                                            |
  |  SERIES LIMIT:   lambda(shortest)  =  --------             |
  |                                          R                 |
  |                                                            |
  |  The series limit is where the lines CROWD TOGETHER and     |
  |  merge into a continuum. Beyond it the atom is IONISED.     |
  +------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## WORKED EXAMPLE 6.1 — longest and shortest in the Lyman series

**Q.** Find the longest and the shortest wavelength of the Lyman series.
Take R = 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1.

```
  Lyman:  n1 = 1

  LONGEST  ->  n2 = 2
      1 / lambda = R ( 1/1 - 1/4 ) = R x (3/4)
      lambda = 4 / (3 R) = 4 / (3 x 1.097e7) = 4 / 3.291e7
             = 1.215 x 10^-7 m  =  1215 angstrom  =  121.5 nm

  SHORTEST ->  n2 = infinity
      1 / lambda = R ( 1 - 0 ) = R
      lambda = 1 / R = 1 / 1.097e7 = 9.116 x 10^-8 m
             = 911.6 angstrom  =  91.2 nm
```

**ANSWER: longest 1215 A, shortest 911.6 A. Both in the ULTRAVIOLET.**

## WORKED EXAMPLE 6.2 — the H-alpha line

**Q.** Calculate the wavelength of the first line of the Balmer series.

```
  Balmer: n1 = 2 ;  first line means n2 = 3

  1 / lambda = R ( 1/4 - 1/9 ) = R ( 9 - 4 ) / 36 = R x 5 / 36

  lambda = 36 / (5 R) = 36 / (5 x 1.097e7) = 36 / 5.485e7

         = 6.563 x 10^-7 m  =  6563 angstrom  =  656.3 nm
```

**ANSWER: 6563 A — this is the RED line, called H-alpha.** It is the red
glow you see from a hydrogen discharge tube and from nebulae in space.

The four visible Balmer lines you should know:

| Line | Transition | Wavelength | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-alpha | 3 -> 2 | 6563 A | Red |
| H-beta | 4 -> 2 | 4861 A | Blue-green |
| H-gamma | 5 -> 2 | 4341 A | Blue-violet |
| H-delta | 6 -> 2 | 4102 A | Violet |
| Balmer limit | inf -> 2 | 3646 A | Ultraviolet (invisible) |

> **TRAP:** "First line of a series" means n2 = n1 + 1 and gives the
> LONGEST wavelength. "Series limit" or "last line" means n2 = infinity and
> gives the SHORTEST wavelength. Students mix these up constantly.

# TOPIC 7 — BOHR'S MODEL OF THE ATOM

Niels Bohr, 1913. He kept Rutherford's nucleus but **broke classical physics
on purpose** to make the atom stable and the spectrum discrete.

## THE THREE POSTULATES — learn word for word

```
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |  POSTULATE 1  (STATIONARY ORBITS)                                  |
  |  An electron in an atom can revolve in certain STABLE orbits       |
  |  WITHOUT emitting radiation, contrary to classical                 |
  |  electromagnetic theory. These are called STATIONARY STATES        |
  |  and each has a fixed, definite energy.                            |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |  POSTULATE 2  (QUANTISATION OF ANGULAR MOMENTUM)                   |
  |  Only those orbits are allowed for which the angular momentum      |
  |  of the electron is an INTEGRAL MULTIPLE of h / (2 pi):            |
  |                                                                    |
  |                                n h                                 |
  |                 m v r   =   ---------      n = 1, 2, 3, ...        |
  |                               2 pi                                 |
  |                                                                    |
  |  n is called the PRINCIPAL QUANTUM NUMBER.                         |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                    |
  |  POSTULATE 3  (FREQUENCY CONDITION / BOHR'S FREQUENCY RULE)        |
  |  Radiation is emitted ONLY when an electron JUMPS from a higher    |
  |  allowed orbit of energy E2 to a lower one of energy E1. The       |
  |  frequency of the emitted photon is given by                       |
  |                                                                    |
  |                 h nu   =   E2  -  E1                               |
  |                                                                    |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Analogy that helps:

```
  A ball rolling down a RAMP can be at any height -> continuous energies
      -> that is Rutherford's atom  -> continuous spectrum.

  A ball on a STAIRCASE can only be on step 1, 2, 3 ... -> discrete
      -> that is Bohr's atom -> it can only drop by fixed amounts
      -> so it can only emit fixed photon energies -> LINE SPECTRUM.

        E                                  E
        |    /                             |            ____ n=4
        |   /                              |       ____ n=3
        |  /                               |  ____ n=2
        | /                                |__ n=1
        |/______                           |____________
           RUTHERFORD                          BOHR
        (any energy allowed)             (only steps allowed)
```

# TOPIC 8 — THE BIG DERIVATION (radius, velocity, energy)

**This is the 8-mark long-answer question.** Write it in this exact order.
Do it for a general nucleus of charge +Ze, then put Z = 1 for hydrogen.

## PART A — RADIUS OF THE nth ORBIT

```
  STEP 1   Coulomb attraction provides the centripetal force:

              m v^2           1          Z e^2
             -------   =   --------- x  -------
                r          4 pi eps0     r^2

                                Z e^2
           ==>   m v^2  =  -----------------            ......(1)
                            4 pi eps0  r


  STEP 2   Bohr's second postulate:

                            n h                    n h
              m v r  =   --------   ==>   v  =  ----------      ......(2)
                           2 pi                  2 pi m r


  STEP 3   Put (2) into (1):

                    +-   n h    -+ 2          Z e^2
              m  x  | ---------- |     =   -------------
                    +- 2 pi m r -+          4 pi eps0 r

                 n^2 h^2                 Z e^2
              ------------------  =  -------------
               4 pi^2 m r^2            4 pi eps0 r

           Multiply both sides by r^2 and rearrange:

                 n^2 h^2                 Z e^2  r
              -------------      =    -------------
               4 pi^2 m                4 pi eps0


  STEP 4   Therefore

     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
     |                                                                 |
     |                  eps0  h^2      n^2            n^2              |
     |       r_n   =   ------------- x -----  =  a0 x -----            |
     |                   pi m e^2        Z              Z              |
     |                                                                 |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+


  STEP 5   Put in the numbers for hydrogen (Z = 1, n = 1):

              eps0 = 8.85e-12 ,  h = 6.63e-34 ,  m = 9.1e-31 ,  e = 1.6e-19

              a0 = 0.529 x 10^-10 m  =  0.529 angstrom
                   ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
                   called the BOHR RADIUS

     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
     |                          n^2                                    |
     |       r_n  =   0.529  x  -----   angstrom                       |
     |                            Z                                    |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+

           So   r  is  PROPORTIONAL to  n^2  and  INVERSELY to  Z.
```

## PART B — VELOCITY IN THE nth ORBIT

```
  From (2):   v = n h / (2 pi m r_n).   Substitute r_n from PART A:

                     n h            pi m e^2  Z
        v_n   =   ---------  x   ----------------
                   2 pi m          eps0 h^2 n^2

     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
     |                                                                 |
     |                   Z e^2                       Z                 |
     |       v_n   =   -----------   =   2.18 x 10^6 x ---  m/s        |
     |                  2 eps0 n h                     n               |
     |                                                                 |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+

        So   v  is  INVERSELY proportional to  n  and PROPORTIONAL to Z.

        For hydrogen, n = 1:   v = 2.18 x 10^6 m/s  =  c / 137

        The number 1/137 is called the FINE STRUCTURE CONSTANT, alpha:

                          e^2                 1
              alpha =  ------------  =  -----------
                       2 eps0 h c           137
```

## PART C — TOTAL ENERGY OF THE nth ORBIT

```
  STEP 1   KINETIC energy.  From (1),  m v^2 = Z e^2 / (4 pi eps0 r) , so

                  1              Z e^2
             K = --- m v^2 =  --------------
                  2            8 pi eps0 r


  STEP 2   POTENTIAL energy of charge -e at distance r from charge +Ze:

                      1        (Z e)(-e)          - Z e^2
             U  =  --------- x ----------  =  --------------
                   4 pi eps0        r          4 pi eps0 r


  STEP 3   TOTAL energy:

                              Z e^2          Z e^2
             E = K + U  =  ------------ -  ------------
                           8 pi eps0 r      4 pi eps0 r

                             Z e^2  -  2 Z e^2         - Z e^2
                        =   --------------------  =  ------------
                                8 pi eps0 r            8 pi eps0 r


  STEP 4   Substitute r = r_n = eps0 h^2 n^2 / (pi m e^2 Z):

                       - Z e^2         pi m e^2 Z
             E_n  =  ------------- x ----------------
                      8 pi eps0        eps0 h^2 n^2

     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
     |                                                                 |
     |                     m  Z^2  e^4              Z^2                |
     |       E_n   =   -  ----------------  =  -13.6 x -----   eV      |
     |                     8 eps0^2 n^2 h^2            n^2             |
     |                                                                 |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+


  STEP 5   Check the number for hydrogen, n = 1:

             E1 = - 13.6 eV = - 2.18 x 10^-18 J
```

## Why is the energy NEGATIVE? (a real 2-mark question)

```
  We CHOOSE the zero of potential energy to be the electron at
  INFINITE distance and at rest — i.e. a FREE electron.

  Inside the atom the electron is BOUND: the nucleus attracts it, so you
  must DO WORK ON IT to pull it out to infinity.

  Energy less than the free state  =>  energy LESS THAN ZERO  =>  NEGATIVE.

  The magnitude |E_n| is exactly the energy you must SUPPLY to free it.

     E = 0     ------------------------------  FREE electron (n = infinity)
                          |
                          |  you must supply 13.6 eV
                          v
     E = -13.6 eV  ------------------------    BOUND electron (n = 1)
```

Rule of thumb: **negative total energy = bound system.** Same reason a
satellite in orbit has negative total energy.

## The three energies together

```
  +------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                      |
  |    KINETIC     K_n  =  + 13.6 Z^2 / n^2   eV         |
  |    POTENTIAL   U_n  =  - 27.2 Z^2 / n^2   eV         |
  |    TOTAL       E_n  =  - 13.6 Z^2 / n^2   eV         |
  |                                                      |
  |         U = 2 E ,     K = - E ,     U = -2 K         |
  |                                                      |
  +------------------------------------------------------+
```

## Quick table for hydrogen (memorise the first four)

| n | Radius (A) | Energy (eV) | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.529 | -13.60 | Ground state |
| 2 | 2.12 | -3.40 | First excited state |
| 3 | 4.76 | -1.51 | Second excited state |
| 4 | 8.46 | -0.85 | Third excited state |
| 5 | 13.2 | -0.54 | |
| inf | infinity | 0 | Ionised / free |

> **TRAP:** n = 2 is the **FIRST** excited state, not the second.
> The ground state is n = 1. "kth excited state" means n = k + 1.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 8.1

**Q.** Find the radius and the energy of the third orbit of hydrogen.

```
  n = 3 , Z = 1

  r3 = 0.529 x 3^2 / 1 = 0.529 x 9 = 4.761 angstrom = 4.761 x 10^-10 m

  E3 = -13.6 / 3^2 = -13.6 / 9 = -1.51 eV
```

**ANSWER: r3 = 4.76 A, E3 = -1.51 eV.**

## WORKED EXAMPLE 8.2

**Q.** The energy of an electron in the nth orbit of hydrogen is -0.85 eV.
Find n and the radius of that orbit.

```
  -13.6 / n^2 = -0.85     ==>   n^2 = 13.6 / 0.85 = 16   ==>  n = 4

  r4 = 0.529 x 16 = 8.46 angstrom
```

**ANSWER: n = 4 (the third excited state), r = 8.46 A.**

## WORKED EXAMPLE 8.3

**Q.** Find the time period of revolution of the electron in the ground
state of hydrogen, and the equivalent electric current.

```
  r1 = 0.529e-10 m ,  v1 = 2.18e6 m/s

           2 pi r      2 x 3.14 x 0.529e-10        3.323e-10
  T   =   --------  =  ---------------------  =  -------------
             v                2.18e6                2.18e6

      =   1.52 x 10^-16 s

  Current  I = charge / time = e / T = 1.6e-19 / 1.52e-16
             = 1.05 x 10^-3 A  =  1.05 mA
```

**ANSWER: T = 1.52 x 10^-16 s ; I = 1.05 mA.**
(Useful scaling to remember: **T is proportional to n^3 / Z^2**.)

## Ionisation energy and excitation energy

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                  |
  |  IONISATION ENERGY                                                |
  |    = energy needed to remove the electron completely, from the    |
  |      GROUND STATE (n = 1) to n = infinity.                        |
  |    = 0 - (-13.6)  =  13.6 eV     for hydrogen                     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  IONISATION POTENTIAL                                             |
  |    = the same number expressed in VOLTS  =  13.6 V                |
  |                                                                   |
  |  EXCITATION ENERGY                                                |
  |    = energy needed to lift the electron from the ground state to  |
  |      some HIGHER level (not out of the atom).                     |
  |                                                                   |
  |      1st excitation energy  =  E2 - E1 = -3.40 -(-13.6) = 10.2 eV |
  |      2nd excitation energy  =  E3 - E1 = -1.51 -(-13.6) = 12.09 eV|
  |      3rd excitation energy  =  E4 - E1 = -0.85 -(-13.6) = 12.75 eV|
  |                                                                   |
  |  BINDING ENERGY of level n  =  + 13.6 Z^2 / n^2  eV               |
  |    (just the magnitude of E_n)                                    |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

> **TRAP:** Ionisation energy is measured **from the ground state**, not from
> wherever the electron happens to be. If the question says "from the first
> excited state", then it is 0 - (-3.4) = **3.4 eV**, not 13.6 eV.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 9.1

**Q.** What is the energy required to ionise a hydrogen atom that is already
in its second excited state?

```
  Second excited state  ->  n = 3   (remember: ground = n1, 1st ex = n2)

  E3 = -13.6 / 9 = -1.51 eV

  Energy needed = 0 - (-1.51) = 1.51 eV
```

**ANSWER: 1.51 eV.**

# TOPIC 10 — HOW BOHR EXPLAINS THE HYDROGEN SPECTRUM

Now the pay-off: derive the Rydberg formula from the model.

```
  STEP 1   The electron jumps from n2 down to n1. Postulate 3:

              h nu  =  E(n2) - E(n1)

  STEP 2   Put in  E_n = - m e^4 / (8 eps0^2 n^2 h^2)  with Z = 1:

                       m e^4     +-   1        1    -+
              h nu = ----------- |  ------ - ------  |
                     8 eps0^2 h^2 +-  n1^2     n2^2 -+

  STEP 3   Use  nu = c / lambda :

                1        m e^4      +-   1        1    -+
             -------- = ------------ |  ------ - ------  |
              lambda    8 eps0^2 h^3 c +- n1^2   n2^2  -+

  STEP 4   Compare with the EXPERIMENTAL Rydberg formula

                1              +-  1        1   -+
             -------- =   R  x |  ----- - -----   |
              lambda           +- n1^2    n2^2  -+

           and read off:

     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
     |                         m e^4                                   |
     |             R    =   ----------------                           |
     |                       8 eps0^2 h^3 c                            |
     |                                                                 |
     |    Numerically this gives  R = 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1                |
     |    which MATCHES the measured value.                            |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**That agreement — a constant measured by spectroscopists coming out of a
theory built from m, e, h, c and eps0 — is why Bohr's model was accepted.**

## The handy eV form

```
                              +-  1        1   -+
      Delta E (in eV)  =  13.6 |  ----- - -----  |  Z^2
                              +-  n1^2    n2^2 -+

                            12400
      lambda (in angstrom) = ---------------
                            Delta E (in eV)

      (the 12400 comes from  h c = 12400 eV.angstrom ;
       you may also see it as 1240 eV.nm)
```

## WORKED EXAMPLE 10.1

**Q.** Find the wavelength of the photon emitted when an electron in a
hydrogen atom jumps from n = 3 to n = 2.

```
  METHOD 1 (energy)
     E3 = -1.51 eV ,  E2 = -3.40 eV
     Delta E = -1.51 - (-3.40) = 1.89 eV
     lambda = 12400 / 1.89 = 6560 angstrom

  METHOD 2 (Rydberg)
     1/lambda = R (1/4 - 1/9) = 1.097e7 x 5/36 = 1.524e6
     lambda = 1 / 1.524e6 = 6.563e-7 m = 6563 angstrom
```

**ANSWER: about 6563 A (red).** Both methods agree — good.

## WORKED EXAMPLE 10.2

**Q.** A hydrogen atom in the ground state absorbs a photon and jumps to
n = 4. Find (a) the photon's energy, (b) its wavelength, (c) how many
different lines can appear when it comes back down.

```
  (a)  E4 - E1 = -0.85 - (-13.6) = 12.75 eV

  (b)  lambda = 12400 / 12.75 = 972.5 angstrom   (ultraviolet)

  (c)  Number of lines = n(n-1)/2 = 4 x 3 / 2 = 6

       Check by listing:  4->3, 4->2, 4->1, 3->2, 3->1, 2->1   = 6 lines
```

**ANSWER: 12.75 eV, 972.5 A, 6 spectral lines.**

## THE NUMBER OF SPECTRAL LINES

```
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |  If a gas of MANY atoms is excited to level n, the total number   |
  |  of DIFFERENT spectral lines observed is                          |
  |                                                                   |
  |                        n ( n - 1 )                                |
  |              N   =   ---------------                              |
  |                             2                                     |
  |                                                                   |
  |  If the atoms are excited from level n1 up to level n2, use       |
  |                                                                   |
  |              N  =  ( n2 - n1 )( n2 - n1 + 1 ) / 2                 |
  +------------------------------------------------------------------+

     n = 2  ->  1 line
     n = 3  ->  3 lines
     n = 4  ->  6 lines
     n = 5  -> 10 lines
     n = 6  -> 15 lines
```

Why? Because the electron can land on any lower level, and the count of
distinct pairs from n levels is just "n choose 2" = n(n-1)/2.

> **TRAP:** A **SINGLE** atom excited to n = 4 can only make **3** jumps at
> most on the way down (e.g. 4->3->2->1). The formula n(n-1)/2 = 6 is for a
> **sample containing many atoms**, where all routes happen somewhere.
> Read whether the question says "an atom" or "a gas / sample".

# TOPIC 11 — HYDROGEN-LIKE ATOMS (He+, Li2+, Be3+)

A **hydrogen-like** (or hydrogenic) ion is any atom stripped down to
**exactly one electron**. Bohr's model then works perfectly, with Z put back in.

```
  He+   :  Z = 2   (helium minus one electron)
  Li2+  :  Z = 3   (lithium minus two electrons)
  Be3+  :  Z = 4
```

```
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
  |                                                                      |
  |                     n^2                                              |
  |     r_n   =  0.529 -----  angstrom     ->  SMALLER for bigger Z      |
  |                      Z                                               |
  |                                                                      |
  |                         Z                                            |
  |     v_n   =  2.18e6 x -----  m/s       ->  FASTER for bigger Z       |
  |                         n                                            |
  |                                                                      |
  |                       Z^2                                            |
  |     E_n   =  -13.6 x ------  eV        ->  DEEPER for bigger Z       |
  |                       n^2                                            |
  |                                                                      |
  |      1              +-  1       1   -+                               |
  |   -------- =  R Z^2 | ----- - -----  |                               |
  |    lambda           +- n1^2   n2^2  -+                               |
  |                                                                      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Plain English: a bigger nuclear charge pulls the electron in **tighter**
(smaller orbit), makes it go **faster**, and holds it **more strongly**
(more negative energy).

| Ion | Z | Ground radius | Ground energy | Ionisation energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | 1 | 0.529 A | -13.6 eV | 13.6 eV |
| He+ | 2 | 0.265 A | -54.4 eV | 54.4 eV |
| Li2+ | 3 | 0.176 A | -122.4 eV | 122.4 eV |
| Be3+ | 4 | 0.132 A | -217.6 eV | 217.6 eV |

## WORKED EXAMPLE 11.1

**Q.** Find the energy and radius of the n = 2 orbit of Li2+ (Z = 3).

```
  E2 = -13.6 x Z^2 / n^2 = -13.6 x 9 / 4 = -30.6 eV

  r2 = 0.529 x n^2 / Z = 0.529 x 4 / 3 = 0.705 angstrom
```

**ANSWER: E = -30.6 eV, r = 0.705 A.**

## WORKED EXAMPLE 11.2 — the classic "identical energy" question

**Q.** Show that the n = 2 level of He+ has the same energy as the ground
state of hydrogen.

```
  He+ :  Z = 2 , n = 2  ->  E = -13.6 x (2^2)/(2^2) = -13.6 x 4/4 = -13.6 eV
  H   :  Z = 1 , n = 1  ->  E = -13.6 x 1/1        = -13.6 eV

  They are EQUAL.
```

**ANSWER: both -13.6 eV.** In general, level n of a Z-ion matches level
n/Z of hydrogen whenever n/Z is a whole number.

# TOPIC 12 — DE BROGLIE'S EXPLANATION OF BOHR'S SECOND POSTULATE

Bohr simply **assumed** `m v r = n h / 2 pi`. He had no reason for it.
In 1924 de Broglie gave the reason: **the electron is a WAVE.**

## The argument

```
  STEP 1   de Broglie: every particle has a wavelength

                 lambda  =  h / (m v)

  STEP 2   For the orbiting electron wave to survive, it must join up
           smoothly with itself after one full trip round the orbit.
           Otherwise successive turns cancel out by interference and
           the wave dies.

           That is exactly the condition for a STANDING WAVE on a
           closed loop:

               circumference  =  a WHOLE NUMBER of wavelengths

                    2 pi r   =   n lambda           n = 1, 2, 3, ...

  STEP 3   Substitute lambda = h / (m v):

                                  n h
                    2 pi r   =   -------
                                  m v

  STEP 4   Rearrange:

              +------------------------------------------+
              |                       n h                |
              |         m v r   =   --------             |
              |                      2 pi                |
              +------------------------------------------+

           which is EXACTLY Bohr's second postulate. QED.
```

## The picture — draw this, it is worth a mark on its own

```
    ALLOWED ORBIT  (n = 4, four whole wavelengths fit)

              _   _   _   _
            /  \_/ \_/ \_/  \
           |                 |         The wave meets itself
           |     ( + )       |         PERFECTLY after one trip.
           |                 |         Constructive interference.
            \  _   _   _   _/          STABLE  =>  ALLOWED ORBIT
              \_/ \_/ \_/ \_/


    FORBIDDEN ORBIT  (not a whole number of wavelengths)

              _   _   _  __
            /  \_/ \_/ \/  X  <---- MISMATCH here
           |                 |
           |     ( + )       |        The wave does not join up.
           |                 |        Destructive interference kills it.
            \  _   _   _ ___/         UNSTABLE  =>  NOT ALLOWED
              \_/ \_/ \_/
```

## Useful consequence

```
      2 pi r_n  =  n lambda_n

                      2 pi r_n
      ==>  lambda_n = ----------
                          n

  For hydrogen in the ground state (n = 1):
      lambda = 2 pi x 0.529e-10 = 3.32 x 10^-10 m = 3.32 angstrom
```

> **TRAP:** The number of wavelengths that fit in the nth orbit is exactly
> **n**. Not n^2, not 2n. In the 3rd orbit, 3 wavelengths fit.

# TOPIC 13 — LIMITATIONS OF BOHR'S MODEL

Guaranteed short-answer question. Learn at least **six**.

```
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 1  | Applies ONLY to hydrogen and hydrogen-like SINGLE-ELECTRON      |
  |    | ions (He+, Li2+). It FAILS completely for atoms with two or     |
  |    | more electrons, because it ignores electron-electron repulsion. |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 2  | It cannot explain the RELATIVE INTENSITIES of spectral lines    |
  |    | (why some lines are bright and others faint).                   |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 3  | It cannot explain the FINE STRUCTURE — with a good              |
  |    | spectrometer each "line" is seen to be several closely spaced   |
  |    | lines. Bohr predicts only one.                                  |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 4  | It cannot explain the ZEEMAN EFFECT (splitting of lines in a    |
  |    | magnetic field) or the STARK EFFECT (splitting in an electric   |
  |    | field).                                                          |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 5  | It cannot explain CHEMICAL BONDING or the formation of          |
  |    | molecules, nor the shapes of molecules.                          |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 6  | It VIOLATES HEISENBERG'S UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE. Bohr assigns    |
  |    | the electron a definite radius AND a definite momentum at the   |
  |    | same time, which is not allowed.                                 |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 7  | It is a HYBRID, not a theory: it uses classical mechanics for   |
  |    | the orbit but bolts on a quantum condition with no              |
  |    | justification. Postulate 1 simply contradicts Maxwell.           |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
  | 8  | It treats the electron only as a PARTICLE in a definite orbit   |
  |    | and ignores its WAVE nature; the modern picture replaces        |
  |    | orbits with PROBABILITY CLOUDS (orbitals).                        |
  +----+----------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**One-line memory hook:**
*"Only one electron; no intensities; no fine structure; no Zeeman/Stark;
no molecules; breaks uncertainty."*

# TOPIC 14 — THE FRANCK–HERTZ EXPERIMENT (in outline)

This is background reading — it is the **direct experimental proof** that
atomic energy levels really are discrete, so it is worth two lines in any
long answer on Bohr's model.

```
   THE FRANCK - HERTZ TUBE  (1914)

     +--------------------------------------------------+
     |                                                  |
     |   [K]        ||||||| G          [A]              |
     |  cathode      grid            anode -- (Ammeter) |
     |    |            |               |          |     |
     |    |<---- V (accelerating) ---->|          |     |
     |    |                            |<- small  |     |
     |    |                            | retarding|     |
     |    +------( V variable )--------+  voltage |     |
     |                                            |     |
     |    Tube filled with MERCURY VAPOUR at low pressure|
     +--------------------------------------------------+


   RESULT:  plot the anode CURRENT against the accelerating VOLTAGE

     I |        /\        /\        /\
       |       /  \      /  \      /  \
       |      /    \    /    \    /    \
       |     /      \  /      \  /      \
       |    /        \/        \/        \
       +---+---------+---------+---------+------> V (volts)
          4.9       9.8       14.7      19.6
```

**What it shows:**

```
  * The current rises with voltage, then DROPS SHARPLY at 4.9 V,
    then again at 9.8 V, 14.7 V ... i.e. at MULTIPLES of 4.9 V.

  * Explanation: below 4.9 eV, an electron colliding with a mercury atom
    bounces off ELASTICALLY - it loses no energy and reaches the anode.

  * At exactly 4.9 eV it can knock a mercury atom up to its first excited
    state. The collision becomes INELASTIC, the electron loses ALL its
    energy, cannot reach the anode, and the current collapses.

  * At 9.8 eV it can do this TWICE on the way. Hence the repeating dips.

  * Confirmation: the excited mercury atoms then emit ultraviolet light of
    wavelength 2537 A. Check:  12400 / 4.9 = 2530 A.  It matches.

  CONCLUSION:  atoms can only absorb energy in FIXED, DISCRETE amounts.
               Bohr's stationary states are REAL.
```

Franck and Hertz shared the 1925 Nobel Prize for this.

# THE FULL SET OF SCALING RULES (learn as ratios — EAPCET loves these)

```
  +----------------------------+---------------------------+
  |  QUANTITY                  |  DEPENDS ON n AND Z AS    |
  +----------------------------+---------------------------+
  |  Radius            r_n     |     n^2 / Z               |
  |  Velocity          v_n     |     Z / n                 |
  |  Total energy      E_n     |     Z^2 / n^2   (negative)|
  |  Kinetic energy    K_n     |     Z^2 / n^2   (positive)|
  |  Angular momentum  L_n     |     n           (only n!) |
  |  Time period       T_n     |     n^3 / Z^2             |
  |  Frequency of rev. f_n     |     Z^2 / n^3             |
  |  Current in orbit  I_n     |     Z^2 / n^3             |
  |  de Broglie wavelength     |     n / Z                 |
  |  Acceleration      a_n     |     Z^3 / n^4             |
  +----------------------------+---------------------------+

  ANGULAR MOMENTUM depends ONLY on n. That is the whole point of
  Bohr's second postulate. It is a favourite one-mark trick.
```

Quick derivations of the less obvious ones, so you never have to memorise:

```
  T = 2 pi r / v      ->   (n^2/Z) / (Z/n)  =  n^3 / Z^2      OK
  f = 1 / T           ->   Z^2 / n^3                          OK
  I = e / T           ->   Z^2 / n^3                          OK
  lambda = 2 pi r / n ->   (n^2/Z) / n  =  n / Z              OK
  a = v^2 / r         ->   (Z/n)^2 / (n^2/Z) = Z^3 / n^4      OK
```
