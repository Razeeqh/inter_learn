## Topic: Thomson and Rutherford

### Q A1
What is the significance of the alpha particle scattering experiment?

**ANSWER:**
```
  It proved that
   (i)  the atom is mostly EMPTY SPACE, and
   (ii) all the positive charge and nearly all the mass are concentrated
        in a very tiny central core called the NUCLEUS.
```

---

### Q A2
Why is the classical (Rutherford) picture of an atom unstable?

**ANSWER:** The revolving electron is continuously **accelerated**, so by
classical electromagnetic theory it must **radiate energy**. Losing energy, its
orbit shrinks and it would spiral into the nucleus in about **10^-8 s**.
Hence the atom would be unstable.

---

### Q A3
What are the drawbacks of Rutherford's atomic model?

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  STABILITY: the accelerated electron radiates, spirals inwards and
      collapses into the nucleus in about 10^-8 s.

  2.  SPECTRUM: since the orbital frequency would change continuously as
      it spirals, the atom should give a CONTINUOUS spectrum. The observed
      hydrogen spectrum is a sharp LINE spectrum.
```

---

### Q A4
Why did Thomson's model of the atom fail?

**ANSWER:** In Thomson's model the positive charge is spread uniformly over
the whole atom, so the electric field inside is weak. Such a field could only
deflect an alpha particle by a fraction of a degree. It cannot explain the
observed **large-angle scattering** (some alphas returning through 180 deg).

---

### Q A5
Why is gold foil used in the scattering experiment? Why must it be thin?

**ANSWER:** Gold is the most **malleable** metal, so it can be beaten into a
foil only a few hundred atoms thick, and it has a **high atomic number**
(Z = 79) giving strong scattering. The foil must be thin so that each alpha
particle suffers only **one** scattering event, not many.

---

### Q A6
Define the impact parameter.

**ANSWER:** The impact parameter **b** is the perpendicular distance of the
initial line of motion of the alpha particle from the **centre of the nucleus**.

```
                Z e^2 cot ( theta / 2 )
       b  =  ------------------------------
                  4 pi eps0  x  K

   b large  ->  theta small ;    b = 0  ->  theta = 180 degrees
```

---

### Q A7
What is the impact parameter for a scattering angle of 180 degrees?

**ANSWER:**
```
  theta = 180 deg  ->  theta/2 = 90 deg  ->  cot 90 deg = 0

  Therefore  b = 0.

  A head-on collision sends the alpha particle straight back.
```

---

### Q A8
Define the distance of closest approach. Write its expression.

**ANSWER:** It is the distance from the nucleus at which an alpha particle
fired head-on **momentarily comes to rest** before being repelled back. At that
point all its kinetic energy has become electrostatic potential energy.

```
                  2 Z e^2
       r0  =  ----------------- ,     K = (1/2) m v^2
               4 pi eps0  x  K
```

---

### Q A9
An alpha particle of 7.7 MeV is fired at a gold nucleus (Z = 79).
Find the distance of closest approach.

**ANSWER:**
```
  K = 7.7 x 1.6 x 10^-13 = 1.232 x 10^-12 J

         9e9 x 2 x 79 x (1.6e-19)^2        9e9 x 4.045e-36
  r0 = ------------------------------  =  ------------------
              1.232e-12                       1.232e-12

                3.640 x 10^-26
     =     ---------------------   =   2.95 x 10^-14 m
                1.232 x 10^-12
```
**r0 = 2.95 x 10^-14 m (about 30 fermi).**

---

### Q A10
What happens to the distance of closest approach if the kinetic energy of the
alpha particle is doubled?

**ANSWER:** Since **r0 is inversely proportional to K**, doubling the energy
**halves** the distance of closest approach. The alpha gets twice as close.

## Topic: Spectra

### Q A11
What is a line spectrum? What does it tell us?

**ANSWER:** A spectrum consisting of a few **sharp, discrete bright lines** on
a dark background, instead of a continuous rainbow. It proves that the atom
can emit only **certain fixed energies**, i.e. its energy levels are discrete.
Each element has its own unique set of lines — a **fingerprint**.

---

### Q A12
Distinguish between an emission spectrum and an absorption spectrum.

**ANSWER:**
```
  EMISSION    -  BRIGHT coloured lines on a DARK background.
                 Obtained when an excited gas emits light.

  ABSORPTION  -  DARK lines on a CONTINUOUS bright background.
                 Obtained when white light passes through a cool gas.

  The dark lines fall at EXACTLY the same wavelengths as the bright lines
  of the same element.
```

---

### Q A13
Write the Rydberg formula and state the value of the Rydberg constant.

**ANSWER:**
```
      1              +-   1          1     -+
   --------  =   R   |  ------  -  ------   |         n2 > n1
    lambda           +-  n1^2       n2^2   -+

   R  =  1.097 x 10^7 per metre
```

### Q A15
Which series of the hydrogen spectrum lies in the visible region?
Which lies in the ultraviolet?

**ANSWER:** The **Balmer** series is visible (n1 = 2). The **Lyman** series is
ultraviolet (n1 = 1). All the others are infrared.

---

### Q A16
Calculate the shortest wavelength of the Lyman series.
(R = 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1)

**ANSWER:**
```
  Shortest wavelength = SERIES LIMIT ->  n1 = 1 , n2 = infinity

     1 / lambda  =  R ( 1 - 0 )  =  R

     lambda = 1 / R = 1 / 1.097e7 = 9.12 x 10^-8 m = 912 angstrom
```

---

### Q A17
Calculate the longest wavelength of the Balmer series.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Longest wavelength = FIRST line  ->  n1 = 2 , n2 = 3

     1 / lambda = R ( 1/4 - 1/9 ) = R x 5 / 36

     lambda = 36 / ( 5 x 1.097e7 ) = 6.563 x 10^-7 m = 6563 angstrom
```
This is the red **H-alpha** line.

---

### Q A18
What is the series limit of a spectral series?

**ANSWER:** The **shortest wavelength** line of a series, obtained by putting
n2 = infinity. Beyond it the lines merge into a continuum, because the atom is
then ionised. `lambda(limit) = n1^2 / R`.

## Topic: Bohr's model

### Q A19
State Bohr's postulates.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  The electron revolves in certain STATIONARY ORBITS of fixed energy
      WITHOUT radiating energy.

  2.  Only those orbits are allowed for which the angular momentum is an
      integral multiple of h / 2 pi :

                m v r  =  n h / ( 2 pi )       n = 1, 2, 3, ...

  3.  Radiation is emitted only when the electron jumps from a higher
      orbit (E2) to a lower one (E1):      h nu  =  E2 - E1
```

---

### Q A20
What is the angular momentum of an electron in the 3rd orbit of hydrogen?

**ANSWER:**
```
   L = n h / (2 pi) = 3 x 6.63e-34 / (2 x 3.14)
     = 1.989e-33 / 6.28
     = 3.17 x 10^-34  J s
```
Note that **L depends only on n**, never on Z.

---

### Q A21
What is the Bohr radius? Give its value.

**ANSWER:** The radius of the **first (ground state) orbit of hydrogen**.
`a0 = 0.529 x 10^-10 m = 0.529 angstrom`.

---

**ANSWER:**

   r3  = 0.529 x 9 / 1 = 4.761 angstrom = 4.76 x 10^-10 m
```

### Q A23
The energy of the electron in the ground state of hydrogen is -13.6 eV.
What is its energy in the second excited state?

**ANSWER:**
```
   Second excited state  ->  n = 3     (ground = 1, first excited = 2)

   E3 = -13.6 / 3^2 = -13.6 / 9 = -1.51 eV
```

---

### Q A24
Why is the total energy of an electron in an atom negative?

**ANSWER:** The zero of energy is chosen for a **free electron at rest at
infinity**. Inside the atom the electron is **bound** by the attraction of the
nucleus, so energy must be supplied to remove it. Being lower than the free
state, its energy is negative. A negative total energy always means a **bound**
system.

---

### Q A25
Define ionisation energy and ionisation potential of hydrogen.

**ANSWER:** **Ionisation energy** is the energy needed to remove the electron
completely from the ground state to infinity = `0 - (-13.6) = 13.6 eV`.
**Ionisation potential** is the same value expressed in volts = **13.6 V**.

```

### Q B2
What conclusions did Rutherford draw from the scattering experiment?
State his model of the atom.

**ANSWER:**

**Conclusions, observation by observation**
```
  +-------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
  |  OBSERVATION                  |  CONCLUSION                         |
  +-------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
  |  Most pass straight through   |  The atom is mostly EMPTY SPACE.    |
  |  A few deflect by > 1 deg     |  The positive charge is             |
  |                               |  CONCENTRATED, not spread out.      |
  |  1 in 8000 turns back         |  That concentrated region carries   |
  |                               |  nearly ALL the MASS and is TINY.   |
  +-------------------------------+-------------------------------------+
```

**Rutherford's nuclear model**
```
  1.  The whole positive charge and nearly the whole mass are in a tiny
      central NUCLEUS of size about 10^-15 m.
  2.  The atom as a whole is about 10^-10 m; the rest is empty space.
  3.  Electrons revolve around the nucleus in circular orbits (the
      PLANETARY model).
  4.  The electrostatic attraction supplies the centripetal force:

               1        Z e^2         m v^2
           --------- x  ------   =   -------
           4 pi eps0     r^2            r

  5.  The number of orbiting electrons equals the number of protons, so
      the atom is neutral.
```

### Q B3
Derive an expression for the distance of closest approach of an alpha particle
fired at a nucleus. Hence estimate the size of the nucleus.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Let an alpha particle (charge +2e, mass m, speed v) be fired head-on at a
  nucleus of charge +Ze.

  FAR AWAY:     KE = (1/2) m v^2 = K ,      PE = 0

  AT DISTANCE r0 (momentarily at rest):
                KE = 0 ,
                          1        ( 2 e )( Z e )
                PE  =  --------- x --------------
                       4 pi eps0        r0

  By conservation of energy,  initial KE  =  final PE :

           1                1          2 Z e^2
          --- m v^2   =  --------- x  ---------
           2             4 pi eps0        r0

     +-----------------------------------------------+
     |                   2 Z e^2                     |
     |        r0   =   ---------------               |
     |                  4 pi eps0 K                  |
     +-----------------------------------------------+

  ESTIMATE:  for a 5.5 MeV alpha on gold (Z = 79):
       K = 8.8e-13 J
       r0 = 9e9 x 2 x 79 x (1.6e-19)^2 / 8.8e-13
          = 3.64e-26 / 8.8e-13 = 4.1 x 10^-14 m

  Since the alpha stopped and turned back WITHOUT touching the nucleus,
  the nucleus must be SMALLER than 4.1 x 10^-14 m. This is how the nuclear
  size was first estimated as being of the order of 10^-14 to 10^-15 m,
  i.e. about 100000 times smaller than the atom.
```

### Q B5
State the drawbacks (limitations) of Rutherford's atomic model.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  INSTABILITY OF THE ATOM
      An electron moving in a circle is continuously accelerated. By
      classical electromagnetic theory an accelerated charge radiates
      energy. Losing energy, the electron's total energy
      E = -e^2/(8 pi eps0 r) becomes more negative, so r decreases and
      the electron SPIRALS INTO THE NUCLEUS in about 10^-8 s.
      But atoms are stable for billions of years. Contradiction.

  2.  WRONG TYPE OF SPECTRUM
      While spiralling, r decreases continuously, so the frequency of
      revolution f = v/(2 pi r) also changes continuously. The atom would
      emit a CONTINUOUS spectrum. The observed hydrogen spectrum consists
      of SHARP DISCRETE LINES. Contradiction.

  3.  It says nothing about the ARRANGEMENT of the electrons, so it cannot
      explain the periodic table or chemical bonding.
```

---

### Q B6
State Bohr's postulates and explain how they overcome the failures of
Rutherford's model.

**ANSWER:**
```
  POSTULATE 1 (stationary orbits)
     An electron can revolve in certain stable orbits WITHOUT emitting
     radiation. Each such orbit has a fixed energy.
     ==> This directly REMOVES the spiralling problem: the electron in a
         stationary orbit simply does not radiate, so the atom is stable.

  POSTULATE 2 (quantisation of angular momentum)
     Only those orbits are allowed for which
                 m v r  =  n h / (2 pi) ,   n = 1, 2, 3, ...
     ==> This picks out DISCRETE orbits, hence DISCRETE energies.

  POSTULATE 3 (frequency condition)
     Radiation is emitted only when the electron jumps from a higher
     energy state E2 to a lower one E1, with
                 h nu  =  E2  -  E1
     ==> Since only certain E values exist, only certain differences
         (E2 - E1) exist, so only certain frequencies are emitted.
         This gives a LINE SPECTRUM instead of a continuous one.
```

### Q B7
Derive an expression for the radius of the nth Bohr orbit of a hydrogen atom.

**ANSWER:**
```
  STEP 1  Coulomb attraction = centripetal force  (Z = 1 for hydrogen):

              1        e^2         m v^2                     e^2
          --------- x ------  =  -------   =>  m v^2 = -------------  ...(1)
          4 pi eps0    r^2          r                   4 pi eps0 r

  STEP 2  Bohr's second postulate:

                        n h                     n h
          m v r  =  ---------   =>    v  =  ----------            ...(2)
                       2 pi                  2 pi m r

  STEP 3  Substitute (2) into (1):

                +-    n h    -+ 2         e^2
           m  x |  ---------- |    =  -------------
                +-  2 pi m r -+        4 pi eps0 r

              n^2 h^2                e^2
           -------------   =   -------------
            4 pi^2 m r^2        4 pi eps0 r

  STEP 4  Solve for r:

     +-----------------------------------------------------+
     |                    eps0 h^2                         |
     |        r_n   =   -------------  x  n^2              |
     |                    pi m e^2                         |
     +-----------------------------------------------------+

  STEP 5  Substituting eps0 = 8.85e-12, h = 6.63e-34, m = 9.1e-31,
          e = 1.6e-19 and n = 1:

              r1  =  0.529 x 10^-10 m  =  0.529 angstrom  =  BOHR RADIUS

          Hence      r_n  =  0.529 n^2  angstrom      ( r  ~  n^2 )
```

### Q B8
Derive an expression for the total energy of an electron in the nth Bohr orbit
of hydrogen. Why is it negative?

**ANSWER:**
```
  KINETIC ENERGY.   From  m v^2 = e^2 / (4 pi eps0 r) :

             1              e^2
       K =  --- m v^2  =  -------------
             2             8 pi eps0 r

  POTENTIAL ENERGY of charge -e at distance r from +e :

                     - e^2
       U   =   -----------------
                4 pi eps0 r

  TOTAL ENERGY:

                       e^2            e^2              - e^2
       E = K + U =  ----------- - -----------   =   -------------
                    8 pi eps0 r   4 pi eps0 r        8 pi eps0 r

  Substituting  r = r_n = eps0 h^2 n^2 / (pi m e^2) :

     +-----------------------------------------------------------+
     |                     m e^4               13.6               |
     |       E_n  =   -  --------------  =  -  ------   eV        |
     |                    8 eps0^2 n^2 h^2       n^2              |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------+

  WHY NEGATIVE:  the zero of energy is taken for a free electron at rest
  at infinity. Inside the atom the electron is BOUND, so energy must be
  supplied to free it. Its energy therefore lies BELOW zero, i.e. it is
  negative. The magnitude 13.6/n^2 eV is exactly the energy needed to
  remove the electron from the nth orbit.
```

### Q B9
Show how Bohr's model explains the spectral series of hydrogen. Obtain an
expression for the Rydberg constant.

**ANSWER:**
```
  From Bohr's third postulate, a jump from n2 down to n1 gives

       h nu  =  E(n2) - E(n1)

              m e^4      +-    1          1    -+
       h nu = ---------- |  ------  -  ------   |
              8 eps0^2 h^2 +-  n1^2      n2^2  -+

  Putting nu = c / lambda :

         1            m e^4        +-   1         1    -+
      -------- =   -------------   |  ------  - ------  |
       lambda      8 eps0^2 h^3 c  +-  n1^2      n2^2  -+

  Comparing with the experimental Rydberg formula

         1              +-   1         1    -+
      -------- =   R    |  ------  - ------  |
       lambda           +-  n1^2      n2^2  -+

     +---------------------------------------------+
     |                    m e^4                    |
     |          R   =  ---------------             |
     |                  8 eps0^2 h^3 c             |
     +---------------------------------------------+

  Numerically this gives R = 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1, which agrees exactly with
  the measured value. The FIVE SERIES then correspond to the five possible
  landing levels:

       n1 = 1  -> LYMAN     (ultraviolet)
       n1 = 2  -> BALMER    (visible)
       n1 = 3  -> PASCHEN   (infrared)
       n1 = 4  -> BRACKETT  (infrared)
       n1 = 5  -> PFUND     (far infrared)
```

### Q B11
Explain de Broglie's justification of Bohr's second postulate.

**ANSWER:**
```
  De Broglie proposed that the electron behaves as a WAVE of wavelength

                lambda  =  h / ( m v )

  For the electron wave to persist in a circular orbit, it must join up
  with itself smoothly after one complete revolution — otherwise
  successive turns interfere destructively and the wave dies out.
  That is the condition for a STANDING WAVE on a closed loop:

                2 pi r   =   n lambda            n = 1, 2, 3, ...

  Substituting lambda = h / (m v) :

                2 pi r   =   n h / ( m v )

     +-------------------------------------------+
     |               m v r   =   n h / ( 2 pi )  |
     +-------------------------------------------+

  which is exactly Bohr's second postulate. So the "allowed" orbits are
  simply those that hold a WHOLE NUMBER of electron wavelengths.

         ALLOWED (n = 4)                 NOT ALLOWED
            _   _   _   _                  _   _  __
          /  \_/ \_/ \_/  \              /  \_/ \/  X  <- mismatch
         |      ( + )      |            |     ( + )   |
          \  _   _   _   _/              \  _   _ ___/
            \_/ \_/ \_/ \_/                \_/ \_/
```

### Q B12
State the limitations of Bohr's model of the atom.

**ANSWER:**
```
  1.  It applies only to HYDROGEN and hydrogen-like one-electron ions
      (He+, Li2+). It fails for atoms with two or more electrons, because
      it ignores electron-electron repulsion.

  2.  It cannot explain the RELATIVE INTENSITIES of the spectral lines
      (why some lines are bright and others faint).

  3.  It cannot explain the FINE STRUCTURE - each line is actually made of
      several closely spaced lines.

  4.  It cannot explain the ZEEMAN EFFECT (splitting in a magnetic field)
      or the STARK EFFECT (splitting in an electric field).

  5.  It cannot explain chemical bonding or the formation of molecules.

  6.  It violates HEISENBERG'S UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE, since it assigns the
      electron a definite orbit radius and a definite momentum at once.

  7.  It is an arbitrary mixture of classical mechanics and a quantum
      condition; the first postulate simply contradicts Maxwell's theory.
```

### Q B13
The ground state energy of hydrogen is -13.6 eV. Find (a) the kinetic and
potential energies in the ground state, (b) the energy needed to excite the
atom to n = 3, (c) the wavelength of the photon emitted when it returns
from n = 3 to n = 1.

**ANSWER:**
```
  (a)   K = -E = +13.6 eV
        U = 2E = -27.2 eV
        Check: K + U = 13.6 - 27.2 = -13.6 eV = E   OK

  (b)   E3 = -13.6 / 9 = -1.51 eV
        Energy needed = E3 - E1 = -1.51 - (-13.6) = 12.09 eV

  (c)   Delta E = 12.09 eV

        lambda = 12400 / 12.09 = 1025.6 angstrom
               = 1.026 x 10^-7 m       (ultraviolet, Lyman series)
```

### Q B16
Explain the terms excitation energy, excitation potential, ionisation energy
and ionisation potential, with values for hydrogen.

**ANSWER:**
```
  EXCITATION ENERGY   -  the energy needed to lift the electron from the
                         ground state to a higher level (still bound).
                         1st excitation energy = E2 - E1 = 10.2 eV
                         2nd excitation energy = E3 - E1 = 12.09 eV

  EXCITATION POTENTIAL - the same value expressed in volts.
                         1st excitation potential = 10.2 V

  IONISATION ENERGY   -  the energy needed to remove the electron
                         completely, from n = 1 to n = infinity.
                         = 0 - (-13.6) = 13.6 eV

  IONISATION POTENTIAL - the same value in volts = 13.6 V
```

### Q C1
State Bohr's postulates. Derive expressions for the radius, the velocity and
the energy of an electron in the nth orbit of a hydrogen atom.

**MODEL ANSWER — with the mark split**

```
  ------------------------------------------------  BOHR'S POSTULATES  [2]
  1.  The electron revolves in certain STATIONARY ORBITS of definite
      energy without radiating.
  2.  Angular momentum is quantised:   m v r  =  n h / (2 pi)
  3.  Radiation is emitted only on a jump:  h nu = E2 - E1


  ------------------------------------------------  RADIUS            [3]
  Coulomb attraction = centripetal force :

          1        e^2         m v^2                      e^2
      --------- x ------  =  --------  =>  m v^2  =  ------------- ...(1)
      4 pi eps0    r^2          r                    4 pi eps0 r

  From postulate 2 :     v  =  n h / ( 2 pi m r )                 ...(2)

  Put (2) in (1) :

            n^2 h^2                e^2
        --------------   =   -------------
         4 pi^2 m r^2         4 pi eps0 r

     +----------------------------------------------+
     |               eps0 h^2  n^2                  |
     |    r_n   =   -----------------  = 0.529 n^2 A|
     |                 pi m e^2                     |
     +----------------------------------------------+


  ------------------------------------------------  VELOCITY          [1]
  From (2) with r = r_n :

     +----------------------------------------------+
     |               e^2                    1       |
     |    v_n  =  ------------ = 2.18e6 x -----  m/s|
     |             2 eps0 n h               n       |
     +----------------------------------------------+


  ------------------------------------------------  ENERGY            [2]
                1               e^2
      K  =  --- m v^2   =   -------------
                2            8 pi eps0 r

                  - e^2
      U   =   -------------
               4 pi eps0 r

                          - e^2
      E = K + U   =   -------------
                       8 pi eps0 r

  Substituting r = r_n :

     +-------------------------------------------------+
     |                m e^4              13.6          |
     |   E_n  =  -  --------------  =  - ------   eV   |
     |               8 eps0^2 n^2 h^2      n^2         |
     +-------------------------------------------------+


  ------------------------------------------------  COMMENT           [1]
  * r  ~  n^2       so orbits get rapidly wider
  * v  ~  1/n       so the electron slows in outer orbits
  * E  ~  -1/n^2    so levels crowd together and approach 0 as n -> inf
  * E is NEGATIVE because the electron is BOUND.
  * For hydrogen n = 1:   r = 0.529 A ,  v = 2.18e6 m/s ,  E = -13.6 eV
```

### Q C4
Write Bohr's theory for hydrogen-like atoms. Show how the radius, velocity and
energy depend on Z, and calculate the ionisation energy of He+ and Li2+.

**MODEL ANSWER**

```
  A hydrogen-like ion has a nucleus of charge +Ze and exactly ONE electron
  (He+ with Z = 2, Li2+ with Z = 3, Be3+ with Z = 4). Repeating the whole
  Bohr derivation with e^2 replaced by Z e^2 gives:               [3]

     +-----------------------------------------------------------+
     |                    n^2                                    |
     |     r_n  =  0.529 -----  A         ( r  ~  n^2 / Z )      |
     |                     Z                                     |
     |                                                           |
     |                        Z                                  |
     |     v_n  =  2.18e6 x -----  m/s    ( v  ~  Z / n )        |
     |                        n                                  |
     |                                                           |
     |                       Z^2                                 |
     |     E_n  =  -13.6 x ------  eV     ( E  ~  Z^2 / n^2 )    |
     |                       n^2                                 |
     +-----------------------------------------------------------+

  PHYSICAL MEANING: a larger nuclear charge pulls the electron into a
  SMALLER orbit, makes it move FASTER, and binds it MORE TIGHTLY.   [1]

  IONISATION ENERGY = 13.6 Z^2 eV                                   [2]

      He+  (Z = 2):   13.6 x 4  =  54.4 eV
      Li2+ (Z = 3):   13.6 x 9  =  122.4 eV
      Be3+ (Z = 4):   13.6 x 16 =  217.6 eV

  SPECTRUM:                                                        [2]
        1                +-   1        1    -+
     -------- =   R Z^2  |  -----  - -----   |
      lambda             +-  n1^2    n2^2   -+

  So every line of a hydrogen-like ion is shifted to a wavelength
  Z^2 times SHORTER than the corresponding hydrogen line.
  Example: the n=2 level of He+ has exactly the same energy (-13.6 eV) as
  the ground state of hydrogen, since 13.6 x 2^2 / 2^2 = 13.6.
```

### Q C5
Explain, with the necessary theory, why Rutherford's model had to be replaced
by Bohr's model. Include the de Broglie justification and the limitations that
remain.

**MODEL ANSWER SKELETON**

```
  1.  Rutherford's model and its centripetal condition                [1]
  2.  Drawback 1: radiation from an accelerated charge -> spiral
      collapse in 10^-8 s                                             [2]
  3.  Drawback 2: continuously changing frequency -> continuous
      spectrum, but a LINE spectrum is observed                       [1]
  4.  Bohr's three postulates and how each drawback is removed        [2]
  5.  De Broglie: 2 pi r = n lambda gives m v r = n h / 2 pi, so the
      second postulate is not arbitrary after all                     [1]
  6.  Limitations that REMAIN in Bohr's model: only one-electron
      atoms, no line intensities, no fine structure, no Zeeman or
      Stark effect, no molecules, violates uncertainty principle      [1]
```
