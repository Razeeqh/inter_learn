# PATTERN 1 — Wavefronts and Huygens' principle

### Q1
The wavefront of light coming from a very distant star is

(a) spherical &nbsp; (b) cylindrical &nbsp; (c) plane &nbsp; (d) elliptical

**ANSWER: (c)** — the sphere is so huge that the small patch reaching us has
negligible curvature, so it is treated as **plane**.

---

### Q2
A ray of light is always

(a) parallel to the wavefront &nbsp; (b) normal to the wavefront
(c) at 45 degrees to the wavefront &nbsp; (d) unrelated to the wavefront

**ANSWER: (b)** — a ray is the **normal** to the wavefront, drawn in the
direction of propagation.

---

### Q3
According to Huygens' principle, the new wavefront is obtained as

(a) the sum of all the wavelets &nbsp; (b) the backward envelope of the wavelets
(c) the forward envelope (common tangent) of the secondary wavelets
(d) the centre of the wavelets

**ANSWER: (c)**

> **SHORTCUT:** the two magic words in every Huygens question are
> **"secondary wavelets"** and **"forward envelope"**.

---

### Q4
A point source of light is placed at the focus of a convex lens. The wavefront
emerging from the lens is

(a) spherical converging &nbsp; (b) spherical diverging
(c) plane &nbsp; (d) cylindrical

**ANSWER: (c)** — a source at the focus gives a parallel beam, and parallel
rays mean a **plane** wavefront.

---

### Q5
Huygens' wave theory of light could NOT explain

(a) reflection &nbsp; (b) refraction &nbsp; (c) interference &nbsp;
(d) the photoelectric effect

**ANSWER: (d)** — the photoelectric effect needs the **particle** (photon)
model. Newton's corpuscular theory, by contrast, failed on interference,
diffraction and polarisation.

# PATTERN 2 — Speed, wavelength and frequency on refraction

### Q6
When light passes from air into glass, which quantity remains unchanged?

(a) speed &nbsp; (b) wavelength &nbsp; (c) frequency &nbsp; (d) amplitude

**ANSWER: (c) frequency**

> **SHORTCUT:** frequency belongs to the **source**, not the medium. It NEVER
> changes at a boundary. This exact question appears almost every year.

---

### Q7
Light of wavelength 6000 Å in air enters a medium of refractive index 1.5. Its
wavelength in the medium is

(a) 9000 Å &nbsp; (b) 6000 Å &nbsp; (c) 4000 Å &nbsp; (d) 3000 Å

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
            lam       6000
   lam' =  -----  =  ------  =  4000 A
             mu       1.5
```

---

### Q8
The speed of light in a medium of refractive index 1.25 is

(a) 3.75 x 10^8 m/s &nbsp; (b) 2.4 x 10^8 m/s &nbsp; (c) 2.0 x 10^8 m/s
&nbsp; (d) 1.5 x 10^8 m/s

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
          c        3 x 10^8
   v  =  ----  =  ----------  =  2.4 x 10^8  m/s
          mu         1.25
```

---

### Q9
A monochromatic beam of frequency 5 x 10^14 Hz travels from air into water
(mu = 4/3). Its frequency in water is

(a) 3.75 x 10^14 Hz &nbsp; (b) 5 x 10^14 Hz &nbsp; (c) 6.67 x 10^14 Hz
&nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (b)** — unchanged.

> **SHORTCUT:** any option that changes the frequency is wrong on sight.
> Answer in two seconds and move on.

---

### Q10
Huygens' wave theory predicts that the speed of light in a denser medium is

(a) greater than in air &nbsp; (b) less than in air &nbsp; (c) the same
&nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (b)** — and this was the prediction that experiment confirmed,
proving the corpuscular theory wrong.

# PATTERN 3 — Doppler effect in light

### Q11
The spectral lines of a distant galaxy are shifted towards longer wavelengths.
This means the galaxy is

(a) approaching &nbsp; (b) receding &nbsp; (c) stationary &nbsp; (d) rotating

**ANSWER: (b) receding** — a longer wavelength is a **red shift**.

---

### Q12
A spectral line of wavelength 6000 Å is observed at 6002 Å. The speed of the
source is

(a) 1 x 10^5 m/s &nbsp; (b) 2 x 10^5 m/s &nbsp; (c) 1 x 10^4 m/s
&nbsp; (d) 3 x 10^5 m/s

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
           d(lam)              2
   v  =  ---------- x c  =  -------- x 3 x 10^8
             lam             6000

      =  3.33 x 10^-4 x 3 x 10^8  =  1 x 10^5  m/s   (RECEDING)
```

> **SHORTCUT:** `v = c x (shift / wavelength)`. Just divide and multiply by
> 3 x 10^8.

# PATTERN 4 — Amplitude, intensity and the Imax : Imin ratio

### Q13
Two coherent sources have intensities in the ratio 9 : 1. The ratio of the
maximum to the minimum intensity in the interference pattern is

(a) 9 : 1 &nbsp; (b) 3 : 1 &nbsp; (c) 4 : 1 &nbsp; (d) 16 : 1

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   a1 : a2  =  sqrt(9) : sqrt(1)  =  3 : 1

    Imax       (3 + 1)^2       16
   ------  =  -----------  =  ----  =  4          ->  4 : 1
    Imin       (3 - 1)^2        4
```

> **SHORTCUT:** ALWAYS take the square root of the intensity ratio FIRST to
> get the amplitudes, then use (sum)^2 / (difference)^2.

---

### Q14
In an interference pattern Imax : Imin = 25 : 1. The ratio of the amplitudes of
the two waves is

(a) 5 : 1 &nbsp; (b) 3 : 2 &nbsp; (c) 25 : 1 &nbsp; (d) 4 : 1

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
    a1 + a2
   ---------  =  sqrt(25)  =  5

    a1 - a2

   a1 + a2 = 5a1 - 5a2   ->   6 a2 = 4 a1   ->   a1 : a2 = 3 : 2
```

> **SHORTCUT:** with `k = sqrt(Imax/Imin)`, the amplitude ratio is
> `(k + 1) : (k - 1)`. Here `(5+1):(5-1) = 6:4 = 3:2`. One line.

---

### Q15
Two identical coherent sources each of intensity I0 interfere. The maximum
intensity is

(a) I0 &nbsp; (b) 2 I0 &nbsp; (c) 4 I0 &nbsp; (d) 8 I0

**ANSWER: (c) 4 I0** — because `Imax = (sqrt(I0) + sqrt(I0))^2 = 4 I0`.

---

### Q16
At a point where the path difference is lam / 4, the intensity in a double slit
pattern is I. The intensity at the central maximum is

(a) I &nbsp; (b) 2 I &nbsp; (c) 4 I &nbsp; (d) I / 2

**ANSWER: (b) 2 I**
```
                      2 pi     lam        pi
   phase difference = ------ x -----  =  ----
                       lam       4         2

   I  =  4 I0 cos^2 ( phi / 2 )  =  4 I0 cos^2 (pi/4)  =  4 I0 x 0.5
                                 =  2 I0

   Central maximum  =  4 I0  =  2 x (2 I0)  =  2 I
```

---

### Q17
Interference of light does not violate the conservation of energy because

(a) energy is created at the bright fringes
(b) energy is destroyed at the dark fringes
(c) energy is only redistributed from the dark to the bright fringes
(d) light carries no energy

**ANSWER: (c)**

# PATTERN 5 — Fringe width: the straight substitution

### Q18
In a Young's experiment, d = 1 mm, D = 1 m and lam = 500 nm. The fringe width is

(a) 0.5 mm &nbsp; (b) 1.0 mm &nbsp; (c) 0.25 mm &nbsp; (d) 2.0 mm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
             D lam        1 x 5 x 10^-7
   beta  =  -------  =  -----------------  =  5 x 10^-4 m  =  0.5 mm
                d            1 x 10^-3
```

---

### Q19
For the same experiment the ANGULAR fringe width is

(a) 5 x 10^-4 rad &nbsp; (b) 5 x 10^-3 rad &nbsp; (c) 1 x 10^-4 rad
&nbsp; (d) 2 x 10^-3 rad

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
              lam        5 x 10^-7
   theta  =  ------  =  ------------  =  5 x 10^-4  radian
                d        1 x 10^-3
```

> **SHORTCUT:** angular fringe width = `lam / d`. It contains NO D — moving
> the screen does not change it.

---

### Q20
In a double slit experiment the fringe width is beta. If the distance between
the slits is doubled and the distance to the screen is halved, the new fringe
width is

(a) beta &nbsp; (b) beta / 2 &nbsp; (c) beta / 4 &nbsp; (d) 4 beta

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
             D lam                      (D/2) lam        1    D lam
   beta  =  -------      ->   beta' =  ------------  =  --- x -------
                d                          2 d           4       d

                                     =  beta / 4
```

> **SHORTCUT:** treat beta = D lam / d as a fraction. Whatever you do to D,
> lam or d, do the same to beta (invert for d).

---

### Q21
If the wavelength used in a double slit experiment is changed from 4000 Å to
6000 Å, the fringe width becomes

(a) 2/3 times &nbsp; (b) 3/2 times &nbsp; (c) unchanged &nbsp; (d) 9/4 times

**ANSWER: (b)** — beta is directly proportional to lam, so it becomes
`6000/4000 = 3/2` times.

---

### Q22
In a Young's experiment the fringes are widest for

(a) violet light &nbsp; (b) green light &nbsp; (c) yellow light
&nbsp; (d) red light

**ANSWER: (d) red** — the longest wavelength gives the largest
`beta = D lam / d`.

# PATTERN 6 — Positions of fringes and counting

### Q23
In a Young's experiment with beta = 0.4 mm, the distance between the 3rd bright
fringe and the 8th bright fringe on the same side is

(a) 2.0 mm &nbsp; (b) 2.4 mm &nbsp; (c) 3.2 mm &nbsp; (d) 4.4 mm

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
   Number of fringe widths  =  8 - 3  =  5

   distance  =  5 x 0.4  =  2.0 mm
```

> **SHORTCUT:** count the **gaps**, not the fringes. "Between the mth and the
> nth" always means `(n - m) beta`.

---

### Q24
The distance between the 2nd and the 5th DARK fringe in a pattern of fringe
width beta is

(a) 2 beta &nbsp; (b) 2.5 beta &nbsp; (c) 3 beta &nbsp; (d) 3.5 beta

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
                (2n - 1) beta
   y(dark) = ------------------
                     2

   y5 - y2  =  (9 beta / 2)  -  (3 beta / 2)  =  6 beta / 2  =  3 beta
```

> **SHORTCUT:** dark fringes are spaced by beta too, so the answer for "the
> mth to the nth" is just `(n - m) beta` for dark fringes as well.

---

### Q25
In a Young's experiment the 10th bright fringe of light of wavelength 6000 Å
coincides with the nth bright fringe of 4000 Å. Then n is

(a) 12 &nbsp; (b) 15 &nbsp; (c) 20 &nbsp; (d) 8

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   n1 lam1  =  n2 lam2

   10 x 6000  =  n x 4000

           n  =  60000 / 4000  =  15
```

---

### Q26
If one of the two slits in Young's experiment is covered with an opaque sheet,
the screen shows

(a) the same fringes &nbsp; (b) brighter fringes &nbsp;
(c) a single slit diffraction pattern &nbsp; (d) complete darkness

**ANSWER: (c)** — interference needs two slits; with one slit you get its
**diffraction** pattern.

# PATTERN 7 — Liquids, sheets and missing orders

### Q27
A Young's apparatus gives a fringe width of 0.4 mm in air. When the whole
apparatus is immersed in water (mu = 4/3), the fringe width becomes

(a) 0.53 mm &nbsp; (b) 0.4 mm &nbsp; (c) 0.3 mm &nbsp; (d) 0.2 mm

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
              beta        0.4        0.4 x 3
   beta'  =  ------  =  -------  =  ---------  =  0.3 mm
                mu        4/3           4
```

> **SHORTCUT:** immersing the apparatus DIVIDES the fringe width by mu. If an
> option is bigger than the original, it is wrong.

---

### Q28
A thin sheet of thickness t and refractive index mu is placed over one slit.
The fringe pattern

(a) shifts and the fringe width increases
(b) shifts but the fringe width is unchanged
(c) disappears
(d) does not shift

**ANSWER: (b)** — the whole pattern slides by `D(mu-1)t/d` towards the covered
slit; the **spacing** is unaffected.

---

### Q29
A sheet of thickness 10 micrometre and mu = 1.6 is placed over one slit in a
Young's experiment with lam = 6000 Å. The number of fringes the pattern shifts
by is

(a) 5 &nbsp; (b) 10 &nbsp; (c) 12 &nbsp; (d) 16

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
                          ( mu - 1 ) t       (1.6 - 1) x 10 x 10^-6
   number of fringes  =  --------------  =  ------------------------
                              lam                  6 x 10^-7

                          0.6 x 10^-5        6 x 10^-6
                      =  --------------  =  ------------  =  10
                            6 x 10^-7        6 x 10^-7
```

---

### Q30
In a double slit arrangement the slit separation d is four times the slit width
a. The interference orders that are MISSING are

(a) 2, 4, 6 &nbsp; (b) 3, 6, 9 &nbsp; (c) 4, 8, 12 &nbsp; (d) 1, 2, 3

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   Missing when an interference MAXIMUM lands on a diffraction MINIMUM:

        d sin th = n lam        a sin th = m lam

              n        d
             ---  =  -----  =  4      ->    n  =  4 m
              m        a

        m = 1, 2, 3 ...   gives   n = 4, 8, 12, ...
```

> **SHORTCUT:** missing orders are simply the multiples of `d / a`.

# PATTERN 8 — Single slit diffraction

### Q31
In single slit diffraction the condition for a MINIMUM is

(a) a sin th = n lam &nbsp; (b) a sin th = (2n+1) lam/2
(c) d sin th = n lam &nbsp; (d) d sin th = (2n-1) lam/2

**ANSWER: (a)**

> **SHORTCUT — the swap that decides half these questions:**
> `n lam` means **BRIGHT** in interference but **DARK** in diffraction.

---

### Q32
A slit of width 0.2 mm is illuminated with light of wavelength 600 nm. On a
screen 1 m away, the width of the central maximum is

(a) 3 mm &nbsp; (b) 6 mm &nbsp; (c) 1.5 mm &nbsp; (d) 12 mm

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
                 2 D lam       2 x 1 x 6 x 10^-7        1.2 x 10^-6
   width  =  -----------  =  --------------------  =  ---------------
                    a              2 x 10^-4             2 x 10^-4

                            =  6 x 10^-3  m   =   6 mm
```

---

### Q33
If the width of the slit in a single slit experiment is doubled, the width of
the central maximum

(a) doubles &nbsp; (b) halves &nbsp; (c) stays the same &nbsp;
(d) becomes four times

**ANSWER: (b) halves** — `a` is in the denominator of `2 D lam / a`.

> **SHORTCUT:** slit and pattern do OPPOSITE things. Narrow slit -> wide
> pattern. Wide slit -> narrow pattern.

---

### Q34
The first minimum in a single slit pattern occurs at 30 degrees for light of
wavelength 5000 Å. The slit width is

(a) 5 x 10^-7 m &nbsp; (b) 1 x 10^-6 m &nbsp; (c) 2.5 x 10^-7 m
&nbsp; (d) 1 x 10^-5 m

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   a sin th = lam    ->    a x 0.5 = 5 x 10^-7    ->    a = 1 x 10^-6 m
```

---

### Q35
In a single slit diffraction pattern the central maximum is

(a) as wide as the other bands
(b) half as wide as the other bands
(c) twice as wide as the other bands
(d) three times as wide as the other bands

**ANSWER: (c) twice** — the central band runs from the 1st minimum on one side
to the 1st minimum on the other, i.e. `2 D lam / a`, while every other band is
`D lam / a`.

---

### Q36
The intensity of the first secondary maximum in single slit diffraction, as a
fraction of the central maximum, is approximately

(a) 50 % &nbsp; (b) 25 % &nbsp; (c) 4.5 % &nbsp; (d) 0.5 %

**ANSWER: (c) about 4.5 %** (the ratio is 1/22).

---

### Q37
Diffraction of light is easily observed when

(a) the obstacle is much larger than the wavelength
(b) the obstacle is comparable to the wavelength
(c) the light is polarised
(d) the light is incoherent

**ANSWER: (b)**

# PATTERN 9 — Resolving power and Fresnel distance

### Q38
A telescope has an objective of diameter 1.22 m. Its limit of resolution for
light of wavelength 5000 Å is

(a) 5 x 10^-7 rad &nbsp; (b) 6.1 x 10^-7 rad &nbsp; (c) 1.22 x 10^-6 rad
&nbsp; (d) 2.5 x 10^-7 rad

**ANSWER: (a)**
```
              1.22 lam       1.22 x 5 x 10^-7
   d(th)  =  ----------  =  ------------------  =  5 x 10^-7  radian
                  D                1.22
```

> **SHORTCUT:** when the diameter is given as 1.22 m, the 1.22 cancels and the
> answer is just lam. Examiners choose that number on purpose.

---

### Q39
The resolving power of a telescope can be increased by

(a) using a larger objective &nbsp; (b) using a smaller objective
(c) using longer wavelength light &nbsp; (d) increasing the eyepiece power

**ANSWER: (a)** — `RP = D / 1.22 lam`, so a bigger D (or a shorter lam) helps.
The eyepiece changes magnification, not resolving power.

---

### Q40
For an aperture of 4 mm and light of wavelength 400 nm, the Fresnel distance is

(a) 4 m &nbsp; (b) 20 m &nbsp; (c) 40 m &nbsp; (d) 400 m

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
            a^2       (4 x 10^-3)^2       1.6 x 10^-5
   z(F) = -------  =  --------------  =  -------------  =  40 m
            lam          4 x 10^-7         4 x 10^-7
```

# PATTERN 10 — Polarisation and Brewster's law

### Q41
Polarisation of light proves that light waves are

(a) longitudinal &nbsp; (b) transverse &nbsp; (c) electromagnetic only
&nbsp; (d) particles

**ANSWER: (b) transverse**

---

### Q42
The polarising angle for a medium of refractive index sqrt(3) is

(a) 30 deg &nbsp; (b) 45 deg &nbsp; (c) 60 deg &nbsp; (d) 75 deg

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   tan i_p  =  mu  =  sqrt(3)   ->   i_p  =  60 degrees
```

---

### Q43
At the polarising angle, the angle between the reflected and the refracted ray
is

(a) 0 deg &nbsp; (b) 45 deg &nbsp; (c) 90 deg &nbsp; (d) 180 deg

**ANSWER: (c) 90 degrees**

> **SHORTCUT:** at Brewster's angle, `i_p + r = 90` and reflected is
> perpendicular to refracted. Two facts, one question.

---

### Q44
Light is incident at the polarising angle 60 degrees on a glass slab. The angle
of refraction is

(a) 60 deg &nbsp; (b) 30 deg &nbsp; (c) 45 deg &nbsp; (d) 90 deg

**ANSWER: (b)** — `r = 90 - i_p = 90 - 60 = 30 degrees`.

---

### Q45
Light scattered by air molecules is completely plane polarised when viewed at

(a) 0 deg to the incident beam &nbsp; (b) 45 deg &nbsp; (c) 90 deg
&nbsp; (d) 180 deg

**ANSWER: (c) 90 degrees**

---

### Q46
The blue colour of the sky is due to

(a) reflection &nbsp; (b) refraction &nbsp; (c) scattering &nbsp;
(d) interference

**ANSWER: (c) scattering** — Rayleigh scattering, with intensity proportional
to `1 / lam^4`.

# PATTERN 11 — Malus' law

### Q47
Unpolarised light of intensity I0 passes through a single polaroid. The
transmitted intensity is

(a) I0 &nbsp; (b) I0 / 2 &nbsp; (c) I0 / 4 &nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (b) I0 / 2** — always exactly half, whatever the orientation.

> **SHORTCUT:** the word **UNPOLARISED** is an instruction to halve. Do that
> first, then start using cos^2.

---

### Q48
Unpolarised light of intensity I0 falls on two polaroids with their axes at 45
degrees. The emergent intensity is

(a) I0 / 2 &nbsp; (b) I0 / 4 &nbsp; (c) I0 / 8 &nbsp; (d) zero

**ANSWER: (b)**
```
   I  =  (I0 / 2) cos^2 (45)  =  (I0 / 2) x (1/2)  =  I0 / 4
```

---

### Q49
Plane polarised light of intensity I0 falls on an analyser. The transmitted
intensity is I0 / 4 when the angle between the axes is

(a) 30 deg &nbsp; (b) 45 deg &nbsp; (c) 60 deg &nbsp; (d) 90 deg

**ANSWER: (c)**
```
   I0 cos^2 th  =  I0 / 4    ->    cos^2 th = 1/4   ->   cos th = 1/2
                                                    ->   th = 60 degrees
```

---

### Q50
Two polaroids are crossed (90 degrees apart) so that no light passes. A third
polaroid is inserted between them at 45 degrees to the first. If unpolarised
light of intensity I0 is incident, the emergent intensity is

(a) zero &nbsp; (b) I0 / 2 &nbsp; (c) I0 / 4 &nbsp; (d) I0 / 8

**ANSWER: (d)**
```
   After polaroid 1:   I1  =  I0 / 2

   Polaroid 2 at 45 deg to 1:
        I2  =  (I0/2) cos^2 (45)  =  (I0/2)(1/2)  =  I0 / 4

   Polaroid 3 at 90 deg to 1, hence 45 deg to 2:
        I3  =  (I0/4) cos^2 (45)  =  (I0/4)(1/2)  =  I0 / 8
```

> **SHORTCUT:** crossed polaroids give zero, but slipping a third one in
> BETWEEN them lets light through again. If the middle one is at 45 degrees
> the answer is always **I0 / 8**. Memorise that number.

---

### Q51
When an analyser is rotated through one complete turn in front of plane
polarised light, the number of times the intensity becomes maximum is

(a) 1 &nbsp; (b) 2 &nbsp; (c) 3 &nbsp; (d) 4

**ANSWER: (b) 2** — `cos^2 theta` peaks at 0 and 180 degrees.

---

### Q52
The average value of `cos^2 theta` over a full rotation is

(a) 0 &nbsp; (b) 1/4 &nbsp; (c) 1/2 &nbsp; (d) 1

**ANSWER: (c) 1/2** — which is exactly why unpolarised light is halved by the
first polaroid.
