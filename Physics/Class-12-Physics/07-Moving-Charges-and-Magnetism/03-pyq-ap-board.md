## Topic: Oersted, magnetic field, force on a charge

### Q A1
What is the importance of Oersted's experiment?

**ANSWER:** It showed for the first time that a **current-carrying conductor
produces a magnetic field around it**, proving that electricity and magnetism
are not separate subjects but two aspects of the same phenomenon. A magnetic
needle placed near a current-carrying wire deflects, and reverses its
deflection when the current is reversed.

---

### Q A2
How do magnetic field lines differ from electric field lines?

**ANSWER:**
```
  ELECTRIC LINES          start on +ve charge, end on -ve charge (OPEN)
  MAGNETIC LINES          form CLOSED continuous loops, no start, no end

  Reason: isolated magnetic poles (magnetic monopoles) do not exist,
          but isolated electric charges do.
```

---

### Q A3
Write the expression for the force on a charge moving in a magnetic field.
State the condition for the force to be maximum and minimum.

**ANSWER:**
```
      F  =  q v B sin(theta)        vector form   F = q ( v x B )

  MAXIMUM  when theta = 90 deg  (v perpendicular to B)  ->  F = q v B
  MINIMUM  (zero) when theta = 0 or 180 deg  (v parallel or antiparallel
           to B), and also when v = 0.
```

---

### Q A4
Why does a magnetic field do no work on a moving charge?

**ANSWER:** The magnetic force `F = q(v x B)` is always **perpendicular** to
the velocity. Work `W = F s cos(90) = 0`. Hence the **speed** and the
**kinetic energy** of the particle never change — only the **direction** of
motion changes.

---

### Q A5
Define the tesla.

**ANSWER:** One **tesla** is the magnetic flux density at which a conductor of
length 1 metre carrying a current of 1 ampere, held perpendicular to the field,
experiences a force of 1 newton.

```
      1 T  =  1 N / ( A m )  =  1 Wb / m^2  =  10^4 gauss
```

---

### Q A6
What is the Lorentz force? Write its expression.

**ANSWER:** The **total** force experienced by a charged particle moving in a
region where both an electric field and a magnetic field are present.

```
      F  =  q E  +  q ( v  x  B )   =   q [ E  +  ( v x B ) ]
```

---

### Q A7
What is a velocity selector? Write the condition for the particle to pass
undeflected.

**ANSWER:** A device with **mutually perpendicular** electric and magnetic
fields that allows only particles of one particular speed to pass straight
through.

```
      q E  =  q v B          ->        v  =  E / B

  It does not depend on the charge or the mass of the particle.
```

## Topic: Motion in a magnetic field, cyclotron

### Q A8
A charged particle enters a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to it.
What is the path? Write the expression for the radius.

**ANSWER:** The path is a **circle** (the magnetic force acts as the
centripetal force).

```
                  m v^2               m v
      q v B  =  --------      ->  r = -----
                    r                  q B
```

---

### Q A9
Show that the time period of a charged particle in a magnetic field is
independent of its speed.

**ANSWER:**
```
             2 pi r          m v                     2 pi     m v      2 pi m
      T  =  --------  and r = ---   ->    T  =     ------- x -----  = --------
                v             q B                     v       q B       q B

  The expression  T = 2 pi m / (q B)  contains NO v and NO r.
  A faster particle moves in a bigger circle but covers it faster,
  and the two effects cancel exactly.
```

---

### Q A10
When does a charged particle follow a helical path in a magnetic field?
Write the expression for the pitch.

**ANSWER:** When the velocity makes an angle `theta` (other than 0, 90 or 180)
with B, so that it has one component along B and one across it.

```
                                       2 pi m v cos(theta)
      pitch  =  v cos(theta) x T   =  ---------------------
                                              q B
```

---

### Q A11
What is the cyclotron frequency? On what does it depend?

**ANSWER:**
```
             q B
      f  =  --------
             2 pi m

  It depends only on the charge, the mass of the particle and the
  magnetic field. It does NOT depend on the speed or the radius.
```

---

### Q A12
Why cannot a cyclotron accelerate electrons?

**ANSWER:** An electron is extremely light, so even a small energy gain makes
its speed comparable to the speed of light. Its mass then increases
relativistically, `T = 2 pi m / qB` increases, it arrives late at the gap, and
the **resonance with the oscillator is lost**.

---

### Q A13
Why cannot a cyclotron accelerate neutrons?

**ANSWER:** A neutron is electrically neutral (`q = 0`), so it experiences
neither the electric force `qE` in the gap nor the magnetic force `qvB` inside
the dees. Nothing acts on it.

## Topic: Current-carrying conductors

### Q A14
Write the expression for the force on a current-carrying conductor placed in a
magnetic field.

**ANSWER:**
```
      F  =  B I L sin(theta)        vector form   F  =  I ( L x B )

  theta = angle between the conductor and B.
  F is maximum (BIL) when the conductor is perpendicular to B,
  and zero when it is parallel to B.
```

---

### Q A15
State Fleming's left hand rule.

**ANSWER:** Stretch the **first finger, second finger and thumb of the LEFT
hand** mutually perpendicular. If the **first** finger points along the
**field** and the **second** finger along the **current**, then the **thumb**
gives the direction of the **force (motion)**.

---

### Q A16
Two long parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. Do they attract
or repel? What if the currents are opposite?

**ANSWER:**
```
  SAME direction      ->   they ATTRACT
  OPPOSITE direction  ->   they REPEL

  (Exactly the opposite of what two electric charges do.)
```

---

### Q A17
Define the ampere.

**ANSWER:** One **ampere** is that steady current which, when maintained in
each of two infinitely long straight parallel conductors of negligible
cross-section, placed 1 metre apart in vacuum, produces a force of
**2 x 10^-7 newton per metre** of length between them.

---

### Q A18
What is the net force on a closed current loop placed in a uniform magnetic
field?

**ANSWER:** **Zero.** The forces on opposite sides are equal and opposite. The
net **torque**, however, is generally not zero — it is `tau = NIAB sin(theta)`.

---

## Topic: Biot-Savart, Ampere, dipole

### Q A19
State the Biot-Savart law.

**ANSWER:** The magnetic field `dB` produced at a point P by a small current
element `I dl` is directly proportional to `I`, to `dl`, to `sin(theta)` (theta
being the angle between the element and the line joining it to P) and
inversely proportional to `r^2`.

```
              mu0      I dl sin(theta)
      dB  =  ------ x -----------------      dB is perpendicular to the
              4 pi          r^2              plane containing dl and r
```

---

### Q A20
State Ampere's circuital law.

**ANSWER:** The line integral of the magnetic field around any **closed** path
equals `mu0` times the **total current enclosed** by that path.

```
      INT  B . dl   =   mu0  I_enclosed
```

---

### Q A21
Write the expression for the magnetic field at the centre of a circular coil
of N turns.

**ANSWER:**
```
              mu0  N  I
      B  =  ------------          Note: NO pi in the denominator.
                 2 a
```

---

### Q A22
Write the expression for the magnetic field inside a long solenoid and inside
a toroid.

**ANSWER:**
```
  SOLENOID :   B  =  mu0  n  I          n = turns PER METRE

                     mu0  N  I
  TOROID   :   B  =  -----------        N = TOTAL number of turns
                       2 pi r           r = mean radius
```

---

### Q A23
Define the magnetic dipole moment of a current loop. Give its SI unit.

**ANSWER:** `M = N I A`, the product of the number of turns, the current and
the area of the loop. Its direction is along the normal to the loop, given by
the right hand rule. **SI unit: A m^2.**

---

### Q A24
Define the current sensitivity and the voltage sensitivity of a galvanometer.

**ANSWER:**
```
  CURRENT SENSITIVITY  =  deflection per unit current  =  phi / I  =  NAB / C
  VOLTAGE SENSITIVITY  =  deflection per unit voltage  =  phi / V  =  NAB/(CG)

  Voltage sensitivity  =  ( current sensitivity ) / G
```

---

### Q A25
Why is a radial magnetic field used in a moving coil galvanometer?

**ANSWER:** In a radial field the plane of the coil is **always parallel** to
`B`, so `sin(theta) = 1` in every position. The deflecting torque `NIAB` is
then the same for all positions, the deflection is directly proportional to
the current, and hence **the scale is uniform (linear)**.

---

### Q A26
Why is a soft iron core used in a moving coil galvanometer?

**ANSWER:** It concentrates the magnetic field lines, making the field
**stronger and radial**, which increases the sensitivity. Soft iron is chosen
because it is easily magnetised and demagnetised.

---

### Q A27
How is a galvanometer converted into an ammeter? Into a voltmeter?

**ANSWER:**
```
  AMMETER   :  connect a LOW resistance (SHUNT) in PARALLEL
                     S  =  Ig G / ( I - Ig )

  VOLTMETER :  connect a HIGH resistance in SERIES
                     R  =  V / Ig  -  G
```

---

### Q A28
What is the resistance of an ideal ammeter and an ideal voltmeter?

**ANSWER:** Ideal ammeter → **zero** resistance (so it does not change the
circuit current). Ideal voltmeter → **infinite** resistance (so it draws no
current).

### Q B1
Derive an expression for the radius, time period and frequency of a charged
particle moving in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to its velocity.

**ANSWER:**
```
  A charge q enters a uniform field B with speed v, perpendicular to B.

  STEP 1  The magnetic force  F = q v B  is always perpendicular to v,
          so it cannot change the speed - it only turns the particle.
          A constant force always at right angles to the motion produces
          UNIFORM CIRCULAR MOTION.

  STEP 2  The magnetic force supplies the centripetal force :

                              m v^2                     m v
                q v B   =    -------       ->     r =  -----
                                r                       q B

  STEP 3  Time period = circumference / speed :

                     2 pi r        2 pi     m v          2 pi m
             T  =   --------  =   ------ x -----   =   ---------
                        v            v      q B           q B

  STEP 4  Frequency :

                     1           q B
             f  =   ---   =   ---------
                     T          2 pi m

  RESULT :  T and f do NOT contain v or r  -  they are independent
            of the speed. This is the basis of the cyclotron.
```

### Q B3
Explain the motion of a charged particle entering a uniform magnetic field at
an angle theta. Derive the expression for the pitch of the helix.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Resolve v into two components :

      v_par  = v cos(theta)   ALONG B   ->  sin(0) = 0  ->  NO force

      v_perp = v sin(theta)   ACROSS B  ->  force q v_perp B
                                            ->  uniform circular motion

  Circle + steady drift along the axis  =  HELIX.

                m v sin(theta)                    2 pi m
       r  =   ----------------- ,      T  =     ---------
                     q B                           q B

       PITCH = distance moved along B in one full revolution

                                         2 pi m v cos(theta)
             = v cos(theta) x T   =   -----------------------
                                                q B
```

### Q B4
Derive the expression for the force between two long parallel current-carrying
conductors and hence define the ampere.

**ANSWER:**
```
       ------ I1 -----> wire 1
              |
              | d
              |
       ------ I2 -----> wire 2

  STEP 1  Field made by wire 1 at wire 2 :

                     mu0 I1
              B1  =  --------
                     2 pi d

  STEP 2  Wire 2 lies perpendicular to B1, so the force on a length L is

              F  =  B1 I2 L  =   mu0 I1 I2 L / ( 2 pi d )

  STEP 3  Force per unit length :

               F        mu0 I1 I2        2 x 10^-7 I1 I2
              ---  =  ------------  =  -------------------
               L        2 pi d                 d

  STEP 4  By Fleming's left hand rule the wires ATTRACT when the currents
          are in the SAME direction and REPEL when OPPOSITE.

  DEFINITION OF THE AMPERE
  Putting  I1 = I2 = 1 A  and  d = 1 m  gives  F/L = 2 x 10^-7 N/m.
  Hence one ampere is that steady current which, flowing in each of two
  infinitely long straight parallel conductors of negligible cross-section
  placed 1 metre apart in vacuum, produces a force of 2 x 10^-7 newton per
  metre of their length.
```

Using Ampere's circuital law, derive the expression for the magnetic field

**ANSWER:**

         |       |

### Q B8
Derive the expression for the torque on a current-carrying coil placed in a
uniform magnetic field.

**ANSWER:**
```
  Rectangular coil, N turns, sides l and b, area A = l b, current I,
  in a uniform field B. theta = angle between the NORMAL and B.

  STEP 1  The two sides of length l are perpendicular to B.
          Force on each :   F = B I l   (equal, opposite, not collinear)

  STEP 2  They form a COUPLE.
          Perpendicular distance between them = b sin(theta)

  STEP 3  Torque = F x perpendicular distance
                 = ( B I l ) ( b sin theta )
                 = B I ( l b ) sin(theta)
                 = B I A sin(theta)

  STEP 4  For N turns :

  +----------------------------------------------------------+
  |     tau  =  N I A B sin(theta)  =  M B sin(theta)        |
  |     where  M = N I A       vector form  tau = M x B      |
  +----------------------------------------------------------+

  tau is MAXIMUM (= M B) when the PLANE of the coil is PARALLEL to B,
  and ZERO when the plane of the coil is PERPENDICULAR to B.
```

### Q B15
Distinguish between the Biot-Savart law and Ampere's circuital law.

**ANSWER:**

| Biot-Savart law | Ampere's circuital law |
|---|---|
| Gives the field of a **small current element** | Gives the **line integral** of B round a closed loop |
| `dB = (mu0/4pi) I dl sin(theta)/r^2` | `INT B.dl = mu0 I_enclosed` |
| Applicable to **any** shape of conductor | Useful only for **symmetric** situations |
| Analogous to **Coulomb's law** | Analogous to **Gauss's law** |
| Integration can be very difficult | Very quick when symmetry exists |
| Used for: circular loop, arc, finite wire | Used for: straight wire, solenoid, toroid |

**Describe the construction and working of a CYCLOTRON with a neat labelled

**DIAGRAM**

**PRINCIPLE**

```
  A charged particle can be accelerated to very high energies by making it
  cross the same small potential difference again and again, in step
  ("in resonance") with an alternating voltage.
  This is possible because the time taken for one semicircle,
  t = pi m / (q B), is INDEPENDENT of the speed and of the radius.
```

**CONSTRUCTION**

```
  * Two hollow semicircular metal boxes called DEES (D1, D2), separated
    by a small gap.
  * A high-frequency alternating voltage applied across the dees.
  * A strong electromagnet whose field B is perpendicular to the plane
    of the dees.
  * An ion source S at the centre of the gap.
  * The whole assembly in an evacuated chamber.
```

**WORKING**

```
  1.  A positive ion produced at S is attracted by whichever dee is
      negative at that instant, and crosses the gap, gaining energy qV.
  2.  Inside a dee there is NO electric field (a hollow conductor
      shields its interior), so only the magnetic field acts, bending
      the ion into a SEMICIRCLE of radius  r = m v / (q B).
  3.  The time for that semicircle,  pi m / (q B), is the same for every
      speed. By the time the ion returns to the gap, the AC has reversed
      its polarity, so the ion is accelerated AGAIN.
  4.  Each crossing adds qV of energy, so v increases and hence r
      increases - the path is an outward SPIRAL of semicircles.
  5.  At the largest radius R the beam is pulled out by a deflecting
      plate and used to bombard a target.
```

**RESONANCE CONDITION (cyclotron frequency)**

```
      Time period of revolution   T  =  2 pi m / ( q B )

      The oscillator frequency must equal

                       q B
             f_c  =  ---------
                      2 pi m
```

**MAXIMUM KINETIC ENERGY**

```
  At the outermost radius R :

                 m v_max                     q B R
        R  =  ------------      ->  v_max = --------
                  q B                          m

                  1                 1        q^2 B^2 R^2
      KE_max  =  --- m v_max^2  =  --- m x -------------
                  2                 2           m^2

  +-------------------------------------------------+
  |                     q^2  B^2  R^2               |
  |        KE_max  =  -----------------             |
  |                          2 m                    |
  +-------------------------------------------------+

      Number of revolutions  n  =  KE_max / ( 2 q V )
```

**LIMITATIONS**

```
  1.  Cannot accelerate ELECTRONS - they become relativistic almost at
      once and fall out of resonance.
  2.  Cannot accelerate NEUTRAL particles such as neutrons (q = 0).
  3.  At very high speeds the relativistic mass increase
      m = m0 / sqrt(1 - v^2/c^2) increases T, destroying the resonance.
  4.  Very high energies would need an impractically large and
      expensive magnet.
```

### Q C2
**Describe the construction and working of a MOVING COIL GALVANOMETER. Derive
the relation between current and deflection. Define current sensitivity and
voltage sensitivity. How is it converted into an ammeter and a voltmeter?**

**MODEL ANSWER — mark split**

```
  Diagram (labelled) ..................... 2 marks
  Construction ........................... 1 mark
  Principle and working .................. 1 mark
  Derivation  N I A B = C phi ............ 2 marks
  Sensitivities / radial field ........... 1 mark
  Ammeter and voltmeter conversion ....... 1 mark
```

**DIAGRAM**

```
                     TORSION HEAD
                         ___
                        |___|
                          |
                          |  PHOSPHOR BRONZE SUSPENSION
                          |
              +-----------+-----------+
       +------|-----------------------|------+
       |      |    +-------------+    |      |
       |      |    |  SOFT IRON  |    |      |
  N    |  ((  |    |    CORE     |    |  ))  |    S
 pole  |      |    | (cylinder)  |    |      |  pole
       |      |    +-------------+    |      |
       |      |         COIL          |      |
       +------|-----------------------|------+
              +-----------+-----------+
                          |
                          |  lower (hair) spring
                         _|_
                        |___|  terminal

        concave pole pieces  ->  RADIAL magnetic field
        a MIRROR on the suspension gives a lamp-and-scale reading
```

**PRINCIPLE**

```
  A current-carrying coil placed in a magnetic field experiences a torque
  which turns it; the twisted suspension provides an equal and opposite
  restoring torque, and the coil comes to rest where the two balance.
```

**CONSTRUCTION**

```
  * A rectangular coil of many turns of fine insulated copper wire wound
    on a light non-magnetic frame.
  * Suspended by a thin phosphor bronze strip between the CONCAVE poles
    of a strong permanent horse-shoe magnet.
  * A cylindrical SOFT IRON CORE inside the coil (does not touch it).
  * A lower hair spring to complete the circuit.
  * A small mirror on the suspension, with a lamp and scale arrangement.
```

**THEORY / DERIVATION**

```
  Deflecting torque on the coil    =   N I A B sin(theta)

  Because the field is RADIAL, the plane of the coil is always parallel
  to B, so theta = 90 and sin(theta) = 1 in every position :

  Deflecting torque    =   N I A B

  Restoring torque of the suspension  =  C phi
  (C = torsional constant, phi = deflection)

  At equilibrium :

  +---------------------------------------------------+
  |             N I A B   =   C phi                   |
  |                                                   |
  |                    C                              |
  |           I   =  --------  phi                    |
  |                   N A B                           |
  |                                                   |
  |     I is directly proportional to phi             |
  |     -> the scale is UNIFORM (LINEAR)              |
  +---------------------------------------------------+
```

**WHY A RADIAL FIELD AND A SOFT IRON CORE?**

```
  RADIAL FIELD (from the concave pole pieces)
     -> plane of the coil always parallel to B, sin(theta) = 1 always
     -> the torque does not depend on the position of the coil
     -> the deflection is proportional to I  ->  UNIFORM SCALE

  SOFT IRON CORE
     -> concentrates the field lines, makes B stronger
     -> increases the sensitivity
     -> helps make the field radial
```

**SENSITIVITY**

```
   CURRENT SENSITIVITY  =  phi / I  =  N A B / C       (div per ampere)

   VOLTAGE SENSITIVITY  =  phi / V  =  N A B / ( C G ) (div per volt)
                        =  current sensitivity / G

   FIGURE OF MERIT      k  =  I / phi  =  C / ( N A B )
                        =  the current for one division of deflection

   To increase sensitivity : increase N, A, B ; decrease C.
   NOTE: increasing N increases the current sensitivity but also
   increases G, so the VOLTAGE sensitivity may not increase.
```

**CONVERSION**

```
   TO AN AMMETER  -  shunt S in PARALLEL (low resistance)

              +----[ G ]----+                   Ig G
     ---> I --+             +---> I      S = ----------
              +----[ S ]----+                 I  -  Ig

   TO A VOLTMETER  -  resistance R in SERIES (high resistance)

              +--[ G ]--[ R ]--+                V
              |                |         R  =  ----  -  G
              |<------ V ----->|                Ig
```

### Q C3
**State the Biot-Savart law. Using it, derive an expression for the magnetic
field at a point on the axis of a circular current-carrying coil. Hence find
the field at the centre.**

**MODEL ANSWER — mark split**

```
  Statement of Biot-Savart law ......... 2 marks
  Diagram .............................. 1 mark
  Derivation ........................... 4 marks
  Field at the centre .................. 1 mark
```

**STATEMENT**

```
  The magnitude of the magnetic field dB produced at a point P by a
  current element I dl is
      (i)   proportional to I
      (ii)  proportional to dl
      (iii) proportional to sin(theta), theta being the angle between
            dl and the line joining the element to P
      (iv)  inversely proportional to r^2

              mu0      I dl sin(theta)
      dB  =  ------ x -----------------
              4 pi          r^2

  The direction of dB is perpendicular to the plane containing dl and r.
```

**DIAGRAM**

```
                        dl
                         |
                    _____|_____
                   /     .     \
                  /      |      \                      dB
                 |    a  |       |                     /|
                 |<----->|       |                    / | dB sin(alpha)
                  \      |      /                    /  |  (cancels)
                   \_____|_____/                    /   |
                         |    \  r                 *----+
                         |      \                  P  dB cos(alpha)
                         O--------*------------------>  axis
                         |<-- x ->|                     (adds)
                      centre
```

**DERIVATION**

```
  STEP 1  Every element dl of the loop is perpendicular to the line
          joining it to P, so theta = 90 and sin(theta) = 1.

              r  =  sqrt( a^2 + x^2 )

              mu0     I dl              mu0        I dl
       dB  = ------ x ------   =      ------ x -------------
              4 pi     r^2             4 pi     a^2 + x^2

  STEP 2  Resolve dB into a component ALONG the axis, dB cos(alpha),
          and one PERPENDICULAR to the axis, dB sin(alpha).

  STEP 3  For every element there is a diametrically opposite element
          whose perpendicular component is equal and opposite.
          Hence ALL perpendicular components CANCEL, and only the
          AXIAL components add up.

  STEP 4                a                     a
          cos(alpha) = ---  =  ---------------------------
                        r        sqrt( a^2 + x^2 )

  STEP 5  Integrate the axial components round the loop :

                                mu0      I                     a
       B  =  INT dB cos(alpha) = ---- x ------------- x ---------------- INT dl
                                4 pi     a^2 + x^2      sqrt(a^2+x^2)

              INT dl  =  2 pi a

              mu0        I a  ( 2 pi a )
          =  ------ x -----------------------
              4 pi     ( a^2 + x^2 )^(3/2)

  +---------------------------------------------------------+
  |                        mu0  N  I  a^2                   |
  |            B     =   ----------------------             |
  |                       2 ( a^2 + x^2 )^(3/2)             |
  |                                                         |
  |   directed ALONG THE AXIS, by the right hand rule       |
  +---------------------------------------------------------+
```

**FIELD AT THE CENTRE (put x = 0)**

```
               mu0 N I a^2        mu0 N I a^2         mu0 N I
       B  =  ---------------- =  -------------  =   -----------
               2 ( a^2 )^(3/2)      2 a^3               2 a
```

**FAR ON THE AXIS (x >> a)**

```
              mu0 N I a^2        mu0     2 M
       B  =  -------------  =  ------ x ------      with  M = N I (pi a^2)
                 2 x^3          4 pi     x^3

   which is the field of a magnetic dipole on its axis - showing that a
   current loop behaves as a MAGNETIC DIPOLE.
```

### Q C4
**State Ampere's circuital law. Apply it to find the magnetic field (a) due to
a long straight current-carrying wire (b) inside a long solenoid (c) inside a
toroid.**

**MODEL ANSWER — mark split**

```
  Statement + explanation of the Amperian loop ....... 2 marks
  (a) straight wire ................................. 2 marks
  (b) solenoid (diagram + derivation) ............... 2 marks
  (c) toroid  (diagram + derivation) ................ 2 marks
```

**STATEMENT**

```
  The line integral of the magnetic field B around any CLOSED path is
  equal to mu0 times the total current enclosed by that path.

           INT   B . dl    =    mu0  I_enclosed
        (closed loop)

  The closed path is called an AMPERIAN LOOP. It must be chosen so that
  B is constant in magnitude on it and is either parallel or
  perpendicular to dl at every point.
```

**(a) LONG STRAIGHT WIRE**

```
                  . - - - - .
               .               .           Amperian loop = circle of
             .        (I)        .         radius r centred on the wire
               .               .           B is tangential and constant
                  . - - - - .

        INT B.dl  =  B ( 2 pi r )      and    I_enclosed  =  I

        B ( 2 pi r )  =  mu0 I

  +--------------------------------+
  |         B  =  mu0 I / (2 pi r) |     B is proportional to 1/r
  +--------------------------------+
```

**(b) LONG SOLENOID**

```
         a       b
         +-------+     rectangular Amperian loop abcd, ab = L inside
         |       |
   x  x  |x    x |  x   x   x   x   x
  ==============================================
         |  ---> B (uniform, along the axis)
  ==============================================
   .  .  |.    . |  .   .   .   .   .
         +-------+
         d       c

        along ab  =  B L          (B parallel to dl)
        along bc  =  0            (B perpendicular to dl)
        along cd  =  0            (B = 0 outside a long solenoid)
        along da  =  0            (B perpendicular to dl)

        INT B.dl  =  B L

        turns enclosed = n L , so  I_enclosed = n L I

        B L  =  mu0 n L I

  +--------------------------+
  |      B  =  mu0  n  I     |     n = turns per METRE
  +--------------------------+
```

**(c) TOROID**

```
                 _ . - - - - . _
              .'                 `.
            /     _ . - - . _      \
           |    .'    O      `.     |     Amperian loop = circle of
           |   |  <---- r ---->|    |     mean radius r through the core
           |    `.           .'     |
            \     ` - - - - '      /
              `. _           _ . '
                  ` - - - - '

        INT B.dl  =  B ( 2 pi r )      and    I_enclosed  =  N I

        B ( 2 pi r )  =  mu0 N I

  +---------------------------------+
  |      B  =  mu0 N I / (2 pi r)   |   N = TOTAL turns
  +---------------------------------+

  In the hollow interior and outside the toroid, B = 0, because those
  Amperian loops enclose zero net current.
```

### Q C5
**Derive an expression for the force on a charged particle moving in a uniform
magnetic field. Discuss its motion when it enters (a) perpendicular to the
field (b) at an angle to the field. Explain the working of a velocity
selector.**

**MODEL ANSWER — skeleton with the mark split**

```
  F = q v B sin(theta), vector form, direction rules ........ 2 marks
  (a) circular motion : r, T, f derivation .................. 3 marks
  (b) helical motion : v sin / v cos , radius, pitch ........ 2 marks
  Velocity selector : diagram + v = E/B ..................... 1 mark
```

```
  1.  FORCE
        F  =  q v B sin(theta)  ;  F = q ( v x B )
        theta is the angle between v and B.
        F = 0 when theta = 0 or 180 ;  F = qvB (max) when theta = 90.
        Direction : right hand rule for v x B (reverse it for -ve charge),
        or Fleming's left hand rule.
        The force is always perpendicular to v, so W = 0 and the SPEED
        and KINETIC ENERGY are constant.

  2.  (a) PERPENDICULAR ENTRY  ->  CIRCLE

              q v B  =  m v^2 / r     ->   r  =  m v / ( q B )

              T  =  2 pi r / v  =  2 pi m / ( q B )
              f  =  q B / ( 2 pi m )

        T is independent of the speed and of the radius.

      (b) OBLIQUE ENTRY  ->  HELIX

              v cos(theta) along B  ->  no force  ->  uniform drift
              v sin(theta) across B ->  circular motion

              r     = m v sin(theta) / ( q B )
              T     = 2 pi m / ( q B )
              pitch = v cos(theta) x T = 2 pi m v cos(theta) / ( q B )

  3.  VELOCITY SELECTOR

     +  +  +  +  +  +  +  +  +           E downward
              qE (down)
                |
        --> v  -+-  q                    B into the page
                |
              qvB (up)
     -  -  -  -  -  -  -  -  -
     x   x   x   x   x   x   x

        Undeflected when   q E  =  q v B     ->     v  =  E / B

        The selected speed is independent of q and m.
        Used at the entrance of a mass spectrometer.
```
